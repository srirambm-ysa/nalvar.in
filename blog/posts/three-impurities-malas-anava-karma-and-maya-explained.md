*Mala* means impurity, stain, husk. Saiva Siddhanta insists there are three — **Anava**, **Karma**, and **Maya** — forming the *pasa* rope introduced in the previous article. Beginners often hear “impurity” as moral condemnation; it is diagnostic physics. Each mala has substance, function, and antidote. Knowing them lets you cooperate with the only force that can remove them: *Arul* (grace) operating through ritual, practice, and suffering — all re-described as medicine.

Crucially, malas are *not* bodies. Bodies are instruments given by Maya to exhaust karma while Anava ripens. This reframes embodiment as clinic, not prison.

### 1. What ‘Mala’ Means

**Etymology:** *Mal* = darkness, cloud. The *Tirukural* uses *malam* for dross in gold. In Siddhanta, mala is that which limits the soul’s innate omniscience and bliss, making an eternal, all-pervading knower feel limited, sequential, and needy. It is *anadi* (beginningless) but not *nitya* (indestructible) — beginningless yet terminable by grace, like a seed’s dormancy ended by water.

**Why three?** Because limitation shows up three ways in experience: (1) I feel separately existent and agentive (*Anava*), (2) I feel pushed by past actions and their fruits (*Karma*), (3) I feel housed in a world of forms that both enables and distracts (*Maya*). One mala could not explain all three; three together braid the felt world.

**Rice analogy persistence:** Unmilled paddy = soul + three husks (hull = Anava, bran = Karma, husk = Maya). No husk is grain; all hide grain. Husk is not destroyed by blaming grain. Tool and heat are needed — *diksha* (initiation), *charya-kriya-yoga-jnana* (four padas), and *malaparipakam* (ripening).

### 2. The Three Malas Deep Dive

**Anava Mala — “I-ness”:** The root impurity. It is atomic, pervasive, first limiting *Shiva’s* pervasive consciousness into a point-like “I.” Function: *avarana* (concealing) and *vikshepa* (projecting). You feel separate not because you decide to, but because Anava projects agency onto you. Classical image: verdigris on copper — not added from outside, inherent to copper’s tendency to oxidize; needs polishing. Anava is *ekam* (single) per soul but manifests as egoity, selfishness, lust for doership (*ahankara*), competitive comparison, and spiritual pride (“I am advanced”). Siddhiyar calls it *sahaja mala* — congenital, not acquired. Antidote is not self-negation but *self as servant* — Appar’s hoe, Ammaiyar’s pey form. When soul acts as *adiyar*, Anava loosens without being fed.

**Karma Mala — action residue:** Every intentional act deposits a seed (*vasana*) stored in the soul-field. Siddhanta taxonomy: *sanchita* (stored heap), *prarabdha* (portion fructifying as current life), *agami* (new seeds being created now). Unlike Vedantic karma, Siddhanta treats karma as *dravya* (substance), not just momentum, and as beginningless. Its fruit is not punishment but pedagogical feedback: pleasure binds via attachment, pain via aversion — both reinforce Anava’s “I am doer/enjoyer.” The world’s inequality is thus not cruelty but curriculum differentiation: each soul gets the body that will ripen its particular heap fastest. Antidote: *nishkamya karma* (desireless action) and ritual transferred to Shiva. Offering fruit of action to Shiva (“Shivarpanam”) converts binding karma into *sattvic* momentum that purifies *chitta*.

**Maya Mala — materiality:** The “stuff” of bodies and worlds. Divided into *suddha maya* (pure, made of *Shiva tattvas* — the five — serving liberation) and *asuddha/misra maya* (impure/mixed, the remaining 31 tattvas: *vidya, kala, niyati*, etc. down to earth). Maya is not illusion; it is Shiva-Shakti’s instrument, real and necessary. Without Maya, Anava could never be made conscious enough to be removed — you need a body to feel hunger to learn non-greed. Its function is *to make the imperceptible perceivable* — to give Anava a mirror. Misuse is binding (identifying as body), proper use is scaffolding. Antidote: *Agamic* use of matter — temple, icon, mantra, ash, water — consecrated matter teaches unconsecrated matter how to carry consciousness.

How they braid: *Anava* says “I am”; *Karma* says “I did/will enjoy”; *Maya* provides “this is where.” In waking state all three operate; in dream, Maya thins; in deep sleep, only Anava remains — which is why you wake as “I again” even after forgetting karma and world.

- Vijnanakalar — one mala (Anava alone thin), e.g., advanced yogis.

- Pralayakalar — two malas (Anava + Karma), bodies dissolved at cosmic rest but seeds remain.

- Sakalar — three malas (us, embodied). All need *saktinipadam* (descent of power).

> “Anava makes the eye think it is the light; Karma scratches the eye; Maya gives the eye something to see.” — Classroom verse, Tiruvavaduthurai.

### 3. Ripening, Removal, and Daily Practice

**Malaparipakam (ripening):** Malas ripen like fruit, not like sin punished. Signs: disgust with one’s own deceit (*nirvedam*), compassion without fatigue, incapacity for triumphalism, involuntary tears in Tiruvasagam. Ripening is Shiva’s *tirodhana* (concealment) becoming *anugraha* (grace) — same power, opposite face. Beginners often try to force ripening through austerity; Siddhanta says ripening is gift observed, owed to past *sadhana* and present *sat-sanga*.

**Removal is surgical, not auto:** Grace operates via *diksha* (initiation) and the four padas. Initiation destroys *karmic* seeds in ritual logic; prolonged *charya/kriya/yoga/jnana* thins *maya* and erodes *anava*. Final *Anava* alone remains till last moment, then *Siva-yoga* burns it like last film on crystal.

**Beginner daily protocol:**

- Morning vibhuti while chanting *Namachivaya* — addresses Anava through submission gesture.

- One act of *Shivarpanam* daily — cook or work and offer fruit verbally — addresses Karma.

- Consecrate one material object — water, flower, book — by seeing it as Shakti’s form — addresses Maya.

- Evening Tiruvasagam verse reading (even one page) — monitors ripening signs without self-judgment.

4. Mala in Family and Work: Field Guide
Malas are not abstractions for retreat; they show up at breakfast.

**Anava at home:** Needing to be right in a family argument after you know you’re wrong is Anava flaring — the “I” defending its fantasy. Remedy: deliberately concede one small preference daily (“You choose dinner”), perform *dasa* act, and note internal fizz without feeding it. Fizz passes faster when watched.

**Karma at work:** A project fails despite effort; resentment says “I deserve success.” That is prarabdha fruit plus agami expectation braided. Remedy: *Shivarpanam* journal — at day’s end write “Today’s effort offered, fruit whatever Shiva gives.” Over weeks, outcome-attachment thins measurably; colleagues notice calm before you do.

**Maya in consumption:** Endless scrolling for “one more thing to complete me” is Maya as distraction, not world as evil. Remedy: consecrate device — before opening, apply vibhuti gesture to phone edge and chant *Namachivaya* once. Ritual frames maya as instrument, not master.

Community tip: share mala observations with one trusted devotee weekly, not as complaint but as diagnosis. Secrecy feeds Anava; shared naming thin it. That is why *adiyar kootam* (company of servants) is called medicine in Tevaram.
