Every seeker who tastes Saiva Siddhanta's *Pati-Pasu-Pasa* eventually asks the Vedantic question: Isn't this just Advaita in Tamil? Both claim non-dual union, both deny difference ultimately, both invoke *Brahman / Pati* as sole reality? The advanced answer is precise and liberating: Saiva Siddhanta is **Suddhadvaita** (pure non-dualism) — non-dualism *without monism*, union *without identity-loss* — while classical **Advaita Vedanta** (Sankara, 8th c.) is *Kevaladvaita / Nirvisheshadvaita* (absolute non-dualism sans difference) where individual soul (*jiva*) ultimately is Brahman (*jivo brahmaiva na aparah*) and difference is *mithya* (provisionally real). Siddhanta disagrees not to be contrarian but because *grace, love, and knowledge* as lived in Tirumurai require real distinction to remain real; dissolve distinction and you dissolve the addressee of love.

This is not word-game. Advaita says *ekam eva advitiyam* — One without second — and must then explain why experience shows many. Answer: *avidya / maya* as superimposition (*adhyasa*) causing apparent many on non-dual substrate, removed by knowledge. Siddhanta accepts Advaita's thirst for unity but relocates error: error is not thinking you are many (you *are* many as distinct souls eternally), error is thinking your *anava-identified ego* is soul itself. Remove *anava* (egoity) by Arul, and you see you were always distinct from Brahman/Pati, but *non-different in union* (*abedha in advaita*) — like salt in sea water: inseparable, non-dual in taste-pervasion, yet salt remains salt, sea remains sea. To call that monism is to mistake solution for dissolution.

Historically, this debate was not abstract: 13th–14th c. Tamilakam saw Advaita vidwans debate Meykandar's lineage at Chidambaram and Madurai; Arulnandi's *Sivajnana Siddhiyar Parapaksham* devotes 50+ verses to refuting *Mayavada* (Advaita) after refuting Nyaya, Buddhism, Samkhya. For advanced study, primary poles are [Shaivam.org — Suddhadvaita vs Advaita overview](https://shaivam.org/suddhadvaita), [WisdomLib — Advaita definition and typology](https://www.wisdomlib.org/definition/advaita), [Project Madurai — Sivajnana Siddhiyar Parapaksham on Advaita](https://www.projectmadurai.org/pm_etexts/utf8/pmuni0124.html), and [Archive.org — Siddhiyar English translation (Gordon Matthews)](https://archive.org/details/sivajnana-siddhiyar-english).

### 1. What Suddhadvaita Claims — Three Realities in One Love
Suddhadvaita's core proposition: *Pati, Pasu, Pasa* are three eternally real categories, but in *mukti* Pasu dwells in *advaita* with Pati — not as identity (*aikyam*) but as *inseparable union like eye and sunlight*. Sundarar's lens:

**(a) Pati is cit-shakti sovereign, never bound.** Siva as *Pati* is not affected by Pasa, not superimposed upon, not needing liberation. Even during soul's bondage, Pati remains *suddha* (pure) and *arul-mayam* (grace-full). This differs from Advaita's *saguna Brahman* (Ishvara) which is Brahman under Maya — ultimately sublated.

**(b) Pasu is anadi, anu, cit — beginningless, atom-like, conscious — distinct from Pati** As *anu* (infinitesimal) consciousness with *vibhu-jnana* (pervasive knowing when enabled by Arul), pasu in bondage knows sequentially and partially; in mukti knows by Arul's pervasion, but still knows as *pasu* (seer), not as Pati. Pasu never becomes Pati ontologically, even while becoming *pati-nilay-advaita* epistemologically — standing where Pati stands, seeing as Pati sees.

**(c) Pasa is also real, not mithya:** *Anava, Karma, Maya* are *dravyas* (substances) with own being, not mere misperception. Anava is *ekadesa-avarana* (veil covering part of soul's omniscience); Maya is real evolving material cause; Karma is real *adrishta* seed-store. In Advaita, maya is *anirvachaniya* (inexplicable as real/unreal); in Siddhanta, maya is real but dependent on Pati's will.

Hence *suddhadvaita* = advaita (not two as separated competitors) but suddha (pure: neither assimilates nor sublates the other). Classical image: eye and sun are non-dual in act of seeing — no seeing without both; but eye remains eye, sun remains sun. Vedantic Advaita image is wave-water: wave was always water; distinction was never real.

- *Epistemology of difference:* Siddhanta insists difference is pramana-given (perception shows many, inference shows Lord required for insentient world to be intelligible, Agamic sabda confirms three). To declare difference later illusory violates *pramana* ethics: you cannot use perception to gain teaching and then declare perception wholesale false.
- *Soteriology of love (anbu*): Thiruvasagam weeps I, dog, yet Thou tookest me — weeper requires permanent *I-Thou* distinct sufficient for love to circulate. If I am Thou already, love is theatre, not bond.
- *Grace requirement:* If soul is Brahman already, grace is redundant recognition-tool only. Siddhanta's Arul is operative power needing real patient; monism makes grace decorative.

> Tirumandhiram 2066: Anbe Sivam — Love is Siva. To be love, there must be lover, beloved, and loving — three, yet one flowing. That is Suddhadvaita. (gisted).

### 2. How Advaita Vedanta Argues — And Where Siddhanta Presses
Respectfully stated, Advaita's argument runs: Brahman alone is *paramarthika* (ultimately real); jiva and world are *vyavaharika* (empirically real) and *pratibhasika* (apparent), caused by *avidya*. *Mahavakyas* Tat Tvam Asi, Aham Brahmasmi reveal identity; *neti neti* removes attributes; *adhyasa-bhashya* shows superimposition; *jivanmukti* is realization of always-was identity.

Sivajnana Siddhiyar's Parapaksham + Mapadiyam press four rejoinders, worth weighing beyond polemic:

**(1) Who is under avidya?** If Brahman is pure consciousness without parts, avidya cannot reside in Brahman (would taint purity). If it resides in jiva, jiva already required pre-existent to lodge avidya — circular. Siddhanta's three-realities avoid this by lodging ignorance (anava) in *pasu* and *mala*, not in Pati. Advaita replies avidya is *anirvachaniya* — Siddhanta counters that inexplicable is not explanation but deferral.

**(2) If jiva is Brahman, who is liberated?** Advaita: apparent jiva liberated from apparent bondage. Siddhanta: this makes liberation apparent too — trivial. Tevaram's tears at forgiveness presuppose real bond, real release. Apare vidya that liberates must act on real patient.

**(3) Scripture as pramana:** Advaita takes mahavakyas as *akhandartha* (impartite identity). Siddhanta reads Tat Tvam Asi as *tadatmya-advaita* — you are of That, in non-difference as belonging, not you are That as equation. Tamil usage Ennove en Thane (you are mine, I am yours) signals inseparable belonging, not numerical oneness. Sivagra Yogi cites Tirukkural's anbin vazhiyatu uyir nilai — life abides in love — as semantic control.

**(4) Experience of multiplicity persists post-knowledge:** Advaitin living jivanmukta still eats, teaches, distinguishes disciple from non-disciple — behaving as if many real. If Brahman alone real, why behavior retains duality? Advaita answers for loka-vyavahara. Siddhanta: if action retains duality effortlessly, perhaps duality is real and non-dual is mode of relation, not ontology.

- This dispute is not anti-Vedanta but *Veda-Agama synthesis*: Siddhanta calls itself *Vaidika-Saiva*, retaining Vedic *Rudra* but interpreting Upanishadic sentences via Agama-pramana (Siva as Pati reveals three, not one).
- Modern misreading: Suddhadvaita sometimes confused with Vallabha's Suddhadvaita (Krishna-centric) — Tamil Siddhanta's suddhadvaita is distinct lineage via Meykandar, not Vallabha lineage; both use same Sanskrit term with different referent. Clarify citation.

> If identity alone liberated, teaching would be one word: You are Him. Why 12 sutras, why temple, why Guru's feet that teach? Because relation liberates, not equation. — paraphrase of Siddhiyar Supaksha 210 on mahavakya.

### 3. Living Non-Dual Without Monism — Practice, Hymn, and temple Today
For the advanced practitioner, difference between monisms is lived, not merely logged:

**Hymn test:** Sing Tiruvasagam  Arutperunjothi or Sambandar's Thodudaya Seviyan as Advaitin (I am already That) vs as Siddhantin (I am Thine, Thou art near, make me Thine fully). Notice bodily tone: first closes with quiet satisfaction, second opens with aching surrender that still moves hands to serve. Which births service?

**Meditation test:** In silent sitting, ask Who knows breath? Advaitin rests as awareness self-luminous; Siddhantin rests as *soul knowing by Arul's light* — includes gratitude for light. Both stillness, second adds *bhava* of being seen.

**Temple test:** Circumambulate linga seeing linga as Siva's sign (Siddhanta) vs seeing linga as reminder I am Siva (Advaita). First keeps *pradakshina* as relational orbiting; second risks orbiting self. Siddhanta linga is *other* who invites approach without consumption.

Ethically, suddhadvaita yields service without saviour-complex and humility without self-denigration: Because soul remains soul, service remains meaningful (real other needs real care); because soul stands in Arul, pride remains pointless (care is borrowed light). Advaita yields fine universal compassion (all is Self), but Siddhanta argues that compassion that sees other as myself may subtly appropriate other; compassion that sees other as Siva's distinct beloved guards otherness.

- *Reading plan:* Week 1: Siddhiyar Parapaksham on Mayavadin (verses 165–230) with Shri R. Arunachalam's English notes (Archive.org). Week 2: Siddhiyar Supaksha on Suddhadvaita (verses 350–400). Week 3: Bodham Sutra 1–2 with Mapadiyam on Pati existence vs Advaita nirguna. Keep hymn daily.
- *Debate without hatred:* Siddhanta's refutation genre is *karuna* — showing why Advaita saint still lovable but philosophy incomplete. Learn to argue without othering opponent — advaita as friend who stopped one station early.
- *Integration:* Study Shankara's Vivekachudamani Ch. 1–3 and Umapati's Tiruvarutpayan Ch. 9 side by side; note both praise Guru, but one Guru removes veil, other Guru bestows eye.

> Sivajnana Bodham Sutra 11: As soul knows by Arul, it stands as Arul, non-different as eye by sun, yet eye remains eye. That standing is mukti — not becoming sun.

To abide non-dual without erasing face of beloved — that is Tamil Siddhanta's gift to Vedanta-rich modern Hinduism. Learn it not to win debate but to weep more accurately, serve more steadily, and know more humbly whose light lets you know.
