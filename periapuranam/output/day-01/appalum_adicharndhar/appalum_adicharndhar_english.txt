Chapter 01 — Appalum AdiCharndhar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Part 1009, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Paththarai Panivar Sarukkam, Appalum Adicharnthar Puranam. This section describes the qualities of the devotees known as Appalum Adicharnthar, forming the seventh Puranam in the eleventh Sarukkam, Paththarai Panivar Sarukkam, of the Periyapuranam composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Firstly, in the collection of works, our Lord Sundaramurthy Swamigal, in the tenth verse of Thiruthondathogai, has sung, "Appalum Adicharnthar Adiyarkkum Adiyen" (I am a servant to the servants of those who have attained beyond). Here, "Appalum" signifies "beyond place and time." Thus, the devotees who have attained the sacred feet of Lord Shiva, who is beyond place and time, are the collective devotees known as Appalum Adicharnthar. The meaning here is, "I am a servant to the servants of such devotees." Therefore, "Appalum" refers to both time and space, meaning "beyond both." "Beyond time" is what we are contemplating. We see that the devotees praised in Thiruthondathogai, composed by our Lord Sundaramurthy Swamigal, have their Puranam extensively elaborated as Periyapuranam by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Therefore, the devotees who attained the sacred feet of Lord Shiva before and after the time of the devotees praised in Thiruthondathogai, composed by our Lord Sundaramurthy Swamigal, are those who are beyond time. Now, regarding devotees who are beyond place, or beyond country, it refers to those residing outside the Tamil-speaking land governed by the Three Kings, which is said to have a total of 18 languages. Beyond the Tamil-speaking land governed by the Three Kings, there are 17 other language-speaking countries, and even beyond those countries, there are devotees who praise Lord Shiva and have attained His sacred feet. These are the devotees who are beyond country. Thus, Sundaramurthy Swamigal has sung, "I am a servant to the servants of those devotees who are beyond time and space." Now, in the classified work, Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi, Nambiandar Nambigal Peruman has praised the Appalum Adicharntha devotees in the 76th verse. That verse is as follows: "Varukkam adaiththu nannavalur mannavan van thamizhal perukka madhuraththogai piraisoodi than peykazharke orukku manaththodu appal adicharnthavar endru ulagil therikkum avar sivan palkanaththor nam sezhunthavare." The meaning of the verse is: "The king of good Navalur, Nambigal Sundaramurthy Swamigal, with his rich Tamil, has extensively sung this sweet Thiruthondathogai, stating that those who, with a unified mind, attained the sacred feet of the Lord, who wears the crescent moon, are known in the world as Appalum Adicharnthar. They are the numerous ganas of Shiva, our exalted ascetics." Therefore, it is understood that those who are called "Appal Adicharnthavargal" (those who have attained beyond) by place and time, due to their advanced penance, are indeed the Shiva ganas. Such collective devotees are praised as Appalum Adicharnthargal. Now, in the extensive work, Periyapuranam, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, the Puranam of Appalum Adicharnthar is the 64th Puranam in sequence. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman has composed the Puranam of Appalum Adicharnthar in one verse. We see that in Thiruthondathogai, 60 individual devotees are praised by our Lord Sundaramurthy Swamigal. In addition, Nambiandar Nambigal has sung about 63 individual devotees, including Sundaramurthy Swamigal's divine mother Isaignaniyar, his divine father Sadaiyanar, and our Lord Sundaramurthy Swamigal himself. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman has composed the history of these individual devotees in the extensive work as their Puranam. Furthermore, in Thiruthondathogai, the collective devotees are praised as nine collective devotees. Among them, Appalum Adicharnthar are the ninth collective devotees. Therefore, according to Thiruthondathogai, the Puranam of the collective devotees concludes with this. The last of the collective devotees are Appalum Adicharnthargal. Since they are praised as those who have attained the sacred feet of Lord Shiva, transcending time and country, this Puranam praises all such devotees who have attained the sacred feet of Lord Shiva. Thus, Periyapuranam becomes a complete Puranam that speaks of the glory and greatness of all the sacred devotees who have attained the sacred feet of the Lord. Now, the one verse in Appalum Adicharnthar Puranam is the 4169th verse in the Periyapuranam series. "Moovendhar Thamizh vazhangum naattukku appal muthalvanar adicharntha muraimaiyorum Naavendha Thiruthondathogaiyil koorum nattrondar kaalaththu munnum pinnum Poovendha nedunjadaimel adambu thumbai puthiya madhi nadhi ithazhi poruntha vaiththa Sevendhu velkodiyan adicharnthargal seppiya appalum adicharnthar thaame" is the verse. It means: "Those who, beyond the Tamil-speaking lands of the Three Kings, have attained the sacred feet of the Lord, and those who, before and after the time of the good devotees mentioned in the Thiruthondathogai, composed by the divine tongue of Sundaramurthy Swamigal, have attained the sacred feet of Lord Shiva, who has placed the Adambu flower, Thumbai flower, new crescent moon, river Ganga, and Konrai flower on His long, flower-laden matted hair, and who has a victorious flag with the emblem of the bull – these are the ones praised as Appalum Adicharnthar." Here, the verse refers to "beyond the Tamil-speaking lands of the Three Kings." There were also non-Tamil speaking lands under the rule of the Three Kings. Hence, it was stated thus. Moreover, most of the devotees praised in Thiruthondathogai belonged to Tamil-speaking lands. Therefore, there are also devotees who attained the feet of Lord Shiva in other 17 language-speaking countries, which are not Tamil-speaking lands. Thus, these are praised as those who, in terms of country, are beyond the Tamil-speaking country and have attained the feet of Lord Shiva. Now, secondly, regarding "beyond time," it refers to the time of the individual devotees mentioned in this Thiruppathigam, Thiruthondathogai, and the time before and after that period. By considering the time of the devotees praised in Thiruthondathogai as the present, those who attained the sacred feet of Lord Shiva in the past, present, and future are also considered Appal Adicharnthavargal. Thiruvengattu Adigal and others are devotees praised in this category. Therefore, the quality of the collective devotees known as Appalum Adicharnthar, as described in the Appalum Adicharnthar Puranam, is that they are those who attained the feet of Lord Shiva in countries beyond the Tamil-speaking lands of the Three Kings, and also before and after the time of the devotees praised in Thiruthondathogai. This Puranam thus concludes. With this sacred Puranam, the sacred Puranam of the nine collective devotees also concludes here. We have been observing that Periyapuranam consists of 13 Sarukkams, with one Sarukkam for each verse of Thiruthondathogai, making 11 Sarukkams in the middle, and two Sarukkams at the beginning and end. Among them, the 11th Sarukkam, Paththarai Panivar Sarukkam, corresponds to the tenth verse, which begins with "Paththarai Panivargal." In the tenth verse, Sundaramurthy Swamigal concludes by singing, "Aruran Aruril Ammanukku Aale." Accordingly, in Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi and in Periyapuranam composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, we see the praise of Sundaramurthy Swamigal at this point. In that context, the concluding part of the tenth verse is "Aruran Aruril Ammanukku Aale." It means, "I, Nambi Arurar, am not only a servant to the collective devotees mentioned earlier, but also a servant, a slave, to the Lord residing in Thiruvarur." Now, at this point, the 77th verse in the classified work, Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi, composed by Nambiandar Nambigal Peruman, praises Sundaramurthy Swamigal. That verse is as follows: "Suzhunir vayal mudukunrinil senthamizh paadi veyya Mazhu nil thadakkaiyan eentha pon aangu kollathu vantha Pozhil nil tharu thiruvaruril vaasiyum ponnum kondon Kezhu nil pugazh thiruvaruran endru naam ketpathuve." That verse refers to the history of our Lord Sundaramurthy Swamigal, who, after singing to the Lord in Thirumudukunram, received 12,000 gold coins, and then, at the Lord's request, placed them in the Thirumanimuthu river there, and by the Lord's grace, received them back in the Kamalalayam pond in Thiruvarur. When he received them, the purity was less, and then by divine grace, he received them back as gold of unreduced purity. We have extensively discussed this in the Puranam of Eiyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar. Therefore, the meaning here is that Nambi Arurar, who has abundant and lasting fame, is the one who sang the pure Tamil pathigam in Thirumudukunram, surrounded by fertile fields, and did not accept the gold given by the Lord, who held the desirable battle-axe in His large hand, but received that gold of unreduced purity in Thiruvarur. This is the truth we learn from the words of the elders. Now, the extensive work, Periyapuranam, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, this Sarukkam, Paththarai Panivar Sarukkam, is the 11th Sarukkam. The praise of Sundaramurthy Swamigal, which concludes this Sarukkam, is the 4170th verse in Periyapuranam. The verse is: "Settrartham puram eriththa silaiyar selva thirumurugan poondinil sellum pothil Suttrarum silai vedar kavarnthu konda thogu nithiyin parappellam sumanthu kondu Muttratha mulai umaiyal paagan bhootha mutharkaname udan sella mudiya peru pettar tham kazhal parava adiyen munnai piravinil seytha thavam periyavame." It means: "When our Lord Sundaramurthy Swamigal was traveling on the path to Thirumuruganpoondi, rich in wealth, of Lord Shiva, who burned the three cities of His enemies with His mountain bow, along with his retinue and devotees, the surrounding bow-wielding hunters seized and plundered all the vast treasures. The chief of the Bhootha ganas, who is the part of Umaiyal with unblemished breasts, Himself carried all those treasures and accompanied him. To praise the sacred feet of Nambi Arurar Sundaramurthy Swamigal, who received such immeasurable great blessings, the penances I performed in my previous births are indeed great." Thus, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman praises Sundaramurthy Swamigal, composing this praise of Sundaramurthy Nayanar. In this way, the Appalum Adicharnthar Puranam concludes, and the Paththarai Panivar Sarukkam concludes. Next, the 12th Sarukkam is Manniya Seer Sarukkam. The first Puranam in it is Poosalar Nayanar Puranam. We will contemplate that next. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of Appalum Adicharnthar! Praise, praise to the sacred feet of our Lord Sundaramurthy Swamigal! Praise, praise to the sacred feet of Nambiandar Nambigal Peruman! Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the divine Sekkizhar Peruman! Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the commentator Aiyaa! Thiruchitrambalam.
