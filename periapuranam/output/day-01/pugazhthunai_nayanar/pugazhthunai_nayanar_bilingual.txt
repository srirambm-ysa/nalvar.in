# English

Chapter 01 — PugazhThunai Nayanar

Tiruchitrambalam. The Periya Puranam, Part 991, of the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, the chapter on the Sea-Girt World, the Puranam of PugazhThunai Nayanar. Following the Puranam of Seruthunai Nayanar in the divine Sekkizhar Peruman's Periya Puranam, the Puranam of PugazhThunai Nayanar is presented as the 56th Puranam. Firstly, in the Thogai Nool, the Thiruthondathogai composed by our Lord Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, he praises PugazhThunai Nayanar in the ninth verse. He praises him saying, "I am a servant to PugazhThunai, whose mind was set on the golden feet of the Lord, who dances with a snake on the tiger skin around his waist." The meaning is, "I am a servant to the Nayanar named PugazhThunai. Such a PugazhThunai Nayanar was one whose mind was set on the golden feet of the Lord, who dances with a snake on the tiger skin around his waist. PugazhThunai Nayanar's mind was set only on the golden feet of the Lord. The Lord wears a tiger skin garment around his waist, and a snake dances upon it as he performs his sacred dance. PugazhThunai Nayanar's mind was set on those golden feet, and I am his servant." "Golden feet" refers to the sacred feet of the Lord. The word "golden" signifies preciousness, as we see in phrases like "pon poonda ponnaarndha" (adorned with gold, golden) praising the sacred feet. We see in the Thevaram of Thirunavukkarasar Peruman, "Ponnaar Thiruvadikku ondru undu vinnappam" (There is one request to the golden feet). PugazhThunai Nayanar was one whose mind was set on the golden feet of the Lord. Therefore, he was one who did not set his mind on anything else, but considered the Lord's sacred feet as his only refuge, and served without thinking of anything else. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal praises him, saying, "I am a servant to him." Now, in the Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi, which is a Vagai Nool, the 67th verse praises PugazhThunai Nayanar. "Seruvilipputhur PugazhThunai, when the world suffered from famine, his body weakened and without food, he performed ablutions for Umaikkon (Lord Shiva). When his hand grew tired and the vessel slipped and fell, he, who was a mountain of grace, received a coin daily." This is the verse. PugazhThunai Nayanar was born into the sacred lineage of Shiva Brahmins, the Adisaiva tradition, and excelled greatly in the inner service of Lord Shiva. He worshipped Lord Shiva according to the principles of Tattva and the rules of the Shivagamas. While he was performing Parartha Puja for the Lord in the temple, a famine (varkadam) came, and poverty struck. He suffered from hunger. Even then, he resolved not to abandon the sacred service of puja to the Lord, and continued to worship day and night with flowers and water without fail. One day, greatly weakened by hunger, while performing the sacred ablution (Thirumanjanam) for the Lord, he was unable to hold the Thirumanjana vessel. It slipped from his hand and fell upon the Lord's sacred head. He himself trembled and fell. At that moment, by the grace of the Lord, sleep came to him. The Lord appeared in his dream and blessed him, saying, "Throughout this period of poverty, we will give you one coin daily." PugazhThunai Nayanar woke up from his sleep, found a gold coin beneath the pedestal, and continued his Shiva Puja without fail, using that coin. The Lord continued to give him a coin daily until the period of famine and poverty ended. Thus, PugazhThunai Nayanar worshipped the Lord. Nambiandar Nambigal Peruman explains this sacred history in this verse. Seruvilipputhur is the sacred place where PugazhThunai Nayanar was born and lived. The meaning of this verse is that when a famine came to the world, PugazhThunai, who came from Seruvilipputhur, his body weakened and without food, suffered. While performing Thirumanjanam for Lord Shiva, his hand grew tired, and the water vessel fell upon the Lord's sacred head. He suffered greatly, and at that time, by the grace of the Lord, he received a coin daily. Now, in the Periya Puranam, the extensive work by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, the Puranam of PugazhThunai Nayanar is explained in seven verses by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, as the 56th sacred Puranam in sequence. It is the fourth Puranam in the 10th chapter, the chapter on the Sea-Girt World. The first verse is: "In Seruvilipputhur, among the sacred lineage of Shiva Brahmins, who are like mountains, there is no one equal to him in this world in the service of the Lord. His fame spreads far and wide, and he is known by the name PugazhThunai." Seruvilipputhur is a sacred place. It is a Thiruppathi (sacred place) praised in the Thevarams of the Three Saints. It is also known as Arisilkkarai Puthur. They also call it Azhagathiri Puthur, Azhagar Puthur. It is the 66th sacred place on the south bank of the Kaveri. The Lord's name is Padikkasu Vaitha Paramar (The Lord who placed the coin). The Goddess's name is Azhagammaiyar. It is a place that has received three Thiruppathigams. It is located south of Kumbakonam, on the way to Kudavayil. PugazhThunai Nayanar was born into the sacred lineage of Shiva Brahmins, the Adisaiva Shivacharyar lineage, who reside in that sacred place of Seruvilipputhur. Therefore, the sacred service of touching the Lord's form and performing Parartha Puja in temples is a privilege belonging only to the Adisaiva Shivacharyar lineage. PugazhThunai Nayanar was unparalleled in this inner service to Lord Shiva. He was known by the name PugazhThunai, whose unique fame spread far and wide in this world. He was incomparable in the inner service and devotion to the Lord. Inner service includes touching the Lord's form, performing Thirumanjanam, adorning, offering food, worshipping, and cleaning the sanctum sanctorum. In this service, he was unparalleled in this world, and his fame spread far and wide. Therefore, the name PugazhThunai (support of fame) is a descriptive and appropriate name. The second verse: "While worshipping their Lord through penance and according to the principles of Tattva, when famine struck this vast world, and hunger afflicted him, he said, 'I will not abandon my Lord,' and day and night, he worshipped with fragrant flowers and cool water." He worshipped his Lord through penance and according to the principles of Tattva. Penance here refers to Shiva Puja. Worshipping according to the principles of Tattva means that the Shivagamas, which teach the path to salvation by placing all things in their respective Tattvas and realizing the truth, are followed. Therefore, during the days when he performed Shiva Puja, which is penance, daily and without fail according to the Shivagama rules, a famine (varkadam) came to this world. Varkadam means famine, a period of scarcity. This famine enveloped the world. During this period of famine and poverty, PugazhThunai Nayanar suffered greatly from hunger, being without food. Nevertheless, he said, "I will not abandon the sacred puja of my Lord," and day and night, he worshipped with many fragrant flowers and cool water. He worshipped with flowers and water. "For those who do good deeds, there are flowers and water," says Thirumooladevar Nayanar in his Thirumandhiram. "Carrying flowers and water and praising," says Thirunavukkarasu Peruman. Thus, he performed the puja called Archana. The third verse: "When he was performing Thirumanjanam for the Lord, who is unknowable to Vishnu and Brahma, he was greatly afflicted by the disease of hunger and became weak. Unable to hold the vessel filled with sacred water, he let it fall upon the head of the Lord with the blue throat, and then he collapsed." When he was performing Thirumanjanam for the Lord, who is difficult for Thirumal and Brahma to find, PugazhThunai Nayanar was greatly afflicted by the disease of hunger and became weak. The Thirumanjana vessel contained sacred water, adorned with flowers, Arugu grass, etc., and purified by mantras. It was a vessel of pure and sacred Thirumanjana water. While holding this vessel and performing Thirumanjanam for the Lord, he became weak due to hunger and was unable to hold the water-filled Thirumanjana vessel. He let the vessel fall upon the Lord's sacred head. He became very weary and collapsed. The fourth verse: "By the grace of Shankaran, sleep came upon him. The Lord, in his dream, bestowed his grace, saying, 'Until the days of scarcity of food are over, we will place one coin daily for you here.' He rose, his distress gone." In that state of physical exhaustion and collapse, by the grace of Shankaran, sleep came upon him. Shankaran means "one who bestows happiness." This refers to Lord Shiva. The name Shankaran is attributed only to Lord Shiva. When PugazhThunai Nayanar collapsed from physical exhaustion, sleep came upon him by the grace of the Lord. Sleep came upon him. When performing Shiva Puja, sleep, exhaustion, and laziness should not occur. If they do, atonement must be performed, according to the Shivagama rules. Therefore, here, sleep came upon PugazhThunai Nayanar during Shiva Puja. It came by the grace of the Lord. Therefore, to indicate that this is not a fault, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman explains it as "by the grace of Shankaran, sleep came upon him." We saw in the Puranam of Naminandi Adigal Nayanar that sleep came upon him by the grace of the Lord when he went to bathe for puja. In the sacred history of our Lord Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, we saw that when he did not receive the gold he requested in Thiruppugalur, sleep came upon Sundaramoorthy Swamigal in the temple courtyard due to the grace of the Lord. We reflected there that all these were states that came by grace. Similarly, here too, sleep came upon PugazhThunai Nayanar by the grace of the Lord. Sleep came upon him. In that sleep, the Lord bestowed his grace in a dream. He said, "Until the period of scarcity of food is over, we will place one coin daily for you here." He blessed him, saying, "Until this period of famine and poverty, when there is a shortage of food, ends, I will place one coin for you every day." We saw that when a period of poverty came to the sacred place of Thiruveezhimizhalai, our great saints, Aludaiya Pillaiyar Thirugnanasambandar Peruman and Aludaiya Arasugal Thirunavukkarasu Peruman, were each given one coin daily by the Lord. We saw that the Lord continued to provide this until the period of poverty ended, and rain fell, and food became abundant. Famine and scarcity are given by the Lord as a result of the karmic experiences of living beings. The Lord here blesses them by providing a coin daily until that accumulated karmic state is resolved. Therefore, it is understood that the suffering from these karmic effects does not affect those who are devoted to Shiva. When such situations arise, when poverty, famine, or scarcity come, the Lord himself will provide a solution and protect those who are devoted to Shiva, as the Jnana Shastras state. The fifth verse: "The Lord, who delights in riding the bull, placed a coin beneath the pedestal. The devotee, taking it in hand, and though his body was withered by great hunger, rejoiced with a radiant face, filled with complete knowledge." The Lord, who delights in riding the bull, places a gold coin beneath the pedestal. He gives a gold coin to alleviate suffering. PugazhThunai Nayanar takes it in his hand. Although his body was withered by great hunger, and the suffering of hunger was only physical for him, he rejoiced upon taking the gold coin bestowed by the Lord. He rejoiced, realizing that the Lord had blessed him as revealed in his dream. Such a devotee, PugazhThunai Nayanar, was filled with complete knowledge. "Filled with complete knowledge" means that when the karmic state of living beings and the state of the Lord's grace are understood without attachment, and when one clings to Him, the state of the Lord's grace is revealed. This refers to the mature state of understanding these philosophical truths, which is complete knowledge, a great realization. "Thalai nirambudhal" means to be clearly manifest, to be abundant. Taking the gold coin given by the Lord, he rejoiced with a radiant face, filled with such great realization. Thirugnanasambandar Peruman has praised this miraculous event of divine grace in his Thevaram on Arisilkkarai Puthur. "The Lord, who is the nature of earth, water, fire, air, and ether, made a spider into a Chola king with red eyes. For the devotee who suffered, He gave a coin each day. In the morning and evening, they praise Puthur." This is the saying of Thirugnanasambandar Peruman. In this, Thirugnanasambandar Peruman has sung in his sacred words about this miraculous event of divine grace, where the Lord gave a coin daily to the devotee, PugazhThunai Nayanar. Our Lord Sundaramoorthy Swamigal has sung about this in the sixth verse of his Thevaram on Arisilkkarai Puthur. That verse is: "The Brahmin who performs inner service brings water from Arisil and performs ablutions. When he becomes very weak and lets the vessel fall upon the head, and trembles, You, the holy one of Thirupputhur, who is pleased, made a coin come into PugazhThunai's hand, saying that a daily allowance will come for him." In the sixth verse, our Lord Sundaramoorthy Swamigal has sung about how the Lord bestowed grace upon PugazhThunai Nayanar and the greatness of PugazhThunai Nayanar. We see this miraculous event of divine grace specifically indicated in the words of Thirunavukkarasu Peruman, "Praising the holy one of Thirupputhur with devotion, all who serve will see the path of righteousness," and "His body is like pure gold." The sixth verse: "Just as on that day, with the coin given daily, he performed the sacred puja until the painful disease of hunger and the famine passed. After that, he performed the true service of devotion to the Lord with the radiant red matted hair, and attained the sacred feet of the Lord, worshipped by the celestials in the golden world." The Lord gave a gold coin. Just as on that first day, the Lord gave a coin daily. With that coin, he performed the sacred puja until the painful hunger and the famine that caused distress passed. After that famine ended, he continued to perform the inner service, the true service of devotion, to the Lord with the radiant red matted hair. PugazhThunai Nayanar attained the sacred feet of Lord Shiva, worshipped even by the celestials in the heavenly world. The state of liberation attained by the Nayanmars is worthy of worship even by the celestials. PugazhThunai Nayanar attained such a state of liberation. In a temple, the service performed with inner devotion, training the external organs of the body and the external senses to remain in the path of Shiva, such as sweeping and cleaning, is the service of Chariya. This is the state of external service. At the same time, the state of performing Kriya services like Abhishekham and Archana with inner devotion, training the inner organs like the mind and intellect to remain in the path of Shiva, is the state of inner service. This takes place within the sanctum sanctorum, the inner core of the temple. It is about approaching Shiva, who is the inner core of the inner core, through inner effort. Therefore, it is called inner service. In that inner service, even in a state of complete helplessness and weakness due to famine, PugazhThunai Nayanar, with the resolve, "I will not abandon my Lord," continued to perform Abhishekham and Archana. The steadfastness and integrity of his service, and the compassion of Lord Shiva, who specifically provided him with a gold coin daily to uphold the steadfastness of his service, are all worthy of our understanding and praise. That is why our Lord Thirugnanasambandar Peruman, our Lord Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, and Appar Swamigal have praised PugazhThunai Nayanar and the miracle the Lord performed for him in their sacred words. Such a PugazhThunai Nayanar always abides in the liberation of the Lord's sacred feet. The seventh and concluding verse: "Praising the sacred feet of PugazhThunai, whose mind was a companion to the Lord's sacred feet, which are embraced by the Goddess with soft fingers, we will now speak of the deeds of Kotpuli Nayanar, the unique hero with shoulders adorned with garlands of fragrant flowers." Thus, praising and blessing the sacred feet of PugazhThunai Nayanar, and with his support, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman says that he will now speak of the sacred service of Kotpuli Nayanar. He concludes this sacred Puranam of PugazhThunai Nayanar and begins to narrate the sacred Puranam of Kotpuli Nayanar. We too, bowing at the sacred feet of PugazhThunai Nayanar, and with his support, will continue to reflect on the sacred Puranam of Kotpuli Nayanar. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of our Lord PugazhThunai Nayanar. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of our Lord Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of Nambiandar Nambigal Peruman. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the commentator, Sivakavimani Ayya. Tiruchitrambalam.
