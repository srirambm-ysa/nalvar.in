Chapter 01 — Narasinga Munaiyaraiya Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Part 972, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. The Puranam of Narasinga Munaiyaraiya Nayanar from the Poiyadimai Illatha Pulavar Sarukkam. The Puranam of Narasinga Munaiyaraiya Nayanar is the third puranam in the eighth sarukkam, Poiyadimai Illatha Pulavar Sarukkam, of the Periyapuranam composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. The seventh verse of the Thiruthondathogai, which belongs to this sarukkam, is "Meiyadiyan Narasinga Munaiyaraiyarkku Adiyen," where Sundaramoorthy Swamigal praises this Nayanar. The meaning of this is, "I am a servant of Narasinga Munaiyaraiyar, the true devotee." "Meiyadimai" refers to the unwavering love, devotion, and steadfastness towards the sacred form (Thiruvedam) adorned with sacred ash (Thiruneeru), which signifies servitude in the Lord's divine service. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal thus praises this Nayanar, Narasinga Munaiyaraiyar, a true devotee, in the Thogai Nool (collection of verses). Now, the 51st verse in the Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi composed by Nambiyandar Nambigal praises Narasinga Munaiyaraiya Nayanar. That verse is "Pugazhumbadi Embaram Ethavarkku Narp Pon Eeduvon Igazhumbadi Or Thavan Madavar Punaikkolam Engum Nigazhumbadi Kandu Avarkku Andru Iratti Pon Ittavan Thigazhum Mudi Narasinga Munai Arasan Thiramai." This verse describes the greatness of Narasinga Munaiyaraiya Nayanar. Narasinga Munaiyaraiya Nayanar would give 100 gold coins to Shiva devotees every Thiruvathirai day. He observed this as a sacred service. One day, while this was happening, a devotee arrived whose body displayed signs of lust, with clear marks of desire, yet he was also adorned with sacred ash. Others, seeing this devotee's appearance, recoiled from him. But Narasinga Munaiyaraiya Nayanar, disregarding the signs of lust, was pleased by the sacred ash on his forehead and gave him double the usual amount of gold, delighting him. Nambiyandar Nambigal Perumaganar thus marvels in the Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi, "How can I praise the prowess of such a Narasinga Munaiyaraiya Nayanar?" Now, in the extensive work of Periyapuranam, the Puranam of Narasinga Munaiyaraiya Nayanar is the 42nd puranam. The section narrating the history and character of Narasinga Munaiyaraiya Nayanar is the third puranam in the eighth Poiyadimai Illatha Pulavar Sarukkam, in sequential order. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman describes this sacred puranam in nine verses. The first verse is: "Kodatha Neri Vilangum Kodi Marabin Arasalithu Madaga Manigandar Thiruneere Manam Kolvar Thedatha Peruvallathil Sirandha Thirumunaippadi Nadalum Kavalanar Narasinga Munaiyaraiyar." Narasinga Munaiyaraiya Nayanar was by nature a king who ruled the land of Thirumunaippadi, which was rich in great wealth. He belonged to a lineage of minor kings who ruled justly, never deviating from the path of righteousness. His mind was filled with the sacred ash of Lord Neelakantha, which he considered his greatest wealth. His sacred name was Narasinga Munaiyaraiyar. He firmly believed that sacred ash was true wealth and nothing else was. His life story is characterized by his disregard for anything else in the presence of sacred ash. His land, Thirumunaippadi, was a land of great prosperity. It was naturally abundant, without the need to seek out desired goods. Narasinga Munaiyaraiyar was the protector of that land. When we reflect on the Puranam of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, we considered how Narasinga Munaiyaraiyar raised Sundaramoorthy Swamigal as his beloved son when he was young. This puranam is the sacred history of that great one. The second verse is: "Immunaiyar Perunthagaiyar Irundhu Arasu Purandhu Poy, Them Munaigal Pala Kadandhu Theengu Neri Paangu Agala, Mummunai Neel Ilaich Choola Mudarpadaiyar Thondu Puri, Ammunaiyar Adi Adaive Arum Perum Peru Ena Adaivar." This great king belonged to the Munaiyar lineage. Munaiyatharaiyar is a lineage of minor kings. He ruled from his city, having come from that lineage. He protected his country well, removing all enmities of enemies and evil deeds, so that enemies would not invade his country and trouble his people. He lived his life believing that attaining the sacred feet of the devotees who serve Lord Shiva, who holds the three-pronged, long, leaf-shaped trident (Thrisoolam), was the rare and great blessing. The third verse is: "Sinavidaiyar Koyil Thoram Thiruchchelvam Perukku Neri, Anavidaiyar Uyir Thurakka Varum Eninum Avai Kaathu, Manavudai Aamai Thodaiyal Ani Marbar Vazhithondu, Kanavidaiyagilum Vazhuva Kadan Atrich Chelkindrar." Lord Shiva is adorned with a garland made of tortoise shells on His sacred chest. Narasinga Munaiyaraiya Nayanar protected the path that increased the sacred wealth in all the temples where Lord Shiva resides. "Increasing sacred wealth in temples" refers to the excellence of worship in temples. This means the performance of daily and occasional pujas and festivals. These are the sources of wealth. These are truly "Thiru." "Thiru" means wealth related to Shiva. Therefore, he ensured the proliferation of these paths of Shiva's wealth. Even if it meant sacrificing his life in this endeavor, he did not care, considering the protection of Shiva's wealth as superior to his own life, and continued his sacred service. If there was any deficiency in the Lord's temple, one should sacrifice one's life. By sacrificing one's life, one should protect the Shiva temple. It is an Agamic rule that those who sacrifice their lives in such service of protecting Shiva temples will attain Shivapuri. Even in our country, India, we see through our history how many devotees sacrificed their lives to protect temples like Somnath from enemy invasions. If it is considered heroic heaven for soldiers who fight in the army to protect a country, then it is natural that those who sacrifice their lives to protect Shiva temples, which ensure life, will attain Shivapuri, isn't it? "Sankaran Aalayathil Thavarurinne Saaga Thunindhazhigai Paange Avar Amalan Purithanai Saarvar" is a saying from Shiva Dharmottaram. Therefore, Narasinga Munaiyaraiya Nayanar performed all these duties, even in his dreams, without fail. The fourth verse is: "Aranindha Sadai Mudiyarkku Aathirai Naal Thorum Endrum Veru Nirai Vazhipadu Vilangiya Poosanai Mevi, Neeraniyum Thondar Anaintharkkellam Nigazh Pasum Pon Nuru Kuraiyamal Alithu In Amudhu Nugarvippar." Narasinga Munaiyaraiya Nayanar performed abundant and distinguished pujas to Lord Shiva every Thiruvathirai day. In addition, he would give 100 gold coins without fail to all the devotees adorned with sacred ash who came there on that day, and also offered them sweet sacred food (Thiruamudhu). He also performed the Maheswara Puja. Thiruvathirai is a special day dear to Lord Shiva. "Aathirai Naal Ugandhanum Aroor Amarndha Ammane" is a sacred saying from Thevaram. We also find sacred sayings like "Aathirai Nannal Andrannai" and "Naal Aathirai Andre" and "Irai Naal Kaanadhe." We see in the sacred sayings, including the history of Thiruthondar Puranam, that it is "Mathiyani Punitha Nannal" and "Aranukku Isaintha Per Vazhi." Shiva Agamas prescribe that worshipping Lord Shiva on Thiruvathirai day is very special. We saw in Periyapuranam the history that the divine incarnation of Aludaiya Pillaiyar, Thiru Gnanasambandha Peruman, and his partaking of the nectar of wisdom, occurred on Thiruvathirai day. Therefore, this is a Naimittikam (occasional) worship. This is a worship that comes about due to an occasion, distinct from Nithyam (daily) worship. These Naimittika pujas should be performed separately from the daily pujas. This is a special worship. Every Thiruvathirai day, this Nayanar performed a special individual puja for Lord Shiva and also performed the Maheswara Puja, which is the worship of devotees, by giving 100 gold coins to each of the devotees who came there and offering them sacred food. The fifth verse is: "Aana Seyalmurai Purivar Oru Thiruvathirai Naalil Menmai Neri Thondarkku Vilangiya Pon Idum Pozhuthil Maana Nilai Azhithanmai Varum Kamak Kuri Malarndha Oona Nigar Meniyarai Oruvar Neeranindhu Anainthar." On one such Thiruvathirai day, while he was performing the Thiruvathirai puja and giving gold to the devotees of the exalted Shiva path, a person arrived. His body displayed signs of lust. He had a body with such flaws, yet he was adorned with sacred ash. Signs of lust refer to symptoms of disease and nail marks, etc., which were clearly visible on his body. He came with such a flawed body, and also adorned with sacred ash, which was completely incongruous with his appearance. He understood that wearing sacred ash was the characteristic of a devotee. Therefore, even though signs of lust were evident, he had come wearing sacred ash. The sixth verse is: "Matru Avardham Vadivu Irundhapadi Kandu Marungulor Utra Igazhchiyaraagi Odhunguvar Thammai Kandu Kottavanar Ethir Sendru Kaikuvithu Kodupozhndhu Ap Petriyinar Thammai Mikavum Kondadip Penupar." Seeing his appearance, those nearby, filled with disdain, moved away from him in disgust. Even after seeing the sacred ash worn by that devotee, they considered only his physical condition and recoiled in disgust. Narasinga Munaiyaraiya Nayanar observed this. The king went forward to that devotee, saluted him with folded hands, brought him in, and greatly praised and honored him. There are two points here. We see two points in the sacred act of Narasinga Munaiyaraiya Nayanar. One is that the state of being adorned with sacred ash is itself worthy of respect and worship. If the people of this world do not respect it and instead disdain him based on his physical condition, they will suffer the evil consequences of that action. They will suffer the evil consequences of that karma of hatred. Therefore, the other point is the compassion he had for the people of this world, to protect them from suffering evil consequences by not hating this person who came with signs of lust and sacred ash, and to show them the path of Shiva. We see these two actions in the sacred act of Narasinga Munaiyaraiya Nayanar. First, he went and saluted that devotee for his own adherence to the path. Second, for the benefit of the world, so that they would not suffer by disdaining that devotee, for the sake of others. Therefore, we see this in two ways: his own conduct, and the way he demonstrated to establish this world in the Saiva path. Moreover, he was in the position of a king. Therefore, in accordance with the saying "As the king, so the people," he demonstrated it there. If that devotee was wearing sacred ash with signs of lust, he honored him in the manner of honoring a devotee. He honored him without showing any discrimination. The seventh verse is: "Seelamilare Eninum Thiruneeru Serndharai Nalam Igazhndhu Arun Naragu Nannamal Ennuvar Palanaindhar Thamakkalitha Padi Iratti Pon Koduthu Melavarai Thozhuthu Iniya Mozhi Vilambi Vidai Koduthar." Even if they lack the worldly virtue of good conduct, if people of the world disdain devotees who are adorned with sacred ash, they will go to hell as a result of that evil deed. Narasinga Munaiyaraiya Nayanar, thinking that they should be saved from going to hell, gave that devotee double the usual 100 gold coins, which is 200 gold coins, which he customarily gave to those who came there. Having given that, he further saluted him, spoke sweet words, honored him, and sent him off. This is what the king, who had compassion for the people of this world, thought and did. Among the duties of a king is to protect the lives of his subjects from falling into lower paths and perishing. To protect them thus, it is a great and excellent duty for every king to compassionately lead and guide them. Moreover, "Evarenum Thaamaga Ilaadathitta Thiruneerum Saadhanavum Kandal Ulgi Uvaraadhe Avar Avaraich Kandapodhu Ugandhu Adimaithiram Ninaindhu Angu Ugandhu Nokki" is a sacred saying from Thevaram composed by Thirunavukkarasu Peruman. "Nalamigaraga Nalamadhu Undaaga Naadavar Naadarugindra Kulamilaraga Kulamadhu Undaaga Thavam Puri Kulachchirai" is a sacred saying from Thevaram composed by Thiru Gnanasambandha Peruman. Therefore, it is shown here that whoever it may be, if they are associated with sacred ash, that devotee should be honored and saluted. The eighth verse is: "Iv Vagaye Thiruthondin Arumai Neri Ennalum Sevviya Anbinil Aatri Thirundhiya Sindhaiyaragi Paivalar Vaal Aravu Anindhar Paadhamalar Nizhal Serndhu Meivagai Avvazhiya Anbin Meeladha Nilai Petrar." In the manner described in the previous actions, he always performed the rare path of sacred service with pure love. He was a person with a refined mind. Narasinga Munaiyaraiya Nayanar, believing firmly that the sacred form itself is the true essence and other physical forms are not, lived accordingly. Such a Narasinga Munaiyaraiya Nayanar attained the state of liberation, never to return, through true love, having sought the shade of the lotus feet of the Lord who is adorned with the snake. The ninth verse is: "Vidanagam Anindha Piraan Meithondu Vilaindha Nilai Udanagu Narasinga Munaiyar Piraan Kazhal Ethi Thadanaga Madham Soriyum Thananjoriyum Kalan Serum Kadalnaga Athipaththar Kadalnaga Kavin Uraippam." The divine Sekkizhar Peruman, having praised the sacred feet of Narasinga Munaiyaraiya Peruman, whose true devotion to Lord Shiva, who wears the poisonous snake, blossomed without fail, now says, "We shall now describe the beauty of the sacred service of Adhipattha Nayanar, who hails from Kadalnaga, which is Nagapattinam, where elephants shed their ichor and ships laden with wealth arrive." Thus, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman concludes the Puranam of Narasinga Munaiyaraiya Nayanar and begins the Puranam of Adhipattha Nayanar, which follows. Let us also praise the sacred feet of Narasinga Munaiyaraiya Nayanar and continue to reflect on the Puranam of Adhipattha Nayanar. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of our Lord Narasinga Munaiyaraiya Nayanar. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of our Lord Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of Nambiyandar Nambigal Peruman. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the commentator, Aiyya. Thiruchitrambalam.
