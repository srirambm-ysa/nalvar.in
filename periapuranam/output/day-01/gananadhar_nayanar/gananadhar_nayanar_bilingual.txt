ஞான சாஸ்திர உண்மைகள் நாயனாரது திருப்புராணத்திலே வைத்து காட்டப்படுகின்றன. அவ்வாறு மீளாத நிலை என்ற பரமுக்தியை அடைந்தவர் கணநாத நாயனார். ஏழாவது மற்றும் நிறைவு திருப்பாடல். உலகம் உய்ய நஞ்சுண்டவர் தொண்டினில் உறுதி மெய் உணர்வு எய்தி அளவில் தொண்டருக்கு அறிவு அளித்தவர் திறம் அவனியின் மிசை ஆக்கும் மலர் பெரும் புகழ் புகழியில் வரும் கணநாதனார் கழல் வாழ்த்தி குலவு நீற்று வன் கூற்றுவனார் திறம் கொள்கையின் மொழிகின்றாம். உலக உயிர்கள் உய்யும் பொருட்டு விடத்தினை உண்ட பெருமானது திருத்தொண்டின் உண்மை திறத்தில் உறுதியாகிய மெய் உணர்ச்சி பொருந்தப்பெற்று அளவில்லாத திருத்தொண்டர்களுக்கு அவ்வவர் தொண்டில் அறிவு அளித்து அவர்களது திறங்களை உலகிலே ஆக்கும் விரிந்த பெரும் புகழினை உடைய சீர்காழியில் அவதரித்த கணநாத நாயனாரினுடைய திருவடிகளை துதித்து இனி திருநீற்றுச் சார்பு பூண்ட வன்மை உடைய கூற்றுவ நாயனாரது திறத்தினை கொள்கையின்படி மேலே சொல்லப் புகுகின்றோம் என்று தெய்வ சேக்கிழார் பெருமான் கூறி கணநாத நாயனாரது திருப்புராணத்தை நிறைவு செய்து இனி கூற்றுவ நாயனாரது திருப்புராணத்தை தொடங்குகின்றார். நாமும் கணநாத # English

Chapter 01 — GanaNadhar Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. The Periyapuranam, Part 965, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Varkonda Vanamulaiyal Sarukkam. The Puranam of GanaNadhar Nayanar. In the Periyapuranam, which is the Puranam of the holy devotees, the Puranam of GanaNadhar Nayanar is placed after the Puranam of Kazharitrarivar Nayanar. It is the 38th Puranam. The sixth hymn in the Thiruthondath Thogai, composed by Sundaramurthy Swamigal, begins with "Varkonda Vanamulaiyal." In it, Sundaramurthy Swamigal has sung about GanaNadhar Nayanar, saying, "I am a servant to the servants of GanaNadhan of Kadarkazhi." The meaning of this is, "I am a servant to the servants of GanaNadhan, who was born in the city of Seerkazhi, which has a seashore." GanaNadhan is the sacred name of the Nayanar. GanaNadhan means the leader of Shiva's Ganas (celestial attendants). This is a sacred name of Lord Shiva. It was bestowed upon our Nayanar, and since he shone as GanaNadhan, it became a descriptive name. This is the meaning of the part in the Thiruthondath Thogai where Sundaramurthy Swamigal sang about GanaNadhar Nayanar. Now, in the descriptive work Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi, Nambiyandar Nambigal Swamigal praises GanaNadhar Nayanar in the 46th hymn. That hymn is as follows: "Thondarai aakki avar avarkku etra thozhilgal seivithu Andardham konak kanathukku nayagam petravan kaan Kondal konderiya minnukku kola madalgal thorum Kandal venchoru alikkum Kadarkazhi GanaNadhane." The meaning of this is that GanaNadhar, the Nayanar who was born and lived in the city of Seerkazhi, created devotees and made them perform sacred services according to their respective qualifications, thereby becoming the leader of the celestial attendants of Lord Shiva, the chief of the Devas. In this, while describing the city of Seerkazhi, he has sung about its prosperity, saying, "Seerkazhi on the seashore, where the screwpine trees offer white rice to the lightning, which is like a daughter, shining high up, taking the clouds as its abode." The inner part of the screwpine flower is called "rice." It will be white. The long leaves of the screwpine flower are beautiful. They are long. The lightning creeper in the cloud is like a daughter to this screwpine flower, and this screwpine, like a mother, offers its white flowers as "white rice" to that daughter, the lightning creeper. Thus, the screwpine leaf is described here as the mother who offered rice to the lightning, which was like a daughter from above. Now, GanaNadhar Nayanar was engaged in the sacred service of creating devotees. He created holy devotees through initiation, instruction, training, and assistance, and trained them to perform services such as Chariyai, suitable for their physical condition, mental state, and maturity. These include sacred sweeping, sacred smearing, sacred ablutions, sacred garlands, and sacred lamps. Thus, the descriptive work Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi is structured. Now, in the extensive work Thiruthondar Puranam, the Puranam of GanaNadhar Nayanar is explained by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman in seven hymns. It is the 38th Puranam in chronological order. This section describes the history and character of GanaNadhar Nayanar. In the seventh Sarukkam, "Varkonda Vanamulaiyal Sarukkam," the Puranam of GanaNadhar Nayanar is the fifth Puranam. The first hymn: "Aazhi maanilathu agilam eendru aliththaval thirumulai amuthunda Vaazhi Gnanachambandhar vandhu aruliya vanappinathu alappu illaa Oozhi maa kadal vellaththu mithandhu ulaginukku oru muthalaay Kaazhi maanagar thiru maraiyavar kulakkaavalar GanaNadhar." GanaNadhar, the protector of the lineage of the sacred Brahmins of the great city of Kaazhi, which is the origin of the world, having floated in the immeasurable great ocean flood of the eons, and which is of immeasurable beauty, blessed by the coming of Thirugnanasambandhar, who was nourished by the divine milk from the breast of the Mother who bore and protected all beings in this great ocean-surrounded world.

The great city of Seerkazhi is renowned for its beautiful greatness, where the venerable Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, whose birth was a rare blessing, incarnated. Seerkazhi is a primordial place that, even when the vast ocean floods of the eons swelled immeasurably, did not sink but floated, becoming the origin for the world to be reformed. In that sacred place, GanaNadhar shone as the leader of the Brahmin community. The second hymn: "Aaya anbar thaam animadhil Shanbaiyin amar perum thiruththoni Nayanaarkku nal thiruppaniyaayina naalum anpodu seythu Meya aththiruththondinil vilanguvaar virumbi vandhu anaivaarkku Thuya kaiththiruththondinil avarthamai thuraithorum payilvippar." Such a devotee, GanaNadhar, daily performs good sacred services with love to Thiruththoniyappar, who resides with pleasure in the city of Seerkazhi. He shines with distinction in performing such sacred service. Among all good things, the best good is that which leads to liberation. The Nayanar is engaged in such sacred service and excels in it. He also trains those devotees who come to him with a desire to perform these sacred services, in the various aspects of pure manual service (chariyai). What these chariyai services are is explained in the next verse. Those who stand in the sacred service of Saivism will, in due course, attain Kriya, Yoga, and Gnana, and thereby achieve liberation. Therefore, it is manual service that first bestows qualification and purity for attaining liberation. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman explains what these sacred services are in the next, the third, hymn. "Nalla nandhavanap pani sey pavar narunthunar malar koyvor Pal malar thodai punaipavar konar thirumanjanap panikku ullor Allum nanpagalum thiru alagittu thirumezhukku amaippor Ellaiyil vilakku erippavar thirumurai ezuthuvor vaasippor." Those who work in a good sacred garden. A sacred garden is a good sacred garden. That goodness involves cultivating and protecting sacred plants, trees, and creepers for the adornment of the Lord, and using them for the sacred service of Lord Shiva; these are benevolent services untainted by evil, services embodying compassion. Such are those who perform sacred garden work. Garden work includes preparing the soil, planting seeds and saplings, watering, and weeding. Next, those who pluck fragrant flowers. They pluck flowers that are suitable for plucking from fragrant clusters, selecting only flawless flowers that are neither unripe buds nor already withered. Those who perform such sacred services, and those who make garlands, wreaths, and other floral decorations from the collected flowers. Next, those who bring sacred water for ablutions from other holy water bodies. It is implied that those who perform this service must be pure and qualified by tradition. Next, those who purify Shiva temples through physical effort, leaving no place untouched. This is described as the chariyai service of sacred sweeping and sacred smearing in the Agamas. This sacred sweeping and smearing should be done continuously day and night. These services of sacred sweeping and smearing should be performed as a precaution in the mornings and evenings so as not to disturb devotees who come to worship in the temples. Next, those who light lamps without limit. Sacred lamps should always be lit. Therefore, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman mentions "those who light lamps without limit." This also indicates that lamps are lit in immeasurable numbers. Next, those who write and read Thirumurai. Thirumurai refers to the Thevaram Thirumurais, Thirumandhiram, the works composed by the Mother, and other similar works. Apart from these,

It is a great error and sin to call any other books "Thirumurai." Writing and providing these Thirumurais is also a great chariyai service. In earlier days, it was customary to write and provide Thirumurais. There were also people in those days who made a living by writing and copying Thirumurais and other books, Puranas, literature, and grammar onto palm-leaf manuscripts. Nowadays, printed books are very easily available. In these days, the work of creating and distributing these printed books has replaced the work of writing, and this can be considered appropriate for the current times. Next, those who read Thirumurai, that is, training in reading Thirumurai. Arranging for this is a chariyai state of Shiva's merit. Thevaram schools, Shiva Agama schools, etc., are included in this sacred service. Those who are not trained in reading will read these Thirumurai books with errors. Therefore, reading with errors will render it fruitless. Moreover, it will become an offense to Shiva. To avoid this, one should practice reading and reciting Thirumurai before doing so. Providing such training is also a very great Shiva's merit. Next, training those who write and read Thirumurai, and arranging everything necessary for them to excel in their respective services. Thus, creating Shiva's devotees is also Shiva's chariyai service and worship. "Reading books of wisdom, teaching them, making others listen to good meanings, listening oneself, and contemplating the good, flawless meaning—these five are the beautiful wisdom worship that leads to the feet of the Lord," says the 23rd verse of the 8th Sutra of Sivagnana Siddhiyar. Now, the fourth hymn: "Inaiyap bakthip panigalil anainthavarkku etra aththiruththondin vinai vilangida vendiya kuraiyellam mudiththu mevidach seythe Anaiya aththiram purithalil thondarai aakki anbura vaaymai manai aram purindhu adiyavarkku inburav vazhipadum thozhil mikkaar." Thus, the great devotee, who attained such great distinction, always delighted the hearts of all devotees in Seerkazhi, a city made glorious by the presence of the child, Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, who partook of true great wisdom. With a mind overflowing with unparalleled love, he performed archana worship three times a day, every day, with increasing devotion, and never faltered. The sixth hymn: "Aana thondinil amarndha per anbarum agal idaththinil endrum Gnanam undavar pundareegak kazhal archanai nalam pettru Thoo narum kondrai mudiyavar sudar nedung kayilai maal varai eythi Maana narp perum ganangalukku naatharaam vazhiththondin nilai pettraar." GanaNadhar Nayanar, the great devotee who was devoted to the sacred service mentioned earlier, and who always attained the benefit of worshipping the lotus-like feet of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman in this wide world, as a result, reached the great Kailash mountain, the abode of the Lord with a pure and fragrant Kondrai-adorned crown, and attained the position of leader of the great and noble Ganas, thereby establishing his status in that sacred service. Here we see the truths of Gnana Shastra being presented, such as how through the state of Chariyai, the worship of the moving (Jangama) and thereby the worship of the Guru, one attains the Lord. How? Archana, the benefit derived from that archana, and as a result, he created devotees. As a result of that, the worship of devotees, which is Jangama worship, and as a result of that, the worship of the feet of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, the child of the Lord, which is Guru worship, and as a result of that, he went to Thirukailash, the world of the Lord, which is attaining the Lord. Thus, the truths of Gnana Shastra are presented in a cause-and-effect sequence in the Puranam of the Nayanar. Thus, GanaNadhar Nayanar attained the state of permanent liberation (Paramukthi). The seventh and concluding hymn: "Ulagam uyya nanjundavar thondinil uruthi mey unarvu eythi Alavil thondarukku arivu aliththavar thiram avaniyin misai aakkum Malar perum pugazh Pugazhiyil varum GanaNadhanar kazhal vaazhththi Kulavu neetru van koottruvanar thiram kolgaiyin mozhigindram." The divine Sekkizhar Peruman concludes the Puranam of GanaNadhar Nayanar by saying, "Having attained true realization in the steadfast service of the Lord who consumed poison for the salvation of the world, and having imparted knowledge to countless devotees, thereby establishing their abilities in the world, we praise the feet of GanaNadhar Nayanar, who came from Pugazhi, a city of widespread great fame, and now we shall proceed to speak of the deeds of Koottruva Nayanar, who was strong in the sacred ash, according to tradition." We too, praising the feet of GanaNadhar Nayanar, shall now proceed to contemplate the Puranam of Koottruva Nayanar. Praise, praise to the feet of GanaNadhar Nayanar! Praise, praise to the feet of our Lord Thirugnanasambandha Peruman! Praise, praise to the feet of our Lord Sundaramurthy Swamigal! Praise, praise to the feet of our Lord Nambiyandar Nambigal Swamigal! Praise, praise to the feet of the divine Sekkizhar Peruman! Praise, praise to the feet of the commentator Aiyya! Thiruchitrambalam.
