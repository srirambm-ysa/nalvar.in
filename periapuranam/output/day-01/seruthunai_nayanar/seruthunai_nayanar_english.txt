Chapter 01 — Seruthunai Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, authored by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Part 990. Kadal Suzhntha Sarukkam. Seruthunai Nayanar Puranam. Following the puranam of Ilangali Nayanar in the Periyapuranam authored by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, the puranam of Seruthunai Nayanar is presented as the 55th puranam. First, in the Thogai Nool (collection of hymns) composed by our Lord Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, in the ninth verse of Thiruthondathogai, he praises Seruthunai, the king of Thanjai, saying, "I am a servant to his servants." The meaning of this is, "I am a servant to the servants of Seruthunai, the leader of Thanjavur." This Nayanar, Seruthunai Nayanar, was the leader of Thanjavur. This Thanjavur is the town of Marugal Nadu Thanjavur. It is not the Thanjavur that is currently widely known. This is Marugal Nadu Thanjavur. It is a sacred place near Thiruvarur, southeast of Thirumarugal and south of Thiruchengattangudi. It can also be reached via Thiruvirkudi and Thirupayattur. It is located on the northern bank of the Valapparu river. It is called Keezhai Thanjavur. The idol of Seruthunai Nayanar is installed in the temple here. There is also a traditional belief that Seruthunai Nayanar resided in Kumarakkottam in Thiruvarur. Therefore, our Lord Sundaramoorthy Swamigal sang, "I am a servant to the servants of Seruthunai, the king of Thanjai." Now, in the 66th verse of Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi, a type of literary work, Nambiandar Nambigal has praised this Nayanar. "Kalinil kadal nanju yindrarkku irundha kadimalarai mozhi neel pugazh kalarsingan than devi mun mothalume ezhil neel kumizh malar mookkarindhan endru iyambuvaaral sezhuneer marugan nattu amar Thanjai Seruthunaiye." This is the verse. We have already discussed the sacred history of this Nayanar when we considered the puranam of Kalarcinga Nayanar. There, we saw that Seruthunai was the one who cut off the nose of the queen of Kalarcinga Nayanar because she picked up a flower lying in the Pushpa Mandapam in Thiruvarur and smelled it. Therefore, Nambiandar Nambigal, in this verse, praises this Nayanar by referring to that sacred history. Now, in the Periyapuranam, a detailed work authored by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, the puranam of Seruthunai Nayanar is the third puranam in the tenth sarukkam called Kadal Suzhntha Sarukkam, and the 55th puranam in the series of Nayanmar puranams. This puranam has been sung by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman in seven verses. In the first verse, the special features and prosperity of Marugal Nadu Thanjavur, where the Nayanar was born, are described by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. "Ullum puramum kula marabin ozhukkam vazhuva orumai neri kollum iyalbil kudi mudhalor malindha selva kulappathiyam thellum thiraikkan madagutharum selum kayalum sellum aniyum thallum ponni neer nattu marugal nattu Thanjavur." This verse describes Thanjavur, located in Marugal Nadu, a subdivision within the Chola country, which is a land watered by the Cauvery river, where clear waves push fish like "sel" and "kayal" and rich gems through its sluices. It is a place inhabited by ancient people who adhere to the principles of conduct that do not deviate from the traditions of their lineage, both internally and externally. It is a place of glory where they live in prosperity. That is the place called Marugal Nadu Thanjavur. The second verse states: "Seerin vilangum appathiyil thirundhu velan kudi mudhalvar neerin malindha seyya sadai neetrar kootrin nenjiditha veri malarndha poonguzhal suzh meiyyanbudaiya saivar ena paaril nigazhndha Seruthunaiyar paravum thondin neri nindrar." Our Nayanar, named Seruthunai, was born as a leader in the Velalar community in the glorious Marugal Nadu Thanjavur and lived there. He was a true devotee of Shiva, always contemplating the sacred feet of the Lord. He followed the path of Shiva's service. The third verse describes: "Aana anbar Thiruvarur Aazhithther Viththagar koyil Gnana munivar imaiyavarkal nerungum nalan ser muntrilinul Maana nilavu thiruppanikal seythu kaalangalil vanangi koonal izhai venpirai mudiyar thondu pozhiya kulavu naal." This devotee, Seruthunai Nayanar, performs excellent sacred services in the temple of Aazhithther Viththagar, the Lord of Thiruvarur, in the auspicious courtyard where wise sages and celestials gather, in front of the Devasiriya Mandapam. These are the sacred services performed in the temple courtyard. These are called Chariyai Thiruppanikal (preliminary services). Seruthunai Nayanar performs these Chariyai services such as sweeping the courtyard, cleaning it with a broom, removing stones, grass, thorns, and other debris, watering the Nandavanam (flower garden) and plants inside the temple, and weeding. He also unfailingly worships Aazhithther Viththagar during all the puja times. One day, a miraculous divine event occurred. The fourth and fifth verses describe it. "Ulagu nigazhndha Pallavar Kochengar urimai perundhevi nilavu thiruppoo mandapathu marungu neengik kidandhadhoru malarai eduthu mondhadharku vandhu poramai vazhiththondar ilaga sudar vaay karuvi eduthu ezhundha vegaththaal eydhi kadidhu muttri mattraval than karumen koondhal pidithu eendhu padiyil veezhththi manimookkai patri paramar seyya sadai mudiyil erum thiruppoo mandapathu malar mondhidum mookkai thadivan endru karuviyinaal arindhaar thalaimai thaniththondar." On that day, while Seruthunai Nayanar was performing his sacred service, the queen of Pallava Kochengar, who ruled the world, the wife of Kalarcinga Nayanar, picked up a fresh flower lying near the Thiruppoo Mandapam in the Thiruvarur temple and smelled it. Seruthunai Nayanar, a devotee who followed the path of righteousness, could not tolerate this sacrilege. He rushed forward with great speed, grabbed the queen's dark, soft hair, pulled it, threw her to the ground, and holding her beautiful nose, he declared, "I will cut off the nose that smelled the flower meant for the sacred hair of the Lord," and cut off her nose with a sharp instrument. It is understood without saying that everything offered to the pure Lord in worship must be pure in all respects. Therefore, every item offered for the Lord's worship must be taken from an unpolluted environment by someone who possesses both internal and external purity according to religious customs, and these items must be handled with Shiva consciousness. This is one of the rules of puja. It is also strictly forbidden to smell the fragrance of puja items or to salivate due to their fragrance and quality. That is why puja items should be kept far away from the tongue and nose. Moreover, the nose and mouth should be covered as much as possible with a clean cloth, especially when picking flowers for puja. These are the rules. Here in this puranam, we see that the queen picked up and smelled a flower that had fallen in the Thiruppushpa Mandapam. Even though it had fallen, it was a flower that had been consecrated for puja. Even in its fallen state, it was still a puja item, and preserving the sanctity of puja items is the way. Just as there are rules like not stepping on the Nirmalyas (used flowers and leaves) that have been offered to the Lord, this is also a rule, a principle. It is the lofty principle that even if a flower consecrated for puja falls, it is still considered a puja flower. Therefore, even if they accidentally fall, smelling them with a sense of enjoyment is a fault. This idea is strongly emphasized in the puranams of Seruthunai Nayanar and Kalarcinga Nayanar. These puranams are structured to convey the importance of this. The great awareness that Seruthunai Nayanar had, that even a flower lying on the ground in the Thiruppushpa Mandapam was a flower brought to be adorned on the sacred head of Lord Shiva, was the reason for his action. That is why the divine Sekkizhar Peruman states, "I will cut off the nose that smelled the flower meant for the sacred hair of the Lord." Preserving the sanctity of Shiva's offerings is a practice that comes from great love, cultivated through many years, even many lifetimes, of penance. This is a unique quality, paramount among all qualities. That is why the divine Sekkizhar Peruman praises Seruthunai Nayanar as "Thalaimai Thaniththondar" (the foremost unique devotee). The sixth verse states: "Ulagariya seytha adal eru anaiyavar thaam thoduttha thaama malar idhazhi mudiyar adimaiththondu kadal uduttha ulaginil nigazhach seythu uyyach seyya pon mandrul eduttha paadha nizhal adaindhe iravaa inbam eydhinaar." Seruthunai Nayanar, who was like a powerful lion and made his Shiva service known to the world, performed his service of devotion to Lord Shiva, making it manifest in this world surrounded by oceans, so that all living beings could attain salvation. He attained eternal bliss by taking refuge in the shade of the raised foot of the Lord in the golden hall. Eternal bliss is the bliss of liberation (mukti). The Lord grants Seruthunai Nayanar this eternal bliss by uniting him with mukti. Now, the seventh and concluding verse states: "Sengan vidaiyar thirumuntril vizhundha thiruppalliththaam angkan eduthu mondha adarku arasurimai perundhevi thunga mani mookku arindha Seruthunaiyar thuya kazhal irainji engu nigazhndha Pugazhthunaiyar urimai adimai eduthu uraippaam." The divine Sekkizhar Peruman concludes the puranam of Seruthunai Nayanar by saying, "Worshipping the pure feet of Seruthunai Nayanar, and with their support, we shall now proceed to narrate the devoted service of Pugazhthunai Nayanar, which is renowned everywhere." We too shall bow down and worship the sacred feet of Seruthunai Nayanar and then proceed to contemplate the puranam of Pugazhthunai Nayanar. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of our Lord Seruthunai Nayanar! Praise, praise to the sacred feet of our Lord Sundaramoorthy Swamigal! Praise, praise to the sacred feet of Lord Nambiandar Nambigal! Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the divine Sekkizhar Peruman! Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the commentator, Ayya! Thiruchitrambalam.
