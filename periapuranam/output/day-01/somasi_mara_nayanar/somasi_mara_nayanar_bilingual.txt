Chapter 01 — Somasi Mara Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Part 914, by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, the chapter of Vambara Vari Vantu, the Puranam of Somasi Mara Nayanar. In Periyapuranam, following the Puranam of Moorka Nayanar, the Puranam of Somasi Mara Nayanar is the 33rd Puranam. In the fifth verse of Thiruthondathogai, composed by Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, which begins with "Vambara Vari Vantu," Sundaramoorthy Swamigal has sung about Somasi Mara Nayanar, saying, "I am a servant to Ambaran Somasi Maran." The meaning of the Thiruthondathogai verse "Ambaran Somasi Maranukkum Adiyen" is that "I am a servant to Somasi Mara Nayanar, who was born in the sacred city of Thiru Ambar." Ambar is the name of a sacred place, also known as Thiru Innampar and Ambar Maakkaalam. We have already discussed the history of this holy place in detail. Our Nayanar, who was born in that holy place and performed Shiva Yagas regularly according to the Vedas and Agamas, was given the title "Somasi." "Somayaji" is pronounced as "Somasi" in Tamil. He was one who performed such Soma Yagas according to the rules prescribed in the Shiva Agamas. "Marar" is the real name of the Nayanar. Therefore, the sacred name of the Nayanar is "Somasi Marar." It is said that in the great Yaga he performed, Lord Shiva and his consort appeared in the form of a fisherman and partook of the Havis (offering). This history is especially celebrated in the Sthala Mahatmyas of Thiru Ambar. For this reason, a festival called "Ambar Yagam" is celebrated annually. This is the history associated with the Sthala Puranam. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal sings that "I am a servant to Somasi Mara Nayanar, who was born in such a city called Ambaran Thiru Ambar." The phrase "Mararukkum" (to Marar also) indicates that Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, who had already enslaved himself to the Nayanars he had sung about before, also became a servant to Somasi Mara Nayanar. Thus, he sang in the Thiruthondathogai, a collection of verses. Now, in the 39th verse of Thiruthondar Thiruvandathi, a detailed work, Nambiyandar Nambigal Swamigal has composed a verse about Somasi Mara Nayanar. That verse is as follows: "Sutha Pozhil Ambar Andanan Somasi Maran Enban Vedapparul Anjezhuthum Vilambiyallan Mozhiyan Neethi Param Mannu Nithya Niyamam Paravai Ennum Mathukku Kaanthan Vanthondan Thanaku Magizh Thunaiye." The meaning of this verse is: "He who is called Somasi Maran, a Brahmin living in Thiru Ambar, a city surrounded by groves of mango trees (even today, we can see the abundance of mango trees and groves around Ambar city), constantly recites the sacred five-letter mantra, which is the inner meaning of the Vedas, and does not utter any other word. He is steadfast in his daily observance of righteousness, which is eternal. He is a joyful companion to Vanthondan (Sundaramoorthy Swamigal), the husband of Paravai Ammaiyar." Now, in the detailed work of Periyapuranam, the Puranam composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman is the Puranam of Somasi Mara Nayanar. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman has composed this Puranam in five verses. The Puranam of Somasi Mara Nayanar is the section that describes the historical life and character of Somasi Mara Nayanar. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman begins to describe the Puranam of Somasi Mara Nayanar as the sixth Puranam in the sixth chapter, the chapter of Vambara Vari Vantu. The first verse is as follows: "Sutham Payilum Pozhil Ambaril Thuyya Vaimai Vedam Payilum Maraiyalar Kulathin Melor Etham Puriyum Meyil Setravarkku Anbar Vanthal Padam Paninthu Ar Amuthu Oottu Narpunbin Mikkar." In the city of Thiru Ambar, surrounded by groves where mango trees abound, there lived a person of high distinction in the lineage of Brahmins who practiced the Vedas with purity and truthfulness. He was endowed with excellent qualities, such as bowing at the feet of devotees of the Lord who burned the three cities that caused harm to the world, and offering them sacred food. He was not only a Brahmin by birth but also excelled as a Brahmin through his disciplined conduct. The second and third verses are: "Yazhin Mozhiyal Thanipagarai Potrum Yagam Uzhin Muraimai Vazhuvathu Ulagangalan Ezhum Uvappa Purinthu Inpura Seytha Petral Vazhum Thiram Eesar Malarkazhal Vazhthal Enbar. Eththanmaiyarainum Eesanukku Anbar Endral Atthannamaiyanamai Aalbar Endru Kolvar. Sitham Theliya Sivan Anjezhuthu Othum Vaimai Nitham Niyamam Ena Potrum Neriyil Nindrar." He was one who performed Shiva Yagas, praising Lord Shiva, the unique half of Umaiyal, whose speech is like the sweet sound of a Yaazh (lute), without deviating from the prescribed rituals. He had the blessing of performing these Yagas in a way that delighted and brought joy to all seven worlds. A Yaga performed for Lord Shiva is a Shiva Yaga. Yagas performed for other deities like Indra, Brahma, Vishnu, etc., are Kamya Yagas. Shiva Yagas are different from them. Other Yagas bestow benefits like heavenly pleasures, but once the merits are exhausted, they lead to rebirth. However, Shiva Yagas lead to liberation, which is a path of no return. In the Puranam of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, we have seen the history of how Sivapada Hrudayar, the father of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, performed these Shiva Yagas, and how Aludaiya Pillaiyar (Thirugnanasambandha Peruman) prayed to Lord Shiva and obtained wealth for the performance of these Yagas. It is said that through such Shiva Yagas, the evils of Kali Yuga will be removed, and the world will flourish with joy. He was distinguished by the great fortune of performing Shiva Yagas. To have such determination, one needs prior penance. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman highlights the special history of the Sthala Mahatmya regarding the significance of the Yaga performed by Somasi Mara Nayanar at this place. Somasi Mara Nayanar, regardless of their lineage or other qualities, considered devotees of Lord Shiva as their masters. To attain clarity of mind, he always adhered to the practice of reciting the sacred five-letter mantra of Shiva according to the rules, considering it his daily observance. He possessed the noble quality of serving devotees of Lord Shiva, even if they were of lower birth, wealth, or character, believing that they were the ones who owned him. As Nambiyandar Nambigal stated, "Anjezhuthum Vilambiyal Nan Mozhiyan Nithya Niyaman," the divine Sekkizhar Peruman also emphasizes the same here. The daily observance of reciting the five-letter mantra is an excellent means to attain clarity of mind. Sivagnana Bodham states that it is the five-letter mantra that bestows knowledge. The meaning of these should be learned directly from experienced teachers. Now, the fourth verse: "Seerum Thiruvum Pozhiyum Thiruvaroor Eythi Aaram Thigal Marbin Anukka Vanthondarkku Anbal Sarum Perunnanbu Sirappu Adainthu Thangi Paarum Visumbum Paniyum Patham Patru Ullar." Thiruvaroor is a place that bestows great glory and sacredness of Shaivism. "Thiru" refers to the true sacredness of Shaivism, which is the sacredness of liberation. This comes from association with the Lord. "Seerum Thiruvum Pozhiyum Seer" refers to the glory of Shaivism. This benefit comes from the company of devotees. Both of these flourish greatly in Thiruvaroor. The glory that comes from the composition of Thiruthondathogai for the salvation of the world, and the sacredness of liberation that comes from the rule "birth in Thiruvaroor leads to liberation," are prevalent in this city. In such a Thiruvaroor, our Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, with the appearance of royal and Vedic sacredness, resided there by the command of the Lord. Somasi Mara Nayanar, with great love for Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, the close devotee and Vanthondar, attained great friendship and stayed there, holding on to the sacred feet of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, which are worshipped by both the earth and the heavens. The feet of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal are worshipped by those in the heavens. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, in the past, made the Halahala poison very small and offered it to the Lord, thereby protecting the Devas. Moreover, now, in his incarnation as Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, when he is about to go to Thirukailayam, Indra, Vishnu, Brahma, and other celestial beings will come to welcome him and escort him on a white elephant to Thirukailayam. Therefore, the feet of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal are worshipped by both the earth and the heavens. Somasi Mara Nayanar held on to the sacred feet of such Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Now, the fifth verse: "Thuntrum Pulan Ainthanudan Aaru Thogutha Kutram Ventru Ingu Ithu Nanneri Serum Vilakkam Endre Vanthondar Padam Thozhuthana Sirappu Vayppa Endrendrum Nilavum Sivalogathil Inpamutrar." Somasi Mara Nayanar conquered the five senses and the six vices. Conquering the five senses means not being led by them, but rather controlling them and making them follow one's own path. Control does not mean suppressing their ability to act, but rather making them align with one's will. The Thirumandira verse says, "Ainthanum Adakkil Asethanamam Ondittu Ainthanum Adakka Arivarinthane" (If one controls the five, it becomes inert; I have known the wisdom of not controlling all five). "Kankal Kanminkalo Sevikal Kenminkalo" (Oh eyes, see! Oh ears, hear!) - according to this command, making the five senses obey our instructions is what it means to conquer them. If one does not conquer them, then the quality of bowing at the feet of devotees, the noble quality of offering them sacred food, the determination to praise the sacred feet of the Lord, the daily observance of reciting the five-letter mantra of Shiva for clarity of mind, and the state of holding on to the feet of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal can only be achieved if these five senses are conquered. Now, he conquered the six vices. The six vices are Kama (lust), Krodha (anger), Lobha (greed), Moha (delusion), Mada (arrogance), and Matsarya (envy). Conquering them means preventing them from approaching oneself. Thus, by conquering the five senses and the six vices, Somasi Mara Nayanar attained the special blessing of worshipping the feet of Vanthondar, believing that this was the way to join the good path of Shaivism in this world and attain salvation. He enjoyed eternal bliss in Shivaloka. Just as a lamp guides those walking in darkness and shows them the path and pits, in this worldly journey filled with evils such as ego, sensual pleasures, lust, and other vices, the feet of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal are our guide, leading us on the right path. That alone removes darkness, shows light, and guides us on the right path. That is why the divine Sekkizhar Peruman sang, "Thiruthondathogai Munpanitha Thiruvalan Vasamalar Menkazhal Vananga Vantha Pirappai Vananguvam" (Let us worship the birth that came to worship the soft, fragrant feet of the noble one who composed Thiruthondathogai). The author will also say, "Oruvar Saraname Saranamaai Vanthadainthome" (We have taken refuge in the feet of one person). Thus, by worshipping the feet of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal and attaining that special blessing, Somasi Mara Nayanar attained the joy of living in the eternal, imperishable Shivaloka. This is the bliss of liberation, a state of no return. Somasi Mara Nayanar has joined there and is enjoying bliss. In this Puranam, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman has presented the Puranam in a cause-and-effect manner, stating that due to the greatness of his Brahmin lineage and his service (Somasi Mara Nayanar's service), and due to his performance of Yagas, he attained a life of Shiva consciousness. Through these two, he attained the state of following devotees as his leaders and the daily observance of reciting the sacred five-letter mantra of Lord Shiva. As a result of these, he attained the great friendship of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, the state of holding on to the feet of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, and thereby the life in Shivaloka. Thus, the sacred Puranam of Somasi Mara Nayanar concludes. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of our Lord Somasi Mara Nayanar. We are currently contemplating the sixth chapter, known as the Vambara Vari Vantu chapter. It is the sixth chapter among the 13 chapters of Periyapuranam. This chapter was composed to match the fifth verse of Thiruthondathogai. The entire Puranam of the Nayanmars praised in the fifth verse of Thiruthondathogai concludes with the Puranam of Somasi Mara Nayanar. As per "Vambara Vari Vantu Mananara Malarum Mathumalar Narkondraiyan Adiyallal Pena Embiram Sambandhan Adiyarkkum Adiyen," we contemplated the Puranam of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman. As per "Eiyarkon Kalikkaman Adiyarkkum Adiyen," we contemplated the Puranam of Eiyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar, and within it, the central part of the Puranam of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. As per "Nambiran Thirumoolan Adiyarkkum Adiyen," we contemplated the Puranam of Thirumooladeva Nayanar. As per "Nattamigu Thandirkkum Moorkarkkum Adiyen," we contemplated the Puranam of Thandi Adigal Nayanar and Moorka Nayanar. As per "Ambaran Somasi Maranukkum Adiyen," we contemplated the Puranam of Somasi Mara Nayanar. Thus, in this chapter, we have contemplated the Puranams of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, Eiyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar (and within it, the history of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal), Thirumooladeva Nayanar, Thandi Adigal Nayanar, Moorka Nayanar, and Somasi Mara Nayanar, in order, in a way that matches the respective verses. Now, the concluding line of this verse is "Aruran Aruril Ammanukku Ale." The meaning of this is that Sundaramoorthy Swamigal sings that "I, Aruran, who am a servant to these six devotees praised in this verse 'Vambara Vari Vantu,' am also a servant to the Lord of Thiruvaroor." Therefore, in accordance with this, Nambiyandar Nambigal Swamigal composed a praise for Sundaramoorthy Nayanar in Thiruthondar Thiruvandathi. That verse is the 40th verse in Thiruthondar Thiruvandathi, as follows: "Thunaiyum Alavum Illathavan Thannarule Thunaiya Kanaiyum Kathir Neduvellum Karutha Kayal Inaiyum Pinaiyum Nikartha Kan Sangili Peramaithol Irandum Anaiyum Avan Thiruvaruran Agindra Arputham." The meaning of this verse is that Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, who is called Thiruvarooran, is a wonder. He took the grace of the Lord, who has no equal or measure, as his support. He was blessed to be the husband of Sangili Ammaiyar, whose eyes resemble two dark carp fish, an arrow, and a radiant long spear, and whose shoulders are like bamboo. Nambiyandar Nambigal thus composed the praise of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, in accordance with the lines composed by Sundaramoorthy Swamigal in the collection, "Aruran Aruril Ammanukku Ale." Now, following the same method, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman presents the praises of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal in a verse as the concluding verse of this chapter, the Vambara Vari Vantu chapter of the detailed work Periyapuranam. We see this method at the end of every chapter. At the end of every chapter, we see the divine Sekkizhar Peruman composing a praise for Sundaramoorthy Nayanar. In that way, the praise of Sundaramoorthy Nayanar that concludes this chapter is the 3635th verse in Periyapuranam. The verse is: "Panaiyum Thadamum Pudaisuzhum Otriyuril Pagaththor Thunaiyum Thammum Piriyathar Thozhath Thambiranarai Inaiyum Kongai Sangiliyar Ezhil Menpanaithol Eythuvikka Inaiyum Oruvar Saraname Aranamai Adainthome." The meaning of this is that the divine Sekkizhar Peruman has composed the praise of Sundaramoorthy Nayanar, saying that in Thiruottriyur, surrounded by fields and ponds, the Lord, who is inseparable from his consort Umaiyal, made Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, his friend, marry Sangili Ammaiyar, whose breasts are like tender bamboo. We have taken refuge in the feet of such a person, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, as our protection. With this verse, the sixth chapter, the Vambara Vari Vantu chapter, concludes. Now, the seventh chapter, the Varkonda Vanamulaiyal chapter, is about to begin. We will continue to contemplate it. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of our Lord Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of Nambiyandar Nambigal. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the commentator Sivakkavimani Ayya. Thiruchitrambalam.
