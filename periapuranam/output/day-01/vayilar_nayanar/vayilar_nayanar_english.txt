Chapter 01 — Vayilar Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Part 984, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Karaikandan Sarukkam, Vayilar Nayanar Puranam. Following the Nindra Seer Nedumaran Nayanar Puranam in the Periyapuranam composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, the Vayilar Nayanar Puranam is the 51st sacred purana. Firstly, in the Thirutthondathogai, a collection of works, our Lord Sundaramoorthy Swamigal praises Vayilar Nayanar as follows. This is the eighth sacred verse of Thirutthondathogai. He praises him saying, "I am a servant to the devotees of Vayilar of ancient Mayilai, whose red coral, brought from the sea, illuminates and dispels darkness." The red corals, brought by the waves from the sea, possess the quality of emitting light that dispels darkness. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal praises that he is a servant to the devotees of Vayilar Nayanar, who came from the ancient Thirumayilapur. 'Thurai' means seashore. 'Sembaavalam' refers to corals brought by sea waves. The corals mentioned here are small, luminous insects that live by forming coral-like structures. They are brought to the forefront by the waves and emit light even at night. They glow at the edges of the waves even at night. This light is characteristic of these coral insects. Moreover, by mentioning coral, we can also include pearls and other related items. Such an ancient city is Mylapore. Its antiquity and greatness are seen in the sacred path of Aazhuda Pillaiyar. We also see it through the miraculous divine grace that became known to the world. The meaning here is that he is a servant to the devotees of Vayilar Nayanar, who lived in such an ancient Mylapore. Now, in the 61st sacred verse of Thiruththondar Thiruvandhadhi, a classified work, Nambiyandar Nambigal Peruman praises Vayilar Nayanar. The sacred verse states, "They will declare that Vayilar of glorious Mylai, who established the unchanging grace of Aran in the temple of his mind, lit the beautiful lamp with inexhaustible grace, offered glorious flowers of the inner mind, and gave the true nectar of love." Vayilar Nayanar is a special Nayanar who unfailingly performed the inner worship (Agapooja) mentioned in Saiva Siddhanta. Vayilar Nayanar lit the lamp of wisdom in the temple of his mind for the Lord, performed the sacred ablution of bliss, prepared the sacred nectar of love, and daily performed inner worship without fail. His sacred history states that he was blessed to reside in the shade of the Lord's feet, having performed such inner worship for a long time. The Thiruththondar Thiruvandhadhi verse explains this. He established the unchanging grace of Lord Shiva in the temple of his mind, lit the beautiful lamp with inexhaustible grace, offered the eight flowers (Ashtapushpam) of inner worship, such as Ahimsa and Kshama, and offered the nectar of truth, which is love. Elders say that he is Vayilar, who was born in the glorious Thirumayilapur. This is the meaning of this sacred verse. This Nayanar attained liberation by sitting in Sivayoga and continuously performing inner worship. This is how the classified work is structured. Now, in the Periyapuranam, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, which is an elaborate work, the Vayilar Nayanar Puranam is the fourth purana in the ninth chapter, Karaikandan Sarukkam. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman praises the Vayilar Nayanar Puranam in ten sacred verses. Now, the first sacred verse of Vayilar Nayanar Puranam in Periyapuranam. "Thirumayilapuri, a prosperous city of truthfulness, where many great families and long lineages of wealth flourish, is located in Thondai Nadu, renowned for its glorious words." Thirumayilapuri, a city of great prosperity and truthfulness, is located in Thondai Nadu, which is famous for its glorious words, as described in many texts. Thirumayilapuri is a place where many great families and long lineages of wealth flourish. We have extensively discussed the sacred purana of Thirumayilapuri, or Mylapore, in various puranas such as Thirukurippuththonda Nayanar Puranam, Thirunavukkarasar Peruman Puranam, and Thirugnanasambandar Peruman Puranam. Such a city is Thirumayilapuri. The second sacred verse.

"The city, with its swaying flowery creeper-like mansions, is like a great treasure trove, where the vast sea stores its treasures, and ships laden with goods approach its shores." The city of Thirumayilapur itself is like a great treasury. Mylapore is so rich and prosperous, like a great treasury that the vast sea uses to store all its treasures, such as gems. We have already discussed in various places that Thirumayilapuri was a port city. The third and fourth sacred verses. "The ships unload black elephant calves, and the clouds that drift over the sea, along with the prosperous buffalo calves, are indistinguishable. The white mansions line the roads, adorned with fluttering flags. The pure white moon enters, and seeing the faces of the women of Mylapore, it seems to shy away and hide in a secluded place." The divine Sekkizhar Peruman sings about the wealth of Mylapore. On the vast seashore, ships brought from other countries are unloading elephant calves, importing them. There are clouds drifting over the sea. There are prosperous buffalo calves. These two are indistinguishable there. White mansions line the roads, adorned with many flags. The pure white moon enters through them. It seems to shy away and hide in a secluded place, seeing the faces of the women of Mylapore. The fifth sacred verse. "Throughout the streets, festive decorations abound, and bees, like messengers of young men, gather in the hair of women. Everywhere, there are beautiful treasures and decorations, and magnificent mansions adorned with wealth." Throughout the streets, there are festive decorations. Bees, like messengers sent by young men, move about and gather in the hair of women. In all the mansions, beautiful treasures and decorations are constantly present. The sixth sacred verse. "In this great city of Thirumayilapuri, renowned for its enduring glory, the ancient lineage of Sutras, known for its long history, flourished with excellence. From this lineage, Vayilar, a great ascetic, was born, embodying goodness." In this great city of Thirumayilapuri, which possesses such enduring glory, the ancient lineage of Sutras, known for its long history, flourished with greatness. The ancient lineage of Sutras refers to the Vellalar community. In the history of Thiruththondar Puranam, composed by Srimat Umapathi Sivachariyar, we see this Nayanar praised among the 13 Vellalars. 'Sutra' does not imply a low meaning. This is the principle held by our elders. 'Sutra' means one who possesses the authority of mantras, which are firm and subtle. We previously saw "Nambu Vaymaiyin Needu Sutra Narkulam" (the good lineage of Sutras, long-standing in truth). In such a lineage, Vayilar, a great ascetic, was born, as if to say that he was the best of all the good things that lineage had received. 'Vayilar' means one who is like a gateway to attain goodness. He is a 'Thapothanar'. 'Thapothanar' means one whose wealth is penance. He appeared as a Thapothanar at birth itself. As per Thirukkural, "If one is born, let it be with fame." The seventh sacred verse. "Vayilar, born into a great and ancient lineage, was the first to appear in that pure and great tradition. He shone with immense love and devotion in the sacred service of the Nayanars." Vayilar is the sacred name of this Nayanar. In fact, Vayilar is a family name. Just as Sekkizhar is a family name and became associated with our Nayanar, our great author of Periyapuranam, similarly, this Nayanar's sacred name became Vayilar because he was the first to appear in the ancient and great lineage of Vayilar, a pure and great tradition. Therefore, Vellalar is the caste. Its sub-division is 'kudi' (family). Its sub-division is 'marabu' (tradition). That tradition is the Vayilar tradition. That itself became the sacred name of this Nayanar. He shone with immense love and devotion in the sacred service of Lord Shiva, his master.

The divine Sekkizhar Peruman explains what he did in the subsequent sacred verses. The eighth and ninth sacred verses. "He established the Lord in the temple of his mind, built with unwavering remembrance, and lit the luminous lamp of understanding, which reveals the nature of the relationship. He performed the sacred ablution with the imperishable bliss and offered the nectar of love to the Lord, who wears the serpent. He performed inner worship. This holy and true devotee performed the inner worship, which blossomed from his heart, with ever-increasing love for the Lord, continuously for a long time, and resided in the shade of Lord Shiva's feet, praising Him." In this sacred verse, it is explained how Vayilar Nayanar performed inner worship. Sivagama calls this Antaryagam. Gnana Sastra states that Antaryagam itself is Gnana Puja, and it purifies the soul. He establishes the Lord within the temple of his mind, built with the instrument of unwavering remembrance of the Lord. Vayilar Nayanar establishes and firmly places Him. The temple of the mind, built with the instrument of unwavering remembrance, is in accordance with the Thirumandiram, "The mind is the great temple, the body is the shrine." Here, 'temple' refers to the sanctum sanctorum, and 'shrine' refers to the other circumambulatory paths. Thus, he establishes the Lord within the temple of the mind, which is built with the instrument of unwavering remembrance, and lights the luminous lamp of wisdom, which illuminates the understanding of the Lord. How does he perform the sacred ablution for the Lord? He performs the sacred ablution with the water of imperishable supreme bliss. All other forms of bliss are temporary and can lead to decay, but the bliss of Shiva's experience alone is imperishable. He performs the sacred ablution with the water of that supreme bliss, which is Sivananda. There, he offers the sacred nectar of love to Lord Shiva, who wears the serpent. Thus, he performs this inner worship. In this inner worship, which blossomed from his heart, he continuously performs the worship, which is filled with ever-increasing love for Lord Shiva, for a long time. This inner worship, mentioned in the Sivagamas, is the means to liberation. Chariya (righteous conduct) and sacred services include sweeping, cleaning, lighting lamps, bowing the head, and singing the praises of Shiva. These are like training the external organs such as hands, head, and tongue to follow the path of Shiva. Similarly, Kriya (ritualistic worship) and sacred services, such as offering incense, lamps, flowers, water, and food, are ritualistic services to Shiva. They train the internal organs, such as the senses and the inner mind, to follow the path of Shiva. This directly connects the internal organs, the inner mind, with Shiva, who is the innermost. Therefore, it is superior and more powerful than the Chariya service performed with external organs. Thus, the indispensability of this Kriya service is obtained. The sacred words of Thevaram, "Those who did not praise the golden feet of Aran with their hands, those who did not utter the name of Aran with their tongues, those who wandered seeking food for their bodies, will become food for crows and perish," illustrate this. If the senses and the inner mind are to function, life is indispensable. But if these inner mind and senses have the power to make this life dependent and enslave it to bodily pleasures, it is because they are under the command of the Creator who created these senses, bodies, and worlds. This is the truth of self-knowledge. Therefore, training these inner and outer organs to face the Lord and be transformed by His grace, through deep engagement in the service of the Lord, who is the Commander, is the only way for us to avoid being enslaved by these instruments. Therefore,

The only suitable way for the soul to transcend this state of dependence and attain self-reliance, where it merges with its body, Shiva, is through Shiva Puja. Thus, the indispensability of Shiva Puja is obtained. Sivagnana Siddhiyar emphasizes this: "The senses, though attached to me, do not obey my will; they drag me towards their own desires. This is because they act according to the command of the one who created them. If there are actions that arise from this, which allow one to remain free from suffering by worshipping the feet of the Lord, who owns them, and by His grace, then they are the actions of a liberated soul, and no karmas will afflict them, and they will not return." Thus, to ensure that the inner effort of directing the senses and the inner mind towards Lord Shiva gains strength, the practice of establishing an external form of Lord Shiva and performing Shiva Puja with incense and lamps has been prescribed. Therefore, we see that Antaryagam, or inner worship, is a prerequisite before performing external worship. Moreover, it is believed that Shiva Puja performed without Antaryagam, or inner worship, will lose its efficacy. This has been explained in texts such as Saiva Samaya Neri. Thus, this inner worship, which is the inner foundation of Shiva Puja, is practiced by those who have attained true knowledge, in a way that is conducive to their inner spiritual state. In external worship, the components such as the temple, Panchasuddhi (five purifications), incense, lamp, ablution, and offerings are each symbolically represented by an inner state, and the mind is seen as the Sivalinga and worshipped. The details of this type of worship are described in the Siva Agamas and Thirumurais. These should be learned properly from a qualified guru. Thirunavukkarasu Nayanar's Thevaram states, "We offered worship to the Lord, making the body the temple, the firm mind the foundation, truthfulness as purity, the mind as the luminous Lingam, love as ghee and milk, and pure water for ablution." And in Thirumandiram, Thirumooladevar Nayanar has said, "The mind is the great temple, the fleshy body is the shrine, the generous Lord's mouth is the gopuram's entrance, for those who are clearly enlightened, the soul is the Sivalingam, the five deceptive senses are the unextinguishable lamps." Vayilar Nayanar adopted this type of inner worship as his suitable sacred service, and through it, he attained bliss at the feet of Lord Shiva. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman says, "This holy and true devotee performed the inner worship, which blossomed from his heart, with ever-increasing love for the Lord, continuously for a long time, and resided in the shade of Lord Shiva's feet, praising Him." In Vayilar Nayanar's Shiva Puja, unwavering remembrance, Shiva consciousness, bliss, and love, which are inner qualities, are respectively the temple, the sacred lamp, the ablution, and the offering. The puja continued for a long time. Vayilar Nayanar was blessed with the shade of Lord Shiva's feet, as the Lord was pleased with his puja. Thus, Pandit Kanthaiya hints at this point. Now, the tenth and concluding sacred verse. "Having praised the great Vayilar Nayanar, who established Lord Shiva with matted locks in the temple of his mind, worshipped Him with immense love, and attained the unwavering path of liberation among the devotees, we shall now speak of the prowess of Munaiyaduvar of Thiruneedur, who is full of glory." The divine Sekkizhar Peruman concludes the sacred purana of Vayilar Nayanar by stating that he will now speak of the prowess of Munaiyaduvar Nayanar, who lived in Thiruneedur, after praising Vayilar Nayanar, who established Lord Shiva in the temple of his mind, worshipped Him with immense love, and attained the unwavering path of liberation among the devotees. We too shall continue to contemplate the sacred purana of Munaiyaduvar Nayanar, with the help of Vayilar Nayanar's sacred feet. We also see a separate shrine for Vayilar Nayanar in the Kapaleeswarar Temple in Thirumylai. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of our Lord Vayilar Nayanar. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of our Lord Thirumoorthy Swamigal. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of Nambiyandar Nambigal Peruman. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the commentator Sivakavimani Ayya. Thiruchitrambalam.
