Chapter 01 — Moorkka Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. The Periyapuranam, Part 913, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, the chapter on Moorkka Nayanar, is presented after the chapter on Thandiyadigal Nayanar in the Periyapuranam. In the Thirutthondathogai, a collection of hymns composed by Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, in the fifth verse beginning with "Vambarā Varivandu," Sundaramoorthy Swamigal praises Moorkka Nayanar, saying, "Nāttamigu Thandikkum Moorkkarkkum Adiyēn." Therefore, "Moorkkarkkum Adiyēn" is the way Sundaramoorthy Swamigal praised him. Its meaning is, "I am a devotee of the Nayanar named Moorkkar." Now, in the Thirutthondar Thiruvandhadhi, a type of literary work, in the 38th verse, Nambiyandar Nambigal has spoken about Moorkka Nayanar. The verse is: "Thandalai chūzh Thiruvērkāttūr mannan tharugavatrāl Konda vallāyam vanchūdharai venru munkonda porul Mundra nannītran adiyavarkku ībavan Moorkkan enbar Nandhalai nīr on kudandhaiyil mēvu narchūdhanaiyē." Moorkka Nayanar, in the fertile Thondai Nadu, at the sacred place of Thiruvērkādu, performs the Mahēswara Puja, which is a sweet nectar for the devotees, and also performs Shiva's services. Engaged in this, he spent all his wealth on it. Thinking, "Now, to perform Mahēswara Puja for the devotees, wealth is needed," he came to the sacred place of Kumbakonam. There, he accumulated wealth by gambling and performed Mahēswara Puja for the devotees. This Nayanar was skilled in gambling. Therefore, using that skill, he won in gambling for the sake of the devotees, performed the necessary Mahēswara Puja for them, and attained the sacred feet of Lord Shiva. This is a brief summary of the life of Moorkka Nayanar. Nambiyandar Nambigal Swamigal has presented this in the Thirutthondar Thiruvandhadhi. The leader of Thiruvērkādu, surrounded by groves, is one who, through his skill in gambling, defeats all gamblers and uses the wealth he earns to provide for the devotees of Shiva, who wear sacred ash on their foreheads. Scholars say that the good gambler who lives in the town called Thirukudandhai is known by the sacred name Moorkka Nayanar. Thus, Nambiyandar Nambigal has presented the sacred history of Moorkka Nayanar in the Thirutthondar Thiruvandhadhi, a type of literary work. Now, in the Periyapuranam, which is the detailed work, the history of Moorkka Nayanar is narrated in 12 verses by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. This is the section that describes the life history and character of Moorkka Nayanar.

The first verse: "Mannippērugum perunthondai valanādu adhanil vayal parappum Nanniththila ven thirai Pāli nadhiyin vadapāl nalankul pathi Annappedagal kudaivāvi alarpukkāda varanginadai Minnakkodigal thukil kodigal vizhavil kādu Vērkādu." In the great and prosperous Thondai Nadu, which is ever-flourishing, the Pāli river flows, making it fertile. As the Pāli river flows, it spreads pearls with its waves across the fields of that water-rich Thondai Nadu. To the north of such a Pāli river, the sacred place of Thiruvērkādu shines as a prosperous town. What is the prosperity of this sacred place? It is the place where the Nayanar, who made even the inauspicious act of gambling auspicious, was born. Therefore, Thiruvērkādu is praised by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman as a prosperous town. Swans play in the ponds, diving into the water and entering the lotus and other flowers. In the dance halls of that place, women dance. In those mansions, silk flags flutter. Thus, Thiruvērkādu is a place full of festivals, a place full of dances during festivals. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman sings, "Alarpukkāda vizhavil kādu."

He sings. The Nayanar of this story, Moorkka Nayanar, first gambled here, and then, as the festivals filled the town, he gambled in Kudandhai. The divine poetic skill of Sekkizhar Peruman is evident in how he describes the town's prosperity, hinting at this story. Vērkādu is the name of the place. It is called Vērkādu because of the dense growth of Vēl trees. The name Vērkādu also came to this place because of the sacred spear of Lord Muruga and the sacred tank created by that spear. We have already discussed the greatness of the sacred place of Thiruvērkādu in great detail. It is a place with various special features, such as Lord Vedapurīswarar, Mother Balambigai, Velayudha Theertham, and the place where Lord Agasthya was shown the divine wedding form. Now, the second verse. "Semporpurisai Thiruvērkādu amarndha seyyasadaikkattrai Nambarkku umbarkku amudhaliththu nanjai amudhu seydhavarkku Imbar thalaththil vazhiyadimai enrum kunrādha iyalbil varum Thampatru udaya nilai vēllān kulaththil thalaimai sārndhu uḷḷār." Our Nayanar, Moorkka Nayanar, was born in the Velalar community, which holds a prominent position, with an unwavering devotion to the Lord, who resides in Thiruvērkādu with his red matted locks, and who, as the Lord, gave nectar to the celestials and consumed poison as nectar, and to whom this Nayanar offered hereditary service in this world. He was devoted to Lord Shiva and Shiva's devotees. He was firm in performing Shiva's services for Lord Shiva and services including Mahēswara Puja for the devotees. He was born in the Velalar lineage to perform hereditary service. The glory of hereditary lineage is a special characteristic of the Velalar community in Thondai Nadu. We have seen this in the history of Pazhayanur and Thiruvalangadu. The Nayanar's original name is not mentioned. "Moorkkar" is a name given to the Nayanar at the end of this story, based on his actions. Thus, our Moorkka Nayanar performs Shiva's services and devotees' services in Thiruvērkādu.

The third verse. "Kōdhin marabil pirandhu vaḷarndhu arivu koṇḍa nāḷ thoḍangi Ādhi mudhalvan thirunītrin adaivē poruḷ enru arindha Aranār Kādhala adiyārkku amudhākki amudhu seyyakkaṇḍu uṇṇum Nīthi muraimai vazhuvādha niyathi pūṇḍa nilaimaiyār." Moorkka Nayanar was born and grew up in an impeccable lineage. From the day he gained knowledge, he understood that the sacred ash of Lord Shiva, the primordial Lord, was the true essence. He would prepare food for the beloved devotees of Lord Shiva, watch them partake of it, and only then would he eat, adhering to this unwavering principle of justice. From his early childhood, when he began to gain knowledge, loving Shiva's path comes only through past penance. This was bestowed upon this Nayanar. We have seen that it began after birth and after gaining consciousness. We have seen in Ammaiyar's wonderful Thiruvandhadhi, "Pirandhu mozhi payinra pin ellām." Among the many Saiva paths that adhere to the sacred ash, this Nayanar excelled in performing service to the devotees. We see in the sacred histories of many Nayanars that they gave sacred bowls, loincloths, and whatever the devotees needed. Many Nayanars undertook both devotee service and Shiva service. Among them, this Nayanar followed the rule of preparing food for the devotees, feeding them first, and then eating himself. Therefore, Moorkka Nayanar is classified as one who attained liberation through devotion to Jangamas (wandering ascetics).

The fourth verse. "Thūya adisil ney kannal suvaiyin kaṟigal avai amaiththu Mēya adiyār thamai pōtri viruppāl amudhu seiviththē Āya poruḷum avar vēṇḍum paḍiyāl udhavi anbu miga Ēyumāṟu nāḷthōrum iṇaippani seydhu iṉputṟār." He prepares pure food, ghee, jaggery, and delicious curries, and respectfully serves the devotees who come, feeding them with love. In addition, he provides other things as the devotees desire, and thus, with great love, he performs this service daily and finds joy.

The fifth verse. "Inna seyalin ozhugu nāḷ adiyār migavum ezhundharuḷa Munnam uḍaimaiyāna poruḷ muzhuvadhum māḷa aḍimaiyuḍan Manna kāṇiyāna nilam matrum uḷḷa thiṟam viṟṟē Annam aḷiththu mēnmēlum ārā manaththār āyinār." As he continued these actions, devotees kept coming in large numbers. Moorkka Nayanar performed this service until all his possessions were exhausted. He sold all his belongings to provide food for the devotees. In addition, he spent all his savings and the wealth accumulated by his ancestors to perform this service for the devotees. He first spent the wealth he possessed, then the wealth accumulated by his ancestors, and thus continued the service. He even sold non-asset items like clothes and ornaments to perform service for the devotees. Even after doing all this, his mind was not satisfied, and he continued the service of feeding, always wanting to feed more. He had a mind that desired to do more.

The sixth and seventh verses. "Aṅgaṇ avvūr thamakkoṟu paṭṭu aḍiyār thaṅgaṭku amudhākka Eṅgum kāṇā vagai thōṇḍa iḷampāḍu eydhi irundhu ayarvām Thaṅgum vagaiyāl thāṉ munbu kaṭṭra thanmai naṟchūdhāl Poṅgum poruḷ ākkavum aṅgu peruvār iṇmaiyiṉil pōvār Peṭṭram ēṟi palikku varum perumāṉ amarum thāṅgaḷ Uṭṭra aṉbāl seṉṟu eydhi urugum uḷḷaththōḍum paṇindhu Kaṭṭra sūdhāl niyathiyām kaḍaṉum muḍiththē karudhār Ūr seṭṭra silaiyār Thirukkuḍandhai aḍaindhār vandhu sila nāḷil." These are the verses. After exhausting all his possessions to prepare food for the devotees, he reached a state where he had no support. Poverty, which is called "iḷampāḍu," befell him. The Nayanar had learned gambling well in his youth. He was skilled in gambling. He learned it in his youth, but the Nayanar had not used it to earn money until now. The skill he had in gambling was at the level of artistic knowledge. So, when the Nayanar had no possessions, the gambling he had learned earlier, which he had not forgotten, came to his mind. Therefore, he tried to earn money through gambling and attempted to accumulate wealth by gambling in Thiruvērkādu. Until then, gambling was at the level of artistic knowledge, but after this, it was to be used for preparing food for the devotees. Therefore, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman refers to it as "narchūdu" (good gambling) here. Gambling is one of the five great sins and needs to be explained as such. But when this same gambling is used for the benefit of Shiva's devotees, it becomes good, and the divine Sekkizhar Peruman refers to it as "narchūdu." We must understand the difference between the nature of gambling in stories like Nala's and the Mahabharata and the nature described here. Sometimes, those who engage in gambling get great benefits and good things. Some people, tempted by gambling, thinking they can easily earn a lot of money, lose all their possessions and suffer. Gambling, which initially seems to bring prosperity, makes bad desires flourish and thus leads to poverty. Even in these days, there are many types of gambling, such as lotteries, horse racing, and betting. People get involved in these, lose their minds, and wander in confusion. We know that in the current era, gambling has taken a very modern form.

However, Moorkka Nayanar engaged in this same gambling, earned money, and spent it on Mahēswara Puja. There was no one in Thiruvērkādu who could defeat Moorkka Nayanar in gambling. We have seen that Thondai Nadu is a good country, full of scholars. Therefore, gambling here was only learned as an art. So, Moorkka Nayanar left Thiruvērkādu, as he had no means to provide food for the devotees there. He realized that he could not earn money by engaging in the gambling he had learned, so he set out in search of another town. The Nayanar visited many places where Lord Shiva resides with love and devotion. He went not with the intention of earning money through gambling, but with the love to care for the devotees in any way possible. Having completed his daily prescribed duties, he reached Thirukudandhai, also known as Kumbakonam, where Lord Shiva resides. In the world, there are both good and evil. Both are subject to the creation of Lord Shiva. These were created by the Lord for beings to experience their karma, exhaust it, and become refined. Both are desirable for the sake of beings experiencing according to their maturity. The words of Pattinathar say, "Nalamil vazhikkē, nanmai theemai nin seyal aadhaliṉ nāṉē amaiyum." And the words say, "Meimmaiyum poimmaiyum āyinārkku sōdhiyumāy iruḷ āyinārkku thunbamumāy inbam āyinārkku bandhamumāy vīḍu āyinārkku." Therefore, evils like gambling are ordained by God to refine the wicked through experience. They are like medicine for diseases and schools for reforming the guilty. As long as there are beings with different levels of maturity, good and evil will exist in the world according to the command of God. To say and attempt to make everyone equally prosperous without considering their maturity is a thought that comes from foolishness. One cannot overcome these without abiding by the command of God. Gambling also takes place during festivals. There is a difference between gambling that takes place in other parts of the world and gambling that takes place during festivals in sacred places of God. In other places, those who practice gambling get caught up in it and perish. But those who practice gambling in sacred places during festivals, without being so, receive good fortune due to the unconscious merit of remembering those pure places, festivals, devotees, and the Lord, and seeing Him. Therefore, they stand on the path of salvation due to that unconscious merit. Thus, our Nayanar arrives at the sacred place of Thirukudandhai, also known as Kumbakonam.

The eighth and ninth verses. "Iruḷārum maṇikaṇḍaṉ aḍiyārkku iṉ amudhākka Poruḷāyam eydhudhaṟku pugazh Kuḍandhai ambalaththē Uruḷāya sūdhu āḍi oru poruḷ veṉṟaṉa Nambar Aruḷāgavē koṇḍu aṅgu amudhu seiviththu iṉpuruvār. Mudhal sūdhu thām thōtṟu mudhal paṇaiyam avargaḷ piṉ Sūdhu palamuṟaiyum veṉṟu perum poruḷ ākki soṟ sūdhal Maruththārai surigai uruvi kuththi naṟ sūdhar Moorkkar enum peyar peṭṭrār nāṉilaththil." To offer sweet sacred food to the devotees of Lord Shiva, who has a dark beautiful throat, he gambled with rolling dice in the public place of the renowned Thirukudandhai (Kumbakonam) to acquire wealth. He considered the winnings and the wealth gained as the grace of God and joyfully prepared and offered food to the devotees there. When he gambled, he would first intentionally lose, allowing his opponent to win the first stake. This would make the opponent more engrossed in the game, leading them to stake large sums. When they staked large sums, he would win all those items through his gambling skill, making them his own, and thus accumulate vast wealth. If the defeated gamblers refused to hand over the winnings, he would draw his sword, stab them, and take the items to prepare food for the devotees in this world. Because of this, he earned the sacred name Moorkkar. Therefore, "Moorkkar" became a descriptive name for this Nayanar.

The tenth and eleventh verses. "Sūdhiniḻ veṉṟu eydhum poruḷ thurisu atṟa nal uṇarvil Thīdh agala amudhu ākkavār koḷḷa thām thīṇḍār Kādhalaḍan aḍiyārkaḷ amudhu seyyakkaḍaippanthi Ēdham ilā vagai thāmum amudhu seydhu aṅgu irukkum nāḷ Nāthaṉ than aḍiyārkku nal aḍisil nāḷthōrum Ādharaviṉāl amudhu seiviththu aṅgu aruḷālē Ēdhaṅgaḷ pōy agala ivvulagai viṭṭathaṉ piṉ Bhūthaṅgaḷ isai pāḍa āḍuvār puṟam pukkār." As mentioned before, he would use the wealth gained from gambling, without any taint, with a pure mind, to prepare sacred food, without even touching it himself. He would lovingly serve the food to the devotees. After that, he would sit at the end of the line, without any fault, and partake of the food himself, remaining in that sacred place. Therefore, the Nayanar, to avoid the sin of gambling and the sin of enjoying the wealth obtained through gambling, would not even touch the wealth obtained through gambling with his own hands, but would use it directly for preparing sacred food. If he ate at the end of the line after all the devotees had eaten, it was because it was the remnants of the devotees' food, and also because he wanted to live to prepare food for the devotees. Thus, he accepted the food at the end of the line. In this way, by following Shiva's grace and being devoid of self-consciousness, in a state where self-knowledge was destroyed, all the evils arising from gambling did not affect the Nayanar. All those faults disappeared with his body. Without any action for himself, becoming one with God, the actions of the devotees who stand in God's service become his own actions, and God's grace prevents the faults arising from those actions from reaching the devotees. Even if mistakes occur, because he has no actions for himself and stands as that, God makes the actions of the devotees His own. Therefore, after our Moorkka Nayanar left this world, having been freed from faults by the grace of God, after daily preparing good sacred food with love for the devotees of Lord Shiva, the leader, he entered the world of Shiva, Shivapuram, where Shiva dances with the Bhutaganas singing. This is referred to as the bliss of liberation, a path from which there is no return. After leaving this worldly life, the Nayanar does not take another birth but enters the life in Shiva's world and enjoys bliss in the state of pure Advaita Saiva Siddhanta liberation. Thus, the sacred history of the Nayanar is presented.

Now, the twelfth and final verse. "Vallārgaḷ thamai veṉṟu sūdhāl vandha poruḷ Allārum karaikaṇḍar aḍiyavargaḷ thamakku ākkum Nallār naṟchūdharām Moorkkar kazhal nām vaṇaṅgi Sollār sīr Sōmāsi Mārar thiṟam sollvōm." This is the verse. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman, having completed the sacred history of Moorkka Nayanar, says that we worship the sacred feet of Moorkka Nayanar, who is a good person and a good gambler, who defeated gamblers and used all the wealth obtained through gambling to prepare sacred food for the devotees of Lord Shiva, who has a dark throat, and now we will speak of the greatness of Sōmāsi Māra Nayanar, who is praised with abundant words and possesses the excellence of Vedic truth. Let us also, by bowing at the sacred feet of Moorkka Nayanar and taking refuge in those feet, now proceed to contemplate the sacred history of Sōmāsi Māra Nayanar. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of our Lord Moorkka Nayanar. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of Nambiyandar Nambigal. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the divine Sekkizhar Peruman.

Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the commentator, our esteemed teacher. Thiruchitrambalam.
