# English

Chapter 01 — Sirappuli Nayanar

The chapter of Varkonda Vanamulaiyal, the Puranam of Sirappuli Nayanar. In Periyapuranam, the Puranam of Sirappuli Nayanar follows the Puranam of Sakkiya Nayanar. In Tiruttondattogai, a collection of works, Sundaramurtti Swamigal sang, "I am a devotee of Sirappuli, the generous one of renowned fame." The word "Seer" means special. What kind of special? It is the special merit of devotion to Shiva's devotees, providing them with everything they needed. Such was this Nayanar, a generous one of renowned fame. As Tirukkural states, "Fame stands on those who give to the dying," he was named "Valla" (generous) due to his charitable nature of never refusing those who sought help. This indicates the merit of serving living beings (pasu punniyam). The "Seer" mentioned earlier refers to the merit of serving Shiva (pati punniyam). Such a great soul was Sirappuli Nayanar. Sirappuli is the sacred name of the Nayanar. Thus, Sundaramurtti Swamigal sang about this Nayanar in the sixth verse of Tiruttondattogai, "I am a devotee of Sirappuli, the generous one of such renowned fame." Now, in the detailed work, Tiruttondar Tiruvandadi, in the 42nd verse, Nambiyandar Nambigal praises Sirappuli Nayanar as follows: "He is our ancient Sirappuli, who stood firm in Shiva's discipline, a Brahmin, the chief of Thennakkur, renowned on earth, the cause of truth, who bestowed special favors upon the ascetics adorned with sacred ash and rudraksha, shining brightly on this earth." This means that our Sirappuli Nayanar was a Brahmin, the chief of Thennakkur, renowned on this earth, who possessed the quality of being the cause of truth, bestowing special favors upon the devotees of Shiva, the ascetics who wore sacred ash and rudraksha, shining brightly on this earth, and who stood firm in Shiva's discipline. Now, in the extensive work Periyapuranam, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman has composed the Puranam of Sirappuli Nayanar as the 35th sacred puranam. In the seventh chapter, Varkonda Vanamulaiyal, it is the second puranam. This section describes the historical life and character of Sirappuli Nayanar. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman has composed the sacred puranam of Sirappuli Nayanar in six verses. The first verse is: "Ponni neer nattin needum porpati puvanatthu ullor inmaiyal irandu sendrorkku illai ennade eeyum tanmaiyar endru nanmai sarnda vediyarai Sanpai mannanar aruli seyda marai tiru Akkur Akkur." This is the first verse. It speaks of an ancient and beautiful town in the Chola country, made fertile by the waters of the Kaveri. The town is called Akkur. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman has sung this in a beautiful play on words, "Tiru Akkur Akkur." The divine Sekkizhar Peruman has sung it to mean "Akkur, the town that creates wealth." When singing about Tiru Ambur, the sacred place where Tirunavukkarasu Peruman was born, he sang in a similar way, which comes to our mind. If we split it as "Tiruvakku ur," it also means "the town praised by sacred words." Tirugnanasambandar Peruman has praised this place in his Tevaram Tiruppadigam. "Inmaiyal sendru irandorkku illai ennadu eendu uvakkum tanmaiyal Akkuril tan tondri madame" - this is how Pillaiyar's Tevaram is structured. In the same Tiruppadigam, he sang about the Vellalas in that place, and then about the devotees in that place.

Tirugnanasambandar Peruman sings about these devotees. This was a town where many Brahmins lived. Therefore, he sings about the Brahmins with this quality. Sirappuli Nayanar was also a Brahmin, so the divine Sekkizhar Peruman has composed and sung this to highlight the greatness of the Nayanar's lineage. It mentions "Marai Tiru Akkur." "Marai" refers to the Tevarams. "Marai" also means that which is like the Vedas, revealed by God. This town had the blessing of receiving those Tevaram words. Now, the second verse: "Tumalarcholai torum sudar todu madamturum mamalai muzakkam tala marai oli muzakkam ongum pu mali marugil itta pugai agil tubam tala oma nal velvichalai ahuti tubam ongum." This verse describes how in every grove filled with pure flowers, and in every tall mansion shining with light, the sound of the Vedas rises, overpowering the roar of great clouds. The smoke from the agil incense offered in the fragrant streets is overshadowed by the smoke of the offerings from the sacrificial halls where good homas are performed. Such is this sacred place. This is a town of Brahmins, and so the divine Sekkizhar Peruman describes its glories in this way. The "Marai muzakkam" (sound of Vedas) in the groves refers to the sounds emanating from the branches of young Vedic students who are studying the Vedas. At the same time, the "Marai muzakkam" in the mansions refers to the sounds of Vedic mantras recited during the three fire sacrifices performed in the homes of those Brahmins. It is said that when Vedic mantras are recited in a high pitch (nishada swaram), they sound like the roar of clouds or the trumpeting of elephants. The Vedic sounds there were even louder than those. This is a meaningful sound, and because many people recited it in a high pitch, it became a roar. In the streets, fragrant smoke was offered for good aroma and for the well-being of the people. But the smoke rising from the homes of the Brahmins, where they performed fire rituals and sacrifices, rose higher than that incense. This means that this place excelled in both worldly and Vedic glory. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman explains this to us through these two examples. Now, the third verse: "Alai suzh puga veli attiru Akkur tannil nalamar pugazhin mikkar nanmarai kulattin ullar nilamar kandattu en tol nirtthar tam tiruttondu etra silaraay salum eegai tirattinil siranda nirar." This verse describes that in that sacred Akkur, surrounded by sugarcane mills and fences of areca palms, there lived people of great renown in the world, belonging to the lineage of Brahmins who recited the four Vedas. They were virtuous, having undertaken the sacred service of the Dancer (Shiva) with the dark throat and eight shoulders, and excelled in the noble art of giving. The "salum eegai tiram" mentioned here refers to the nobility of giving, which means giving with discernment. As in the verse from Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam, in the chapter where Vadhavuradigal was instructed, "like those who weigh poverty and benefit and then give," such should be the art of giving. In the worldly sense, giving to the poor is this art of giving. Our Nayanar possessed this art of giving in such a way. What is mentioned here is "pasu punniyam" (merit from serving living beings), which is measurable. Now, the "pati punniyam" (merit from serving Shiva) performed by the Nayanar, which is giving to Shiva's devotees, is described in the next verse. The fourth verse: "Alum angganarukku anbar anainda podu adiyil tazhndu mulum adaravu ponga munbu nindru iniya kuri nalum nal amudam utti nayandana ellam nalki nilum inbattul tangi nidi mazhai mari pondrar." This verse describes that when the devotees of Shiva, the Lord who rules all worlds, arrived, he would fall at their feet, his overflowing love surging forth, and stand before them, speaking sweet words. Every day, he would feed them good sacred food and give them everything they desired. Thus, he lived in ever-increasing joy, like a cloud that showers wealth like rain. Only those who have lovingly worshipped and served Shiva's devotees can truly know this joy. For others in the world, this joy is unknowable. This is an enduring joy, a joy that extends until the ultimate joy of liberation. Now, the fifth verse: "Anjezhuttu odi angi vettu nal velvi ellam nanjani kandar padam nannida seydu nalattu enjalil adiyarkku endrum idaiyara anbal vallal tan seyal vayppa isar tal nizhal tanginarare." This verse states that by regularly chanting the five sacred letters (Panchakshara), maintaining the three sacred fires, and performing all good sacrifices in a way that pleased the feet of Shiva, who has a poison-adorned throat, and by continuously showing unwavering love to the countless devotees of Shiva in the world, he, the generous one, made his actions align with his nature, and thus resided in the shade of the Lord's feet. "Anjezhuttu odi angi vettu" refers to these daily rituals. "Angi" refers to the three sacred fires (nittiyagni) maintained in the homes of Brahmins. "Vettu nal velvi seydu" refers to performing good sacrifices. These sacrifices are distinct from the nittiyagni and are performed for specific purposes as needed. These are typically performed in sacrificial halls located in urban areas. Now, "nanju ani kandar pada nannidutal" refers to Shiva yagas. These are different from sacrifices performed for other deities or for other benefits. These are sacrifices described in the Shiva Agamas. By performing such sacrifices and continuously offering love to Shiva's devotees, our Nayanar shone as a generous one. Thus, by performing such sacred service, the Nayanar attained the blessing of residing forever in the shade of the Lord's feet. This sacred puranam concludes with the sixth and final verse: "Arattinil men mikka menmai andar Akkur tannil maraipperu vallalar van Sirappuliyar tal vazhtti sirappudai Tiruchengattangudiyinil semmai vaynda viral Siruttondar seyda tiruttondil vilambalutren." The divine Sekkizhar Peruman sings this concluding verse, stating, "Having praised the feet of Sirappuli Nayanar, the great and generous Brahmin from Akkur, a town where Brahmins of exalted virtue reside due to their adherence to dharma, I now proceed to narrate the sacred service performed by Siruttondar Nayanar, who possessed a virtuous character, in the glorious Tiruchengattangudi." Thus, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman concludes the sacred history of Sirappuli Nayanar, which he has been narrating, and sets the stage for the upcoming sacred history of Siruttondar Nayanar. We too, having bowed to the sacred feet of Sirappuli Nayanar and with his grace, shall now contemplate the sacred history of Siruttondar Nayanar, the sacred puranam, in the manner shown by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. We shall continue to contemplate the Puranam of Siruttondar Nayanar, a sacred puranam that melts even a stony heart. Glory, glory to the sacred feet of Sirappuli Nayanar! Glory, glory to the sacred feet of Sundaramurtti Swamigal! Glory, glory to the sacred feet of Nambiyandar Nambigal! Glory, glory to the sacred feet of the divine Sekkizhar Peruman! Glory, glory to the sacred feet of the commentator! Tiruchitrambalam.
