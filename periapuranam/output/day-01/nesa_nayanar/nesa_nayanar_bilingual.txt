# English

Chapter 01 — Nesa Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Part 1013, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. The Puranam of Nesa Nayanar from the Manniya Seer Sarukkam. In the divine Periyapuranam composed by Sekkizhar Peruman, the Puranam of Nesa Nayanar is the 67th Puranam, following the Puranam of Mangaiyarkkarasi Ammaiyar. Within the 12th Sarukkam, Manniya Seer Sarukkam, the Puranam of Nesa Nayanar is the third Puranam. Firstly, in the Thogai Nool, Thiruthondath Thogai, composed by our Lord Sundaramurthy Swamigal, he praises, "I am a devotee of Nesan." The sacred name of this Nayanar is Nesan. This is a descriptive and indicative name. The luminous spiritual understanding, known as Tejas or Tesu, which praises Lord Shiva and His devotees, is called Nesam. He who possesses that is Nesan, and thus the sacred name of the Nayanar is Nesa Nayanar. The meaning of "I am a devotee of such Nesan" is this. Now, in the Vagai Nool, Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi, Nambiandar Nambigal Peruman praises Nesa Nayanar in the 80th verse. "On that day, Vishnu, with his eye, worshipped and received the victorious discus weapon. Those who hold His feet are the Saivites, they say. Nesan, the weaver from Kambil, who wove and gave loincloths for the devotees to wear around their waists, attained goodness in this world." Lord Vishnu, on a previous day, plucked out his own eye and offered it in worship to Lord Shiva. The Lord, who bestowed the victorious discus weapon upon Vishnu for such worship, had His feet held by Nesa Nayanar, they say. The great Saiva devotees say that he, Nesan, wove and gave the clothes to be tied around the waist and the loincloths for the devotees of Shiva to wear around their waists, and thereby attained goodness in this world. He, Nesa Nayanar, was born in the Saliyar clan in the city of Kambil. Everyone would say that he was one who held the feet of the Lord. This is the meaning of this verse. To hold the feet of the Lord means to always keep the Lord's feet in one's mind and meditate upon them. Thus, the history of Nesa Nayanar is narrated in this verse. Kambil is the sacred place where Nesa Nayanar incarnated and lived. This city of Kambil is located near Bellary in Karnataka state. Nesa Nayanar incarnated in the Saliyar clan in that place. This is also revealed in the Vagai Nool. Now, in the Vira Nool, the Puranam of Nesa Nayanar, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, consists of five verses, which the divine Sekkizhar Peruman explains to us beautifully. The first verse: "The city of Kambil, renowned for its growing glory, where people live with unwavering righteous conduct, with minds full of love and truthfulness, and goodness, a city of ancient fame, whose mansions' peaks touch the moon and whose turrets reach the dark clouds." In this verse, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman sings about the glory of the city of Kambil. It is a city where people live with excellent conduct, fostering virtues, with unwavering righteousness in their actions. It is a city where people live who are superior in the ethics of life, which are higher than worldly ethics. They are people who possess minds full of love, truthfulness, and good deeds. The excellence of this city is established by the nobility of its citizens. Mind, speech, and action – those great people living in this city are good in all three. Such a place is the city of Kambil. This ancient city, filled with abundant fame due to the good people living there, has mansions whose peaks touch the moon and whose turrets reach the clouds. The second verse: "Those who lived in that city, belonging to the Aruvaiyar clan, attained eminence in their traditional profession. They were devotees of the Lord adorned with snakes, taking His service as paramount, holding His feet on their heads and worshipping Him, they were the glorious ones named Nesar." Nesar, who is called Nesa Nayanar, lived in that sacred city of Kambil. He was born in the Aruvaiyar clan, which is the Saliyar clan. The Aruvaiyar clan is the lineage of weavers. It is also called the Aruvai Vanigar lineage. The name of the Sangam poet, Madurai Aruvai Vanigan Ilavettanar, is found in ancient texts. Aruvai refers to clothing. It is called Aruvai because it is cut from the loom. Similarly, clothes are called 'thuni' because they are cut, and 'vetti' because they are cut, these are descriptive names for clothes. Our Nayanar was born in the Aruvaiyar clan, the Saliyar clan, which performs such weaving work. This is about the excellence of his traditional profession in this worldly state. Similarly, we have reflected on the histories of Nayanmars like Thiruneelakanta Nayanar, Thirunalaipovar Nayanar, Adhipathar Nayanar, and others. Therefore, we see the excellence of those who excelled in their traditional profession and, by adhering to that profession, demonstrated the greatness of their sacred service. Such Nesa Nayanar lived taking the service of the devotees of Lord Shiva, who wears snakes as ornaments, as his primary duty. He performs the service of the devotees with utmost reverence. "If you make me a servant to serve your devotees, then bliss will come by itself, O Paraparam!" are the sacred words of Thayumanavar Swamigal. To bow at the feet of the devotees and take up their service means that the worship of the devotees is done through the mind, through speech by praising them, and through the body by taking it upon the head, thus indicating worship of the devotees through all three instruments. Such a glorious one, who performs the services of the devotees without fail, will attain spiritual enlightenment. That spiritual light is called Tesu. He who possesses that Tesu is Tesinar, he is Nesar. Therefore, Tesu means Tejas, light, fame, and love. Here, this word refers to love. The second verse. "He dedicated the actions of his mind to the sacred feet of Lord Hara, the actions of his exalted speech to the exalted five-letter mantra, and the actions of his hands to the devotees of the Lord, weaving appropriate clothes, lower garments, and faultless loincloths." In this verse, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, drawing upon the vocabulary and meaning of Thiruvachagam, beautifully illustrates instances in Periyapuranam. This is one of the finest such instances. Like the 26th verse in Thiruvachagam, Thiruchathagam, this verse is structured similarly. "My mind, I made it yours, my eyes, I made them for your sacred feet, my worship, I made it for those flowers, my speech, I made it for your precious words," just as it comes in Thiruvachagam, Thiruchathagam, this verse comes as: "He dedicated the actions of his mind to the sacred feet of Lord Hara, the actions of his exalted speech to the exalted five-letter mantra, and the actions of his hands to the devotees of the Lord." We see it this way. In that state, Nesa Nayanar dedicated the actions of his mind to the lotus feet of Shiva. This means that his mind constantly meditated on the feet of Lord Shiva. It means constantly meditating on the immense grace that Lord Shiva continuously bestows upon living beings. This comes through continuous practice. Thus, the action of Nesa Nayanar's mind was dedicated to the lotus feet of Shiva. Now, the action of Nesa Nayanar's exalted speech was dedicated to the exalted five-letter mantra. The action of speech means continuously chanting and uttering the five-letter mantra without interruption. It is the exalted five-letter mantra. The five-letter mantra is the foremost among all mantras. "The prime mantra is the five letters," as we saw in the Puranam of Thirugnanasambandar Peruman. This five-letter mantra is the Maha Mantra. There is no deity superior to Shiva, and no mantra superior to the five-letter mantra, so goes the proverb. This is a high mantra. It is superior to all mantras. This Sri Panchaksharam, the five-letter mantra, is the very form of the Shiva Shakti that operates all those mantras from within. "My speech, I made it for your precious words," as our Lord Manickavasagar Swamigal has said, those precious words are the five-letter mantra.

We also see the sacred words "Shiva's five letters, the sacred name of five letters, the precious words of a flawless gem." We also see the custom of referring to this as "iruthalai manikkam" (a gem with two ends). Such a five-letter mantra, it is the elevating five-letter mantra, meaning it elevates. Interpreting it as a causative verb, it is the five-letter mantra that lifts souls from the pit of birth. Therefore, Nesa Nayanar dedicated the actions of his speech to the exalted five-letter mantra. He dedicated all the actions performed by his sacred hands to the devotees of his Lord. With his sacred hands, he performed his traditional weaving work, and thus wove clothes, lower garments, and faultless loincloths, all for the devotees of the Lord. The fourth verse: "Weaving clothes, good lower garments, and peerless loincloths, he continuously gave them as desired by the devotees of the Bull-rider, and daily worshipped and praised their feet, attaining goodness, and reached the shade of Hara's feet." Nesa Nayanar wove clothes, good lower garments, and peerless loincloths, and gave them to the devotees of the Lord. This loincloth was a peerless loincloth. We read about the greatness of the loincloth in the Puranam of Amarnithi Nayanar, where it is mentioned as "the greatness of the exalted loincloth." Such clothes, good lower garments, and peerless loincloths, he wove them and continuously gave them as desired by the devotees of the Lord who rides the Bull. He worshipped and praised the feet of those devotees every day, attaining goodness. Such Nesa Nayanar reached the shade of Lord Shiva's feet. He attained the liberation that is the path of no return. Thus, the sacred Puranam concludes. Now, the fifth and concluding verse. "We shall now speak of the greatness of Kochchengatchozha Nayanar, the victorious king who ruled the earth, having built numerous temples for the Lord who rides the Bull, having understood his previous birth through the grace of Nesa, who cut off the karmic ties that bound him, and whose feet we worship, so that he might reach the feet of the Lord with matted locks." Having said this, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman concludes the Puranam of Nesa Nayanar and now begins the sacred Puranam of Kochchengatchozha Nayanar. Let us also worship the sacred feet of Nesa Nayanar and, with his grace, continue to reflect on the sacred Puranam of Kochchengatchozha Nayanar.
