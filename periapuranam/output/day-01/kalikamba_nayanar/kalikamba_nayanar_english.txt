Chapter 01 — Kalikamba Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. The Periya Puranam, Part 975, by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, the chapter on Poets Who Are Not False Devotees, the Puranam of Kalikamba Nayanar. Following the Puranam of Adhipatta Nayanar in the Periya Puranam by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, the Puranam of Kalikamba Nayanar is placed. This is the 44th Puranam. In the seventh verse of the Thiruthondathogai composed by our Lord Sundaramurthy Swamigal, it states, "I am a devotee to the devotees of Kalikamban, the archer who cut off a hand, Kaliyan, Kazharchathi, and Varinjaiyarkon." Here, "Kaithadinda varisilaiyan Kalikamban" refers to Kalikamba Nayanar, as praised by our Lord Sundaramurthy Swamigal. The phrase "Kaithadinda varisilaiyan Kalikamban adiyarkkum adiyen" is a compound construction. Its meaning is, "I am a devotee to the devotees of Kalikamba Nayanar, the archer who cut off his wife's hand." We will see the detailed explanation of this in the Puranam. "Varisilaiyan" means "archer." "Silai" means "bow." "Varisilai" means "a bow strung tight." "Varisilaiyan" means "the warrior who held that bow." Now, in the Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi, a concise work, the 53rd verse by Nambiandar Nambigal Peruman, praising Kalikamba Nayanar, is as follows: "He who, having shunned falsehood, became a servant to our Pious Lord, when his own servant, in the guise of a Saivite, came, and his wife, feeling ashamed before the world, did not pour water to wash his feet, cut off his wife's hand, that Kalikamban is from Pennagadam." Kalikamba Nayanar was one who removed falsehood and became a servant to our Pious Lord Shiva. He meticulously performed Maheswara Puja for the devotees of Shiva. The Nayanar would welcome the Maheswaras, the devotees of Shiva, who came to his house, and wash their sacred feet. To wash their sacred feet, Kalikamba Nayanar's wife would bring water in a vessel and pour it. One day, while they were regularly performing this ritual, a Saivite devotee arrived. This devotee was someone who had previously worked as a servant for Kalikamba Nayanar. He had now become a devotee of Lord Shiva and had adopted the attire of a Shiva devotee. When he arrived, since he was a Maheswara, Kalikamba Nayanar welcomed him and prepared to wash the devotee's sacred feet as usual. Kalikamba Nayanar's wife brought the water in a vessel, as she always did, for washing the sacred feet. Before pouring the water to wash the sacred feet, Kalikamba Nayanar's wife looked at the devotee. Since he was a former servant of Kalikamba Nayanar, she felt a slight sense of shame or hesitation regarding this worldly situation. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman and Nambiandar Nambigal Peruman have used the word "velki" here. "Velguthal" means to be afraid, to be angry, or to feel ashamed. Therefore, in this manner, she stood without pouring the water. Kalikamba Nayanar understood this. He understood what was in his wife's heart. He took the water vessel from his wife's hand, placed it in his own hand, and cut off his wife's hand. Such is the sacred history of Kalikamba Nayanar. Nambiandar Nambigal explains this very briefly in this one verse, using concise words. Therefore, he who, having removed falsehood, became a servant to his Pious Lord Shiva, when his own servant came in the guise of a Saivite ascetic, and his wife, feeling ashamed before the worldly people, did not pour water to wash his feet, cut off his wife's hand – that Kalikamban is from Pennagadam. This is the meaning of this verse: He is Kalikamba Nayanar, who lives in the sacred place of Pennagadam. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman has elaborated and sung this in ten verses in this Puranam.

The Puranam of Kalikamba Nayanar is the fifth Puranam in the eighth chapter, the chapter on Poets Who Are Not False Devotees, in sequential order. This section describes the historical narrative and character of Kalikamba Nayanar. Now, the verses of the Puranam of Kalikamba Nayanar in the extensive work, the Periya Puranam, by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. The first verse: The ancient town of Pennagadam, located in the west, shines with glory to the world, surrounded by fertile fields where flower gardens, where clouds reside, flourish, and where families live in the righteous path of tradition, adhering to their inherent duties. Pennagadam is a very ancient town. All the inhabitants living there are people of excellent conduct, belonging to a high and ancient lineage. Each tradition, prescribed as its own and coming from the practice of the elders, must excel without deviation in its conduct, as ordained in moral treatises and ancient texts like Tholkappiyam. Pennagadam is an ancient town where such excellent families, living in a high state of domestic life, prosper. This ancient town of Thiruppennagadam, surrounded by fertile fields where flower gardens, where clouds reside, flourish, shines with glory to the world. This world gained glory through the greatness of this town of Thiruppennagadam. How does a town, country, or city gain greatness? It is through the excellent conduct of the people living in that town. This town possessed the greatness of general excellent conduct, and even more so, excellent Shiva-devotional conduct. In this place, Thirugnanasambandar Peruman, the child of God, sang a sacred hymn, commanding the people of the world, "Worship to remove evil." As he sang and proceeded, he desired to visit Thiru Arathurai, and as he went from this place, he stayed in Maranpadi, which was on the way, and received the pearl palanquin and other gifts from the Lord of Thiru Arathurai, thereby illuminating this world. Such is the greatness of Thiruppennagadam. Thirunavukkarasu Swamigal had the bull and trident imprinted in this sacred place. Such is the greatness of this sacred town of Thiruppennagadam. It is in this same sacred town that Achutha Kalappalar lived and gave birth to Sri Meykandadevar Nayanar, who would come to enlighten this world with Shiva-gnana. Such is the glory of the sacred place, Thiruppennagadam. Therefore, this world gained glory. The second verse: In that sacred place, Kalikambar, who had no other attachment, was born into the merchant community that flourished there. He was devoted to the feet of Lord Shiva, who has matted locks, and this devotion grew with him from birth. He was born with a great love for the feet of the Lord. As he grew, his love and devotion for the Lord grew with him. Every day, in that sacred place of Thiruppennagadam, in the sacred Thungaanai Maadam, he served the feet of the Lord, who graciously resides there, known by the names Sudarkkozhundeesar and Pralayakaleswarar, and whose consort is known by the names Amodhanambal, Kadandai Nayagi, and Azhagiya Kaadhali. He was known as Kalikambar, who served the feet of this Lord. This Nayanar had no other attachment except to the feet of Shiva. The third verse: That loving devotee, Kalikamba Nayanar, always served food to the devotees of Shiva, offering excellent cooked rice, delicious curries, ghee, curd, sweet milk, sweet fruits, and jaggery, and joyfully provided all other necessities.

Milk, ghee, curd, sweet condensed milk, fruits sweeter than honey, jaggery, and other such items were offered to the devotees. He also joyfully provided all other things that the devotees desired. The fourth verse: One day, as he was performing such sacred service, he invited all the devotees who had come to his house to partake of the sacred food in the traditional manner. He first called them and attempted to wash their sacred feet. As he was performing such sacred service, one day, he invited all the devotees who had come to his house to partake of the sacred food in the traditional manner. He called them all and attempted to wash their sacred feet. This Nayanar attempted to perform Maheswara Puja in the customary manner, as prescribed in ancient texts. The first step in this is washing the feet of the devotees. We see this in the Puranam of Ilaiyankudi Mara Nayanar: "He brought them into his house and washed their beautiful feet." We saw in the Puranam of Kalaritrarivar Nayanar that he "took a vessel." We saw in the Puranam of Siruthondar Nayanar that "Siruthondar washed the feet of Jyotiar with pure water." Therefore, the first thing to do is to wash the sacred feet. This is called "Pada Prakshalanam" in Sanskrit. The fifth and sixth verses: While his chaste wife diligently prepared all the food, delicious curries, pure water, and other consumable items, and offered the water in a vessel, the great devotee, her husband, was washing the feet of the devotees. At that moment, a devotee, who had previously worked for him as a servant and had left his service, came there, having become a devotee of Lord Shiva, adorned in the sacred attire of Shiva, along with other devotees. He appeared before them, and the great man was about to wash his feet. In general, regarding hospitality and feeding devotees, the wife has duties. All the duties that his chaste wife had to perform are explained in this verse. Kalikamba Nayanar's chaste wife also performed all those duties as required. She prepared sacred food for the devotees. She meticulously arranged the delicious curries, pure water, and other items. In addition, when Kalikamba Nayanar was washing the feet of the devotees, his wife would bring pure water in a vessel and pour it. We saw this in the Puranam of Kalaritrarivar Nayanar: "The divine consorts brought pure water in golden vessels, and he proceeded to wash their beautiful feet." As Kalikamba Nayanar was washing the feet of each of the devotees who had arrived that day, his wife was pouring water from the vessel. At that moment, one of the devotees stood there. Kalikamba Nayanar attempted to wash his sacred feet. This devotee was someone who had previously worked as a servant for Kalikamba Nayanar. He had renounced this worldly life, become a devotee of Lord Shiva, and had come there adorned in the attire of Shiva, along with other devotees. This devotee had thought that serving Shiva was better than serving humans, and so he had done so. In his previous state, he was a servant to Kalikamba Nayanar, performing his duties. Now, having become a devotee of Lord Shiva, Kalikamba Nayanar was in a position to serve this devotee. "The gods who are worshipped will cause their devotees to be worshipped after they themselves have been worshipped," is the sacred saying of Lord Thirunavukkarasu Swamigal in his Thevaram. Kalikamba Nayanar, without considering the past, only thought of the state of devotion, humbling himself to the service of Lord Shiva, and with that great magnanimity, he proceeded to wash the feet of that devotee. The seventh and eighth verses: As he held the devotee's feet with his hand, his beloved wife, who had previously served him, hesitated to pour the water, thinking that he was a former servant who had left his service. As he was about to wash the feet of that devotee, the Nayanar held them. At that moment, his wife thought, "He used to work for us; he left that service." So she delayed pouring the water from the vessel. Kalikamba Nayanar, the devotee, understood this. She was delaying pouring the water meant for washing the sacred feet. Kalikamba Nayanar also understood the reason for this. He thought to himself, "This devotee is a servant of the Lord who wears the Konrai garland on his head. She is hesitant to pour water, feeling ashamed and angry, thinking of his past status, that he was a former servant, and wondering if she should pour water for him." Kalikamba Nayanar realized that she was refusing to pour water for washing the sacred feet. Immediately upon realizing this, he drew his sword, took the vessel from his wife's hand, and cut off his wife's hand. Then, he took the vessel, poured the water, and himself washed the sacred feet of that devotee. First, he took the vessel from his wife's hand and set it aside. This vessel contained sacred water to help the devotee, so it was not proper to punish it. Therefore, he first took it, and then cut off his wife's hand. This hand was guilty of obstructing the pouring of water, so he cut off that hand. This act, an extraordinary deed, brought him salvation. That is why Sundaramurthy Swamigal praises him in the concise work as "Kaithadinda varisilaiyan" (the archer who cut off a hand). The ninth verse: After washing the feet, he himself prepared all that was needed for the sacred food and, with an unwavering mind, served the devotees. That Kalikamba Nayanar, of immeasurable greatness, continued to follow the path of sacred service and, without deviation, joined the devotees under the shade of the Lord's feet, and attained an eternal state from which there is no return. The tenth and concluding verse: We shall now narrate the greatness of Kaliya Nayanar, also known as Kazhineethi Nayanar, a loving devotee who served Lord Shiva, after bowing to the lotus-like feet of Kalikambar, who cut off the hand of his wife who did not recognize the sacred attire of the Lord's devotee, who drank the poison from the ocean. Thus, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, in accordance with his tradition, concludes the Puranam of Kalikamba Nayanar, which he has narrated so far, and is about to begin the Puranam of Kaliya Nayanar. Let us also praise the sacred feet of our Lord Kalikamba Nayanar and continue to contemplate the Puranam of Kaliya Nayanar. Praise, praise to the golden sacred feet of our Lord Kalikamba Nayanar. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of our Lord Sundaramurthy Swamigal. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of Nambiandar Nambigal Peruman. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the commentator, Aiya. Thiruchitrambalam.
