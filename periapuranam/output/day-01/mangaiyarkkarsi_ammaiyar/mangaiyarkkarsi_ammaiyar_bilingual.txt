Chapter 01 — Mangaiyarkkarsi Ammaiyar

Thiruchitrambalam. The Periyapuranam, Part 1012, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. The Puranam of Mangaiyarkkarsi Ammaiyar, from the Manniya Seer Sarakkam. The Puranam of Mangaiyarkkarsi Ammaiyar is the second puranam within the twelfth sarakkam, the Manniya Seer Sarakkam, of the Periyapuranam composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. This section narrates the history and character of Mangaiyarkkarsi Ammaiyar. Firstly, in the eleventh verse of the Thiruthondathogai, a collection of verses by our Lord Sundaramurthy Swamigal, he praises Mangaiyarkkarsi Ammaiyar, saying, "I am a servant to Varivalaiyaal Maani." "Varivalaiyaal Maani" means "Maani, who is glorious." "Varivalaiyaal" refers to Mangaiyarkkarsi Ammaiyar, who wears bangles with patterns. Thus, Sundaramurthy Swamigal, our Lord Vanthondar, sings, "I am a servant to such Mangaiyarkkarsi Ammaiyar." Next, in the Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi, a detailed work by Nambiyandar Nambigal Peruman, he praises and worships Mangaiyarkkarsi Ammaiyar in the 79th verse. "Due to the unbearable heat of the fever that afflicted the Pandya king, who wielded a sharp spear to defeat his enemies, and to ensure the entire world would flourish and be saved by the truth and greatness of Saivism, she informed Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, the repository of Tamil, about the fever that had afflicted the king. Through his grace, Thirugnanasambandha Peruman cured the Pandya king's fever. Such a glorious Pandima Devi, known as Maani, or Mangaiyarkkarsi Ammaiyar, with the support of the divine grace of Aludaiya Pillaiyar, brought destruction to the Jains, known as Aruganthar, in this land. Behold this!" This is the meaning of this verse. Now, in the Periyapuranam, the elaborate work composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, the Puranam of Mangaiyarkkarsi Ammaiyar is the 66th puranam. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman has composed this puranam in three verses. The first verse: "She is the unique queen among women, our deity, the sprout of the glorious Chola lineage, the Maani with bangled hands, the divine maiden who dispelled the disgrace of the Pandya lineage, ruling the maiden land, the goddess of the red lotus. Through the grace of our Lord Sambaiyar Kon, she removed the suffering that befell the beautiful Tamil land and spread the vibrant, bright sacred ash. Can we ever truly praise the feet of those who praise her?" Thus, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman has composed this verse as a eulogy to Mangaiyarkkarsi Ammaiyar. How does he praise Mangaiyarkkarsi Ammaiyar? "Mangaiyarkku thani Arasi" - the peerless queen among all women. All these qualities are praised in the Thirualavai Thirupathigam by Aludaiya Pillaiyar, where Thirugnanasambandha Peruman sings, "Mangaiyarkkarsi, Valavarkon Pavai, Varivalaikkai Madamaani, Pangayachchelvi, Pandima Devi, serving and praising daily." The divine Sekkizhar Peruman praises her here as "Mangaiyarkku thani Arasi," echoing Thirugnanasambandha Peruman's praise of her as "Mangaiyarkku Arasi." The word "thani" further emphasizes her uniqueness. There may be many queens, but Mangaiyarkkarsi Ammaiyar, the Maani, is the unique queen among women. Next, he praises her as "Engal Deivam" (our deity). He praises her as "our deity" because she protected and nurtured the Saiva world and the Saiva path. "Engal" here includes the entire Saiva world. He praises her as "Valavar Thirukkula Kozhundhu" (the sprout of the glorious Chola lineage). The Chola lineage, known as the Valavar lineage, was a lineage that traditionally worshipped the sacred feet of Lord Shiva, who wears the crescent moon on his head and anklets on his feet, following the established Saiva path. Thus, Thirugnanasambandha Peruman sang "Valavarkon Pavai" in the Thirualavai Pathigam, and the divine Sekkizhar Peruman praises her here as "Valavar Thirukkula Kozhundhu." He praises her as "Valaikkai Maani." Thirugnanasambandha Peruman praised her as "Varivalaikkai Madamaani" in the Thirualavai Pathigam, and the divine Sekkizhar Peruman praises her here as "Valaikkai Maani." He praises her as "Sengamalath Thirumadandhai" (the goddess of the red lotus). Mangaiyarkkarsi Ammaiyar is like Gnanaambikai, who resides on a thousand-petalled lotus. Thirugnanasambandha Peruman praised her as "Pangayachchelvi" in the Thirualavai Thirupathigam, and the divine Sekkizhar Peruman praises her here as "Sengamalath Thirumadandhai." Next comes "Kanni Nadu Aal Thennar." "Kanni Nadu" refers to the Pandya country. "Thennar" refers to the Pandyas who rule that Pandya country in the south. He praises her as "Theivap Pavai" (divine maiden) who removed the disgrace of the Thennar lineage. The great disgrace, the sin against Shiva, that befell the lineage was the support the ancient Pandyas gave to Jainism, causing harm to the Saiva path. If one person in a lineage achieves great fame, that fame extends to the entire lineage. Similarly, the disgrace caused by one person also affects the entire lineage; this is the rule, the justice. Therefore, this was the disgrace of the Thennar lineage. Our Mangaiyarkkarsi Ammaiyar was the divine maiden who removed such a disgrace of the Thennar lineage. The implication here is that such a lineage disgrace is removed by a chaste woman born into that lineage. "Pavai" means one who is like a doll. Mangaiyarkkarsi Perumattiyar was the one who, through the grace of our Lord, the leader of Sirkazhi, removed the suffering that befell this great Tamil land and spread the vibrant, luminous sacred ash. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman marvels and praises, asking if it is even possible for us to praise the feet of those who praise the sacred feet of such Mangaiyarkkarsi Perumattiyar. The second verse: "How can we describe the greatness of Mangaiyarkkarsi Ammaiyar, who, through the pure words of the Lord of Pugali, the crown jewel of Brahmins, words that poured forth with divine knowledge, attained the merit of being praised in glorious songs? She remained a steadfast companion on the Saiva path for Nedumaran, the king of the South, whose fame was unblemished, and through her flawless conduct, she was blessed to reside under the shade of the Lord's feet along with him." Mangaiyarkkarsi Ammaiyar is a person of great past merit, worthy of being praised in songs of luminous knowledge, through the pure words of the Lord of Sirkazhi, the leader of the Brahmins, words that poured forth after consuming divine knowledge. The words of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman are pure words that emerged after consuming the milk of knowledge. These are words that do not deviate to anything other than Lord Shiva. Mangaiyarkkarsi Ammaiyar possesses the merit of being praised by such words. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman praises her as "Thesu Udaiya Paadal Perum Thavaththinar" (one who has the merit of being praised in glorious songs). "Thesu" refers to the light of divine knowledge. "Thavaththinar" implies that such a blessing cannot be attained without great past penance. How did Mangaiyarkkarsi Ammaiyar attain the merit of being praised by the words of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman? In the Thirualavai Thirupathigam, which begins with "Mangaiyarkkarsi," Thirugnanasambandha Peruman praised Mangaiyarkkarsi Ammaiyar's service in the first, third, fifth, seventh, and ninth verses. Furthermore, in the eleventh verse, the Thirukkadaikkappu, he praises Mangaiyarkkarsi Ammaiyar. Moreover, in the pathigam "Maani Ner Vizhi," Mangaiyarkkarsi Ammaiyar was praised by Thirugnanasambandha Peruman by being addressed directly. This unique honor of being addressed directly by Pillaiyar belongs exclusively to Ammaiyar Mangaiyarkkarsi Ammaiyar. Such is the great past merit she possesses. If one needs to have past merit to be praised by the words of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, then what about the Jains and Buddhists, these undesirable ones, whom Thirugnanasambandha Peruman also sang about in his words? This means that he sang so that they too might be saved according to their respective merits, and they too attained salvation. This is the meaning. How can we understand and praise the greatness of such an Ammaiyar of great past merit? Mangaiyarkkarsi Ammaiyar was a steadfast companion on the Saiva path for Nedumaran, the Pandya king of unblemished fame, for a long time. Previously, in the Puranam of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, we saw that Pillaiyar, after worshipping all the shrines in the Pandya country, took leave from him and returned to the capital Madurai from Manamelkudi with Nindraseer Nedumara Nayanar and Mangaiyarkkarsi Ammaiyar. After that, we saw his puranam to the extent of his puranam in the Puranam of Nindraseer Nedumara Nayanar. Now, in this context, where the history of Mangaiyarkkarsi Ammaiyar is concluded, we see the divine Sekkizhar Peruman very briefly narrating what happened next, and we conclude here. "Maasil Pugazh Nedumaran" - he was called Nindraseer Nedumaran after his blemish was removed. Before that, he was known as Koonpandiyar. After that blemish was removed by Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, he became Nindraseer Nedumaran. In such a state, Mangaiyarkkarsi Ammaiyar became a companion on the Saiva path for Nindraseer Nedumara Nayanar. This was a companion for his soul. When he was Koonpandiyar, Ammaiyar, as his Pandi Mahadevi, was a physical companion, and she sought and provided good for his soul. After Nindraseer Nedumara Nayanar stood firm in the Saiva path, Mangaiyarkkarsi Ammaiyar remained a companion on the Saiva path for a long time and fostered the path of Shiva. Thus, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman concludes the later history of Ammaiyar. Thus, the Puranam of Mangaiyarkkarsi Ammaiyar is structured. The third and concluding verse: "We begin to speak of the character of Nesa Nayanaar, a great and noble soul who was born into the lineage of the great weavers, who, without fail, on the day the divine Kaveri, which never fails to bring prosperity, overflows, weaves and provides clothing, loincloths, and undergarments to the devotees, just as the Chola lineage fosters their own prosperous land, and who shone as a lamp of glory, illuminating the Pandya country of the Cheras. We praise her sacred, small, divine feet, and with their support, we now begin to speak of the character of Nesa Nayanaar, a great and noble soul born into the lineage of the weavers, who, without fail, always wove and provided clothing, loincloths, and undergarments to the devotees of Shiva." The divine Sekkizhar Peruman, having thus spoken, concludes the Puranam of Mangaiyarkkarsi Ammaiyar and begins the Puranam of Nesa Nayanaar. Let us also praise the sacred feet of Mangaiyarkkarsi Ammaiyar and continue to contemplate the holy puranam of Nesa Nayanaar. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of Mangaiyarkkarsi Ammaiyar! Praise, praise to the sacred feet of Nindraseer Nedumara Nayanar! Praise, praise to the sacred feet of our Lord Thirugnanasambandha Murthy Nayanar! Praise, praise to the sacred feet of our Lord Sundaramurthy Swamigal! Praise, praise to the sacred feet of Nambiyandar Nambigal Peruman! Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the divine Sekkizhar Peruman! Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the commentator Sivakavimani Ayya! Thiruchitrambalam.
