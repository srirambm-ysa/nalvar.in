பொருளுல்லா என் மனத்தும் அன்றியே நாவலர் காவலர் பெருகு நதிகளிய வழிய நடந்து சேவடி போது எப்போதும் சென்னியில் மலர்ந்தனவால். சுந்தரமூர்த்தி சுவாமிகளது திருவடி சிந்தனையும் வணக்கமும் முன்னை செய்த # English

Chapter 01 — Kotpuli Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Part 992, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. The Puranam of Kotpuli Nayanar in the Kadal Soozhndha Sarukkam. The Puranam of Kotpuli Nayanar is the fifth puranam in the Kadal Soozhndha Sarukkam, which is the 10th sarukkam in the Periyapuranam composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. It is the 57th puranam in the Periyapuranam series. First, in the ninth verse of the Thiruthondathogai, a compendium composed by our Lord Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, this Nayanar is praised as "Adal Soozhndha Vel Nambi Kotpulikkum Adiyen." Its meaning is, "I am a devotee of Kotpuli, the valiant Nambi who wields a spear." Nambi Kotpuli Nayanar, who wielded a spear, was the commander-in-chief of the king, and thus he was praised. "Adal Soozhndha" refers to the strength of love and the power in fulfilling its path. Now, in the Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi, a descriptive work, Nambiandar Nambigal Peruman praises Kotpuli Nayanar in the 68th verse. The verse is: "Petram Uyarthon Viraiyaakkali Pizhaithor Thamathu Sutram Arukkum Thozhil Thirunaattiyaththan Kudi Kon Kutram Arukkum Nam Kotpuli Naval Kurisil Arul Petra Arutkadal Endru Ulaku Ettum Perunthagaiye." The meaning of this verse is that Kotpuli Nayanar is one who punished all his relatives who transgressed the command of the Lord with the bull banner, known as Thiruviraiyaakkali. He is the leader of the place called Thirunaattiyaththan Kudi. Our Kotpuli, who removes faults, is a great person revered by the world as an ocean of grace, having received the grace of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal of Thirunavalur. Now, the Periyapuranam, a detailed work composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, contains the Puranam of Kotpuli Nayanar as the 57th puranam, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman in 12 verses. The first verse is: "Nalam Perugum Chozha Naattu Naattiyaththan Kudi Velan Kulam Peruga Vandhu Udiththar Kotpuliyar Ennum Peyarar Thalam Perugum Pugazh Valar Thandhiriyarai Vetru Pulam Peruga Thuyar Vilaiththu Por Vilaiththu Pugazh Vilaippar." Kotpuliyar, bearing that sacred name, was born in the Velalar community in the place called Thirunaattiyaththan Kudi in the Chozha country, so that the community's fame would greatly flourish. As we have already seen in the Puranam of Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar, when we contemplated the sacred history of our Lord Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal visited Thirunaattiyaththan Kudi. He visited Kotpuli Nayanar's sacred mansion. We saw that Kotpuli Nayanar made his two daughters, Vanappagai and Singadi, worship the sacred feet of Sundarar Peruman, and Sundaramoorthy Swamigal graciously accepted these two girls as his daughters. The sacred history of such a great Kotpuli Nayanar is discussed in this puranam. Kotpuli Nayanar, having received the grace of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, shines as one of the 63 Nayanmars. Therefore, the puranam of Kotpuli Nayanar is discussed in detail here. Kotpuli Nayanar was the commander-in-chief of the king. He served as the commander-in-chief of the Chozha king and fought wars, causing great distress to the enemy kings of the Chozha country, thereby earning fame. The second verse is: "Mannavan Pal Perum Sirappin Valamellam Madhi Aniyum Pinjagar Tham Koyil Thorun Thiru Amudhin Padi Peruga Sennel Malaikkuvadaga Seidhu Varum Thiruppaniye Pannendunal Seidhu Ozhugum Pangu Purindhu Ongu Nal." This verse states that he dedicated all the wealth he accumulated in his profession as the commander-in-chief of the Chozha king to ensure the abundance of sacred offerings (Thiru Amudhu) in the Lord's temples. He accumulated red paddy, which is essential for the Lord's sacred offerings, like a mountain peak and continued this sacred service. Ensuring an abundance of sacred offerings for Shiva is a sacred service. Conversely, reducing the quantity of sacred offerings designated for Lord Shiva is a great sin. It also shortens the lifespan of those who commit such a sin. However, without considering these things, in these days, the quality of sacred offerings for Lord Shiva's temples is being reduced. Temple officials and administrators are also indulging in extravagances with temple funds. They spend Shiva's wealth, which was dedicated to performing Shiva Dharma and Shiva Punya, on public welfare activities like hospitals, water facilities, and roads, considering them superior. Temple officials and administrators today spend the Lord's property as they please on public welfare activities, driven by their petty arrogance of authority. We are forced to witness this cruel act. This must be the result of our past actions. Now, the third verse. "Vendhan Evalil Pagainar Vemmunaimeel Selgindrar Panthal Poon Ena Anindhar Thamakkum Amudhu Padiyaga Endhalar Tham Eythum Alavum Vendum Sennel Vaintha Koodu Avai Katti Vazhikolvar Mozhigindrar." As Kotpuli Nayanar was living this way, the king sent Kotpuli Nayanar, his commander-in-chief, to war. Kotpuli Nayanar, preparing to go to war according to the king's command, arranged for a very large quantity of red paddy for the sacred offerings in Lord Shiva's temples until he returned from the battlefield. He built large granaries and filled them with red paddy like mountains. Red paddy is considered superior for Lord Shiva's sacred offerings. Other varieties of paddy are not as special. We see this in the sacred history of Arivattaya Nayanar and in many other places. However, due to greed, we now see temple officials who sell the high-quality, superior Samba paddy meant for the temple and order the use of inferior Kuruvai paddy for the Lord's offerings. All these are also the cruelty of time and the result of our sins. Kotpuli Nayanar accumulated that excellent red paddy like a mountain, stored it safely in granaries, and arranged for the Lord's sacred offerings to continue without interruption until he returned from the war. The fourth verse. "Tham Thamargal Ayinar Thamakkellam Thaniththaniye Endhaiyarkku Amudhu Padiyirkku Ettriya Nel Ivai Azhikka Sindhaiyatral Ninaivar Thiruviraiyaakkali Endru Vandhanaiyal Uraiththu Agandrar Mannavan Matrar Munaimel." After establishing the granaries and accumulating the red paddy, he individually called all his relatives and told them. He made them promise, on the command of Lord Shiva, that they should not use these granaries of paddy, which he had collected for his Lord's sacred offerings, for their own needs. Using an item consecrated for Lord Shiva's sacred offerings for anything else is equivalent to destroying that item. Therefore, he received this as the Lord's command, so that this error should not occur not only in action but also in thought. This is called Thiruviraiyaakkali. Thiruviraiyaakkali is the command of Shiva. Did we not see in the sacred history of Thiruneelakanda Nayanar, "Theenduveerayin Emmai Thiruneelakandam"? Similarly, this is an oath, a vow taken in the name of Lord Shiva, upon Lord Shiva. Therefore, he individually called all his relatives and made them swear that this was Lord Shiva's command. Then Kotpuli Nayanar departed for the battlefield to confront the enemies. The fifth verse. "Matravar Tham Poyina Pin Sila Naalil Varkalam Uttalum Achchuttatthar Unavinri Irappadhanil Petram Uyarthavar Amudhu Padi Kondagilum Pizhaiththu Kutramara Pin Koduppom Ena Koodu Kulaiththu Aliththar." A few days after Kotpuli Nayanar went to the battlefield, a period of famine arrived. A time of scarcity came. Therefore, Kotpuli Nayanar's relatives suffered greatly without food. In that situation, they had already sworn to Kotpuli Nayanar in the name of Lord Shiva. They had taken an oath. They had sworn that they would not use the paddy set aside for Lord Shiva's sacred offerings for any other purpose. Now, with famine upon them, they thought, instead of dying without food, let us survive by taking the paddy meant for the Lord's sacred offerings, and we will return it later without fault. They decided that if they did not have this paddy set aside for the Lord, they would die without food. Instead of dying, they could eat the paddy meant for the offerings and survive. Although this was not a permissible act, they thought that in a situation where life would be lost, they could save their lives by committing a fault. They made this decision, considering this worldly principle. They knew that this was a great sin. Even though they knew, they dared to commit this sin. They did not intend to take this paddy for themselves permanently. But now, they thought they could take this paddy, save their lives, and return it later. We see that even this thought is a crime according to today's worldly laws. Therefore, they used up all the red paddy that had been set aside for the Lord's sacred offerings, thinking that they could use it now and return it after the famine subsided. The sixth verse. "Mannavan Than Them Munaiyil Vinai Vayththu Matru Avan Pal Nannidhin Kuvai Petra Naattiyaththan Kudi Thalaivar Annaalil Thamar Seidha Pizhai Arindhu Ariyamai Thunninar Sutramellam Thunippan Enum Thunivinaray." While this was happening, Kotpuli Nayanar defeated the enemies on the battlefield. He returned victorious to the king. The king bestowed upon him great wealth and treasures. Having received these, Kotpuli Nayanar, the leader of Naattiyaththan Kudi, returned to his Thirunaattiyaththan Kudi. In that town, he learned of the fault committed by all his relatives. He decided to punish all his relatives. His relatives did not know that Kotpuli Nayanar was aware of this. The seventh verse. "Edhirkonda Thamarkku Ellam Iniya Mozhi Pala Mozhindhu Madhi Thangu Sudar Mani Maligaiyin Kan Vandhu Anaindhu Pathigonda Sutraththarkku Ellam Paindhugil Nidhiyam Adhigam Thandhu Alippadhanukku Azhaimingal Endru Uraiththu." Kotpuli Nayanar's relatives all welcomed him. Kotpuli Nayanar spoke sweet words to all those relatives. After arriving at his sacred mansion, Kotpuli Nayanar sent word to his relatives. He called them all to his sacred mansion, saying, "Tell them that I am going to give them good silk clothes and much wealth." The eighth verse. "Ellorum Pugundha Adhanpin Irunidhiyam Alippar Pol Nallartham Peron Mun Kadai Kakka Nathan Than Val Aanai Maruththu Amudhu Padi Azhaiththa Marakkilaiyai Kolladhe Viduvano Ena Kanandru Kolai Purivar." After all the relatives had entered, Kotpuli Nayanar, acting as if he would bestow great wealth upon them, decided to punish his relatives. He placed a guard, who was in his name, at the entrance of his sacred mansion and entered the mansion. Kotpuli Nayanar, enraged, thought, "Shall I let go of these forgetful relatives who defied the Lord's strong command and consumed the paddy meant for the Lord's sacred offerings?" and proceeded to kill them. If Kotpuli Nayanar acted this way, pretending to give great wealth to all his relatives, does it mean that one can have one thought in mind and act differently in speech and body? This was necessary to make these relatives experience the consequences of their actions, to remove their faults, and to help them attain a good state. We can compare this to the Lord's Tirodhana Shakti (concealing power) at work, making beings experience the fruits of their karma. Since the Nayanar acts like the Lord's Tirodhana Shakti here, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman praises him as "Nallar" (good person) in this verse. The meaning is that the Nayanar stood to remove the evil deeds of his relatives and do good for them. Therefore, the Nayanar punished those who committed two great sins: defying the command and consuming the paddy consecrated for Shiva's offerings. The ninth verse. "Thandhaiyar Thayyar Matru Udanpirandhar Tharangaal Panthammar Sutraththar Pathi Adiyar Madhi Aniyum Thandhaiyar Thiruppadi Matru Unna Isaindhar. Kalaiyum Sirandha Val Kodu Thunindhar Theeya Vinai Pavam Thunippar." The Nayanar gathered everyone there: father, mother, siblings, wives, closely related relatives, and the servants of that village. The Nayanar punished all those relatives, including his father and mother, who had agreed to consume the paddy meant for the sacred offerings of our Father, the Lord, by severing their bodies with a sharp sword, so that the fruits of their evil deeds would be cut off. Therefore, although the Nayanar severed the body, the evil deeds accumulated by that body would attach to the soul. The sin arising from that karma, that sin would not attach to them; he severed and cut it off. This is its meaning. Therefore, by cutting off the fruits of that karma, their bonds were severed, and they attained purity. The tenth verse. "Pin Angu Pizhaiththa Oru Pillaiyai Tham Peyaron Av Vannam Thuyththal Idhu Kudikku Oru Pudhalvan Arulum Ena In Nel Undadhu Ena Eduththu Erindhu Minnal Vadi Vaalal Iru Thuniyay Vilai Ettar." After this, the eleventh verse. "Annilaiye Sivaperuman Anbar Edhir Veliyee Nindru Unnudaiya Kaivalal Oru Pasam Arutha Kilai Pon Ulakin Mel Ulagam Pukku Anaiya Pugazhoy Nee Innila Namudan Anaika Endre Ezhundharulinar." All the relatives are punished by the Nayanar. There was only one child there. The guard said that this child did not eat that rice. Moreover, this child is the only son of a family. Therefore, he requested that the child be spared punishment. Even though this child committed no fault, the mother of this child committed the sin of eating the paddy meant for Lord Shiva, turning it into rice. The child drank the milk of that mother. At the moment when the child was about to be punished, Lord Shiva appeared and bestowed His grace. At that very moment, Lord Shiva, the beloved of Kotpuli Nayanar, appeared before him. Lord Shiva blessed Kotpuli Nayanar. "Because your relatives have been punished by the sword in your hand, they have all severed the bonds that bound them and have become pure. Therefore, all your relatives will enter the worlds superior to the world of the gods, experience pleasures, and then, in due course, reach our Shiva Loka. You, in this state, shall come with us now," He commanded and departed. Lord Shiva took Kotpuli Nayanar with Him to Shiva Loka. The worlds of the gods and the higher worlds, the worlds of enjoyment, are what Lord Shiva bestowed upon the relatives. Thus, the relatives will first enter those worlds of enjoyment. This is because their past karmas must be severed. They must be resolved. Therefore, they will first go to the worlds of enjoyment, including Devaloka, experience all their karmas, and then enter Shiva Loka. Thus, after their karmic experiences are exhausted, they attained liberation. Kotpuli Nayanar was free from worldly attachments, material attachments, and attachments to life. He dedicated all his possessions to Lord Shiva's sacred offerings. Therefore, he was free from material attachments. He punished all his relatives. Therefore, he was free from bodily attachments. He was also free from attachments to life. He was attached only to Shiva. Therefore, he immediately attained the sacred feet of Lord Shiva. He was immediately taken to the state of liberation by Lord Shiva. Now, the twelfth and concluding verse. "Aththana Annai Ay Aaru Uyir Ay Amirthagi Muththana Mudhalvan Thal Adaindhu Kilai Mudhal Thadindha Koththu Alarthar Kotpuliyar Adi Vanangi Kootaththil Patthar Ay Panivar Tham Parisinai Yam Pagaruvaam Endru." This verse means that having attained the sacred feet of the Lord, who is the father, mother, six lives, and nectar, and who is the ancient one, Kotpuli Nayanar, who severed the roots of his lineage, we will now speak of the nature of the devotees who worship among the assembly of devotees. Thus, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman concludes the Puranam of Kotpuli Nayanar at this point and states that in the next sarukkam, he will speak of the "Bhakthar Ay Panivar Sarukkam." We too will conclude the sacred history of Kotpuli Nayanar at this point. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of our Lord Kotpuli Nayanar. Thus, the puranams of the five Nayanmars in the Kadal Soozhndha Sarukkam come to an end. It is the tradition of the divine Sekkizhar Peruman to include a praise to Sundaramoorthy Nayanar at the end of each sarukkam. We have contemplated the puranams according to the praises of the Nayanmars mentioned in the ninth verse of the Thiruthondathogai. Now, "Aruran Aruril Ammanukku Ale" is the concluding line in the ninth verse of the Thiruthondathogai. Its meaning is, "I, Aruran, the one from Arur, who is a devotee of the Nayanmars already praised and the Nayanmars praised in the Kadal Soozhndha Sarukkam, am also a devotee of the Lord residing in Thiruvarur." In accordance with this line, Nambiandar Nambigal Peruman has composed the 69th verse in the Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi, a praise to Sundaramoorthy Nayanar. That verse is as follows: "Thagumagal Pesinon Veeyave Nool Pona Sangilippal Pugumanak Kadhalinal Ottriyoor Urai Punniyan Than Migu Malarppadham Panindharula Ivviyan Ulagam Nagum Vazhakke Nanmaiya Punarndhan Navalur Arase." Its meaning is that Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, the king of Navalur, out of love for Sangili Ammaiyar, who followed the path of Shiva's service, worshipped the abundant lotus feet of the Lord of Thiruottriyur, and by the Lord's grace, married Sangili Ammaiyar, thereby bringing goodness to this great world, as is the custom. Now, at the end of this sarukkam, the praise to Sundaramoorthy Nayanar composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman is the 4146th verse in the Periyapuranam. "Mevariya Perunthavam Yan Munbu Vilaiviththana Onno Yavathum Or Porululla En Manaththum Andriye Navalar Kavalar Perugu Nadhiyaliye Vazhiya Nadandhu Sevadi Podhu Eppodhum Senniyil Malarndhanaval." The contemplation and worship of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's sacred feet are not possible without great penance performed in the past. I have not performed any penance in this birth to the extent of wearing Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's sacred feet. But this is not possible without penance. Therefore, one must have performed penance before. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's sacred feet, those lotus feet that walked on the path opened by the overflowing Kaveri river, not only blossomed on the path opened by the Kaveri but also blossomed on my head. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's sacred feet are like lotus feet. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's sacred feet blossomed in my mind, who am nothing. Did I perform great penance before to receive this blessing? Is this its result? Thus, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman praises Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's sacred feet with wonder. We too will praise Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's sacred feet. We conclude this sarukkam called Kadal Soozhndha Sarukkam and will continue to contemplate the 11th sarukkam, Bhakthar Ay Panivar Sarukkam. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of our Lord Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of Nambiandar Nambigal Peruman. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the commentator, Ayya. Thiruchitrambalam.
