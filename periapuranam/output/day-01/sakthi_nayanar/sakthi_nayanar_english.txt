Chapter 01 — Sakthi Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, authored by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Part 977. The Puranam of Sakthi Nayanar from the Poiyadimai Illatha Pulavar Sarukkam. In the divine Periyapuranam authored by Sekkizhar Peruman, following the Puranam of Kaliya Nayanar, the 46th Puranam is that of Sakthi Nayanar. First, the section praising Sakthi Nayanar in the Thiruthondathogai Thiruppathigam, authored by our Lord Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. The line "Kaithadinda varisilaiyaan Kalikkamban Kaliyan Kazhar Sakthi Varinjaiyarkon adiyaarkkum adiyen" (I am a servant to the servants of Kalikkamban, Kaliyan, Kazhar Sakthi, the lord of Varinjaiyur, who wielded a bow that cut hands) concludes with "Kazhar Sakthi Varinjaiyarkon adiyaarkkum adiyen," which is the part that praises Sakthi Nayanar. "Kazhal" signifies a mark of victory. Therefore, "Kazhal Sakthi" means Sakthi Nayanar, who is victorious. Such a Nayanar, bearing the sacred name Sakthi Nayanar, is also the chief of the sacred town of Varinjaiyur. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal praises, "I shall be a servant to those who are servants of such a Nayanar." Now, the 55th Thiruppadal in the Thiruthondar Thiruvandathi, a concise work authored by Nambiandar Nambigal Peruman, praises Sakthi Nayanar. The Thiruppadal is: "Girivallavar tham adiyarai tham munbu keezhmai sonna thiruvillavarai anna arivon thirundharai vellum varivillavan vayal sengazhuneerin maruvu thendral theruvil virai kamazhum then Varinjai thigazh Sakthiye." Sakthi Nayanar would cut the tongues of those who spoke ill of Shiva's devotees in his presence, purifying them with a knife. Sakthi Nayanar performed this sacred service. Nambiandar Nambigal sings about this in this Thiruppadal. The meaning of this Thiruppadal is that Sakthi, who shines in Southern Varinjai, is the one who knows how to cut the tongues of those wretched individuals who speak ill of the devotees of Shiva, who holds the mountain as a bow, in his presence. Southern Varinjai is a town where the gentle breeze, having passed over the red water lilies in the fields, spreads its fragrance through the streets. This sacred place is praised in this Thiruppadal. Now, in the detailed work, the divine Periyapuranam authored by Sekkizhar Peruman, the Puranam of Sakthi Nayanar is the seventh Puranam within the eighth Sarukkam, the Poiyadimai Illatha Pulavar Sarukkam, according to the sequence. It stands as the 46th Puranam in the structure of the Periyapuranam. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman has composed the Puranam of Sakthi Nayanar in seven Thiruppadal. Now, the first Thiruppadal. "Kalamarkatta kamalam pozhintha then kulaniraipathu kolondri en thisaiyalavum aanai sayaththambam naattiya valavar Kaveri naattu Varinjaiyur." The Chola country was one where the Cholas exercised sole sovereignty with their unswerving scepter. The Cholas were those who extended their rule and authority in all eight directions. Varinjaiyur is a town in such a Chola country, watered by the Kaveri. This sacred place is the town of Keezhvelur. It is located between Thiruvarur and Nagapattinam. Thirukeezhvelur is a short distance south of Keezhvelur. Thiruvaryinjaiyur is a short distance southeast of it. In the Shiva temple here, an idol of Sakthi Nayanar is installed and worshipped. This Varinjaiyur is rich in water resources. The Chola country is rich in water resources. Therefore, Varinjaiyur, located within it, is also rich in water resources. Here, the farmers uproot the lotus flowers that have grown abundantly as weeds among the paddy crops in the fields. The honey dripping from these uprooted lotus flowers fills a pond. This natural description also implicitly conveys the inner meaning of this Nayanar's sacred Puranam. There are lotus flowers among the paddy crops in the fields. However, they are considered weeds. Therefore, the farmers uproot these lotus flowers as weeds. They uproot them and throw them into the nearby ponds and tanks scattered among the fields. The honey dripping from these thrown lotus flowers fills that pond and tank. Just as even the uprooted lotuses contribute to filling the pond with honey, the tongues cut by Sakthi Nayanar became instruments for the benefit of correcting this world. This inner meaning of the Puranam, embedded in this description, reveals the divine poetic excellence of Sekkizhar Peruman. Now, the second Thiruppadal. "Varinjaiyurinil vaaimai velan kulam perunjirappu pera pirappu eithinaar virinjan maan muthal vinnavar ennavum arunjilambani sevadiikku aatseyvaar." In Varinjaiyur, a Velala clan known for its truthfulness existed. One of the many special characteristics of the Velala clan is its excellent truthfulness. This is what makes it superior to all and glorifies the Lord. Thus, the Velala clan, renowned for its truthfulness, was inherently special. Moreover, it became even more distinguished because our Nayanar was born into it. "Vaaimai" means the nature of the mouth. Truthfulness is the quality proper to the mouth. That is, praising Shiva and Shiva's devotees. That is the nature of the mouth. "Vaaye vaazhthu kandai" (The mouth is for praise, indeed) is a sacred saying from Thevaram. "Vaazhtha vaayum ninaikka madanenjum thaazhtha senniyum" (A mouth to praise, a foolish heart to contemplate, a head to bow) is also a sacred saying from Thevaram. These illustrate the nature of the mouth to us. Therefore, this Nayanar possessed such a quality. Furthermore, this Nayanar possessed the quality of enabling others to attain the same. "Vaaimai" is the nature of the mouth. "Unmai" (truth) is the nature of the heart. "Meimai" (veracity) is the nature of the body, they say in grammar. Thus, mind, speech, and body – these three are meant to be directed towards Shiva. Such a one, who was born into the Velala clan to bring it glory, served the sacred feet of Shiva, which are difficult for even Brahma and other celestials to contemplate. He is Sakthi Nayanar. The third Thiruppadal. "Aththarakiya angar anbarai iththalathil igazhnthu iyambum urai vaiththa naavai valiththu arisakthiyaal Sakthiyaar enum thirunaamam thaanginaar." He was powerful enough to cut the tongue that spoke ill of Shiva's devotees in this world. Therefore, he bore the sacred name Sakthiyar. Slandering Shiva is a grave sin against Shiva. Slandering Shiva's devotees is even more heinous. Therefore, this Nayanar considered it his sacred duty to cut the tongue that spoke ill of devotees. Insulting a devotee is equivalent to insulting Shiva. Therefore, punishing the offending limb is one of the traditional punishments. We see this in the Puranams of Kalikkamba Nayanar, Kazharsinga Nayanar, and Seruthunai Nayanar. To cut someone's tongue, one must be stronger than them and possess the ability to subdue the opponent through strength. Therefore, it is explained here that he bore the sacred name Sakthiyar, meaning one who possessed that power, that strength, and that the name Sakthiyar came about for that reason. The fourth Thiruppadal. "Theengu sotra thiruvilar naavinai vaanga vaangum thandayaaththinaal valiththu thaangu ayal kaththiyaal arinthu anbudan oongu seer thiruththondin uyarnthanar." Those who spoke ill of Shiva's devotees were devoid of "Thiru." "Thiru" refers to the Saiva true wealth, which is the path to salvation. Those who do not cherish the glory of Shiva and the glory of His devotees are devoid of "Thiru." To pull the tongue of such people, there is an instrument called a "thandayam," which resembles tongs. He performs this sacred service with great love, using such an instrument to pull the tongue and a sharp knife to cut it. Is the act of cutting a tongue an act of love? Yes, it is done out of love. If someone speaks ill of Shiva and His devotees, or slanders them, they will enter the fiery hell and suffer greatly. However, by immediately punishing them in this very birth by cutting their tongue for the evil words they uttered, their going to hell is prevented. They are saved from going to hell. Punishing them immediately in this very birth, instead of letting them suffer terrible agony in hell, is an act of love towards them. This is also the basis for royal punishment and justice. If a citizen commits a crime in a country, and the king imposes a fitting punishment, whether imprisonment or capital punishment, then by experiencing that punishment for the crime in this very birth, they are prevented from suffering in hell for the same crime. This is the system of royal justice in our country. If, by chance, they escape royal punishment for the crime they committed in this birth, they will surely go to hell and suffer torment many times worse than this. The purpose of this punishment is to prevent them from suffering that and to set them on the right path in this very birth. Parimelazhagar states in his introduction to the Thirukkural that "Aram" (righteousness) has three divisions: discipline, custom, and punishment. Therefore, punishment is also out of compassion for them. So here too, by insulting Shiva's devotees, they were punished here by this Nayanar, preventing them from entering hell. Seeing this, the world was saved. The fact that this world stood on the right path, seeing the act performed by this Nayanar that one should not insult Shiva's devotees and Shiva, reveals the love this Nayanar had for the people of this world. Furthermore, to be unable to tolerate hearing Shiva being slandered, or Shiva's devotees being slandered, and to act in this manner, shows extreme devotion to Shiva and Shiva's devotees. For example, even in worldly terms, we have great love for our wives, children, and relatives, who are all related by the body. Their connection lasts only as long as this body exists. We have great love for these temporary karmic relatives – wives, children, and other kin. In such a situation, if someone insults them, we cannot bear to hear it and cause great commotion and fight. This is a truth we observe in the worldly realm. If this is the case for bodily relationships, then our relationship with the Lord and His devotees is a soul relationship. If Shiva, who is a soul relationship, and Shiva's devotees are insulted, then to be unable to tolerate hearing it and to perform the necessary actions is an act performed by those who have love for them, for Shiva and Shiva's devotees. Therefore, since all these are done out of love for Shiva, it becomes a sacred service, a sacred work. The Sivagamas state that if one hears Shiva being slandered, one should punish the slanderer. If one is unable to punish them, then one should cover one's ears with "Shiva Shiva" and leave that place. In the Kandapuranam, in the chapter of Tapam Kaanpadalam, while Parvati Ammaiyar was performing penance, the Lord Himself came as an old man and slandered Shiva. Hearing those words, Parvati Ammaiyar immediately left that place, chanting the name of Shiva, "Shiva Shiva." Ammaiyar told the old man to leave. Even after hearing this, the old man remained there, continuing to slander Shiva. Therefore, since this old man refused to leave, Ammaiyar immediately left that place, her soft, cotton-like feet hurting, showing us this discipline here. Ammaiyar thus advises us. Now, the fifth and sixth Thiruppadal. "Annathaakiya aanmaith thiruppani mannu perulagaththil valiyudan pannnedum perunaal parivaal seythu senni aatrinan sennneri aatrinan. Aiyam indri ariya thiruppani meyyinaal seytha veeraththiruththondar vaiyam uyya manimantrul aaduvyaar seyya paathaththirunizhal sernthanar." This Nayanar, steadfast in such a courageous sacred service, performed this sacred service with strength and love for many long years in this great world. He performed the sacred service of the Lord's righteous path. He did this out of love, not out of malice or other evil qualities, but purely out of love. Therefore, all our Nayanmars did not perform these actions under the influence of the qualities of Maya, namely sattva, rajas, and tamas. We see that they acted out of a state of love for Shiva, transcending these qualities. Therefore, there are Nayanmars who attained salvation by following the rules and having devotion to Shiva. And there are Nayanmars, like Sakthi Nayanar and Kalikkamba Nayanar, who performed sacred service by transcending rules and following the path of love, as we see in this Periyapuranam. When measured by worldly dharma, adharma, and ethical principles, the actions of the Nayanmars might appear to be inappropriate. The moral codes and dharmas spoken of in the worldly realm are all means to attain liberation. However, our great Nayanmars transcended these means and attained Shiva, who is the ultimate goal. To measure such individuals by worldly dharma and adharma would not be entirely appropriate. All Nayanmars were united with Shiva in knowledge, in an advaitic sense. Therefore, all their actions are considered to be Shiva's actions. They had no separate actions of their own. They did not perform these actions with self-awareness or individual consciousness. By losing individual consciousness and ego and acting from Shiva-consciousness, all these are indeed Shiva's actions. The actions of Nayanmars like Sakthi Nayanar and Kalikkamba Nayanar are such that their bodies become bodies of grace because they are Shiva in knowledge. Therefore, Shiva Himself states that all their actions are His own actions. The actions of Shiva, who possesses supreme knowledge, omniscience, and omnipresence, performed by Shiva's devotees who are united with Him, cannot be measured by the limited knowledge and actions of the individual soul. Therefore, if the question is whether all the actions performed by these Nayanmars were right, then if Shiva Himself took them into His grace and granted them liberation, we infer the cause from this effect. If the sacred deeds of the Nayanmars lead to liberation, and if the effect is liberation, auspiciousness, and divine bliss, then their sacred service has been approved and blessed by Shiva Himself. Therefore, it is understood here that there could not have been even a slight blemish in any of the actions performed by our Nayanmars. If, as we might imagine, there had been a blemish, would the Lord Himself have appeared, granted them a divine vision, and brought them to liberation? Through this inferential proof, we have some room to understand the sacred deeds of our Nayanmars, in the way shown by the elders. Therefore, Sakthi Nayanar, without the slightest doubt as to whether cutting people's tongues was dharma or adharma, performs this sacred service to Shiva with determination, because this action removes the sin against Shiva. Therefore, according to the Thirukkural saying "Seyarkariya seyvar periyar" (Great people do what is difficult to do), we conclude that this is a difficult and extraordinary service that others cannot perform. Therefore, Sakthi Nayanar, the valiant devotee who performed this extremely rare sacred service with his body, without the slightest doubt, joined the sacred shade of the beautiful, righteous feet of Shiva, who dances in the golden hall, for the salvation of the world. The seventh and concluding Thiruppadal. "Nayanar thondarai nalam kooralar saaya naavari Sakthiyaar thaazh paninthu aaya maathavaththu aiyadigal enum thooya kaadavar tham thiram solluvaam." Having bowed at the sacred feet of Sakthi Nayanar, who performed such rare and great sacred service to Shiva and saved this world, and with its support, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman concludes the sacred Puranam of Sakthi Nayanar and is now about to begin the sacred Puranam of Aiyadigal Kadavarkon, who possessed the great penance of the path of Shiva. We too, bowing at the sacred feet of Sakthi Nayanar, shall now proceed to contemplate the Puranam of Aiyadigal Kadavarkon Nayanar. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of our Lord Sakthi Nayanar. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of our Lord Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of Nambiandar Nambigal Peruman. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the commentator, Aiya. Thiruchitrambalam.
