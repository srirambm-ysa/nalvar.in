Chapter 01 — Kaliya Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 976, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. The Puranam of Kaliya Nayanar, from the Poiyadimai Illatha Pulavar Sarukkam. Following the Thiruppuranam of Kazhikkamba Nayanar in the Periya Puranam composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, the Puranam of Kaliya Nayanar is placed. In the Poiyadimai Illatha Pulavar Sarukkam, which is the eighth Sarukkam in order, the Puranam of Kaliya Nayanar is the sixth Puranam. The Puranam of Kaliya Nayanar, the 45th Puranam, has been graciously composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman in 17 Thiruppaadals. Firstly, in the Thogai Nool composed by our Lord Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, in the line "Kaidathinda Varichilaiyaan Kazhikkamban Kaliyan Kazharchchaththi Varinjaiyarkon Adiyaarkkum Adiyen," the sacred name "Kaliyan" appears. We should interpret this as "Kaliyan Adiyaarkkum Adiyen." The sacred name of this Nayanar is Kaliyan. He is praised as Kali Neethiyar in Periya Puranam. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's Thogai Nool states, "I became a servant to the devotees of the Nayanar named Kaliyar." The 54th song in the Thiruththondar Thiruvandhadhi, a Vakai Nool composed by Nambiyandar Nambigal Peruman, praises Kaliya Nayanar. "Kambakkariykum Silandhikkum Nalkiya Kannudhalon Umbarkku Naatharkku Oli Vilakkaetra Kudalanilaanaai Kumbaththayilam Vittrum Sekku Uzhandrum Koll Kooliyinaal Nambarkku Eriththa Kali Ottriyumanaagar Sakkiriye" is the Thiruththondathogai Thiruppaadal. This means that for Lord Shiva, who has a third eye and bestowed grace equally upon the elephant and the spider despite their enmity, Kaliyar, a Nayanar from the Sekkara community living in Thiruvottriyur, lit lamps. He did this by selling oil from pots and working the oil press, using the wages he earned, as he lacked the necessary materials to light the lamps. Sakkiri is the traditional name for the community of oil merchants (Sekkara Vaaniba Samugam). Those who operate oil presses, extract oil, and sell it are known as Sakkiri or Sekkara Kulaththavargal. Thus, the Vakai Nool is structured. Its detailed account is given in 17 Thiruppaadals in the Kaliya Nayanar Puranam composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Now, the first Thiruppaadal of the detailed Nool, Periya Puranam, describes: "Perulagil Ongu Pugazh Perum Thondai Nannadu Neerulavum Sadaikkattrai Niruththar Thiruppathiyaagum Karulavum Malarcholai Kanni Madhil Pudaichuzhndhu Therulavu Nedhuveedhi Sirandha Thiruvottriyur." In this Thiruppaadal, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman sings about the country and city where the Nayanar was born, and the city's special features. Thondai Nannadu is a country renowned for its fame in this great world. Thiruvottriyur is the sacred place where Lord Shiva resides. Thiruvottriyur is a city surrounded by flower gardens where clouds roam, and by Kanni Madhil (virgin walls). Kanni Madhil refers to walls that have stood as they were built from the beginning, never destroyed by enemies. The city of Thiruvottriyur is also distinguished by its long streets suitable for chariot processions. Since the glories of Thondai Nannadu have been extensively described by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman in the Thirukurippuththonda Nayanar Puranam, he presents them very briefly here. Now, the second Thiruppaadal: "Peedugalum Perundheruvum Buththarudan Peeli Aman Vedam Udaiyavar Porul Pol Aakaasa Veli Maraikkum Aadu Kodi Mani Nedhu Maaligai Niraigal Alai Kamugin Kadanaiya Kadal Padappai Ena Vilangum Kavin Kaattum." The streets of Thiruvottriyur are adorned with canopies, archways, and banners, covering the sky to such an extent that it is not visible. This is likened to the Buddhists and Jains, who do not acknowledge the sky as one of the five elements, believing there are only four. In a similar subtle and metaphorical way, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman describes the glory of Thiruvottriyur, where the sky is hidden. This is a coastal city, and next to it is a fertile land (padappai) filled with small crops. The third Thiruppaadal: "Pan Thiruppathiga Isaippaattu Ova Mandapangal Annanadai Madavaargal Aattu Ova Ani Arangu Panmurai Thooriyam Muzhangu Vizhavu Ova Payil Veethi Sennel Adisil Pirangal Unavu Ova Thirumadanggal." Within the city of Thiruvottriyur, there are many mandapams (halls). In these mandapams, Thiruppathigams are recited, and their musical chants constantly resonate. These mandapams, both inside and outside the temple, were built for the Lord's festivals and other purposes. However, many such mandapams in various towns today lie in ruins or have been converted into commercial spaces. In reality, these mandapams should be used for religious practices like studying Thevaram, Vedas, and Puranas. If used for such purposes, they would serve as aids and eliminate the need to search for other places for Vedic studies, Puranic readings, and similar sacred services. We learn from inscriptions that Puranas were recited in the temple mandapams of Thiruvottriyur. Such mandapams abound. Furthermore, there are many dance halls where women with swan-like gaits perform dances ceaselessly. The streets where people come and go are constantly filled with festivals, and thus auspicious musical instruments continuously resound. In all the Thirumadanggal (monasteries), there is an unending abundance of food, piled like mountains, cooked from red rice. The fourth Thiruppaadal: "Kezhumalar Maadhavi Punnnai Kilai Gnaazhal Thazhaiyavizhum Kuzhumugaiya Sanbagangal Kulir Serundhi Valar Kaidhai Muzhumaname Munneerum Kamazha Malar Muruga Uyirkkum Sezhunilavin Thugalanaia Manal Parappum Thirupparappu." The fragrant flowers of the Madhavi (Kurukkaththi), Punnnai trees, branching Kumkum trees, Sanbaga trees, cool Serundhi trees, and growing Thaazhai (screwpine) trees all emit a sweet fragrance. Even the sea water in Thiruvottriyur is fragrant. The sandy beach of Thiruvottriyur shines as if it were powdered moonlight. The fifth Thiruppaadal: "Eyil Anaaiyum Mugil Muzhakkum Erithirai Velaiyin Muzhakkum Payildharu Palliya Muzhakkum Murai Theriyatha Pathi Adhanul Veyil Ani Palmani Mudhalaam Vizhupporul Aavana Vilakkum Thaiyal Vinaiththozhil Marabil Sakkarappaadi Theruvum." The sounds of clouds reaching the ramparts, the roaring waves of the sea, and the various musical instruments all blend together in that sacred place, making it impossible to distinguish them. In Thiruvottriyur, there is a street called Sakkarappaadi Theru. This street is where people of the oil-pressing trade live. Many pure goods shine there, indicating the wealth of its residents. Large lamps kept by oil merchants illuminate all the goods on this street. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman refers to this trade as "Thaiyal Vinaiththozhil" (the craft of tailoring), which is the traditional occupation of the Sekkar community, who extract and sell oil by operating oil presses. Sakkarappaadi means those who live by the trade of the 'chakra' (wheel) machine. The sixth Thiruppaadal: "Akkulaththin Sey Thavaththaal Avanimisai Avathariththaar Mikka Perum Selvathtu Meekkoora Vilanginaar Thakka Pugazh Kaliyanaar Enum Naamam Thalai Nindraar Mukkan Iraivarkku Urimai Thiruththondin Neri Muyalvaar."

He shone with great wealth. He steadfastly followed the path of sacred service, which is dear to Lord Shiva, who has three eyes. His sacred name is Kaliyanar. Kaliyar means one who excels in Kali (strength, valor). Kaliyar, who excels in such Kali, shines with appropriate fame. He excelled in the sacred service that was his birthright through his lineage. His lineage's profession was selling oil. Accordingly, he dedicated himself to the sacred task of lighting lamps in temples by supplying oil, ensuring they were always lit. We see this in the seventh Thiruppaadal: "Ellaiyil Palkodi Thanaththai Iraivaraai Ippadiththaan Selva Nerippayan Arindhu Thiruvottriyur Amarndha Kollai Mazhai Vidaiyaar Tham Koyilin Ullum Purambum Allum Nedhu Pagalum Idum Thiruvilakkin Aniviththaar." Kaliya Nayanar was the lord of immeasurable crores of wealth. He understood the benefit of such wealth, realizing that this great wealth came to him because he had worshipped Lord Shiva in the past. He understood that the benefit of this wealth was to worship Lord Shiva and His devotees. Therefore, he used this perishable wealth to perform imperishable sacred service to Shiva. He performed the sacred task of lighting lamps inside and outside the temple of the Lord, who graciously resides in Thiruvottriyur, both day and night. We see lamps lit inside temples in Tamil Nadu. In the mountainous regions, we still see lamps lit both inside and outside temples. This song indicates that this custom also existed in Tamil Nadu in those days. Through this Thiruppaadal, we learn that Kaliya Nayanar lit lamps both inside and outside the temple. The eighth Thiruppaadal: "Ennil Thiruvilakku Nedunaal Ellaam Eriththu Vara Punniya Meiththondar Seyal Pulappaduppaar Arulaale Ullniraiyum Perum Selvam Uyarththum Vinai Seyal Ovi Mannil Avar Iruvinai Pol Maandathu Maatchimaiththaaga." The Nayanar lit countless lamps for a long time, day and night. The Lord willed to make his sacred service known to the world. The Lord's grace ensured that his wealth did not increase further. There is a proverb: "Nindru Thindraal Kundrum Maalum" (If you eat standing, even a mountain will perish). Another proverb is "Kundhi Thindraal Kundrum Kuraiyum" (If you eat sitting, even a mountain will diminish). Therefore, no matter how great the wealth, there must be actions to increase it. If there are no actions to further increase wealth, and one only spends what they have without increasing it, that wealth will surely diminish and disappear one day. The Lord put an end to the actions that increased Kaliya Nayanar's wealth. He stopped the flow of further income. As a result, Kaliya Nayanar's wealth diminished and perished. How did this happen? Just as one's two karmas (iru vinai) are destroyed by Shiva's merit, so too was his wealth destroyed. When the wealth of those who do not follow the path of Shiva perishes, it leads them to a sinful path that causes suffering. However, due to the merit of Shiva's grace, even when wealth diminishes for those who have performed Shiva's meritorious deeds, it brings glory. The ninth Thiruppaadal: "Thirumali Selvathtullani Theyndhu Azhindha Pinaiyum Tham Perumai Nilai Thiruppaniyil Peraadha Peraalar Varu Marabil Ullorpaal Ennai Maari Konarndhu Tharum Iyalbil Kooliyinaal Thamathu Thiruppani Seyvaar." Even after his abundant wealth diminished and perished, Kaliya Nayanar, a great soul, continued his glorious and steadfast sacred service of lighting lamps. He would obtain oil from the wealthy members of his own lineage, sell it, and use the wages earned to continue his sacred service of lighting lamps. The tenth Thiruppaadal: "Valamudaiyaar Paal Ennai Kodhu Poy Maari Kooli Kolla Muyalum Seygaiyum Matru Avar Kodamaiyin Maaraththalaru Manam Udaiyavar Thaam Sakkara Yanthiram Puriyum Kalanil Varum Pani Seydhu Perum Kooli Kaathaliththaar." Even during these days, the wealthy stopped giving him oil. As a result, that trade also ceased. Fearing that his sacred service might be interrupted, the Nayanar, disheartened, desired to work at the oil press, operating the machine, and earn a large wage from it. The eleventh Thiruppaadal: "Sekku Nirai El Aatti Padham Arindhu Thila Thailam Pakkam Ezhiga Migavuzhandhum Paandil Varum Erudhu Uyndhum Thakka Thozhil Perum Kooli Thaam Kondu Thaazhaamai Mikka Thiruvilakku Ittaar Vizhutthondhu Vilakkittaar." The Nayanar went as a wage laborer for the oil pressing trade. He had to know the right consistency for oil to be extracted from sesame seeds and press the oil from the sides of the oil press. This required immense physical labor. He would fill the press with sesame seeds, operate it, and knowing the proper consistency, extract the oil as it emerged, working with great difficulty. Additionally, he had to drive the oxen that circled the oil press. The oxen would pull the press in a circle, causing the oil from the sesame seeds to separate. Enduring such physical hardship and performing the work diligently, he earned a good wage and continued his sacred service of lighting lamps. Lighting lamps is considered conducive to attaining the highest knowledge. "Vilakkittaar Peru Sollin Meigneri Gnaanamagum" (The one who lights the lamp, if spoken of, becomes the path of true knowledge) is a sacred saying from Thiruneri Isai. The twelfth Thiruppaadal: "Appaniyaal Varum Peru Avvilainjar Palar Ularaai Epparisum Kidaiyaadha Vagai Muttra Idar Uzhandhe Oppil Manai Vittru Erikkum Oru Porulum Maandhadhan Pin Sepparum Seer Manaiyaalai Virpatharku Theduvaar." Even though he undertook the oil-pressing work, many people were engaged in that trade, which led to a reduction in wages. He faced difficulties as he could not even get wages. Distressed by this, he sold the house he lived in and used the proceeds to light lamps. The money from selling the house was also exhausted in the sacred service of lighting lamps. He then considered selling his wife to light the lamps. We see in many instances, including the story of Harichandran, that in earlier times, people would borrow money or sell themselves, their wives, and their servants as commodities. The thirteenth Thiruppaadal: "Manam Magizhndhu Manaiyaal Thamai Kondu Valanagaril Thanam Alippaar Thamai Engum Kidaiyaamaiyaal Thalarvu Eythi Sinavidaiyaar Thirukkoyil Thiruvilakku Pani Mutta Kanavinum Munbu Ariyaadhaar Kaiyaravaal Eythinaar." He was willing to sell his wife, but he could not find anyone in the prosperous city who would buy her and provide money. Therefore, the Nayanar, distressed that his sacred service of lighting lamps in the Lord's temple was being interrupted—something he had never even imagined in his dreams—arrived at the temple, feeling helpless. The fourteenth Thiruppaadal: "Panikkollum Padambakka Naayagar Tham Koyilin Ul Ani Kollum Thiruvilakku Panimaarum Amaiyaththil Manivanna Sudar Vilakku Maalin Yaan Maalvan Ena Thunivu Ullam Kolla Ninaindhu Avvinai Mudikka Thodanguvaar." The Lord of Thiruvottriyur is Padambakka Nathar. He is named Padambakka Nathar because he graciously absorbed Vasuki, the great serpent who received Shiva Diksha and worshipped Upamanyu Muni, into his broad, plank-like sacred body, so that the serpent's form is visible on the Lord's body. Vasuki is one of the eight great serpents that support the earth. It is traditional to say that the serpent Vasuki supports the earth on the east side. The Adhipureeswarar Sannidhi in Thiruvottriyur temple is different; Kaliya Nayanar attained glory by performing the lamp-lighting service in the Padambakka Nathar Sannidhi.

At the time of performing the sacred lamp-lighting service inside the temple of Padambakka Nathar, who had accepted him as a servant and his service, the Nayanar resolved that if the lamp-lighting service, which he always performed, were to cease, it would be better for him to die. It is the custom of noble souls to end their lives if the Shiva dharmas they regularly perform are interrupted or cease. We see this in the Thiruppuranams, including the Moorthy Nayanar Thiruppuranam. Thus, the Nayanar resolved to end his life. The fifteenth Thiruppaadal: "Thiruvilakku Thriyittu Angu Agal Parappi Seyal Niramba Oruviya Enneikku Eeda Udal Uthiram Koduniraikka Karuviyinaal Milaru Ariya Ak Kaiyai Kannudhalar Perugu Thirukkarunaiyudan Ner Vandhu Pidiththaruli." The sixteenth Thiruppaadal: "Matru Avardham Munnaaga Mazhaividai Mel Ezhundharula Utra Ooradhu Neengi Oli Vilanga Uchchiyinmel Patriya Anjalinarai Nindra Avarai Paramardhaam Porpudaiya Sivapuriyil Polindhirukka Arul Purindhaar." The Nayanar, believing it better to die than to let the sacred lamp-lighting service be interrupted, placed wicks in all the lamps. He arranged the oil lamps properly. There was no oil to put in the lamps. Therefore, in place of oil, he attempted to cut his own throat with a tool, intending to fill the lamps with his own blood. Lord Shiva, the Lord, caught his hand as he tried to cut it. Manifesting directly with abundant divine grace, He prevented the Nayanar from performing the act he intended. Furthermore, the Lord appeared before the Nayanar, mounted on a bull. Kaliya Nayanar stood with folded hands above his head, in reverence. Lord Shiva then blessed that Nayanar to reside gloriously in the beautiful Sivapuram (Shiva's abode). The seventeenth Thiruppaadal: "Devarpiraan Thiruvilakku Seyal Muttra Milaru Arindhu Mevariya Vinai Mudiththaar Kazhal Vanangi Viyan Ulagil Yaavar Enaadhu Aran Adiyaardhamai Igazhndhu Pesinarai Naavariyum Sakthiyaar Thiruththondin Nalam Uraippaam." The divine Sekkizhar Peruman, having concluded the Thiruppuranam of Kaliya Nayanar, who, when the sacred lamp-lighting service in the temple of the Lord of Devas was interrupted, attempted the impossible act of cutting his own throat, now, in accordance with his tradition, bows at the feet of Kaliya Nayanar and begins to introduce the Thiruththondam of Sakthiyar Nayanar, who would cut off the tongues of those who speak ill of Shiva's devotees in this vast world. Let us also bow at the feet of Kaliya Nayanar and continue to reflect on the Thiruppuranam of Sakthiyar Nayanar. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of our Lord Kaliya Nayanar. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of our Lord Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of Nambiyandar Nambigal Peruman. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the commentator, Ayya. Thiruchitrambalam.
