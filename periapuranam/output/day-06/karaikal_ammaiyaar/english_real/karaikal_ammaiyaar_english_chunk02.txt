Chapter 12 — Karaikal Ammaiyaar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 497. Thirunindra Sarukkam, Karaikal Ammaiyaar Puranam. The relatives of Punitthavathi Ammaiyaar ask Paramadattan, "Why do you fall at your wife's feet and worship her?" Paramadattan replies, pointing to Ammaiyaar, "She is not a human being. She is not an ordinary person. She is a great deity. I have realized that she is a deity. That is why I have left her and come here. After this, I have even named my daughter after her. That is why I worshipped her golden feet. Therefore, you too should worship her in the same way." Paramadattan tells all the relatives, "Just because she is in human form, you should not think she is an ordinary human. If she is not human, then who is she? She is a divine birth. She is a divine being. But unlike minor deities who frighten people and cause harm, she is a great deity who does good. I have understood this. Therefore, you too should worship her and attain salvation, without being deceived into thinking she is a mere human woman. Your mistake was not worshipping her until now. You have not received any good by not worshipping her until now. From now on, realize that Ammaiyaar is a divine woman and worship her." Paramadattan said this to all the relatives and guided them. It is at this point, at this time, that the merchant openly reveals to everyone what he had been thinking in his mind. Until now, Ammaiyaar had lived considering this merchant as her husband. But the merchant had mentally distanced himself from Ammaiyaar. The day the third mango arrived, according to Ammaiyaar's wish, by the grace of the Lord, on that very day, he had left Ammaiyaar. But Ammaiyaar was unaware of his mental state. That is why Ammaiyaar had until now followed the worldly chastity and conduct. Upon hearing this news, Ammaiyaar realizes that her connection with him has also been severed. Upon seeing such a change in the merchant, Ammaiyaar praises the feet of the Lord who wears the Kondrai garland in His matted locks. As it is said, "Think of one thing," Ammaiyaar's thoughts do not wander elsewhere; she fixes her thoughts on the Lord and remains with a deeply absorbed feeling within. "The lamp that splits the lamp, lights the lamp, kindles the lamp within the lamp, and can explain the lamp's light, can attain the feet of the Lord of the lamp," says the Thirumandiram, in the ninth Tantra, under the section Gnanaodayam, the third verse. In this verse, as revealed by Thirumooladeva Nayanar, Ammaiyaar completely discards both "pasa gnanam" (knowledge of bondage) and "pasu gnanam" (knowledge of the soul), and unites solely with "pathi gnanam" (knowledge of the Lord), attaining a state of deep inner perception. From that state of awareness, Ammaiyaar says the following: "This merchant has adopted this principle. This merchant has left me. Therefore, that marital connection has been severed. Now, I shall cast off this burden of a body, made of beautiful flesh, which I have borne for him, and O Lord, in that world, please grant me the form of a ghoul that praises Your feet." She worships the feet of the Lord and wishes to cast off this body of flesh, asking the Lord to grant her the form appropriate for the ghoul hosts who praise the Lord in Kailash. "Since this man has adopted this principle, I shall now cast off this burden of flesh, beautiful and borne for him, and here, O Lord, grant me the form of a ghoul that praises Your feet, in a fitting manner."

Sekizhaar Peruman sings in the 49th verse of Karaikal Ammaiyaar Puranam, "She stood worshipping the feet of the Supreme Lord." In this verse, Ammaiyaar refers to Paramadattan as "this man" (ivan). This is because the connection as husband has been severed. Therefore, she refers to Paramadattan as "this man." Shiva devotees value and consider only Lord Shiva and His devotees as important. Ammaiyaar now stands in that exalted state where she considers nothing else important except Lord Shiva and His devotees. Therefore, she places Paramadattan among the ordinary living beings, who are not important, and refers to him in the singular as "this man." Since Ammaiyaar referred to him in the singular, Sekizhaar Peruman also, throughout the Ammaiyaar Puranam, has sung about Paramadattan in the singular as "this man." Ammaiyaar makes a request to the Lord, saying that she no longer wants this body of flesh that she has borne for him until now. Therefore, it is understood that Ammaiyaar had considered this body as a burden until now. The beauty of the body is evident through its flesh. If the flesh is removed from the body, or if the flesh wastes away, the body will appear as a skeleton to the world. Therefore, Ammaiyaar feels that she has borne this burden of flesh long enough, and has borne it for him long enough, and makes a request to the Lord. Generally, women beautify their bodies, adorn themselves, and wear beautiful clothes and ornaments for their husbands. This was the noble conduct cherished by our ancestors. Therefore, Ammaiyaar too, for the sake of her husband, had until now carried this beautiful fleshy body. Now that the connection as husband has been severed, Ammaiyaar decides that this fleshy body is no longer needed. Therefore, she asks the Lord to cast off this fleshy body here and grant her the form of a ghoul, like the ghoul hosts who worship the Lord in Kailash. Ammaiyaar now wishes for the form of a ghoul, a Shiva Gana, that praises the feet of Lord Shiva. This ghoul form is one of the ghoul hosts in Kailash. She requests the form of that ghoul host. Generally, the ghoul form that Ammaiyaar requested is not the ghoul form that wanders in this world, tormenting people with a body of air. The body of ghouls that wander in this world is called a body of air. This physical body is made up of the five elements. For the ghouls that wander in this world, among the five elements that make up their body, the other four elements disappear, and only air remains as their body. Therefore, these ghouls have bodies made of air, invisible to the eyes, and are bodies of bondage. But the ghoul that Ammaiyaar requested is not this. It is the form of the ghoul hosts, one of the Lord's hosts in Thirukailai. The form Ammaiyaar requested is a body where everything else in the human body disappears, and only the skeleton remains. This is because this form is capable of moving very quickly, like the wind. That form is a skeletal form where only the element of earth remains in abundance. Nevertheless, this ghoul form that Ammaiyaar requested and received is a pure Maya form, purified by removing impurities, like copper that becomes gold after removing its dross. But to the eyes of other people, it appears as a form of light. That is why others were afraid and ran away when they saw that body.

They worshipped. That is why Ammaiyaar, with this body, quickly traversed all the northern lands and went to Thirukailai Mountain. When climbing Thirukailai Mountain and when reaching Thiruvallangaadu, she avoided walking with her feet and walked with her head. The Lord Himself explains this ghoul form as a "form of great glory" to Uma Devi. With this ghoul form, Ammaiyaar sits in Thiruvallangaadu, beneath the Lord's uplifted foot, always filled with joy, singing hymns. It is with this ghoul form that Ammaiyaar composed the Thirupathigams and the Thiruvakkus like Irattai Manimalai and Thiruvandhadhi. Therefore, this ghoul form is a sacred form that possesses the external instruments like speech for singing poetry and music, and internal instruments like the mind, which Ammaiyaar requested. That form is a form of light. Ammaiyaar herself has sung about how she attained the ghoul form in her own sacred verses. The 86th verse of Ammaiyaar's Thiru Arputha Thiruvandhadhi is as follows: "Even if we attain it, we desire nothing else. Even if this comes to us or not, a little enlightenment, He who placed His eye on His forehead, we are one of His good ghoul hosts." In this verse, Ammaiyaar sings about the ghoul form she received, saying that the Lord has kept her as one of His good ghoul hosts. Similarly, whenever Ammaiyaar refers to herself, she calls herself "Karaikal Pei" (the ghoul of Karaikal). She declares this through her own sacred words. Because of this, our Sundaramoorthy Swamigal praises Ammaiyaar as "Peyar" (the ghoul) in his Thiruthondathogai. Ammaiyaar mentions this ghoul form she took in the first verse of Thiruvalangattu Mootha Thirupathigam: "With shrunken breasts and prominent veins, with hollow eyes and white teeth, with a sunken belly, a female ghoul with long, high ankles, a ghoul of Shiva, with long teeth, stayed, crying out in the dry forest, her matted locks spread in eight directions, her body chilled, dancing in the fire, that is the sacred Thiruvalangadu of our Father." In this sacred verse, she sings about herself as a female ghoul, describing her form: "a female ghoul with long teeth and high, long ankles, a ghoul of Shiva." Similarly, in the 11th verse of the same Thirupathigam: "The one without equal, ghouls gathered, hitting each other, dancing with joy, singing, the sweet woman arranged it. The ten Tamil verses sung by Karaikal Pei, the ghoul with matted hair, about the Father in beautiful Thiruvalangadu, those who master them will attain Shiva's path and enjoy bliss." In this verse, Ammaiyaar refers to herself as "Karaikal Pei." Furthermore, in the 11th verse of the second Thirupathigam of Thiruvalangattu Mootha Thirupathigam, Ammaiyaar refers to herself as "Karaikal Pei." That verse is as follows: "By the grace of the Lord who wears the crescent moon on His matted locks, who dances the sacred dance, who wears the snake around His waist, the ten songs of Karaikal Pei, the ghoul with fiery fangs abundant in the forest, if sung and danced to, sins will be destroyed." This is how the verse is structured. Thus, in the Thirupathigams composed by Ammaiyaar, she reveals that she took the form of a ghoul. Thus, our Ammaiyaar makes a request to the Lord, wishing for this ghoul form. Let us continue to contemplate the history of how the Lord granted Ammaiyaar's request. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 13 — Karaikal Ammaiyaar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam by Sekizhaar Swamigal, Thirunindra Sarukkam, Karaikal Ammaiyaar Puranam. Karaikal Ammaiyaar requests the Lord to grant her the form of a ghoul. Sekizhaar Peruman mentions this, saying, "She stood worshipping the feet of the Supreme Lord, asking that the ghoul form be fitting for her." Here, Sekizhaar Peruman has sung about the Lord as "Paramar" (the Supreme One). Although the Lord has many names, Sekizhaar Peruman uses "Paramar" here, saying, "She stood worshipping the feet of Paramar." When the physical husband, Paramadattan, whom Ammaiyaar had until now, left her, Sekizhaar Peruman uses the name "Paramar" here to indicate the Lord as the eternal husband of her soul, who will never leave her. Therefore, when Ammaiyaar requested the ghoul form, the Lord granted it. Sekizhaar Peruman sings about this in the following verse: "At that moment, by the grace of the Dancer in the hall, she, who was to receive what she desired, shed all the beauty of her flesh and became a skeletal body, a ghoul form that the heavens and earth worshipped." Thus, Sekizhaar Peruman describes it. As Ammaiyaar stood worshipping, by the grace of the Lord of the Dance in the hall, the awareness to attain the supreme path came to Ammaiyaar. Ammaiyaar received exactly what she desired. She shed all the flesh and the beauty that manifested through it from her body, and attained a ghoul form with only bones, becoming one of the Shiva Ganas. She received the form of a ghoul, one of the Shiva Ganas, worthy of worship by the heavens, the earth, and all other worlds. Ammaiyaar requested and received this for the sake of supreme awareness. Due to the intensity of that supreme awareness, Ammaiyaar received what she desired, exactly as she desired, at that very moment. How did she receive it? By shedding the fleshy body and the beauty that came with it, Ammaiyaar received a body that was a mere skeleton. This is the ghoul form. This is the form of a Shiva Bhuta Gana. This is the form Ammaiyaar desired. She received that form by the grace of the Lord. The form Ammaiyaar attained is a form worshipped by the heavens and the earth. Generally, when referring to worlds, they are called the upper world, the middle world, and the lower world. Here, Sekizhaar Peruman mentions "worshipped by the heavens and the earth." By mentioning "heavens," Sekizhaar Peruman refers to the upper worlds, and by "earth," he refers to the middle world. Instead of mentioning the lower world explicitly, he says "all worshipped." This "all" refers to the lower world. Sekizhaar Peruman says "all" to refer to the lower world in an auspicious manner, avoiding inauspicious terms. This is one of the unique characteristics of our Sekizhaar Peruman. Sekizhaar Peruman has composed this Puranam, the biography of a devotee, without using inauspicious language. This instance serves as another proof of that. Thus, Ammaiyaar requests and receives the ghoul form. Immediately, divine flower showers fall everywhere. Divine drums (Deva Dundubhis) resound. Devas and sages gather and rejoice. All the Shiva Ganas perform dances and songs, filled with joy. All the relatives who surrounded Ammaiyaar worship her, are afraid, and leave from there. Sekizhaar Peruman mentions this in the following verse: "Flower showers fell everywhere, the sound of divine drums filled all the worlds, and the great sages too rejoiced. All the Ganas danced with joy, and the countless relatives who stood before her worshipped her, were afraid, and left." Sekizhaar Peruman composes this verse, the 51st verse in Ammaiyaar Puranam. As soon as Ammaiyaar received the ghoul form, divine drums resounded. Whenever a manifestation of Shiva's grace occurs, divine drums resound on their own, without anyone playing them. We have seen this in many places in Periya Puranam. Sekizhaar Peruman sings that the sound of the divine drums filled all the worlds. The Devas and sages are very happy. All the Shiva Ganas perform the Kunalai dance. Kunalai dance is a type of dance performed with song when joy is exceedingly great. The ghoul Ganas, who are Shiva Ganas, perform it. All the relatives who stood before her worshipped Ammaiyaar, were afraid, and left. The phrase "stood before her" should be understood in terms of both time and place. Those who stood before her in time were the relatives who had been with Ammaiyaar until now. These relatives who stood before her until now now withdraw. If understood in terms of place, they stood around Ammaiyaar, in front of her. They saw the ghoul form Ammaiyaar had taken, were frightened, and stood aside, retreating. They worshipped her. Even though these relatives saw Ammaiyaar's ghoul form, they did not receive the benefit of seeing that sacred form; instead, they stood aside in fear. Therefore, Sekizhaar Peruman places them at the end of his narration. These relatives were those who came with Ammaiyaar from Karaikal and those who came with the merchant through his second wife. Thus, relatives came from both sides. These people worshipped the ghoul form Ammaiyaar received but did not benefit from it; instead, they retreated. Sekizhaar Peruman thus sings about everyone, even the relatives. But he does not sing about the merchant. This is because the merchant was already worshipping Ammaiyaar as a great deity. He continues in the same state even now. Therefore, Sekizhaar Peruman does not sing about the husband's state here. It can also be understood that he included him as one of the relatives and implicitly mentioned him. Sekizhaar Peruman continues to sing the next verse: "Then, with the unity of knowledge that arose, she composed the wonderful Thiru Arputha Thiruvandhadhi, singing with delight, 'I am one of the good hosts who praise the beautiful, red lotus feet of the Lord of Uma.'" Thus, as soon as she received the ghoul form, Ammaiyaar composes the Arputha Thiruvandhadhi. Sekizhaar Peruman sings about this in this verse. It begins with "with the unity of knowledge that arose." It should be understood as "with the unity of knowledge that arose and manifested." "Unity of knowledge" means knowledge that is completely filled with Shiva, where nothing else appears but Shiva. He sings that this unity of knowledge arose and manifested. "Bhavam" means origin. The prefix "ut" from Sanskrit grammar is added to the word "bhavam." "Ut" is a prefix that intensifies the meaning of the word that follows it. Therefore, "utbavithu" means "having arisen." The unity of knowledge, where nothing else appears but Shiva, completely filled with Shiva, that knowledge arises and manifests. What is unity of knowledge? It is a state where knowledge does not stand as three – "gnanam" (knowledge), "gnathru" (knower), and "gneyam" (known) – but only "pathi gnanam" (knowledge of the Lord), which is Shiva-gnanam, arises, and one's own self ceases to exist. Even before this, Sekizhaar Peruman had sung in verses 1764 and 1741 that Ammaiyaar stood with a unified mind, with intense awareness, and realizing with her mind, forgetting herself. However, now the beginning of that unity of knowledge arises and manifests. In those two instances, Ammaiyaar gave space in her mind to herself, her husband, and other existing things. But now, Ammaiyaar's state is one where there is nothing else in her mind, nothing in her thoughts, nothing in her intellect, except Shiva. Therefore, Sekizhaar Peruman sings "unity of knowledge." However, the "I" in "I am one of the good hosts" is an experience of Shiva's pervasiveness, where her individual soul-consciousness has ceased to exist. Thus, with the unity of knowledge that arose, at that very moment she received the ghoul form, she praises the lotus feet of the Lord. She composes the Arputha Thiruvandhadhi, saying, "I have become one of the good hosts who praise the lotus feet of the Lord." The Shiva Ganas of the Lord are those who only perform the act of praising the Lord's feet without forgetting them. Shiva Ganas do not perform any other actions. Ammaiyaar sings that she has become one of these Shiva Ganas. We can recall here how Thirunavukkarasu Peruman sings in Thiruvangamalai, "Will I be joyful, counted among the many hosts of the Lord, and go and be joyful beneath the sacred feet of the one who holds the small deer?" Ammaiyaar, saying "I am a servant to the servants," sings with joy about the blessing she received, becoming one of those good hosts, referring to the greatness of the devotees. "Who else will receive what the Father granted me? Alas!" says Thiruvachagam. "I have received knowledge that no one else has received beneath the sacred feet of the Lord of Shivaloka. Who else in the world will receive it?" We can recall here the ideas from the seventh verse of Thirupallandu composed by Sendhanar and other sacred verses. Ammaiyaar also sings about this in many places in Arputha Thiruvandhadhi. Therefore, Ammaiyaar's Arputha Thiruvandhadhi is a devotional work sung, immersed in the devotion of servitude to Lord Shiva. The name of this work is Arputha Thiruvandhadhi. The "Arputham" (wonder) here refers to the wonder of servitude. "Arputham" also means knowledge. Thus, Ammaiyaar composes Thiru Arputha Thiruvandhadhi. She composes Thiru Arputha Thiruvandhadhi at the very moment she receives the ghoul form. Sekizhaar Peruman continues to sing the next verse: "Having composed the well-researched Irattai Malai Andhadhi, with overflowing supreme awareness, she approached the great Kailash mountain of silver, where the Lord who burnt the three cities in the past resides, with abundant grace leading the way, and came by the path of worship." This is the meaning of this verse. Sekizhaar Peruman mentions that after composing Thiru Arputha Thiruvandhadhi, Ammaiyaar composes Thiru Irattai Malai. Irattai Malai is also known as Thiru Irattai Manimalai. After receiving the ghoul form, Ammaiyaar first composed Arputha Thiruvandhadhi, praising the power of that state. Following Arputha Thiruvandhadhi, she composed Thiru Irattai Manimalai, praising the glories of the Lord who granted her that ghoul nature. Where were these two compositions, these two devotional works, sung? They were sung near the boundary of the Pandya kingdom, the town where the merchant lived. We previously saw that this town might have been a coastal town in the Pandya kingdom. Similarly, it is noteworthy that there is a temple for Karaikal Ammaiyaar on the way from Thiruchendur to Kulasekarapattinam. Therefore, the merchant might have lived in one of these places. It is when she goes to see him there that this divine event happens to Ammaiyaar. Therefore, Ammaiyaar composes Thiru Arputha Thiruvandhadhi and Thiru Irattai Manimalai at the very same place and moment she received the ghoul form. Thus, praising the glories of Lord Shiva, she sets out to go to the silver Kailash mountain where Lord Shiva resides. Two devotees who desired to go to Thirukailai Mountain and have a vision of Kailash are Thirunavukkarasu Peruman, one of our religious preceptors, and our Ammaiyaar. Therefore, Sivakavimani Ayya, in his great commentary on Periya Puranam, recalls Thirunavukkarasu Peruman and Karaikal Ammaiyaar at this point, and narrates their Kailash pilgrimages and their visions of Kailash as two intertwined garlands. Let us continue to contemplate that. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 14 — Karaikal Ammaiyaar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam by Sekizhaar Swamigal, Part 499, Thirunindra Sarukkam, Karaikal Ammaiyaar Puranam. Karaikal Ammaiyaar desires to see the vision of Kailash Mountain. She intends to go to Kailash Mountain and have a darshan of the Lord. And so, she begins her journey. At this point, Sivakavimani Ayya recalls the Kailash pilgrimage of Thirunavukkarasar Swamigal and Karaikal Ammaiyaar. Thirunavukkarasu Nayanar, when he worshipped the Lord at Thirukalathi Mountain, received a divine indication there and desired to see the vision of Kailash Mountain. From there, he began his Kailash pilgrimage. Here, Ammaiyaar, having requested and received the ghoul form of a Shiva Bhuta Gana, composed Thiru Arputha Thiruvandhadhi and Thiru Irattai Manimalai, and with overflowing supreme awareness, was graced to go to Kailash Mountain and worship the Lord, and she undertook it. Although Thirunavukkarasar desired to go to Kailash Mountain and see the vision of Kailash, the Lord, so that Thirunavukkarasu Peruman could compose many more hymns, brought Thirunavukkarasar to Thiruvaiyaru and granted him the vision of Kailash there. Here, Ammaiyaar receives divine guidance. According to that, she must reach Kailash. And so, she desires to go to Kailash, worship the Lord, and ask for boons. Accordingly, having received boons, Karaikal Ammaiyaar proceeds to Thiruvallangaadu according to the Lord's command. Thirunavukkarasu Peruman, with his human form, walks to Kailash Mountain. Here, Ammaiyaar, with her ghoul form, travels faster than the mind and reaches Kailash Mountain. She walks on her head to Kailash Mountain. Appar Peruman, by the command of the Lord who appeared as a sage in Kailash, after bathing in the pond there, emerged in the Thiruvaiyaru river without leaving any trace. We have seen that sacred history. Here, Ammaiyaar, by the Lord's command, returns to Thiruvallangaadu. Just as she walked on her head to Kailash Mountain without touching it with her feet, she also reaches Thiruvallangaadu walking on her head. Appar Peruman, who emerged in Thiruvaiyaru, later visited many shrines and, in Thirupugalur, became the form of Shivananda Gnana and remained at the Lord's feet. Here, Ammaiyaar remains forever at the Lord's feet in Thiruvallangaadu. Ammaiyaar requested the boon to remain forever at the Lord's feet, and accordingly, she remains forever at the Lord's feet in Thiruvallangaadu. It is our duty to compare and worship the histories of Appar and Ammaiyaar, who went to Thirukailai and returned after seeing it, and thereby attain salvation. This is our duty, says Sivakavimani Ayya at this point. Thus, Sivakavimani Ayya has commented on the Thiru Arputha Thiruvandhadhi and Thiru Irattai Manimalai composed by Ammaiyaar at this point. We can contemplate that commentary in the concluding part of Ammaiyaar's Puranam. Sekizhaar Peruman continues to sing Ammaiyaar's Puranam. Thus, Ammaiyaar, having taken the ghoul form, goes to Thirukailai Mountain. Now, the next verse: "Those who saw her were astonished, feared, and ran away, moving their hands. When they heard the true nature of the form she had taken, they wondered, 'If the Lord of the Devas knows me, then what form am I to these people of the eight directions who do not know the truth?'" Everyone who saw Ammaiyaar coming in this ghoul form was afraid and ran away. But Ammaiyaar was not disturbed in the least by this. She says, "Lord Shiva, the chief of all gods, knows me. So, what does it matter what form I bear for these people of the world? It makes no difference." The first emotion that arose in the minds of those who saw Ammaiyaar's form was astonishment. That astonishment led to fear. As fear arose, all those people ran away from that place as if escaping from an impending danger. But these people did not understand the true nature of this form. All the people who ran away kept saying "ghoul." Was it true that these people kept calling her a ghoul and ran away? No. This is because the ghoul form that our Ammaiyaar received is the form of a ghoul, one of the Shiva Ganas. It is not the ghoul that ordinary people in the world fear. We have seen this in detail before. Therefore, it should be understood that the form Ammaiyaar took is the form of a good ghoul, one of Shiva's hosts, whom everyone comes to worship and benefit from, and not the ghoul form that frightens, torments, and causes much trouble to the people of this world. It is because they did not know this difference that all these people ran away in fear. But Ammaiyaar was not disturbed in the least by all this. Ammaiyaar remains calm, saying, "Lord Shiva, the Lord of the Devas, knows me, and that alone is enough." Sekizhaar Peruman continues to sing: "She quickly traversed all the northern lands, faster than the mind, and reached near the great Kailash mountain of shining light, where the Lord with the trident, wearing a garland of blooming flowers, resides. There, she abandoned walking with her feet and walked on her head on the earth." Ammaiyaar quickly traversed all the northern lands, faster than the speed of thought. She reached very close to Kailash Mountain, which shines with great light, where Lord Shiva, who wears the Kondrai garland and holds the trident in His hand, resides. This is because Ammaiyaar had received the ghoul form, so she traveled very quickly and reached Thirukailai Mountain. She intended to reach Kailash Mountain. Therefore, she traveled north. She quickly traversed all the northern lands. We have contemplated in detail in Thirunavukkarasu Nayanar Puranam how Sekizhaar Peruman described the paths and characteristics of those lands. Therefore, Sekizhaar Peruman does not need to repeat all that here. So, he simply says, "She quickly traversed all the northern lands, faster than the mind." These lands include Thiruparvatham, which is Srisailam, Thirukukarnam, and other northern lands. Here are countries like mountains, Telugu, Kannada, Malavam, Iladam, Madhyama Baitiram, Ganga Nadu, Karchuram, etc. We have contemplated this in detail in Thirunavukkarasu Peruman Puranam. Here, Ammaiyaar travels faster than the mind. Thirunavukkarasu Nayanar travels for many days, enduring much physical hardship, to reach these places. Here, Ammaiyaar has the ghoul form. Therefore, Ammaiyaar reaches Kailash Mountain very quickly. Because of this, Sekizhaar Peruman, who sang about Appar Peruman's Kailash pilgrimage in ten verses, beautifully depicts Ammaiyaar's Kailash pilgrimage in half a verse. As soon as she reached near Kailash Mountain, Ammaiyaar changed her mode of travel. Thinking that her feet should not touch Thirukailai, from the moment she began to ascend Kailash Mountain, she stopped walking with her feet and walked on her head, upside down. Ammaiyaar begins to ascend Kailash Mountain. Therefore, it is understood that Ammaiyaar walked very quickly on her feet through the countries and paths from the town in the Pandya kingdom to the foot of Kailash Mountain. It is understood here that the ghoul form Ammaiyaar received helped her to quickly cover such a long distance. The ghoul form, unlike other bodies, does not have an abundance of the earth element but has an abundance of the air element. Therefore, according to the saying "wind speed, mind speed," this ghoul form helped Ammaiyaar to reach her destination very quickly. Ammaiyaar ascends Kailash Mountain on her head. Kailash Mountain is a sacred place. Therefore, thinking that it is not right to step on it with her feet, she walks on her head. Kailash is the mountain where Lord Shiva resides.

Therefore, its base is considered to be the feet of Lord Shiva. We must place our head at the feet of Lord Shiva. Therefore, Ammaiyaar begins to walk on her head. In accordance with the scriptural concept of "Thadalai" (foot-head), Ammaiyaar walks on Kailash Mountain with her head. It is forbidden to step on sacred places with one's feet. Accordingly, Ammaiyaar also walks on her head in Thiruvallangaadu, not with her feet. Thiruvathigai is a sacred place where Thirunavukkarasu Peruman performed service. Therefore, we have read the history of Aludaiya Nambigal, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, fearing to step on the sacred place of Thiruvathigai, staying in the Sithavadamadam outside Thiruvathigai. We will now see in Thirugnanasambandhar Peruman Puranam the history of Aludaiya Pillaiyar, Thirugnanasambandhar Peruman, fearing to step on the sacred place of Thiruvallangaadu, where Ammaiyaar walked on her head, staying outside that sacred place. Therefore, we can recall here the divine revelations that the Lord spoke to both of them in their dreams to announce these rare deeds to the world. Ammaiyaar walks on her head. This means walking upside down, supporting herself with both hands on the ground. Thus, Ammaiyaar walks on her head on Kailash Mountain, where Lord Shiva, who wears the Kondrai garland and holds the trident in His sacred hand, resides. Here, Sekizhaar Peruman refers to the Lord as "Idhazhi Malai Soolapani" (one with a garland of flowers and a trident in hand), indicating Him through these two symbols. This is because the Kondrai garland is a primal garland, formed in a primal way. The Lord resides within this Kondrai flower. The Thevaram states, "He is within the fragrant Kondrai flower." Secondly, Sekizhaar Peruman refers to the Lord as "Soolapani" (one with a trident in hand). The trident is the trident that destroys the three impurities (Anava, Karma, Maya). The trident in the Lord's hand shows that Lord Shiva is the primeval one who removes the three impurities and grants complete knowledge. Until now, Ammaiyaar has been serving by knowing only the knowledge that is deeply absorbed and understood. In the 61st verse of Thiru Arputha Thiruvandhadhi, she sings, "Even then I became His servant without seeing His form, and even now I do not see His form. What shall I say to those who say, 'What form is He?' What is Your form?" According to this, until now, Ammaiyaar, who served by knowing the Lord's form only through deeply absorbed knowledge, now, as desired in this verse, travels on her head on Kailash Mountain, in a state where she can see the Lord with her eyes. Let us continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 15 — Karaikal Ammaiyaar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam by Sekizhaar Swamigal, Part 500, Thirunindra Sarukkam, Karaikal Ammaiyaar Puranam. Ammaiyaar walks on Kailash Mountain with her head. Sekizhaar Peruman continues to sing: "As she walked on her head and ascended the silver mountain where Shankaran resided, her joy and love overflowed. The beautiful-browed Uma Devi, who is in one half of the Lord with the crescent moon on His matted hair and the eye on His forehead, cast her sacred gaze upon her." Ammaiyaar walks on her head on Thirukailai Mountain. As she ascends the mountain, her joy increases, and her love overflows more and more. When we climb any mountain with our feet, we feel tired and our body becomes weary. Moreover, this silver mountain is very difficult to ascend. It is very difficult to ascend that mountain. We can recall here, from Thirunavukkarasu Peruman Puranam, the Lord's words that Kailash Mountain is not easily accessible to human beings. We also contemplated how Appar Swamigal's body suffered when he reached Kailash Mountain. Therefore, Ammaiyaar walks on her head on the silver Thirukailai Mountain, which is very difficult to walk on even with feet. Nevertheless, Ammaiyaar's joy continues to increase, and her love overflows. This beautifully reveals the firmness of Ammaiyaar's great love and the power of the ghoul form she received. That she walks on her head is very rare. Therefore, Sekizhaar Peruman mentions the fact that Ammaiyaar walked on her head in the previous verse, in this verse, and in the next verse. He mentions the fact that Ammaiyaar walked on her head three times in three verses. Generally, Sekizhaar Peruman's tradition is not to repeat a fact once stated. Here, he slightly relaxes that tradition and mentions it three times. This indicates the firmness of Ammaiyaar's great love and how rare this act is. Why does Ammaiyaar's joy overflow? Because she walks on her head, remembering the great grace the Lord bestowed upon her, with immense delight. "You considered even me, a dog, as worthy and bestowed Your grace. Having done so, I walked on my head, O Shankara, the Lord with the bull!" says the third verse of Sethila Pathu. Here too, Manikkavachagar Peruman refers to the Lord as Shankara. Shankara means one who bestows happiness. He bestows unparalleled bliss. Therefore, before that unparalleled bliss, the great suffering of ascending the silver mountain of Kailash on one's head is nothing. Before that bliss, the weariness and suffering of climbing become nothing. Therefore, in this verse too, Sekizhaar Peruman uses the Lord's name Shankara, meaning one who bestows happiness, in "walking on her head and ascending the silver mountain where Shankaran resided." Similarly, when Manikkavachagar Peruman sings "I walked on my head" in Sethila Pathu, he also uses the name Shankara there. Thus, Sekizhaar Peruman portrays Ammaiyaar's state of mind, with joy and love overflowing. As Ammaiyaar ascends, Uma Devi, the beautiful-browed one, casts her sacred gaze upon Ammaiyaar. Uma Devi, who is in one half of Lord Shiva, who wears the crescent moon and has an eye on His forehead, is Parvati Ammaiyaar, who has a bow-like forehead. Therefore, the sacred gaze of Uma Devi falls upon our Ammaiyaar. Parvati Devi sees Ammaiyaar walking. Lord Shiva and Uma Devi reside as Ammaiyaar and Appar (Mother and Father) in Thirukailai Mountain. Among Ammaiyaar and Appar, Uma Devi's gaze first falls upon Ammaiyaar. The grace of Lord Shiva is Uma Devi. The Lord's compassion is the power of grace. Therefore, one can see Lord Shiva only after receiving His grace. That is why the gaze of Uma Devi, the Lord's power of grace, first falls upon Ammaiyaar. As it is said, "By His grace, worship His feet." Thus, the sacred gaze of Uma Devi falls upon Ammaiyaar. This is a sign of the descent of divine grace. Sekizhaar Peruman continues to sing: "Ambika, astonished in her sacred heart, humbly asked her Lord, 'What is the love of this skeletal body that walks on its head and ascends here, O my Lord?' Then, the Lord, the husband of our Lady, replied to her." Thus, Ambika, who cast her sacred gaze upon Ammaiyaar walking on Thirukailai Mountain in a ghoul form, asks the Lord. Astonishment arises in Ambika's sacred heart. Therefore, with that astonished heart, she asks the Lord, "O my Lord, what is the love of this skeletal body that walks on its head and ascends here?" Uma Devi asks the Lord. In the previous verse, we saw Uma Devi casting her sacred gaze upon Ammaiyaar. Having cast her sacred gaze, she asks the Lord about the wonder she saw. "We saw a wonder," says Thiruvachagam. Thus, she asks the Lord with desire. "This ghoul form, which is a skeletal body, walks on its head and ascends Thirukailai Mountain. What is that love?" she asks the Lord. That form is a ghoul form, a skeletal body. But its action is the wondrous act of walking on its head and worshipping on Kailash Mountain. Therefore, "What is the power of this love? What is the nature of this love?" asks the Great Lady to the Lord with astonishment. Why does the Great Lady ask the Lord this? Because the greatness of Ammaiyaar must be announced to the world through the words of the Lord. Through that, the world must know and praise Ammaiyaar's greatness and attain salvation. It is for this reason that Uma Devi asks the Lord. This shows the compassion Ambika has for the beings of the world. Just as Uma Devi, who performed penance in Thirukamakottam to worship the Lord and save the world, having heard all the Agamic principles revealed by the Lord, out of compassion for the beings, here too, Uma Devi, out of compassion for the beings, asks the Lord so that Ammaiyaar's greatness may be revealed through the Lord's own words, and through that, the world may attain salvation. That is why Sekizhaar Peruman praises Uma Devi as "our Great Lady" in this sacred verse. Thus, as soon as our Great Lady asked the Lord, Lord Shiva, her husband, replies to her. "Nayagan" means one who leads. The Lord is the leader who leads the world. Therefore, He speaks of Ammaiyaar's greatness as bestowing grace upon the beings of the world. The Lord's reply to the question, "What is the nature of the love of that ghoul form, a skeletal body, that walks on its head?" is given by Sekizhaar Peruman in the next verse. It is a very profound verse. The Lord says: "This one who comes, O Uma, is our Mother who cherishes us. She desired and received this glorious form. Then, approaching her, He looked at her and graciously uttered the unique word 'Ammaiye' for the salvation of all worlds." This is the next verse, the 58th verse in Karaikal Ammaiyaar Puranam. The Lord replies to Uma Devi. "O Uma, this one who comes as a ghoul form, a skeletal body, as you said, is our Mother who cherishes us. And she desired and received this glorious form, this ghoul form." As the Lord replies to the Great Lady, Ammaiyaar approaches the Lord and Lady. Then, looking at our Ammaiyaar who had come near, the Lord called Karaikal Ammaiyaar "Ammaiye" and graciously uttered this unique and perfect word for the salvation of all worlds. This is the meaning of this verse. The Lord tells Uma Devi, "This one who comes is our Mother who cherishes us, O Uma." He says "Mother who cherishes." "Cherishing" means feeding and protecting, nurturing. It also means praising and honoring with great love. Here, when the Lord refers to Karaikal Ammaiyaar as "Mother who cherishes," it is in the sense of a nurturing mother. Thiruvachagam praises the Lord as "Motherless, fatherless, He is alone." Thiruvachagam praises the Lord as one who has no mother and no father. Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam says, "Did she come as a mother to You who have no mother?" Therefore, it means that Karaikal Ammaiyaar acted like a mother to the Lord who has no mother, to the Lord who has no parents, and praised and protected Him. Our Ammaiyaar had a mother's love for the Lord. It is because of this mother's love for the Lord that Ammaiyaar also fed and served the devotees of the Lord. We saw this in the earlier part of this history. When devotees came, she worshipped them. Lord Shiva, our Lord, when His devotees came, offered them sacred food. He gave them whatever they desired. He satisfied the hunger of His devotees. We contemplated in detail in verses 1721, 1731, and 1734 how Ammaiyaar offered sacred food to the devotees of the Lord. All these are actions that arose from Ammaiyaar's motherly love for the Lord. How so? When offering sacred food to devotees, the Lord eats from within the devotees. This is the concept of Maheswara Puja. Ammaiyaar had the nature of a mother's love for the Lord. Therefore, it can be understood that when she offered sacred food to the Lord's devotees, the Lord also ate from within them. Furthermore, there are internal evidences in many of the sacred verses of Arputha Thiruvandhadhi and Thiru Irattai Manimalai that Ammaiyaar had the nature of a mother's love for the Lord. Not only that, but her ability to cherish, praise, and worship the Lord was also of that nature. Therefore, the two meanings of "cherishing" – feeding and protecting, and praising and honoring with great love – beautifully apply to Ammaiyaar here. That is why the Lord Himself, with His sacred words, tells Uma Devi, "This one who comes is our Mother who cherishes us." We will see the nature of Ammaiyaar's praise and worship of the Lord in the commentary section of those compositions, the Arputha Thiruvandhadhi and Thiru Irattai Manimalai. The Lord says, "This one who comes is our Mother who cherishes us, O Uma." "Kan" (காண்) here means "see and understand," conveying a sense of certainty. Not only did He say this to Uma Devi, but He also called Ammaiyaar, who had arrived there, "Ammaiye" with His sacred words, referring to her in that same way. As Ammaiyaar was ascending the mountain, Uma Devi asked the Lord, and the Lord replied. At this time, Ammaiyaar arrives near the Lord and Lady. Immediately, the Lord calls Karaikal Ammaiyaar "Ammaiye." Therefore, Sekizhaar Peruman also sings in the next verse, "Anganan (the merciful one) called her Ammaiye." Therefore, when He tells Uma Devi, "This one who comes is our Mother who cherishes us," the word "Ammai" (Mother) appears. When Ammaiyaar comes near, Lord Shiva calls her "Ammaiye." Thus, the word "Ammai" appears a second time. Now, in the next verse, Sekizhaar Peruman, in his own words, sings, "Anganan called her Ammaiye." Thus, the word "Ammai" appears a third time. Just as Sekizhaar Peruman previously explained the greatness of "walking on her head" by mentioning it three times, here too, we see his skill in emphasizing the greatness of the word "Ammai" by using it three times in this verse and the next. We have already seen that when Sekizhaar Peruman began Ammaiyaar's Puranam, he sang "Ammai." He concludes Ammaiyaar's Puranam with the word "Ammai." Similarly, even though there was room to name this Puranam "Peyar Puranam," Sekizhaar Peruman named it "Karaikal Ammaiyaar Puranam." All this we have seen before. Therefore, we can understand that this entire Puranam is to explain the greatness of the Lord calling her "Ammaiye." Furthermore, in the previous verse, Uma Devi calls the Lord "Emperuman." Sekizhaar Peruman has placed that word "Emperuman," which refers to the Lord, in the middle of that verse. Similarly, in this verse, the Lord calls Uma Devi "Umaiye," and he has placed that in the middle. Therefore, Sekizhaar Peruman implicitly conveys to us the state where Shiva and Shakti are both embedded in the midst of Ammaiyaar's greatness. This is in accordance with Thirumandiram's statement, "He is the Lord who embedded coral in crystal," and Thiruvachagam's statement, "Because both of You reside within me, Your servant." Therefore, it means that Shiva and Shakti reside and bestow grace in the midst of Ammaiyaar's greatness. The Lord says, "This one who comes is our Mother who cherishes us, O Uma," and adds, "She desired and received this glorious form." Karaikal Ammaiyaar herself desired this ghoul form, and the Lord granted it. Lord Shiva, with His sacred words, calls that form a "glorious form." Uma Devi asked, "This body made of bones," and in reply, the Lord says that this form is a "glorious form." This form that Ammaiyaar took is not to be considered among other skeletal bodies. The Lord says that this is a very glorious form. "Glorious form" refers to the form of the Bhuta Ganas. What is its greatness? It is a body that is attained through penance, desire, and seeking. This body is the body of the Bhuta Ganas. The Lord calls Karaikal Ammaiyaar, who came near, "Ammaiye." This word "Ammaiye" is a perfect word, a word that brings perfection, a unique word. "Perfection" here means the nature of Shiva, the ultimate reality. Therefore, this word "Ammaiye" is the best means to make us attain the nature of Shiva. Therefore, it is understood that this sacred name "Ammai" of Karaikal Ammaiyaar is the best means to make us attain the nature of Shiva. The Great Lord graciously utters this sacred word for the salvation of all worlds. This word, the sacred name "Ammai" of Ammaiyaar, by worshipping this sacred name alone, this world will attain salvation. This is the path shown by the Lord Himself, the assurance given by Him. Worshipping that word means contemplating and uttering that word with mind and speech. Let us continue to contemplate what Ammaiyaar replies when the Lord calls her "Ammaiye." Thiruchitrambalam.
