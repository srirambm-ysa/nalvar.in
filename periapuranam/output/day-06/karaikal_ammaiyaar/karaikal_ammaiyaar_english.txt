Chapter 01 — Karaikal Ammaiyaar

Tiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 486, Tirunindra Sarukkam, Karaikal Ammaiyaar Puranam. Following the Puranam of Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar in Periya Puranam is the Puranam of Karaikal Ammaiyaar. First, let us briefly consider the history of Karaikal Ammaiyaar, and then we can extensively contemplate her Puranam in the categories of Thogai Nool, Vagai Nool, and Viri Nool. Karaikal is a port city, a city rich in maritime resources. In that town lived a merchant named Dhanadattan. Due to the austerities he performed in all his previous births, Punithavathiyaar was born to him, like Lakshmi herself. Even in her toddling age, Punithavathiyaar spoke in endearing, lisping words that expressed her profound love and devotion to Lord Shiva. She grew up like a beautiful sprout nurtured by her love for Shiva. Whenever she played with other girls, she would speak only of Lord Shiva. As she grew, she reached the age of womanhood. Therefore, in accordance with her lineage, Punithavathiyaar was betrothed and married to Paramadattan, the son of Nidhipathi, who lived in Nagapattinam, a town of ancient lineage. Punithavathiyaar was the only daughter of her father. Therefore, Punithavathiyaar's father made arrangements for her to live in Karaikal itself, rather than going to Nagapattinam with her husband. Dhanadattan, Punithavathiyaar's father, built a beautiful house nearby and provided immeasurable wealth. Paramadattan also lived with Punithavathiyaar in this manner. There, Paramadattan engaged very successfully in trade. Punithavathiyaar, Paramadattan's beloved wife, also lived her life in such a way that her love for Lord Shiva's feet blossomed into a great passion. In addition, she diligently practiced the virtues of domestic life. When devotees of Shiva came to Punithavathiyaar's house, she would offer them good food, and with great affection, she would give them gold, jewels, clothes, and whatever else those devotees desired. She lived her life with a profound awareness of Lord Shiva's feet. One day, when some of Paramadattan's business associates came to see him, they gave him two mangoes. Paramadattan took the mangoes and sent them home with instructions to deliver them. Punithavathiyaar received the two mangoes sent by her husband and kept them in the house. At that time, a devotee of Shiva arrived at Punithavathiyaar's house, feeling very hungry. Seeing the condition of the Shiva devotee, Punithavathiyaar, with a mother's heart, decided to feed him, thinking that he would not be able to bear his hunger until the main meal was prepared. At that moment, the main meal was not yet ready; only the sweet dish (Thiru Amudhu) was available. Therefore, she decided that the first priority was to feed the hungry devotee. Without further thought, she served the sweet dish to the devotee and brought one of the two mangoes that her husband had sent, offering it to the Shiva devotee with joy. The holy devotee, weakened by old age and intense hunger, ate the sweet dish and the mango offered by Punithavathiyaar, praised her actions, and departed happily. After this, in the afternoon, Punithavathiyaar's husband,

Paramadattan, came home, bathed, and prepared to eat. Punithavathiyaar had also prepared good food and curries appropriately. She offered all of them to her husband. In addition, since she had given one of the two mangoes sent by her husband to the Shiva devotee, she placed the remaining mango for her husband to eat. While eating, Punithavathiyaar's husband, who had eaten the very sweet mango, asked, "Isn't there another such fruit? Bring that here too." Ammaiyaar went as if to fetch the second fruit, but then she became distressed, remembering that the fruit had already been given to the Shiva devotee. She went to Lord Shiva and pleaded with him. She felt Lord Shiva's feet with her hands. Immediately, a very sweet fruit fell into her hand. She brought it with great joy and placed it before her husband for him to eat. Punithavathiyaar's husband ate that fruit. He was delighted, finding its taste superior to nectar. Immediately, he asked Punithavathiyaar, "This is not like the mango you gave me before. This mango has a very unique taste. This mango is rare to obtain in all three worlds. Where did you get it? How did you get this fruit?" Punithavathiyaar was troubled about what to say. This was because the mango was a great grace bestowed upon her by the Lord. It is customary not to reveal the great grace bestowed by the Lord to others. Therefore, she should not speak of it. However, a chaste wife must tell the truth when her husband asks. She could not remain silent without telling the truth. Thus, she was distressed, trembling, and bewildered. Then, she somehow made a decision, thinking that it was her duty to tell the truth exactly as it happened, no matter what. She worshipped the Lord and told her husband how the mango came to her. Her husband, upon hearing this, could not understand its true nature. Furthermore, her husband said, "If this is Lord Shiva's grace, then bring another mango like this by Lord Shiva's grace and show it here." Ammaiyaar moved away from him, praised the Lord, and called out to Him, "Oh Lord, if you do not now bestow another mango as my husband has asked, then the truth I have already told my husband will become a lie." At that moment, by the grace of Lord Shiva, another mango appeared in Punithavathiyaar's hands. She gave it to her husband. She placed it in her husband's hand. Her husband took it with wonder. But he could not eat the mango that had come into his hand. Before he could eat it, the mango disappeared from his hands. Seeing this, he was bewildered, frightened, and mentally disturbed, and he considered Ammaiyaar a divine being. Therefore, he decided in his mind that it was not appropriate to live a domestic life with such a miraculous divine woman. He resolved to somehow separate himself from this divine woman, Ammaiyaar. With this hidden intention, without telling anyone openly, he waited for a suitable opportunity. At that time, the merchant announced, "I will now go overseas by ship, engage in trade in foreign lands, and bring back great wealth." His relatives and associates arranged a ship for him. With the necessary goods, Punithavathiyaar's husband boarded the ship and set sail. He traveled to foreign countries, traded all the goods, accumulated immense wealth, and then boarded the same ship again.

He arrived at a port city in the Pandya kingdom. There, he organized all the goods he had earned and made them flourish, beginning to trade there. He married the daughter of a merchant living in that same town. While living this second marriage, a daughter was born to him. He named this daughter after his first wife, Punithavathi Ammaiyaar. He recognized Punithavathi Ammaiyaar as his family deity whom he worshipped, and so he gave her name to his daughter. While he was staying there, Karaikal Ammaiyaar, knowing that her husband, who had gone abroad for trade by sea, had not returned for a long time, waited for him there. At the same time, she continued to perform her Shiva dharmas. During these days, a relative of Ammaiyaar heard the news that Paramadattan was living with great wealth in that city in the Pandya kingdom. Immediately, he sent another relative to ascertain the truth of the news and confirmed it. Then, deciding that it would be good to take Ammaiyaar to where the merchant was, they placed Ammaiyaar in a palanquin, covered her with a screen, and with her companions and relatives, traveled for many days until they reached near that city. They arrived near the city where Paramadattan was living. They sent word to Paramadattan that his wife had arrived. Upon hearing this, the merchant, filled with fear, brought his second wife and the daughter he had with her to where Punithavathi Ammaiyaar was. Paramadattan, his second wife, and their daughter, all three, fell at Punithavathi Ammaiyaar's feet and worshipped her. Worshipping thus, Paramadattan said to Ammaiyaar, "I live by your grace. That is why I have named my daughter after you." He bowed down and said this humbly. Ammaiyaar, seeing her husband worshipping her in this manner, stood aside with fear, among her relatives. All the relatives were astonished, bewildered, and amazed, and they asked Paramadattan, "Why are you worshipping your wife?" To this, he replied, pointing to Ammaiyaar, "She is not an ordinary human being. She is a great deity. I have realized that. That is why I left her, then married a second time, and named this daughter born to me after her. That is why I too fell at her feet and worshipped her. You too should worship her. She is a divine woman." Hearing all these words spoken by the merchant, Ammaiyaar praised the feet of the Lord, and with a focused mind, she made a request to Lord Shiva. "Oh Lord, I can no longer bear the burden of the flesh of this beautiful body that I have carried for him until now. Therefore, please remove all this flesh and bestow upon me the form of a ghoul, praised by the bhoothaganas (demi-gods) there." Thus, Ammaiyaar made her request to Lord Shiva. At that very moment, by the grace of Lord Shiva, as Ammaiyaar had wished, all the beautiful flesh on her body withered away. Ammaiyaar became a skeletal figure, a ghoul form praised by heaven and earth. A shower of flowers rained everywhere. Divine drums (Deva Dundubhi) resounded. Devas and sages rejoiced. Bhoothaganas danced in jubilation. All the relatives, fearing, bowed and moved away. At that time, with the singular wisdom that dawned upon Ammaiyaar, she sang praises to the Lord, and with the thought, "I am one of the good ganas who worship the Lord's feet," she composed and sang the Arputha Thiruvanthathi. She also composed the Thiru Irattai Mani Malai, praising the glories of the Lord.

With a surge of profound spiritual awareness and abundant divine grace, she desired to worship Mount Kailash and set off towards the north. All who saw Ammaiyaar approaching in her ghoul form were astonished, frightened, and fled from the place. They exclaimed, "Ghoul! Ghoul!" describing the form they saw exactly as it was. Hearing this, Ammaiyaar thought, "The Lord knows me. That is enough for me. What does it matter in what form I appear to these ignorant people?" With this thought, she continued, crossing all the northern lands with the speed of thought, and quickly reached near Mount Kailash. She decided that she should not step on Mount Kailash with her feet. Therefore, avoiding walking with her feet, she ascended Mount Kailash by walking on her head, joyfully climbing the mountain. At that moment, the divine gaze of Parvati, the daughter of the Himalayas, fell upon Ammaiyaar. Ambikai, astonished, asked the Lord, "What is this devotion of the skeletal body that ascends here by walking on its head?" The Lord, looking at Ambikai, said, "She who comes is our Ammai (mother/devotee). She has obtained this glorious form by her own request." At that moment, Ammaiyaar also arrived nearby. As soon as she arrived, the Lord uttered the single word "Ammai" for the salvation of all worlds. Hearing this, Ammaiyaar cried out "Appa!" (Father!) and fell at the Lord's feet, then rose. The Lord asked, "What boon do you desire from us here?" Ammaiyaar replied, "I desire eternal, unchanging, blissful love. I desire not to be born again. If, however, there is another birth, I desire never to forget You. And when You dance, I wish to be beneath Your feet, singing joyfully." The Lord granted her wish, saying, "In the south, in the sacred place of Thiruvalangadu in Pazhayanur, you shall witness our great dance and remain there forever, singing our praises with joy." Ammaiyaar worshipped the Lord, descended from Kailash, and with great devotion, immediately reached Thiruvalangadu by walking on her head. There, upon seeing the magnificent form of the Lord dancing, whose feet reached the heavens, she began with "Kongai Thirangi" and composed the Mootha Thirupathikam, praising the sacred dance associated with that holy place, and remained there. On that day, she also composed the pathikam "Etti Ilavam" with the thought of the Lord's divine dance, singing about the sacred dance. Thus, Ammaiyaar received the blessing of forever remaining in the shade of the Lord's raised foot. Praises to the feet of Karaikal Ammaiyaar. This is the brief history of Karaikal Ammaiyaar. Now, let us continue to contemplate her history in detail. Tiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 02 — Karaikal Ammaiyaar

Tiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 487, Tirunindra Sarukkam, Karaikal Ammaiyaar Puranam. In the Tirunindra Sarukkam, the fourth Puranam is that of Karaikal Ammaiyaar. In the Periya Puranam, Karaikal Ammaiyaar Puranam is the 24th Puranam. In the fourth verse of the Thiruthondathogai belonging to the Tirunindra Sarukkam, Sundaramurthy Swamigal has sung, "Perumizhalai Kurumbarkkum Peyarkkum Adiyen" (I am a devotee of Perumizhalai Kurumbar and Peyar). Accordingly, following the Puranam of Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar, is the Puranam of Karaikal Ammaiyaar, who is referred to as Peyar. The section that describes the life history and character of Karaikal Ammaiyaar, who was praised by the name Peyar, is the Karaikal Ammaiyaar Puranam. Sekkizhar Peruman sings the Puranam of Karaikal Ammaiyaar in 66 verses. The greatness of Karaikal Ammaiyaar cannot be fully expressed. Sekkizhar Peruman, stating that he would begin to describe it to some extent, laid the foundation for the Karaikal Ammaiyaar Puranam in the concluding verse of the Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar Puranam, and then continued with the Karaikal Ammaiyaar Puranam. Firstly, in the Thogai Nool, Karaikal Ammaiyaar is sung by Sundaramurthy Swamigal as "Peyarkkum Adiyen." Its meaning is, "I am also a devotee of Ammaiyaar, who is known and praised as Peyar." Sundaramurthy Swamigal sings "Peyarkkum Adiyen" because Ammaiyaar obtained the form of a ghoul by requesting it from the Lord. Ammaiyaar herself has referred to herself as "Karaikal Pey" in her sacred verses. Because of this too, Sundaramurthy Swamigal sings "Peyarkkum Adiyen." Generally, it is Sekkizhar Peruman's tradition to name the Puranam after the name used by Sundaramurthy Swamigal in the Thogai Nool. If he sings "Perumizhalai Kurumbarkkum," Sekkizhar Peruman would name it "Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar Puranam." But here, in the Thogai Nool, Sundaramurthy Swamigal sings "Peyarkkum Adiyen." So, strictly speaking, it should have been named "Peyar Puranam." However, Sekkizhar Peruman has titled this Puranam "Karaikal Ammaiyaar Puranam," referring to Peyar as Karaikal Ammaiyaar. The birth name of Karaikal Ammaiyaar is Punithavathiyaar. Sekkizhar Peruman did not name this Puranam "Peyar Puranam" or "Punithavathiyaar Puranam," but "Karaikal Ammaiyaar Puranam." This is because Karaikal Ammaiyaar had the greatness of being called "Ammai" (mother/devotee) by the Lord. That is why it is named "Karaikal Ammaiyaar Puranam." Even when laying the foundation in the concluding verse of the Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar Puranam, saying "Let us speak of the greatness of Karaikal Ammai," Sekkizhar Peruman referred to her as "Karaikal Ammai." Similarly, in the concluding verse of the Karaikal Ammaiyaar Puranam, he concluded the Puranam of Karaikal Ammaiyaar with the name "Ammai," saying "Geetham Munpaadum Ammai, Geetham Munpaadum Ammai." Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman started the Puranam with "Karaikal Ammai," and concluded it with the name "Ammai," and also titled this Puranam "Karaikal Ammaiyaar Puranam." The reason for this is that Lord Shiva called her "Ammai." In the Saiva tradition, if one generally refers to "Ammaiyaar," it refers to Karaikal Ammaiyaar. That is why, even though Sundaramurthy Swamigal sang "Peyarkkum Adiyen," Sekkizhar Peruman titled this Puranam "Karaikal Ammaiyaar Puranam." Now, let us consider the Vagai Nool, Thiruthondar Thiru Anthathi, where Nambiyandar Nambi has sung about Ammaiyaar. Nambiyandar Nambi has composed the 28th verse. That verse is as follows: "Namban Thirumalai naan midhiyen,"

"Enru thal irandum umbar misai thalaiyal nadanthEra Umai nagalum Sempon oruvan en Ammai enapettraval Sezhumthen Kombinuku Karaikkalinil meya kulathaname." This is that sacred verse. Its meaning is as follows: The sacred mountain where the Lord resides is Mount Kailash. Therefore, resolving not to step on it with her feet, Karaikal Ammaiyaar ascended Mount Kailash by lifting both her feet and walking on her head. At that time, Umaiyammaiyar (Parvati) looked at Karaikal Ammaiyaar and smiled, saying, "What is this love of hers? What is this love of hers?" And Lord Shiva, whose body is like pure gold, said, "She who comes is my Ammai." Thus, Karaikal Ammaiyaar received such a great blessing from the Lord. Nambiyandar Nambi praises such a great Ammaiyaar as "our family treasure who resided in Karaikal, like a flourishing honey-laden branch." Now, let us contemplate Ammaiyaar's sacred Puranam through the Viri Nool, Periya Puranam. In the first verse, Sekkizhar Peruman extensively sings about the greatness of the city of Karaikal where Ammaiyaar was born, and the greatness of the lineage in which Ammaiyaar was born. We know that this is Sekkizhar Peruman's tradition. "Manamigu tharmathin vazhinindru vaaymaiyinil Oonamil seer peru vanigar kudi thuvanri Ongupathi Koonal valai thirai sumanthukondEri mandu kali Kaanal misai uzhavu valam perugu Thirukkaraikkal." Sekkizhar Peruman first sings about the greatness of the city of Karaikal where Ammaiyaar was born, and the greatness of the lineage in which she was born. Karaikal is a port city. It is a city where maritime trade flourished. It is a city that has the greatness of being the birthplace of our Karaikal Ammaiyaar. It is a place where the glory of Sakambari Devi shines. Therefore, it is also known as Sakambaripuram. The Lord there is named Kailasanathar. The Sanskrit Brahmanda Purana describes the glories of this city. Even in the Brahmanda Purana, references to Karaikal Ammaiyaar's history are found. In it, Dhanadattan's wife's name is Dharmavathi, and Ammaiyaar's name is Bhoothavathi. The Pandya kingdom's city where her husband settled is mentioned as Kanakkalam, near the confluence of the Thamirabarani river with the sea, according to the Brahmanda Purana. Besides this, some other details are also found. This Karaikal is a place located in the Chola kingdom. Currently, it is part of the Union Territory of Puducherry. It is a port city. Here, Ammaiyaar's temple and idols are beautifully and newly constructed. The place where Ammaiyaar attained the form of a ghoul (Bhoothagana) and went to Kailash is in the Pandya kingdom. But the place where Ammaiyaar received the mango, that incident happened in the city of Karaikal. Therefore, Karaikal is the place where the incident of her offering food with a mango to a devotee and then, at her husband's request, receiving a miraculously sweet fruit by divine grace, took place, demonstrating her divine nature. Therefore, the Mango Festival is celebrated with great splendor in Karaikal. This Mango Festival is a festival that every devotee must see and celebrate. These are some notes about the city of Karaikal. Such a city of Karaikal is a city filled with sea water. Here, the seas carry conches with their waves and scatter them in the nearby backwaters. Sekkizhar Peruman has mentioned only conches in the verse. Therefore, we should also include other maritime resources like pearls, corals, etc., in our understanding. Karaikal is such a city rich in maritime resources. Maritime trade also flourishes very well.

Therefore, this city was inhabited by many people of the Vaishya community. The lineage in which Ammaiyaar was born was also a merchant lineage. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman sings about the greatness of that lineage in this first verse. Let us continue to contemplate. Tiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 03 — Karaikal Ammaiyaar

Tiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 488. Tirunindra Sarukkam, Karaikal Ammaiyaar Puranam. Sekkizhar Peruman, beginning the Karaikal Ammaiyaar Puranam, first sings about the prosperity of Karaikal city, and then about the greatness of the merchant communities, the Vaishya people, living in Karaikal city. It is Sekkizhar Peruman's tradition to begin the Puranam of a Nayanar by systematically singing about the country where the Nayanar was born, its special features, the city's special features, the resources of that country and city, and the special features of the lineage and family in which the Nayanar was born, before commencing the Nayanar's history. Accordingly, Sekkizhar Peruman, after singing about the greatness and resources of Karaikal city, sings about the greatness of the merchants living there. Sekkizhar Peruman sings about the merchant community as "Manamigu tharmathin vazhinindru vaaymaiyinil oonamil seer peru vanigar kudi" (the great merchant community of impeccable glory, living by the path of honorable dharma and truth). The merchant community stands by honorable dharma. Dharma means righteousness. Manam means honor. Manamigu dharma means honorable dharma. The commentator Parimelazhagar, in his commentary on Thirukkural, explains what honor (perumai) means: "never to fall from one's position, no matter what situation arises, and if by divine will one falls, not to live." Thus, the merchant community possesses such honor in upholding dharma. Furthermore, it is this merchant community that provides goods to the other three divisions and helps sustain this world. This is because it is natural for a particular region in this world to be rich in certain resources. Other regions may not have those resources. Therefore, it is the merchant community that ensures the even distribution of abundant goods produced in one region to other parts of the country through trade, making all goods available to all people. They do this as a matter of dharma, justice, and righteousness, and they possess this greatness. Not only do they adhere to righteousness for the welfare of the world, but they also act with compassion. "Kolluvathuvum migaipadaathu, koduppathuvum kuraipadaathu" (Neither taking excessively nor giving deficiently) is from Pattinappalai. This illustrates the ethical conduct of the merchant community. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman sings that they stand by honorable dharma. What is that path? Our legal texts prescribe six duties for merchants, the Vaishyas. Just as Brahmins have six duties, kings, merchants, and Vellalas also have six prescribed duties. Among these, the duties for merchants include studying their scriptures, performing sacrifices, giving charity, engaging in agriculture, protecting cows, and trade. These six are the dharmas for merchants. Tholkappiyam also mentions these. What is this dharma? It is a dharma whose primary principle is non-violence, sprouted from the seed of compassion. The merchant community inherently possesses this. Furthermore, here, "vaaymai" (truth) refers to moral conduct. These great merchant people not only adhere to the path of dharma but also excel in moral conduct. They consistently follow these principles without deviation or error. As stated in the Vedas, "Dharmam chara, satyam vada" (Practice dharma, speak truth), they speak the truth and live according to dharma. Sekkizhar Peruman refers to this community as "Perunkudi" (great lineage). Perunkudi refers to the sub-divisions within the merchant communities, such as Ippar, Kavippar, Perunkudiyar, etc. There were also many other types of great merchant people, such as Koolavanigar (grain merchants) by trade, and Senaivanigar (army merchants) by profession. In such a prosperous Karaikal, in such a glorious merchant community, lived Dhanadattan, who was the head of that community. "Dattan" was a traditional name given to merchants in earlier times. Here, Dhanadattan, a very wealthy merchant, lived as the head of the merchant community in Karaikal city. Due to the immense austerities Dhanadattan performed in all his previous births, a glorious daughter, referred to as Thirumadanthai, was born to him. It is true that such children, who are devoted to Shiva and excel in Shiva-consciousness, can be born only due to austerities performed in previous births. Dhanadattan was such a person who had performed austerities. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman sings that Thirumadanthai was born to him, referring to the birth of our Karaikal Ammaiyaar. Sekkizhar Peruman sings that Thirumadanthai was born. Thirumadanthai means Parashakti, the goddess of liberation. Thirumadanthai is commonly understood as Lakshmi, meaning she was born like the goddess of wealth. However, "Thiru" refers to the liberation (mukti thiru). Therefore, Sivakavimani Ayya interprets Thirumadanthai here as Parashakti, the goddess of liberation. How is our Karaikal Ammaiyaar comparable to Parashakti? Karaikal Ammaiyaar was worthy of being called "Ammai" by Lord Shiva himself. That is why Sekkizhar Peruman sings that Parashakti, the goddess of liberation, was born. "Vaayum manamum kadantha manonmani Peyum gananum perithudai penpillai Aayum arivum kadantha Aranukku Thaayum magalum nal tharamum aame" This is from Thirumandiram, Fourth Tantra, Eighth Division, Adhara Adheyam, verse 24, composed by Thirumooladeva Nayanar. Therefore, in accordance with such words, Ammaiyaar was worthy of being called "Ammai" by Lord Shiva. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman sings in this manner, implying that only Shakti is comparable to Ammaiyaar. Similarly, at a later time, he explains this by saying, "Thamarai thavisin vaikumm thanithiruvenna" (like the unique Thiru residing on a lotus seat). If we take Thirumadanthai to mean Mahalakshmi, we can also understand that Mahalakshmi, worshipped in the northern gate of Shiva puja, is worshipped as a manifestation of Shiva Shakti. Thus, Thirumadanthai was born to Dhanadattan. Punithavathiyaar, of immense beauty, was born. "Vangamali kadarkaraikalin kan vaazh vanigar thangal kulathalaivanar dhanathathanar thavathal Angavarpaal thirumadanthai avatharithal ena vandhu Pongiya perazhagu miga punithavathiyar piranthar." Sekkizhar Peruman frames the verse thus. Punithavathiyaar, with overflowing great beauty, was born. Her beauty was that of Thirumadanthai, the abode of beauty, hence it was immense beauty. It was a beauty that overflowed more and more. "Nitham manalar niramba azhagiyar" (The eternal Lord is supremely beautiful) is from Thiruvachagam. Thus, Punithavathiyaar was born. Punithavathiyaar is the sacred name given to Ammaiyaar by her parents. Punithavathi is that sacred name. Punithavathi means one who purifies this world through her love. Therefore, we can see that the sacred name Punithavathi is both a meaningful name (karanappeyar) and an arbitrary name (idugurippeyar), a meaningful arbitrary name (karana iduguri). Furthermore, Punithavathi consists of five letters and is also structured as the sacred five letters (Thiruainthezhuthu). Such a glorious Punithavathiyaar was born there. Now, Sekkizhar Peruman sings about Punithavathiyaar's growth. Let us continue to contemplate. Tiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 04 — Karaikal Ammaiyaar

Tiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 489, Tirunindra Sarukkam, Karaikal Ammaiyaar Puranam. Karaikal Ammaiyaar was born into a great merchant family. Ammaiyaar was born as the lamp of that family, illuminating the entire merchant lineage. Because of Ammaiyaar's birth and grace, that entire merchant family, both past and present, was illuminated by this glory. Thus, Ammaiyaar, who was born, grew up. From the time her tender feet began to walk, Ammaiyaar worshipped Lord Shiva with her mind, speech, and body. Ammaiyaar had performed great austerities in her past. The austerities she performed in all her previous births, the habit of worshipping the Lord, came with Ammaiyaar when she was born into this world, continuing seamlessly. Therefore, from the very first day of Ammaiyaar's birth, she worshipped the Lord with her mind, speech, and body. Yet, Sekkizhar Peruman sings that Ammaiyaar worshipped the Lord from the day she began to walk. This is because Sekkizhar Peruman, having described Ammaiyaar's birth here, immediately, with the intention of praising and worshipping Ammaiyaar's feet, sings that she has been serving Lord Shiva from the time she began to walk softly. This implies that worshipping Ammaiyaar's feet shows the path to Lord Shiva's feet. From the time Ammaiyaar began to speak, she praised and glorified the Lord. All these words, which had accumulated in Ammaiyaar's sacred mind, overflowed and emerged as words and expressions. "Pirandhu mozhi payindra pinellam kadhal sirandhu nin sevadiye sernthen" (After birth and learning to speak, my love intensified, and I reached your red feet) is the first verse of Ammaiyaar's Arputha Thiruvanthathi. Sekkizhar Peruman takes Ammaiyaar's own words as internal evidence for Ammaiyaar's Puranam and sings in this manner. She had practiced and accustomed herself to serving the Lord's feet for many days. She had practiced words suitable for expressing that devotion. "Palloozhi kalam Aranan archithal nallarivu satre naguma" (If one worships Arana for many eons, good wisdom will blossom a little) is a sacred saying. Here, Sekkizhar Peruman refers to Ammaiyaar's devotion to the Lord as "Paniyannivar kazharkku adimai pazhagi" (having accustomed herself to serving the feet of Him who wears serpents). "Paniyannivar" means Lord Shiva, who wears serpents. Why does Sekkizhar Peruman mention this here? Because Ammaiyaar, when singing about the Lord in her Thiruvanthathi, has often sung about this aspect, referring to Him as "Paambu alangara Paran" (the Lord adorned with serpents). Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman also mentions here that she accustomed herself to serving the feet of Lord Shiva, who wears serpents. Ammaiyaar has sung about Lord Shiva, who wears serpents, in many places. Thus, in many places in Arputha Thiruvanthathi, Ammaiyaar sings about the Lord wearing serpents as ornaments. That is why Sekkizhar Peruman also sings here that Ammaiyaar, from the day of her birth, worshipped the Lord, stating "Paniyannivar kazharkku adimai pazhagi" (she accustomed herself to serving the feet of Lord Shiva, who wears serpents as ornaments). As Ammaiyaar grew through various stages of life, her father diligently performed all the special rituals appropriate for each stage. He did all this to the delight of all the relatives. From lullabies to bathing rituals, he performed all the celebrations and ceremonies appropriate for girls at each stage, according to tradition. He did all this to make the relatives happy. This is because the ethical text states, "Selvarkku azhagu sezhunkilai thaanguthal" (The beauty of the wealthy is to support their flourishing kin). Thirukkural also states, "Sutrathar sutrapada ozhugal selvan than petrathal petra payan" (To live surrounded by relatives is the benefit a wealthy person gains from their wealth), in the chapter on "Sutranthalal," verse 524. The meaning is that the benefit a wealthy person gains from their wealth is to live in harmony with their relatives. Therefore, one must protect relatives. Especially, wealthy people must protect their relatives, as stated in ethical texts. Dhanadattan, Ammaiyaar's father, lived accordingly. Therefore, he made it a principle to act in a way that pleased his relatives. Furthermore, in the rituals of childbirth and upbringing, relatives should gather and perform these auspicious ceremonies. Thus, Ammaiyaar's father diligently performed all the ceremonies, celebrations, and special rituals appropriate for Ammaiyaar at each stage, to the delight of all the relatives. Sekkizhar Peruman also praises these rituals as "Thirupperugum seyal" (actions that augment sacredness). All the rituals performed for Ammaiyaar were actions that caused the sacredness of liberation (mukti thiru) and Shiva-consciousness to flourish. Thus, as these rituals were performed according to tradition, Ammaiyaar grew up like a blossoming sprout of love and beauty. This refers to both her inner and outer state. Love and beauty grew together. Love signifies the higher state of a being. Beauty signifies the worldly state. Therefore, Ammaiyaar grew excellently in both aspects. Moreover, she grew in such a way that her love matured and blossomed. That is why Sekkizhar Peruman places love first, singing that beauty grew along with love. In Ammaiyaar's history, both of these contribute to the outcome of her story. However, the blossoming of beauty, that maturity, is later shed by Ammaiyaar. The maturity of love, which grew into a ghoul form, became the cause of the outcome of her story, remaining forever at the feet of the Lord. Ammaiyaar's love was a love that, having completely renounced worldly attachments, remained in a humble state towards Lord Shiva. Thus, love and beauty grew together with Ammaiyaar. Whenever she played with other girls, Ammaiyaar would speak only of Lord Shiva's sacred words. When Shiva's devotees came while she was playing in the street, Ammaiyaar would worship them. Even at that young age, she not only praised Lord Shiva but also praised Shiva's devotees. Sekkizhar Peruman describes this as "Vandal payilvanavellam valarmathiyam punaindha sadai Andarpiraan thiru vaarthai anaiya varuvana payindru Thondar varil thozhudhu" (She practiced all that came to her as the sacred words of the Lord of the celestials, whose matted hair is adorned with the crescent moon, and worshipped devotees when they came). Sekkizhar Peruman refers to all of Ammaiyaar's words as "Thiru vaarthai" (sacred words). He does not simply say "vaarthai" (words) but "Thiru vaarthai." This is because all of Ammaiyaar's words were about the glories of Lord Shiva and the glories of Shiva's devotees. Therefore, they become sacred words. Previously, all that Ammaiyaar spoke were lisping words overflowing with mental love. But as she grew further, all the words Ammaiyaar spoke were refined words that revealed the inner meaning, the glories of Lord Shiva and the glories of Shiva's devotees. That is why they become sacred words. Furthermore, a section of Thiruvachagam is titled "Thiru Vaarthai." Thus, Ammaiyaar's actions of speaking about the greatness of Shiva's devotees, speaking about the greatness of Lord Shiva, and worshipping Shiva's devotees when they came while she was playing in the street, all illustrate the ultimate principles of Saiva Siddhanta, which are the worship of devotees and the worship of the Lord. Therefore, the implication here is that from the day of her birth, these two forms of worship were evident in Ammaiyaar and matured. Thus, Ammaiyaar, who grew up, reached the age, the stage, for marriage. "Nallai ena uruppu noolavar uraikkum nalam nirambi Malku peru vanappu meekkoora varum maatchiyinal Illigava paruvathil ivargal marabinukku erkum Thol kulathu vanigar magal pesutharku thodanguvar." Sekkizhar Peruman continues to sing. How did Ammaiyaar grow? She grew very well, with the characteristics described in the science of body features (Samudrika Lakshanam), reaching the age of marriage. Sekkizhar Peruman refers to this age as "illigava paruvam" (the age of not leaving the house). This is the age when a woman's femininity is fully developed. When a girl reached this age, in those days, our ancestors practiced and protected the custom that she should not go out of the house. This was one of the good practices adopted by our ancestors. Tamil Akapporul texts refer to this as "Ircherivithal" (confining to the house). However, this good practice has now been abandoned in the name of modernity. This is becoming the cause of many evils. Sivakavimani Ayya writes that this is very regrettable. Therefore, when Ammaiyaar reached this marriageable age, her father, Dhanadattan, sought a suitable groom for her, in accordance with their family tradition, to arrange her marriage. In ancient times, it was customary to choose a bride or groom from a family that matched in tradition, lineage, family, and customs. Therefore, Dhanadattan also sought a groom who possessed all these qualities, suitable for their merchant family. Hearing this, Nidhipathi, who lived in Nagapattinam, sent elders as messengers to arrange the marriage of Dhanadattan's daughter to his son. It is our tradition for elders, mature in wisdom and experience, from the groom's family to approach the bride's family to initiate marriage talks. The one who sent them was Nidhipathi, a very wealthy merchant from Nagapattinam. He sent elders to ask for Dhanadattan's daughter for his son's marriage. These wise elders, who came to Dhanadattan's house, told Dhanadattan, the girl's father, that he should give his daughter in marriage to Nidhipathi's son. They asked him to give his daughter in marriage to Paramadattan, the son of Nidhipathi, who comes from Nagapattinam. When making such a request, it is customary to announce the groom's name, his father's name, their lineage, and so on. Thus, these wise elders announced them properly. They also explained that the traditions, lineages, and families of both were very compatible. When Dhanadattan heard this from the wise elders, he realized that this groom was suitable for his tradition, lineage, family, and everything else, and he agreed to give Ammaiyaar in marriage. Chapter 05 — Karaikal Ammaiyaar

Tiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 490, Tirunindra Sarukkam, Karaikal Ammaiyaar Puranam. Ammaiyaar's father agrees to give Ammaiyaar in marriage to the son of Nidhipathi from Nagapattinam. The elders who had come as messengers convey this to Nidhipathi. Hearing this, Nidhipathi was overjoyed. The moment he heard this news, Nidhipathi rejoiced as if the marriage had already taken place. Nidhipathi makes all the necessary arrangements to perform this marriage for his incomparable son. "Manam isaintha naalolai selavittu mangala naal anaiya Vathuvai thozhilgal aanavellam amaiyavithe Inar alangal mainthanaiyum manavaniyin ezhil vilakki Panai murasam ezhunthaarppa Karaikkal pathi pugunthar." This is the next verse composed by Sekkizhar Peruman. Thus, since the bride's family agreed to the marriage, the groom's parents send a wedding invitation (naalolai) to the bride's family. This "naalolai" is a letter written to fix the wedding date. The groom's family would write this and send it to the bride's family. As the auspicious day approached, the groom was adorned with wedding ornaments. After doing this, the groom's family, with auspicious musical instruments resounding, arrived in the city of Karaikal. Thus, the groom, his parents, and other relatives, who had arrived in Karaikal, entered Dhanadattan's beautiful house. "Alimidai thar dhanathathan ani maadathtul pugundhu Thelitharu nool vithi vazhiye seyal muraimai seythamaitthu Thaliradi men nagai mayilai thadhavizh thar kaalaikku Kalimagizh sutram potra kaliyanam seytharkal." This is the next verse composed by Sekkizhar Peruman. As the groom entered Dhanadattan's house, all the rituals prescribed by the Sivagamas, which are to be performed before the marriage, were carried out there. After all these rituals were completed, our Ammaiyaar, Punithavathiyaar, with her gentle smile and peacock-like gait, was married to Paramadattan, who was like a bull adorned with a garland of blossoming flowers, with the relatives joyfully praising them. Thus, the marriage of Punithavathi Ammaiyaar and Paramadattan took place auspiciously. In this verse, Sekkizhar Peruman compares Ammaiyaar to a peacock and Paramadattan to a bull. Ammaiyaar has a peacock-like gait, and Paramadattan has the pride of a bull. This is a general, superficial meaning. However, it is said that a bull will be startled if it sees a peacock. It is said that a bull will be afraid if it sees a peacock. Similarly, later in this story, Paramadattan, startled and frightened by our Ammaiyaar, leaves her. Sekkizhar Peruman subtly alludes to this aspect of the story here. Furthermore, peacocks and bulls are incompatible species. Therefore, their marriage turns out to be an incompatible one. Sekkizhar Peruman also subtly alludes to this here. Sivakavimani Ayya notes that the incompatibility of peacocks and bulls as species is also mentioned in astrological texts. "Mangalama mana vinaigal mudithu iyalbin vaigunaal Thangal kudikkoru puthalvi aathalinaal dhanathathan Pongoli neer nagaiyinil pogame kanavanudan Angan amarndhu inithirukka animadam marungu amaithaan." This is the next verse. Thus, the auspicious marriage takes place well. All the rituals that are to follow the marriage are also completed successfully. Punithavathi Ammaiyaar is the only daughter of Dhanadattan. Dhanadattan has no other sons or daughters. Our Ammaiyaar is the only daughter of his lineage. Therefore, after marriage, it is customary for the wife to go and live in her husband's town, in her husband's house. But here, our Ammaiyaar is the only daughter of Dhanadattan. Therefore, unwilling to part with his daughter, he arranges for a good house in Karaikal itself, where our Ammaiyaar and Paramadattan can live happily. No matter how wealthy a woman's father is, it is customary for her to go and live with her husband in his town after marriage. It is very rare and exceptional for her to stay in her parents' town. Even if she has to stay in her parents' town, it is better to live separately with her husband, maintaining a separate household. These were the good traditions prevalent in those days. Therefore, Dhanadattan, who was overjoyed by giving his daughter in marriage, gave immense wealth. Paramadattan, the son of Nidhipathi, who possessed incomparable greatness, also happily settled in that separate house and engaged in trade, suitable for his lineage and tradition. He excelled in that field and became very prominent. Thus, Paramadattan's domestic life there was going very well. Punithavathiyaar, with beautiful hair, whose love for the feet of Lord Shiva, who rides the bull, was a great passion, continuously increased day by day. Punithavathiyaar also engaged in Shiva dharma and lived well, without any shortcomings in it. Therefore, Punithavathiyaar's love for the Lord and the virtues necessary for domestic life combined, and these two, without interruption, led to a very good domestic life. Sekkizhar Peruman explains in a verse how Ammaiyaar's father made arrangements for Ammaiyaar and Paramadattan to live a domestic life. After making these arrangements, Sekkizhar Peruman describes Paramadattan's domestic life and his excellent engagement in trade in one verse. Here, Sekkizhar Peruman describes Ammaiyaar's nature and character in two verses. In the first verse, Sekkizhar Peruman sings about the glory of Ammaiyaar's sweet domestic life. In the next verse, he sings about how she performed Shiva dharma and Shiva service. Therefore, he has dedicated two verses to Ammaiyaar. This is because Ammaiyaar's father's actions and Ammaiyaar's husband's actions remain within the worldly realm. But Ammaiyaar's conduct is a path that leads to the higher state of the soul, related to Shiva-consciousness. Therefore, due to its special nature, Sekkizhar Peruman sings about Ammaiyaar's actions in two verses: one for her domestic life with her husband, the lord of her body, and another for her great life with Lord Shiva, the lord of her soul, which is superior to the former. Paramadattan flourished in trade, increasing his wealth. His wife also lived very well in domestic life, according to domestic dharma. As the saying goes, "Kadhal iruvar karuthu orumithu aadharavu pattathe inbam" (When two lovers are of one mind and supported by each other, that is happiness), they lived without deviating from the glory of domestic life. But what Paramadattan did and earned was worldly wealth. What Ammaiyaar did was the love for the Lord's feet, which blossomed into a form of love. What Paramadattan did was not wealth, but worldly prosperity. What Ammaiyaar did was the wealth of love for the Lord's feet. Sekkizhar Peruman sings how Ammaiyaar performed Shiva service. "Nambar adiyaar anainthal nalla thiru amudhu alithum Semponnum navamaniyum sezhunthukilum muthalana Tham parivinaal avarkku thaguthiyin venduva koduthum Umbar piraan thiruvadi keezh unarvu miga ozhugu naal." Sekkizhar Peruman frames the verse thus. Lord Shiva is desired by all beings. If such Shiva's devotees come to Ammaiyaar's house, she offers them good sacred food. With her affection, she gives them gold, precious gems, rich clothes, and other things they need, according to their worth. How does Sekkizhar Peruman frame the verse here? He sings, "If Shiva's devotees come to her house, she offers them good sacred food." This is because Ammaiyaar's husband, Paramadattan, was not so deeply devoted to Shiva. If he had been devoted to Shiva's service, he would have gone and invited Shiva's devotees to their home and offered them sacred food. But he was not one to act in that manner. Therefore, it was not possible for Ammaiyaar to go out, seek Shiva's devotees, invite them, and offer them sacred food. So, when Shiva's devotees came to her house, Punithavathiyaar gave them what they needed. Ammaiyaar was one who diligently practiced and lived by the virtues of domestic dharma without fault. Therefore, Ammaiyaar did not have the nature of going out of the house on her own to seek out and invite devotees. That was also not proper. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman subtly indicates that if devotees came to her house, she would offer them good sacred food and do what they needed. Furthermore, Sekkizhar Peruman sings that Punithavathiyaar gives gold and precious gems to the devotees. Devotees, however, do not desire gold and gems. They consider gold and ordinary stones to be the same. But why does Ammaiyaar give them gold and gems in this manner? It is so that those devotees can give them to others who come to them in need. Therefore, for that purpose. Moreover, Punithavathi Ammaiyaar is one who perceives the needs of the devotees and gives them what they want even before they ask. Thus, by performing service to devotees and giving them what they needed, Punithavathi Ammaiyaar attained a heightened awareness and love for Lord Shiva's feet. By giving to Shiva's devotees, Ammaiyaar's love for the feet of Lord Shiva, the master, increases more and more. Trade is about giving one thing and receiving another. This is trade. Here, Ammaiyaar gives food and gold, etc., to the devotees. As a result, Ammaiyaar's love for the feet of the Lord increases. Therefore, the cause is giving food and gold, etc., to the devotees. The effect resulting from that cause is the increase of love for the feet of the Lord. Thus, this quality of Ammaiyaar, honoring devotees, becomes the cause of the events in Ammaiyaar's story. It also leads to Ammaiyaar's state of being forever at the feet of the Lord. Let us continue to contemplate. Tiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 06 — Karaikal Ammaiyaar

Tiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 491, Tirunindra Sarukkam, Karaikal Ammaiyaar Puranam. We have seen how Sekkizhar Peruman explained the various ways Karaikal Ammaiyaar performed Shiva's service. Now, Sekkizhar Peruman continues to sing: "Pangudaiya neriyinkan payil Paramathathanukku Maanganigal orirandu vanthanainthar silar koduppa Aangavai thaan munvaangi avar vendum kurai aliththe Eengu ivatrai illathukku kodukka ena iyampinaan." This is the verse composed by Sekkizhar Peruman. It is the 16th verse in Karaikal Ammaiyaar Puranam and the 1732nd verse in Periya Puranam. One day, while Paramadattan was living by engaging in trade, some people came to see him. They gave two mangoes to Paramadattan. Sekkizhar Peruman sings, "Maanganigal orirandu vanthanainthar silar koduppa" (some people came and gave two mangoes). When referring to the mangoes, he mentions "orirandu maanganigal" (two unique mangoes). Here, "or" (one/unique) implies uniqueness. Furthermore, there is another meaning that hints at the events of the later story, which are worth remembering. Sekkizhar Peruman did not just sing "two mangoes" but "two unique mangoes." This is because those two mangoes were not going to be used in the same way. One was to be given to the husband, and the other to a Shiva devotee. Therefore, these two mangoes had different qualities and served different purposes. That is why Sekkizhar Peruman sings "orirandu maanganigal." Moreover, one of these two mangoes served as food for a Shiva devotee once, and for the husband another time, and then for a third time, it appeared as a sign of divine grace. We can also take this as a hint here. The people who came to see Paramadattan gave these mangoes, and he received them. These visitors had a request. They had come to Paramadattan seeking the fulfillment of that request. Therefore, to fulfill their request, they lovingly offered those mangoes to Paramadattan. It is our custom to offer fruits and other items when making a request to elders, kings, or deities. The same custom is followed here. Paramadattan then gave these mangoes to his servant and sent him to deliver them to his house. This indicates that his place of business was different from his home. This has also been a tradition among merchants. "Kanavan thaan varavithutha kani irandum kaikkondu Manamaliyumm malarkkoonthal maatharaar vaitthatharpin Pana aravam punaintharulum paramanaar thiruthondar Unavin migu vetkaiyinaal oruvar manai utpugunthar." This is the next verse composed by Sekkizhar Peruman. Thus, the husband sent two mangoes, and Punithavathiyaar, with her fragrant, flower-adorned hair, received them and placed them aside. At the same time, a devotee of Lord Shiva, who wears a hooded serpent, entered her house with intense hunger. Sekkizhar Peruman mentions the Shiva devotee entering the house as "Paramanaar thiruthondar unavin migu vetkaiyinaal" (a devotee of the Lord, with intense hunger). "Paramanaar thiruthondar" means a devotee of Lord Shiva. It also implies that the Lord himself came as a devotee. Even if we take it to mean that a Shiva devotee arrived, performing Maheswara Puja with true devotion, feeding Shiva devotees, is equivalent to feeding Lord Shiva who resides within them. It is feeding Lord Shiva himself. Lord Paramashiva himself is the one who eats from within them. That is why the mango eaten by him not only reappeared as a miraculously sweet fruit but also reappeared when Paramadattan asked for it again. Thus, the Shiva devotee arrived at Ammaiyaar's house with intense hunger. One should not eat food many times without hunger. One should also not eat food due to a mere desire for food. These are bad habits that are detrimental to health and longevity. It was the custom of our ancestors to eat only after being thoroughly hungry. That is why it is mentioned here that the devotee, who was extremely hungry, came to eat. Ammaiyaar, realizing this, thought, "Seeing the state of this true devotee, I must quickly feed him to appease his hunger." Sekkizhar Peruman sings about the Shiva devotee entering Ammaiyaar's house as "oruvar manaiyul pugunthar" (someone entered her house). Paramadattan was at his place of business, and Ammaiyaar was alone at home. Therefore, he sings that the devotee entered the house where Ammaiyaar was alone. Similarly, in the Siruthondar Nayanar Puranam, a similar situation arises. Siruthondar is not at home. His wife, Thiruvengattu Nangaiyar, is alone at home. Therefore, the Lord, who came as Uthrapathiyar, refuses to enter the house where only a woman is alone and waits under an Aathi tree until Siruthondar arrives. But here, in Ammaiyaar's Puranam, Sekkizhar Peruman sings that even though Ammaiyaar was alone in the house, the Shiva devotee who came entered the house. This is because the visitor was extremely hungry. According to the proverb, "Pasi vanthida pathum paranthu pogum" (When hunger comes, ten things fly away), to convey the nature of hunger, he entered the house, forgetting all traditions and customs due to intense hunger. This is the first reason. The second reason is that Ammaiyaar is the mother of Paramanaar, the mother of Lord Shiva himself. This is Ammaiyaar's inherent nature. Therefore, there are no rules or restrictions for a child to go to its mother. Because of this too, the devotee, who was Lord Paramanaar himself, even knowing that Ammaiyaar was alone, entered as a child goes to its mother without considering time or circumstance. The divine play of Viruthakumara Balan also comes to mind here appropriately. Sekkizhar Peruman continues to sing: "Vedangal mozhintha piraan meytthondar nilai kandu Naathan than adiyaarai pasi theerppen ena nanni Paathangal vilakka neer munn alithu parikalam vaitthu Etham theer nal virunthaaga in adisil oottuvaar." This is the next verse. Ammaiyaar sees the state of the true devotee of Lord Shiva. She approaches the true devotee, who has come with intense hunger, thinking, "I will appease the hunger of my Lord's devotee." She first offers water to wash his feet. This is called "paathiyam." This is the first hospitality to be offered. After performing all such hospitalities and seating the devotee, she places the "parikalam." "Parikalam" refers to a plate for eating, such as a banana leaf. If a banana leaf is not available, other leaves can be used as an alternative. This is stated in our scriptural texts. Using metal plates and other vessels for eating is a later, uncivilized practice. Thus, she places the banana leaf as a "parikalam." And she offers "etham theer nal virunthaaga in adisil oottuvaar" (sweet food as a good, faultless feast). "Etham theer" first means to alleviate the hunger of the eater. Later, it also means that by the devotee eating and his hunger being appeased, this Maheswara Puja will also alleviate the birth-sickness of Ammaiyaar, the one who feeds him. Therefore, it becomes a good, faultless feast. It becomes medicine for both hunger and birth-sickness. Therefore, it becomes a good, faultless feast. The word "virundhu" itself means novelty. That is, a "virundhu" (guest) is one who comes unexpectedly, without prior notice of time or day. Welcoming such guests and offering them sacred food is "virundhu." In Sanskrit, this is called "athithi." "Athithi" means one who comes without looking at the "thithi" (auspicious day). They may be related or unrelated. The Thirukkural, an ethical text, mentions "Thenpulathar, Deivam, Virundhu, Okkal, Thaan" (ancestors, deities, guests, kin, oneself), where "virundhu" is mentioned simply as a guest. But here, Sekkizhar Peruman refers to it as "nal virundhu" (good feast/guest). This is because performing hospitality, which is a duty of householders, is a "pasu dharma" (dharma of the soul). Hospitality is a "pasu dharma." Although it yields good results, it can also be a cause for rebirth. But the "virundhu" Sekkizhar Peruman refers to here is "nal virundhu." This is "pathi punniyam" (merit related to the Lord). Unlike the previous "pasu dharma" of hospitality, this "pathi punniyam" of "nal virundhu" removes rebirth and provides a path to liberation. That is why it becomes a good, faultless feast. Sekkizhar Peruman continues to sing: "Kari amudhu angu uthavaathe thiru amudhu kaikooda Verimalar mel thiru anaiyaar vidaiyavan than adiyaare Peral ariya virunthanaal perru ithan mel illai enum Arivinarai avar amudhu seyvathanukku aatharippaar." The time when the devotee arrived at Punithavathiyaar's house was neither morning nor noon. It was an intermediate time. Therefore, at that time, only "thiruvamudhu" (sacred food, i.e., cooked rice) was ready in Ammaiyaar's house. The other curry dishes, "kari amudhu," which are eaten with sacred food, were not yet ready. Therefore, it was a pre-noon time when only sacred food (rice) was cooked, and no other curries were prepared. Yet, Ammaiyaar, who is like "Verimalar mel thiru anaiyaar" (the sacred one residing on the fragrant lotus), desired to support the devotee. Earlier, when Sekkizhar Peruman sang about Punithavathiyaar as "Thirumadanthai," we contemplated the meaning of "Thirumadanthai." This phrase is related to that. Therefore, so that her devotion to the Lord is never blamed, Ammaiyaar stands praying to the Lord for another mango. As she prayed, a mango came into Ammaiyaar's hands by the grace of Lord Shiva. The mango given by divine grace earlier was for Paramadattan to taste. But this mango, given now, was given as a witness to divine grace. It was not given for Paramadattan to eat. He doubted divine grace and did not believe it. Therefore, this mango came to prove the truth of divine grace and to show a witness to the Lord's grace. As this mango appeared in Ammaiyaar's hands, she takes it and gives it to Paramadattan. Paramadattan, greatly astonished, takes the fruit, wondering what a miracle it is. Earlier, Ammaiyaar served the mango that came by divine grace on Paramadattan's plate for him to eat. That was for his hunger and taste. But now he asks for it as a witness to divine grace. Therefore, Ammaiyaar gives it into her husband's hands instead of serving it on a plate. As soon as Paramadattan takes the mango in his hands, the mango disappears from his hands. Paramadattan, seeing the fruit he had taken disappear, becomes afraid. What fear did he have? He feared that it was improper to live with Ammaiyaar anymore. He feared that he was not worthy to live with Ammaiyaar. He realizes that Ammaiyaar is a divine being, a goddess. Therefore, he decides that it is no longer possible to live with Ammaiyaar, a divine being. He thinks that he is not worthy to live with her and decides to leave Ammaiyaar. Should he leave Ammaiyaar? Can he just abandon Ammaiyaar? Paramadattan is currently living in Karaikal. He lives by doing business in the house provided by Ammaiyaar's father on Ammaiyaar's behalf. Therefore, in this situation, he cannot remove Ammaiyaar from his life. So he decides that he must leave that place. But he was afraid to tell anyone else about all these events. Therefore, without telling anyone else about this, he kept it to himself and decided that he must somehow leave Ammaiyaar. Having decided this, he waited for the right moment. Until then, even though he lived in the same house with Ammaiyaar, he did not live with her. Therefore, to leave Ammaiyaar, he tells his relatives that he needs to go to sea and do business in foreign countries. In those days, it was a very common practice for men to leave their wives to earn money. Therefore, even when Paramadattan said this, no questions arose from his relatives or others. He is a businessman, a merchant. So his relatives all think that he intends to go to many countries across the sea to do business. Therefore, they arrange for the necessary ship. They build a ship and hire skilled people like sailors and those proficient in operating ships. Paramadattan loads the ship with all the goods that are very rare to find in the countries he intended to visit for business. Thus, having loaded the ship with goods, on an auspicious day, Paramadattan worshipped Varuna, the sea god, and set sail. "Seek wealth even by crossing the seas" is an ancient saying by Avvai Moothati. Therefore, in those days, doing business by sea and earning money was a common practice among Tamils from ancient times. In ancient Tamil texts like Pattinappalai and Silappathikaram, it is found how Tamils sailed ships to other countries, did business, and prospered greatly. We can see all those details. Moreover, it is customary for one who goes to earn money to go alone. It is not customary to take one's wife along. Therefore, for this reason, Paramadattan leaves Ammaiyaar in Karaikal and sets sail with the ship. Even in those days, Tamils were skilled in shipbuilding, ship operation, and cross-sea trade, which is evident from this. In that period, merchants prospered by taking our country's goods across the sea to foreign countries and selling them there, and by bringing goods that were very rare in Tamil Nadu from foreign countries and selling them here. This practice continued in Tamil Nadu until the time of our Sekkizhar Peruman. Thus, Paramadattan crosses the sea. He goes to all the countries he intended to do business in, conducts business very well, acquires many goods there, and then boards the ship again and arrives at a coastal town in the Pandya country. This town is mentioned as "Kanakalam" in the Sanskrit Brahmanda Purana. We can infer that it is near the confluence of the Thamirabarani River with the sea. Sekkizhar Peruman does not specify which of the many port cities in the Pandya country this town is. Sekkizhar Peruman refers to it as "a town in the country of spreading waters" in his verse. "Padarpunal" can be rephrased as "punal padar" (spreading waters), meaning a place where rivers flow and meet the sea. Thus, we can understand it as a town located where the Thamirabarani River meets the sea. There were many such coastal towns in the Pandya country. Thirumandira Nagar, now called Thoothukudi, Sethukkarai, Kulasekharan Pattinam, etc., are all port cities in the Pandya country. It is an assumption that this town might have been one of them. Thus, Paramadattan, having arrived at a port town in the Pandya country, starts doing business there with all the goods he had acquired. He also married the daughter of a good merchant there. He lives again, having remarried in a port town in the Pandya country, leaving Punithavathiyar Ammaiyaar in Karaikal. But he lives ensuring that no one knows that he was already married and had deceived Punithavathiyar Ammaiyaar. In all other aspects, he lives a very honest and good life, but he hides the deception he committed against Ammaiyaar from everyone else and lives very happily with the woman he married a second time. Therefore, he was already married, and the Ammaiyaar he married was a divine being. Fearing the divinity of that divine woman, he could not live with her, so he came to a foreign country to do business, told that lie, separated from Ammaiyaar, then did business, came to the Pandya country, lived there, hid all this, and then remarried and lived this way. Thus, the merchant Paramadattan, who did very well in business in the Pandya port, had a daughter born to him as a result of his domestic life with his second wife. Sekkizhar Peruman sings this as: "He obtained a girl, like a lamp of increasing light, with great difficulty." He was delighted to name that girl Punithavathi, Ammaiyaar's sacred name. A girl, like a lamp of increasing light, came to Paramadattan. That girl came to Paramadattan. He first named that girl Punithavathi, Ammaiyaar's sacred name. This event is the original cause for the Saivite devotees to joyfully name their children Punithavathiyar, or Punithavathi, after Ammaiyaar. That is why Sekkizhar Peruman, who sings about this event, sings that "a girl, like a lamp of increasing light, came and arrived." Generally, every girl child is born to illuminate and make their lineage prosper. Especially if the first child is a girl, it is a worldly custom that the lineage will grow and prosper. Therefore, for these two reasons, Sekkizhar Peruman praises the girl child named Punithavathi as "a girl, like a lamp of increasing light." We will continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 11 — Karaikal Ammaiyaar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 496. Thirunindra Sarukkam, Karaikal Ammaiyaar Puranam. Paramadattan performed the auspicious naming ceremony for his daughter, giving her the sacred name of Punithavathiyar Ammaiyaar. Generally, for girls, marriage is considered the first ceremony. However, considering his wealth, he decided to perform naming and other ceremonies for his daughter. Thus, on an auspicious day, he named his daughter Punithavathi, Ammaiyaar's sacred name. This is because Paramadattan knew that Ammaiyaar was a divine being. Therefore, he thought that he was not worthy to live with Ammaiyaar and left her. So, thinking that his connection with Ammaiyaar should not continue, he performs this ceremony, praying, "I am performing a ceremony by naming my child after you. From now on, I wish to be without the connection of you as my wife." Just as people offer Pongal and worship the presiding deities of snakes and other creatures, praying, "May you not appear before our eyes anymore, may poisonous creatures like snakes not appear before our eyes," Paramadattan, following this minor deity worship method, prays to Ammaiyaar, "I wish to be without the connection of you as my wife," and names his daughter after Ammaiyaar. While this merchant was living there, at the same time in Karaikal, our Ammaiyaar continued to perform the dharmas of "pasu dharma" (duties towards creatures) and "pathi dharma" (duties towards husband). While living in household life, one must perform the worldly "pasu dharmas" and also the "pathi dharmas." Ammaiyaar had been performing both of these properly. This period must have been at least two years. In such circumstances, a close relative of Ammaiyaar hears that Ammaiyaar's husband is living in a port town in the Pandya country. Hearing this, and wishing to confirm it, he sends a close relative to investigate and report on the truth of this news. The relative who was sent goes there and confirms that Paramadattan is doing very well in business and is living with a second wife and a daughter. He then returns and informs Ammaiyaar's relative. Upon hearing this, Ammaiyaar's relatives are distressed. They suffer great sorrow. In this situation, the relatives decide to take Ammaiyaar back to her husband. In reality, Paramadattan should have been with Ammaiyaar; that is the norm. But instead, he is staying in another town and living with another marriage. This is truly a punishable offense. Leaving Punithavathi Ammaiyaar deceitfully and marrying again and living that way means that the merchant is guilty of deviating from his duty. Therefore, they could fight him, file a lawsuit, and even get royal punishment according to the law for this crime. These are actions that ordinary people would take. But Ammaiyaar's relatives are highly moral and have high ideals. Therefore, they decide that the wife should be where the husband is, and the wife should be with the husband; that is the custom of their lineage. Therefore, Ammaiyaar's relatives decide to take her to Paramadattan and begin their journey. They arrange this journey with great sorrow. This is because Ammaiyaar is very young. Therefore, they decide that she should not be separated from her husband. They arrange for a palanquin. They seat Ammaiyaar in the palanquin, and the relatives travel from Karaikal to that town in the Pandya country. "In the great palanquin, they seated the graceful, peacock-like woman, like the unique Thirumagal (Lakshmi) residing on a lotus seat. Surrounded by loving relatives, both men and women, and the sweet-voiced woman herself, they traveled a long distance." This is the verse composed by Sekkizhar Peruman. They carry the beautiful, peacock-like Punithavathi Ammaiyaar in the large, beautiful palanquin. They seat Punithavathi Ammaiyaar, like the unique Ambikai (Parvati) residing on a lotus seat, and arrange a curtain for the palanquin. Relatives, both men and women, surround her and travel. Here, Sekkizhar Peruman sings about Ammaiyaar as "the unique Thirumagal residing on a lotus seat." He sings about her as Thirumagal (Lakshmi) residing on a lotus flower. However, in this context, referring to Ammaiyaar as "the unique Thirumagal residing on a lotus" should not be interpreted as her being like Saraswati residing on a white lotus or Lakshmi residing on a red lotus. Ammaiyaar should be understood as Gnanaambikai (Goddess of Wisdom) residing on the thousand-petaled lotus, known as Sahasrara Padmam. We can recall here Thayumanavar's verse, "Nārani, the mistress of the mind, beyond qualities, the ultimate power, residing on the thousand-petaled flower." Thus, Ammaiyaar, seated in the palanquin, travels for many days and reaches the Pandya country, the land of beautiful Tamil. Karaikal is a town in a part of the Chola country. The distance between it and the Pandya country is very great. Therefore, they travel for days and reach the outskirts of the town where Paramadattan lives. Paramadattan hears that they have reached the outskirts of the town. He feels great fear in his heart. He is afraid. Just as he was afraid when the mango, which Ammaiyaar had brought by divine grace, disappeared from his hand, he is afraid now. Therefore, he takes his second wife and his child and comes to the place where Ammaiyaar is. Before Punithavathi Ammaiyaar enters his house, he decides that he must go and worship Ammaiyaar. So he comes with his wife and child to the place where Ammaiyaar is. This was the time when Paramadattan's daughter had started to walk. Therefore, these three people arrive at the place where Ammaiyaar is. As soon as they arrive, Paramadattan, his second wife, and their daughter all fall at Ammaiyaar's feet and worship her. As soon as he arrives, Paramadattan quickly falls at Ammaiyaar's feet and worships her. This is because Ammaiyaar has been separated from her husband for a long time. Therefore, they are now meeting after such a long separation. So, as a wife, before Ammaiyaar falls at Paramadattan's feet, he quickly falls at Ammaiyaar's feet and worships her. This is because, for Paramadattan, he sees and worships Ammaiyaar as a divine woman. However, the relatives are unaware of all these matters. Therefore, Paramadattan

Paramadattan bows at Ammaiyaar's feet and says, "I am living by your grace. I have named this daughter of mine after you," and prostrates himself many times on the ground, worshipping Ammaiyaar. By thus worshipping, he made everyone realize that he worships Ammaiyaar as a deity. Ammaiyaar and her relatives are bewildered, not knowing why he is worshipping in this manner. Ammaiyaar, seeing him fall at her feet and worship her, joins her relatives, standing aside where they are. Sekkizhar Peruman sings, "Seeing her husband worship, the beautiful creeper-like woman, along with everyone, stood aside with fear among the relatives." Here, Sekkizhar Peruman refers to Ammaiyaar as a "creeper-like woman." A creeper needs something to cling to and grow. Until now, Ammaiyaar had been clinging to her husband as her support. Now, with her husband falling at her feet, she cannot cling to that support and instead clings to the relatives nearby as her support. Sekkizhar Peruman uses the simile of Ammaiyaar being like a creeper to convey this. Ammaiyaar is now standing aside with her relatives, with fear. The relatives all ask Paramadattan, "Why are you falling at your wife's feet and worshipping her?" Paramadattan begins to answer. We will continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 12 — Karaikal Ammaiyaar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 497. Thirunindra Sarukkam, Karaikal Ammaiyaar Puranam. The relatives of Punitthavathi Ammaiyaar ask Paramadattan, "Why do you fall at your wife's feet and worship her?" Paramadattan replies, pointing to Ammaiyaar, "She is not a human being. She is not an ordinary person. She is a great deity. I have realized that she is a deity. That is why I have left her and come here. After this, I have even named my daughter after her. That is why I worshipped her golden feet. Therefore, you too should worship her in the same way." Paramadattan tells all the relatives, "Just because she is in human form, you should not think she is an ordinary human. If she is not human, then who is she? She is a divine birth. She is a divine being. But unlike minor deities who frighten people and cause harm, she is a great deity who does good. I have understood this. Therefore, you too should worship her and attain salvation, without being deceived into thinking she is a mere human woman. Your mistake was not worshipping her until now. You have not received any good by not worshipping her until now. From now on, realize that Ammaiyaar is a divine woman and worship her." Paramadattan said this to all the relatives and guided them. It is at this point, at this time, that the merchant openly reveals to everyone what he had been thinking in his mind. Until now, Ammaiyaar had lived considering this merchant as her husband. But the merchant had mentally distanced himself from Ammaiyaar. The day the third mango arrived, according to Ammaiyaar's wish, by the grace of the Lord, on that very day, he had left Ammaiyaar. But Ammaiyaar was unaware of his mental state. That is why Ammaiyaar had until now followed the worldly chastity and conduct. Upon hearing this news, Ammaiyaar realizes that her connection with him has also been severed. Upon seeing such a change in the merchant, Ammaiyaar praises the feet of the Lord who wears the Kondrai garland in His matted locks. As it is said, "Think of one thing," Ammaiyaar's thoughts do not wander elsewhere; she fixes her thoughts on the Lord and remains with a deeply absorbed feeling within. "The lamp that splits the lamp, lights the lamp, kindles the lamp within the lamp, and can explain the lamp's light, can attain the feet of the Lord of the lamp," says the Thirumandiram, in the ninth Tantra, under the section Gnanaodayam, the third verse. In this verse, as revealed by Thirumooladeva Nayanar, Ammaiyaar completely discards both "pasa gnanam" (knowledge of bondage) and "pasu gnanam" (knowledge of the soul), and unites solely with "pathi gnanam" (knowledge of the Lord), attaining a state of deep inner perception. From that state of awareness, Ammaiyaar says the following: "This merchant has adopted this principle. This merchant has left me. Therefore, that marital connection has been severed. Now, I shall cast off this burden of a body, made of beautiful flesh, which I have borne for him, and O Lord, in that world, please grant me the form of a ghoul that praises Your feet." She worships the feet of the Lord and wishes to cast off this body of flesh, asking the Lord to grant her the form appropriate for the ghoul hosts who praise the Lord in Kailash. "Since this man has adopted this principle, I shall now cast off this burden of flesh, beautiful and borne for him, and here, O Lord, grant me the form of a ghoul that praises Your feet, in a fitting manner."

Sekizhaar Peruman sings in the 49th verse of Karaikal Ammaiyaar Puranam, "She stood worshipping the feet of the Supreme Lord." In this verse, Ammaiyaar refers to Paramadattan as "this man" (ivan). This is because the connection as husband has been severed. Therefore, she refers to Paramadattan as "this man." Shiva devotees value and consider only Lord Shiva and His devotees as important. Ammaiyaar now stands in that exalted state where she considers nothing else important except Lord Shiva and His devotees. Therefore, she places Paramadattan among the ordinary living beings, who are not important, and refers to him in the singular as "this man." Since Ammaiyaar referred to him in the singular, Sekizhaar Peruman also, throughout the Ammaiyaar Puranam, has sung about Paramadattan in the singular as "this man." Ammaiyaar makes a request to the Lord, saying that she no longer wants this body of flesh that she has borne for him until now. Therefore, it is understood that Ammaiyaar had considered this body as a burden until now. The beauty of the body is evident through its flesh. If the flesh is removed from the body, or if the flesh wastes away, the body will appear as a skeleton to the world. Therefore, Ammaiyaar feels that she has borne this burden of flesh long enough, and has borne it for him long enough, and makes a request to the Lord. Generally, women beautify their bodies, adorn themselves, and wear beautiful clothes and ornaments for their husbands. This was the noble conduct cherished by our ancestors. Therefore, Ammaiyaar too, for the sake of her husband, had until now carried this beautiful fleshy body. Now that the connection as husband has been severed, Ammaiyaar decides that this fleshy body is no longer needed. Therefore, she asks the Lord to cast off this fleshy body here and grant her the form of a ghoul, like the ghoul hosts who worship the Lord in Kailash. Ammaiyaar now wishes for the form of a ghoul, a Shiva Gana, that praises the feet of Lord Shiva. This ghoul form is one of the ghoul hosts in Kailash. She requests the form of that ghoul host. Generally, the ghoul form that Ammaiyaar requested is not the ghoul form that wanders in this world, tormenting people with a body of air. The body of ghouls that wander in this world is called a body of air. This physical body is made up of the five elements. For the ghouls that wander in this world, among the five elements that make up their body, the other four elements disappear, and only air remains as their body. Therefore, these ghouls have bodies made of air, invisible to the eyes, and are bodies of bondage. But the ghoul that Ammaiyaar requested is not this. It is the form of the ghoul hosts, one of the Lord's hosts in Thirukailai. The form Ammaiyaar requested is a body where everything else in the human body disappears, and only the skeleton remains. This is because this form is capable of moving very quickly, like the wind. That form is a skeletal form where only the element of earth remains in abundance. Nevertheless, this ghoul form that Ammaiyaar requested and received is a pure Maya form, purified by removing impurities, like copper that becomes gold after removing its dross. But to the eyes of other people, it appears as a form of light. That is why others were afraid and ran away when they saw that body.

They worshipped. That is why Ammaiyaar, with this body, quickly traversed all the northern lands and went to Thirukailai Mountain. When climbing Thirukailai Mountain and when reaching Thiruvallangaadu, she avoided walking with her feet and walked with her head. The Lord Himself explains this ghoul form as a "form of great glory" to Uma Devi. With this ghoul form, Ammaiyaar sits in Thiruvallangaadu, beneath the Lord's uplifted foot, always filled with joy, singing hymns. It is with this ghoul form that Ammaiyaar composed the Thirupathigams and the Thiruvakkus like Irattai Manimalai and Thiruvandhadhi. Therefore, this ghoul form is a sacred form that possesses the external instruments like speech for singing poetry and music, and internal instruments like the mind, which Ammaiyaar requested. That form is a form of light. Ammaiyaar herself has sung about how she attained the ghoul form in her own sacred verses. The 86th verse of Ammaiyaar's Thiru Arputha Thiruvandhadhi is as follows: "Even if we attain it, we desire nothing else. Even if this comes to us or not, a little enlightenment, He who placed His eye on His forehead, we are one of His good ghoul hosts." In this verse, Ammaiyaar sings about the ghoul form she received, saying that the Lord has kept her as one of His good ghoul hosts. Similarly, whenever Ammaiyaar refers to herself, she calls herself "Karaikal Pei" (the ghoul of Karaikal). She declares this through her own sacred words. Because of this, our Sundaramoorthy Swamigal praises Ammaiyaar as "Peyar" (the ghoul) in his Thiruthondathogai. Ammaiyaar mentions this ghoul form she took in the first verse of Thiruvalangattu Mootha Thirupathigam: "With shrunken breasts and prominent veins, with hollow eyes and white teeth, with a sunken belly, a female ghoul with long, high ankles, a ghoul of Shiva, with long teeth, stayed, crying out in the dry forest, her matted locks spread in eight directions, her body chilled, dancing in the fire, that is the sacred Thiruvalangadu of our Father." In this sacred verse, she sings about herself as a female ghoul, describing her form: "a female ghoul with long teeth and high, long ankles, a ghoul of Shiva." Similarly, in the 11th verse of the same Thirupathigam: "The one without equal, ghouls gathered, hitting each other, dancing with joy, singing, the sweet woman arranged it. The ten Tamil verses sung by Karaikal Pei, the ghoul with matted hair, about the Father in beautiful Thiruvalangadu, those who master them will attain Shiva's path and enjoy bliss." In this verse, Ammaiyaar refers to herself as "Karaikal Pei." Furthermore, in the 11th verse of the second Thirupathigam of Thiruvalangattu Mootha Thirupathigam, Ammaiyaar refers to herself as "Karaikal Pei." That verse is as follows: "By the grace of the Lord who wears the crescent moon on His matted locks, who dances the sacred dance, who wears the snake around His waist, the ten songs of Karaikal Pei, the ghoul with fiery fangs abundant in the forest, if sung and danced to, sins will be destroyed." This is how the verse is structured. Thus, in the Thirupathigams composed by Ammaiyaar, she reveals that she took the form of a ghoul. Thus, our Ammaiyaar makes a request to the Lord, wishing for this ghoul form. Let us continue to contemplate the history of how the Lord granted Ammaiyaar's request. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 13 — Karaikal Ammaiyaar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam by Sekizhaar Swamigal, Thirunindra Sarukkam, Karaikal Ammaiyaar Puranam. Karaikal Ammaiyaar requests the Lord to grant her the form of a ghoul. Sekizhaar Peruman mentions this, saying, "She stood worshipping the feet of the Supreme Lord, asking that the ghoul form be fitting for her." Here, Sekizhaar Peruman has sung about the Lord as "Paramar" (the Supreme One). Although the Lord has many names, Sekizhaar Peruman uses "Paramar" here, saying, "She stood worshipping the feet of Paramar." When the physical husband, Paramadattan, whom Ammaiyaar had until now, left her, Sekizhaar Peruman uses the name "Paramar" here to indicate the Lord as the eternal husband of her soul, who will never leave her. Therefore, when Ammaiyaar requested the ghoul form, the Lord granted it. Sekizhaar Peruman sings about this in the following verse: "At that moment, by the grace of the Dancer in the hall, she, who was to receive what she desired, shed all the beauty of her flesh and became a skeletal body, a ghoul form that the heavens and earth worshipped." Thus, Sekizhaar Peruman describes it. As Ammaiyaar stood worshipping, by the grace of the Lord of the Dance in the hall, the awareness to attain the supreme path came to Ammaiyaar. Ammaiyaar received exactly what she desired. She shed all the flesh and the beauty that manifested through it from her body, and attained a ghoul form with only bones, becoming one of the Shiva Ganas. She received the form of a ghoul, one of the Shiva Ganas, worthy of worship by the heavens, the earth, and all other worlds. Ammaiyaar requested and received this for the sake of supreme awareness. Due to the intensity of that supreme awareness, Ammaiyaar received what she desired, exactly as she desired, at that very moment. How did she receive it? By shedding the fleshy body and the beauty that came with it, Ammaiyaar received a body that was a mere skeleton. This is the ghoul form. This is the form of a Shiva Bhuta Gana. This is the form Ammaiyaar desired. She received that form by the grace of the Lord. The form Ammaiyaar attained is a form worshipped by the heavens and the earth. Generally, when referring to worlds, they are called the upper world, the middle world, and the lower world. Here, Sekizhaar Peruman mentions "worshipped by the heavens and the earth." By mentioning "heavens," Sekizhaar Peruman refers to the upper worlds, and by "earth," he refers to the middle world. Instead of mentioning the lower world explicitly, he says "all worshipped." This "all" refers to the lower world. Sekizhaar Peruman says "all" to refer to the lower world in an auspicious manner, avoiding inauspicious terms. This is one of the unique characteristics of our Sekizhaar Peruman. Sekizhaar Peruman has composed this Puranam, the biography of a devotee, without using inauspicious language. This instance serves as another proof of that. Thus, Ammaiyaar requests and receives the ghoul form. Immediately, divine flower showers fall everywhere. Divine drums (Deva Dundubhis) resound. Devas and sages gather and rejoice. All the Shiva Ganas perform dances and songs, filled with joy. All the relatives who surrounded Ammaiyaar worship her, are afraid, and leave from there. Sekizhaar Peruman mentions this in the following verse: "Flower showers fell everywhere, the sound of divine drums filled all the worlds, and the great sages too rejoiced. All the Ganas danced with joy, and the countless relatives who stood before her worshipped her, were afraid, and left." Sekizhaar Peruman composes this verse, the 51st verse in Ammaiyaar Puranam. As soon as Ammaiyaar received the ghoul form, divine drums resounded. Whenever a manifestation of Shiva's grace occurs, divine drums resound on their own, without anyone playing them. We have seen this in many places in Periya Puranam. Sekizhaar Peruman sings that the sound of the divine drums filled all the worlds. The Devas and sages are very happy. All the Shiva Ganas perform the Kunalai dance. Kunalai dance is a type of dance performed with song when joy is exceedingly great. The ghoul Ganas, who are Shiva Ganas, perform it. All the relatives who stood before her worshipped Ammaiyaar, were afraid, and left. The phrase "stood before her" should be understood in terms of both time and place. Those who stood before her in time were the relatives who had been with Ammaiyaar until now. These relatives who stood before her until now now withdraw. If understood in terms of place, they stood around Ammaiyaar, in front of her. They saw the ghoul form Ammaiyaar had taken, were frightened, and stood aside, retreating. They worshipped her. Even though these relatives saw Ammaiyaar's ghoul form, they did not receive the benefit of seeing that sacred form; instead, they stood aside in fear. Therefore, Sekizhaar Peruman places them at the end of his narration. These relatives were those who came with Ammaiyaar from Karaikal and those who came with the merchant through his second wife. Thus, relatives came from both sides. These people worshipped the ghoul form Ammaiyaar received but did not benefit from it; instead, they retreated. Sekizhaar Peruman thus sings about everyone, even the relatives. But he does not sing about the merchant. This is because the merchant was already worshipping Ammaiyaar as a great deity. He continues in the same state even now. Therefore, Sekizhaar Peruman does not sing about the husband's state here. It can also be understood that he included him as one of the relatives and implicitly mentioned him. Sekizhaar Peruman continues to sing the next verse: "Then, with the unity of knowledge that arose, she composed the wonderful Thiru Arputha Thiruvandhadhi, singing with delight, 'I am one of the good hosts who praise the beautiful, red lotus feet of the Lord of Uma.'" Thus, as soon as she received the ghoul form, Ammaiyaar composes the Arputha Thiruvandhadhi. Sekizhaar Peruman sings about this in this verse. It begins with "with the unity of knowledge that arose." It should be understood as "with the unity of knowledge that arose and manifested." "Unity of knowledge" means knowledge that is completely filled with Shiva, where nothing else appears but Shiva. He sings that this unity of knowledge arose and manifested. "Bhavam" means origin. The prefix "ut" from Sanskrit grammar is added to the word "bhavam." "Ut" is a prefix that intensifies the meaning of the word that follows it. Therefore, "utbavithu" means "having arisen." The unity of knowledge, where nothing else appears but Shiva, completely filled with Shiva, that knowledge arises and manifests. What is unity of knowledge? It is a state where knowledge does not stand as three – "gnanam" (knowledge), "gnathru" (knower), and "gneyam" (known) – but only "pathi gnanam" (knowledge of the Lord), which is Shiva-gnanam, arises, and one's own self ceases to exist. Even before this, Sekizhaar Peruman had sung in verses 1764 and 1741 that Ammaiyaar stood with a unified mind, with intense awareness, and realizing with her mind, forgetting herself. However, now the beginning of that unity of knowledge arises and manifests. In those two instances, Ammaiyaar gave space in her mind to herself, her husband, and other existing things. But now, Ammaiyaar's state is one where there is nothing else in her mind, nothing in her thoughts, nothing in her intellect, except Shiva. Therefore, Sekizhaar Peruman sings "unity of knowledge." However, the "I" in "I am one of the good hosts" is an experience of Shiva's pervasiveness, where her individual soul-consciousness has ceased to exist. Thus, with the unity of knowledge that arose, at that very moment she received the ghoul form, she praises the lotus feet of the Lord. She composes the Arputha Thiruvandhadhi, saying, "I have become one of the good hosts who praise the lotus feet of the Lord." The Shiva Ganas of the Lord are those who only perform the act of praising the Lord's feet without forgetting them. Shiva Ganas do not perform any other actions. Ammaiyaar sings that she has become one of these Shiva Ganas. We can recall here how Thirunavukkarasu Peruman sings in Thiruvangamalai, "Will I be joyful, counted among the many hosts of the Lord, and go and be joyful beneath the sacred feet of the one who holds the small deer?" Ammaiyaar, saying "I am a servant to the servants," sings with joy about the blessing she received, becoming one of those good hosts, referring to the greatness of the devotees. "Who else will receive what the Father granted me? Alas!" says Thiruvachagam. "I have received knowledge that no one else has received beneath the sacred feet of the Lord of Shivaloka. Who else in the world will receive it?" We can recall here the ideas from the seventh verse of Thirupallandu composed by Sendhanar and other sacred verses. Ammaiyaar also sings about this in many places in Arputha Thiruvandhadhi. Therefore, Ammaiyaar's Arputha Thiruvandhadhi is a devotional work sung, immersed in the devotion of servitude to Lord Shiva. The name of this work is Arputha Thiruvandhadhi. The "Arputham" (wonder) here refers to the wonder of servitude. "Arputham" also means knowledge. Thus, Ammaiyaar composes Thiru Arputha Thiruvandhadhi. She composes Thiru Arputha Thiruvandhadhi at the very moment she receives the ghoul form. Sekizhaar Peruman continues to sing the next verse: "Having composed the well-researched Irattai Malai Andhadhi, with overflowing supreme awareness, she approached the great Kailash mountain of silver, where the Lord who burnt the three cities in the past resides, with abundant grace leading the way, and came by the path of worship." This is the meaning of this verse. Sekizhaar Peruman mentions that after composing Thiru Arputha Thiruvandhadhi, Ammaiyaar composes Thiru Irattai Malai. Irattai Malai is also known as Thiru Irattai Manimalai. After receiving the ghoul form, Ammaiyaar first composed Arputha Thiruvandhadhi, praising the power of that state. Following Arputha Thiruvandhadhi, she composed Thiru Irattai Manimalai, praising the glories of the Lord who granted her that ghoul nature. Where were these two compositions, these two devotional works, sung? They were sung near the boundary of the Pandya kingdom, the town where the merchant lived. We previously saw that this town might have been a coastal town in the Pandya kingdom. Similarly, it is noteworthy that there is a temple for Karaikal Ammaiyaar on the way from Thiruchendur to Kulasekarapattinam. Therefore, the merchant might have lived in one of these places. It is when she goes to see him there that this divine event happens to Ammaiyaar. Therefore, Ammaiyaar composes Thiru Arputha Thiruvandhadhi and Thiru Irattai Manimalai at the very same place and moment she received the ghoul form. Thus, praising the glories of Lord Shiva, she sets out to go to the silver Kailash mountain where Lord Shiva resides. Two devotees who desired to go to Thirukailai Mountain and have a vision of Kailash are Thirunavukkarasu Peruman, one of our religious preceptors, and our Ammaiyaar. Therefore, Sivakavimani Ayya, in his great commentary on Periya Puranam, recalls Thirunavukkarasu Peruman and Karaikal Ammaiyaar at this point, and narrates their Kailash pilgrimages and their visions of Kailash as two intertwined garlands. Let us continue to contemplate that. Thiruchitrambalam.

That astonishment led to fear. As fear arose, all those people ran away from that place as if escaping from an impending danger. But these people did not understand the true nature of this form. No. This is because the ghoul form that our Ammaiyaar received is the form of a ghoul, one of the Shiva Ganas. It is not the ghoul that ordinary people in the world fear. We have seen this in detail before. Thinking that her feet should not touch Thirukailai, from the moment she began to ascend Kailash Mountain, she stopped walking with her feet and walked on her head, upside down. Ammaiyaar begins to ascend Kailash Mountain. Therefore, it is understood that Ammaiyaar walked very quickly on her feet through the countries and paths from the town in the Pandya kingdom to the foot of Kailash Mountain. It is understood here that the ghoul form Ammaiyaar received helped her to quickly cover such a long distance. The ghoul form, unlike other bodies, does not have an abundance of the earth element but has an abundance of the air element. Therefore, according to the saying "wind speed, mind speed," this ghoul form helped Ammaiyaar to reach her destination very quickly. Ammaiyaar ascends Kailash Mountain on her head. Kailash Mountain is a sacred place. Therefore, thinking that it is not right to step on it with her feet, she walks on her head. Kailash is the mountain where Lord Shiva resides.

Therefore, its base is considered to be the feet of Lord Shiva. We must place our head at the feet of Lord Shiva. Therefore, Ammaiyaar begins to walk on her head. In accordance with the scriptural concept of "Thadalai" (foot-head), Ammaiyaar walks on Kailash Mountain with her head. It is forbidden to step on sacred places with one's feet. Accordingly, Ammaiyaar also walks on her head in Thiruvallangaadu, not with her feet. Thiruvathigai is a sacred place where Thirunavukkarasu Peruman performed service. Secondly, Sekizhaar Peruman refers to the Lord as "Soolapani" (one with a trident in hand). The trident is the trident that destroys the three impurities (Anava, Karma, Maya). The trident in the Lord's hand shows that Lord Shiva is the primeval one who removes the three impurities and grants complete knowledge. Therefore, it is understood that this sacred name "Ammai" of Karaikal Ammaiyaar is the best means to make us attain the nature of Shiva. The Great Lord graciously utters this sacred word for the salvation of all worlds. This word, the sacred name "Ammai" of Ammaiyaar, by worshipping this sacred name alone, this world will attain salvation. This is the path shown by the Lord Himself, the assurance given by Him. Worshipping that word means contemplating and uttering that word with mind and speech. Let us continue to contemplate what Ammaiyaar replies when the Lord calls her "Ammaiye." Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 16 — Karaikal Ammaiyaar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Part 501, by Sekkizhar Swamigal, Thirunindra Sarukkam, Karaikal Ammaiyaar Puranam. On Mount Kailash, the Lord addresses Ammaiyaar as "Ammaiye." When Ammaiyaar calls the Lord "Appa" here, what does this word "Appa" mean? It means the one who is the cause for the entire world to appear, exist, and then dissolve. This is the meaning of the word "Appa." "Oduṅki malattu uḷḷatām" is the first sutra of Sivagnana Botham. This world, having dissolved in Lord Shiva, the cause of dissolution, will reappear from Lord Shiva, the cause of dissolution, when it is created again. Therefore, the word "Appa" signifies that Lord Shiva is the cause for the entire world to appear, exist, and then dissolve. "The one who gave birth to all the worlds" is from Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's Thevaram, for the Thiruvettipakkam site. "He is the father and mother to all" is from Thirugnanasambandha Peruman Puranam, 69th verse. "I come into your creation, stand, and then dissolve back into you" is the implication of Ammaiyaar calling Him "Appa" here. The Lord, out of the love with which she cherished Him, bestows His grace by calling her "Ammaiye." But even that grace, that quality of being called "Ammaiye," came from the Lord. Therefore, "You gave me that quality too. So, you are the first one," Ammaiyaar says here through the word "Appa." In Thirunavukkarasu Peruman Puranam, Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, who is Aludaiya Pillaiyar, lifts Thirunavukkarasu Nayanar, who prostrated at his feet, and calls him "Appare." Thus, before Lord Shiva, who had graced her by saying "Ammaiye," Ammaiyaar prostrated at His feet, saying "Appa," and then rose. The Lord's grace in calling Ammaiyaar "Ammaiye" before Ammaiyaar called Him shows the abundance of His compassion. Not only that, but with the help of that gracious word, Ammaiyaar uttered the gracious word "Appa." The birth of that gracious word is also implied here. Sivakavimani Aiya offers a very subtle commentary on this sacred verse. First, it says, "I desire unending blissful love, and then I desire." First, Ammaiyaar desired unending blissful love. This should be understood in two ways: unending bliss and unending love. "Irravaadha" means imperishable, indestructible, eternal. Therefore, Ammaiyaar desires eternal bliss and eternal love. She desires it in the negative form. She desires "unending love" in the negative form. She uses this negative form to emphasize the ultimate reality. It is a poetic tradition that stating something in the negative emphasizes the meaning more than stating it positively. Accordingly, Ammaiyaar, who asks for eternal love and eternal bliss, states it as "I desire unending blissful love" in a negative form to convey the ultimate reality. Here, bliss and love are two inseparable qualities. "Bliss itself is my love" is from Thiruvasagam. "They rejoice in bliss through love" is from Periyapuranam, Thirukkootta Sirappu, 342nd verse. Therefore, bliss and love are eternally inseparable qualities. Why does she desire this love? Because this love is the foundation for all attainments. The foundation for all attainments is the love that souls have for the Lord. Therefore, Ammaiyaar first desires and obtains that love. And that love, too, should be eternal love, imperishable love, everlasting love, she desires and obtains. "I desire unending blissful love, and then I desire." After desiring and obtaining this love, she desires again. What does she desire? She desires "not to be born again." All people who have taken birth, if they truly wish for something, if they want to obtain a boon, it is the boon of "not being born again." That boon of "not being born again" is the boon that all great people have said is essential for people who suffer in birth to desire and obtain. Why do we come here by being born? To avoid being born again. This is a truth that all great people have unanimously shown. "To cleanse the impurity, the consciousness is the world-form" is from Thirunavukkarasu Swamigal's Thevaram, Fourth Thirumurai, 75th Thiruppathikam, seventh verse.

"O Avinashi, my Lord, I pray to You for no more births," is from Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's Thevaram, Thiruppukkozhiyur, also known as Avinashi, third verse. "He protected and saved me from being born again" and "Not to come back here and be entangled in karma and rebirth" and "He will cut off this ruined birth, and I will praise You" are the sacred words from Thiruvasagam, composed by Manikkavasagar Peruman. And countless other sacred words pray to the Lord, saying, "I do not want birth." "If you desire, desire not to be born again; other desires lead to non-desire" is the 362nd Kural from the chapter "Avar Aruthal" (Cutting off desire). "If I do not see my Lord and prostrate, and do not perform service with my hands, even if I attain the heavens, I do not desire it," and "I thought I would cut off birth" are the sacred words from Ammaiyaar's wonderful Thiruvanthathi. Since it is said, "If you desire, desire not to be born again," if one desires, that is the only thing to desire. Since it is said, "If you desire, that desire will lead to non-desire," "Desire non-desire from Him" is the 40th verse of Thirukalittruppadiyar. Thus, all great people have desired only "not being born again." Ammaiyaar here desires from the Lord, "I desire not to be born again." She continues to desire. "If there is rebirth again, I desire never to forget You." Why does Ammaiyaar mention "If there is rebirth again" here? Because first, Ammaiyaar desires not to be born again. The Lord also gives what the devotees desire. He will give what those who desire want. Therefore, the Lord will grant the boon of not being born again, as Ammaiyaar desired. But that will only be attained in due course. Until the time comes to attain that state of not being born again, one must be born to exhaust the karmic residues of past actions. Therefore, Ammaiyaar sings, "If by chance I am born again," and mentions it here as "If there is rebirth again." So, until the state of not being born again is attained, the accumulated karmas must be exhausted. Such births might occur again for the sake of karmic exhaustion. If such births occur, Ammaiyaar asks, "I desire never to forget You in all those births." We can recall Arjuna's tapas here. When Arjuna performed tapas desiring the Pasupatastra, and the Lord appeared before Arjuna, Arjuna then asked the Lord for liberation, or Mukti. So, the Lord was about to give Arjuna both the Pasupatastra, for which he had performed tapas, and then Mukti. But how does He give it? He tells Arjuna, "Now receive the Pasupatastra for which you performed tapas. The Mukti you asked for upon seeing Me, I will grant you in your next birth." And so, in the next birth, He caused Arjuna to be born as Kannappa Nayanar on Thirukalathi mountain and granted him Mukti there. Therefore, the Lord makes souls experience the remaining karmic accounts that need to be experienced and then grants them Mukti. The Lord grants the state of not being born again. Therefore, souls do not know the account of the remaining karmic residues that need to be experienced. That is why Ammaiyaar says, "If there is rebirth again," meaning, "if there is a birth," and thus asks that in all those births, no matter what kind of birth it is, "I desire never to forget You." Thirunavukkarasu Peruman's prayer, "Even if I am born as a worm, O Punnaiya, grant me the boon that Your feet never leave my mind," comes to our mind here. Therefore, Ammaiyaar desires that in any kind of birth, "I desire never to forget You." She desires "never to forget You."

What does "never" mean here? It means that no matter what the situation, whether in distress or weakness, whether living or dying, no matter what the birth, no matter what the time, she desires never to forget Him. This is the meaning of the word "never." She prays to the Lord that she should never forget Him. The state of not forgetting the Lord comes to us only if the Lord grants it. It can never happen through our own efforts. To make us realize this, Ammaiyaar asks the Lord Himself for the boon of not forgetting Him. "Showing illusion, showing birth, showing a mind that does not forget, showing the body" is from Thirunavukkarasu Swamigal's Thevaram, Thiruvarur Thevaram. Therefore, she asks, "If there is rebirth, I desire not to forget." Ammaiyaar does not ask for a specific kind of birth. This is because births are given according to one's karma. Therefore, Ammaiyaar does not ask for a specific birth. She asks that in any birth, she should not forget Him. Remembering and forgetting occur in all births. But the quality of not forgetting the Lord is the only one that will yield all the benefits and blessings of other austerities. Therefore, Ammaiyaar desires, "If there is rebirth, I desire never to forget You." She desires more. Now, the boon Ammaiyaar is about to ask for is the ultimate and final boon. Earlier, she desired not to be born again. If born, she desired not to forget. Beyond these two states, after the karmic exhaustion, she asks for the ultimate state she desires, right here. Even if she attains not being born again, as she desired earlier, or if she attains the state of not forgetting the Lord if born, ultimately, she desires to reside at the Lord's feet. To reside at the Lord's feet, singing, is the boon Ammaiyaar asks for now. When she asks this, she addresses the Lord as "Arava" and asks, "When You dance, I wish to be at Your feet." She addresses the Lord as "Arava." Dharma is the form of the Lord. Here, the meaning of Dharma is the grace that the Lord bestows on all beings and the justice with which He applies that grace according to each being. Thus, addressing the Lord as "Arava," she asks, "When You dance, I wish to be at Your feet, singing with joy." Why is it necessary to ask for this now? Because attaining not being born again, as Ammaiyaar desired earlier, or attaining not forgetting in any birth until that state of not being born again is achieved, is not within our control. These are all within the Lord's control. Therefore, Ammaiyaar considered this to be the best and ultimate boon: that no matter what comes, she should always remain at the Lord's feet, singing. The idea here is that if this boon is not obtained, if this boon is not received, then the previous two states are incomplete boons. "Hear the three principles of Mukti: the soul experiences pure experience" is from Unmai Vilakkam, 50th verse. "The soul itself is entitled to the experience of Shiva" is from Sivagnana Siddhiyar, Payaniyal, 11th Sutra, verse 10. Therefore, in accordance with what the scriptures say, Ammaiyaar, as the ultimate conclusion, desires the boon that she should always be singing at the Lord's feet when the Lord is dancing. The fact that she addresses the Lord as "Arava" amidst these boons and uses that name indicates her desire for Him to be within her.

Ammaiyaar desires to sing at the Lord's feet, to be singing forever and ever. This is the action of the Bhoothas (celestial beings) who sing along with the Lord's sacred dance even during the time of dissolution. These references are also found in Ammaiyaar's Thiruppathikams. Thus, Ammaiyaar asks for all these wonderful boons in this sacred verse. Sivakavimani Aiya writes in his commentary on this verse that it would be beneficial for every devotee of Shiva to recite this sacred verse daily. We will continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.

This is because, among all the compositions structured as Pathikams, Ammaiyaar's Thiruppathikams are the oldest in terms of time. Therefore, the Pathikams composed by Ammaiyaar are called Mootha Thiruppathikams. It is our Saivite tradition to refer to these two Thiruppathikams, now composed by Ammaiyaar, as ThiruvAlangattu Mootha Thiruppathikam One and ThiruvAlangattu Mootha Thiruppathikam Two. Among these two Thiruppathikams, the one beginning with "Kongai Thirangi" is placed as the first Thiruppathikam of ThiruvAlangattu Mootha Thiruppathikam. Even in Thiruvasagam, there is a Thiruppathikam called "Koyil Mootha Pathikam." In Chidambaram, which is called Koyil, two Pathikams were sung first, and because this Pathikam was sung first among those two, it is named "Koyil Mootha Pathikam" in Thiruvasagam. Similarly, here, Ammaiyaar's Pathikams are called Mootha Thiruppathikams. Sekkizhar Peruman refers to this Thiruppathikam sung by Ammaiyaar as a "good ancient Thiruppathikam." Thus, this Mootha Thiruppathikam sung by Ammaiyaar is compiled in the 11th Thirumurai. Generally, all the compositions by Ammaiyaar are compiled in the 11th Thirumurai. Ammaiyaar composed a total of four works. Among them, "Thiru Arputha Thiruvanthathi" and "Thiru Irattai Manimalai" are compositions she sang when she obtained the form of a ghoul in the city of Pandya country, as we saw there. Among the four compositions by Ammaiyaar, the two ThiruvAlangattu Mootha Thiruppathikams are those now composed by Ammaiyaar. Among them, "Kongai Thirangi" is compiled as the first Thiruppathikam, and the Pathikam beginning with "Etti Ilavam" is compiled as the second Thiruppathikam. Here, as mentioned by Sekkizhar Peruman, the Mootha Thiruppathikam beginning with "Kongai Thirangi," now sung by Ammaiyaar, is set to the tune of Nattapadai. "With shrivelled breasts, veins standing out, deep-set eyes, white teeth, a swollen belly, red lips, long protruding teeth, and long, high-heeled legs, a female ghoul, shrieking and drying up in the forest, with matted locks spread in eight directions, our Appan, whose body is cool, dances in the fire. His abode is ThiruvAlangadu." This is the first verse. Thus, Ammaiyaar sings the tenth concluding verse as: "The one without adornment, when the ghouls gather, striking each other, singing in a discordant manner, and the Lord dances, the Karaikal ghoul, who is like a tender sprout, sang these ten beautiful Tamil verses about the Lord of ThiruvAlangadu, who is our Appan, and those who master them will attain Shivagati and experience bliss." With this tenth verse, Ammaiyaar concludes this Thiruppathikam. According to the divine command received in Thirukailai, Ammaiyaar reached ThiruvAlangadu by walking on her head, to witness the sacred dance and sing at His feet. She worshipped that site, which was a great forest filled with banyan trees. There, she saw many ghouls singing, dancing, and playing musical instruments with joy. Thus, the Lord showed His sacred dance, where He raised His foot to the heavens, to Ammaiyaar, in the presence of all these ghouls. The place where this was seen is ThiruvAlangadu, and Ammaiyaar composed this Thiruppathikam, praising the site. It is characteristic of Ammaiyaar's Pathikams to praise the site by focusing on the sacred dance performed by the Lord. Worshipping and praising a site is one of the methods of worship. Sekkizhar Peruman also instructs us on this idea, saying, "When seeing the form of Him dancing in Alangadu, sing and praise the dance." Sekkizhar Peruman then continues to sing about the second Mootha Thiruppathikam composed by Ammaiyaar. Ammaiyaar, who had worshipped the sacred dance of Lord Shiva, adorned with fragrant Konrai flower garlands, with ever-increasing love and overflowing desire, then composed another Thiruppathikam, consisting of complete verses, starting with "Etti Ilavam Eegai" and concluding with "The Lord dances while drums beat and ghouls sing." In this verse, Sekkizhar Peruman refers to the Lord as "Mattavizh Konraiyinar" (one adorned with fragrant Konrai). Konrai is the Lord's sacred garland. It is the form of Pranava. Similarly, the sacred dance performed by the Lord is also the form of Pranava. Therefore, by singing "Mattavizh Konraiyinardam Thirukkootthu" (the sacred dance of the Lord adorned with Konrai flowers), Sekkizhar Peruman implicitly conveys that it is the sacred dance of Lord Shiva. Ammaiyaar had a great desire to witness this sacred dance.

She had a great fondness for it. As her desire to worship this dance grew and overflowed, that love became immense, transforming into wonder. In that state of mind, considering the great blessing she received by worshipping the Lord's sacred dance, Ammaiyaar composed the second Thiruppathikam. Through her description in this Pathikam, where she portrays the Lord Shiva, the beautiful one, dancing while all the ghouls, including herself, beat drums and sing, we understand Ammaiyaar's state of mind. "Etti Ilavam Eegai" is the beginning of this second ThiruvAlangattu Mootha Thiruppathikam. This second Thiruppathikam is set to the tune of Indalappann. "In the forest where Etti, Ilavam, Eegai, Surai, Karai creep everywhere, surrounded by the cremation ground, with Kalli trees, where intestines are caught, and scattered corpses lie, the ghouls with drum-like eyes beat drums, and the beautiful Lord dances while ghouls sing." This is the first verse in this Thiruppathikam. Ammaiyaar continues to sing, and in the 11th concluding verse, she composes as follows: "By the grace of the Lord who has the cool moon on His matted locks, who dances the swirling sacred dance, and who has a serpent tied around His waist, the Karaikal ghoul, with fiery fangs, sang these ten verses, and by singing and dancing, sins are destroyed." With this concluding verse, Ammaiyaar completes this Thiruppathikam. Ammaiyaar composes that in ThiruvAlangadu, surrounded by ghouls, the Lord dances in rhythm, with music and instruments playing. Here, the Lord shows this sacred dance to Ammaiyaar, and Ammaiyaar sings about the state she witnessed, so that the people of the world may know it as it is. The Lord is the first cause, the cause of dissolution. Ammaiyaar is in the form of a ghoul, one of the Lord's sacred hosts. Therefore, Ammaiyaar sees the Lord dancing, surrounded by ghouls. The place where the Lord dances is the cremation ground. There are trees like Vagai, Kalli, Eegai, etc. Among the ghouls living there are mothers and children. Jackals also live there. Owls and other birds sing there. The red fire lit for the rituals of the deceased serves as the lamp there. The ghouls gather, run, dance, and sing with joy. They sing the seven notes of music. They play many musical instruments. Thus, in the hot cremation ground, our Appan, with a cool body, dances with grace. That place is ThiruvAlangadu. This is the essence of this Thiruppathikam. Sekkizhar Peruman then composes the next verse. "Sekkizhar Peruman composes the next verse, the 65th verse, saying, 'How can one describe the immense glory of Ammaiyaar, who was graced by the Lord with matted locks holding the overflowing Ganga, who called her with sweet words, "Ammai," and who always remains at the feet of His sacred raised foot in His glorious dance?'" Lord Shiva is the one who holds the overflowing Ganga in His matted locks. He held that great flood like a small dewdrop in His matted locks and contained it. Such a Lord Shiva called our Ammaiyaar, Karaikal Ammaiyaar, with His sweet and gentle words, "Ammaiye." Our Ammaiyaar, of such great glory, always remains at the feet of the Lord when He performs His sacred dance. The Lord's foot is the raised sacred foot. The Lord's foot is the one that lifts souls from the mire of anava (ego) and places them at His feet, bestowing bliss. Thirunavukkarasu Peruman sings in Thevaram about this as "Eduttha Porpadham" (the raised golden foot). The raised foot is different from the planted foot. According to the Unmai Vilakkam verse, "Seek Mukti at the raised lotus feet," the raised foot is the foot that bestows grace. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman refers to it here as "Eduttharulum Sevadi" (the raised sacred foot that bestows grace). Here, the raised foot of the Lord is the foot that granted Ammaiyaar that irreversible state, that liberation. Our Ammaiyaar always remains at that sacred foot.

This state is the state that is equally attained by everyone who reaches the Lord's feet. However, this state attained by Ammaiyaar is special because she specifically requested, "I wish to be at Your feet when You dance." Thus, Ammaiyaar is one who attained great glory. She is worthy of the greatness of having received exactly what she asked for. Who can describe the greatness of Ammaiyaar in this way? It is beyond our capacity. The specialness of Ammaiyaar, that great specialness, is immeasurable, and I cannot describe it. Not only that, no one can describe it. Ammaiyaar's specialness is immeasurable. Ammaiyaar not only remains at the Lord's feet when He dances but also has the special privilege of singing. Sekkizhar Peruman says that no one can describe the great specialness of such an Ammaiyaar. Sekkizhar Peruman then composes the concluding verse, the 66th verse. "When the Lord, who has no beginning or end, performs His dance of grace, I praise the radiant lotus feet of Ammaiyaar, who sings before Him. Now I shall begin to praise the sacred service performed by Appoodhi Adigal, the great sage, in Thingoor, surrounded by cool water-filled fields." Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman concludes the Puranam of Ammaiyaar. The Lord's sacred dance is a dance of grace without beginning or end. Sekkizhar Peruman says in this verse, "When the Lord performs that dance of grace, I praise the radiant lotus-like feet of Ammaiyaar, who sings melodious songs at His feet." Sekkizhar Peruman concludes by emphasizing Ammaiyaar singing at the Lord's feet here. This is because this is the boon Ammaiyaar asked for and received. This boon encompasses all other boons. It is in this manner of singing that all other historical narratives, all other histories of Ammaiyaar, come to mind. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman sings and concludes this entire history by focusing on this. Sekkizhar Peruman sings, "Ammaiyaar, who sings before Him." Music that has the Lord as its inner meaning is the music that can grant us salvation. All other music, all other songs, are empty music that are limited to sensory pleasure. But a song that has the Lord as its inner meaning, that music, will bring us salvation. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman, who concludes this sacred Puranam by using the name "Ammai" with which the Lord called her, praises Ammaiyaar's lotus feet, as she forever sings before the Lord. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman, praising and concluding the sacred Puranam of our Ammaiyaar, praises Ammaiyaar's feet and then states that he will now begin to narrate the sacred service performed by Appoodhi Adigal, the great sage, who lived in Thingoor. Thus, he sets the stage for the upcoming Puranam of Appoodhi Adigal Nayanar. We too, having happily concluded Ammaiyaar Puranam, will now continue with Appoodhi Adigal Nayanar Puranam, with the support of Ammaiyaar's feet. At this point, Sivakavimani C.K.S. Aiya has provided detailed commentaries on Ammaiyaar's ThiruvAlangattu Mootha Thiruppathikam One, Two, Thiru Irattai Manimalai, and Thiruvanthathi. Devotees can read and learn from them.
