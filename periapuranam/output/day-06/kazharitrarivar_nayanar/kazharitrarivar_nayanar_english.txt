Chapter 01 — Kazharitrarivar Nayanar

Tiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Part 937, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Varkonda Vanamulaiyal Sarukkam. Kazharitrarivar Nayanar Puranam. In the Tiruttondar Puranam, which is the Periyapuranam composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, the Puranam of Kazharitrarivar Nayanar is the 37th Puranam, following the Puranam of Siruttonda Nayanar. The Peruman who is praised by all as Cheraman Peruman Nayanar is Kazharitrarivar Nayanar. This section speaks about his sacred Puranam. This Sarukkam, named Varkonda Vanamulaiyal Sarukkam, is not the Sarukkam corresponding to the sixth verse in the Tiruttondattogai composed by our Lord Sundaramurthy Swamigal. Sundaramurthy Swamigal has praised Cheraman Peruman Nayanar. He praises him saying, "I am a servant to Kazharitrarivar, whose generosity is like the rain-bearing clouds." The sixth sacred verse of Tiruttondattogai, "Varkonda Vanamulaiyal Umaipangan Kazhale Maravadu Kallerinda Sakkiyarkkum Adiyen Sirkonda Pugazh Vallal Sirappulikkum Adiyen Sengattangudi Meya Siruttondarkku Adiyen," continues with Sundaramurthy Swamigal singing, "Karkonda Kodai Kazharitrarivarkkum Adiyen." Therefore, in this Sarukkam, the Puranam of Sakkiya Nayanar, the Puranam of Sirappuli Nayanar, and then the Puranam of Siruttonda Nayanar are followed by the fourth Puranam, which is the Puranam of Kazharitrarivar Nayanar. In the compendium Tiruttondattogai, Sundaramurthy Swamigal praises him thus. The meaning of "Karkonda Kodai Kazharitrarivarkkum Adiyen" is that "I am a servant to Kazharitrarivar Nayanar, whose generosity surpasses that of the rain-bearing clouds." "Kar" means cloud. "Kolluthal" means to conquer or surpass. To surpass the nature of the cloud. The nature of the cloud is to give without expecting anything in return. The Tirukkural states, "Kaimaru Venda Kadappadu Marimattu En Attungollo Ulaku," meaning, "What return can the world make to the rain, which gives without expecting anything?" Similarly, the cloud helps all living beings in this world by showering rain. What return can these living beings make to such a cloud? Likewise, those who are like these clouds, their generosity and charity, they do not expect any return. What return can the living beings who benefited from their generosity make to those who gave that charity? This is what the Tirukkural states. Our Cheraman Peruman Nayanar, Kazharitrarivar Nayanar, surpassed even the generosity of such clouds. Surpassing the generosity of the clouds is due to his special quality of aligning with the nature of Shiva. Moreover, there is a hint here that all clouds obey his command. Accordingly, even today, in the mountainous country of Cheraman Peruman Nayanar, rain falls without fail according to the season, and with the help of that rain, we see rice and other crops growing even on elevated lands. In our plains like Tamil Nadu, we store river water in dams and reservoirs, and then channel it through canals to cultivate rice and other crops. However, in the mountainous country of Cheraman Peruman Nayanar, due to the abundant rain that falls without fail, we see rice and other crops growing even on elevated lands without the need for dams or reservoirs to store water. Therefore, it also means that the clouds obey our Lord's command. The Tirumugappasuram also praises this, saying, "Paruvappon Muppadi ena." The Nayanar's sacred name is Kazharitrarivar. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman himself will explain the meaning of this name in the 3761st sacred verse of the Puranam. Thus, the meaning of the phrase "Karkonda Kodai Kazharitrarivarkkum Adiyen," sung by Sundaramurthy Swamigal in the compendium Tiruttondattogai, is established. Now, in the detailed work Tiruttondar Tiruvandadi, Nambiyandar Nambi praises the Nayanar in two sacred verses, numbers 44 and 45. The 44th sacred verse is: "Mannar Piran Edir Vannaan Udal Uvar Ooriya Neettra En Piran Tamar Pola Varudhalum Tazh Vananga Enna Piran Adiyanna En Adiccheran Ennum Thennar Piran Kalaritrarivon Ennum Cheralane." Cheraman Perumal Nayanar, having been crowned, is riding on an elephant, making a procession through the city. At that time, he sees a washerman coming towards him, carrying a bundle of alkaline earth on his back. Due to the rain, the alkaline earth in the bundle carried by the washerman, who was coming on the elephant, dissolved and seeped, making his entire body white. Cheraman Perumal Nayanar, recognizing this form as the sacred attire of a devotee, quickly dismounted from the elephant and bowed to the washerman. The washerman, his mind disturbed, bowed and said, "O King, I am Adi Vannaan." Cheraman Perumal Nayanar heard this and bowed, saying, "I am Adi Cheran. You have done me the favor of reminding me of the sacred attire, which is like the sacred ash, by your form." Cheraman Perumal Nayanar had great devotion to the sacred ash. This is what Nambiyandar Nambi explains in this first sacred verse, the first verse praising Cheraman Perumal Nayanar in the Tiruttondar Tiruvandadi. Seeing the washerman, whose body was soaked with alkaline earth and appeared as if smeared with sacred ash, coming like a devotee of Shiva before the crowned king, Cheraman Perumal Nayanar bowed at the washerman's feet. The washerman said, "My Lord, I am Adi Vannaan." To this, Cheraman Perumal Nayanar, who had the power to say, "I am Adi Cheran," is the Chera king known as Kalaritrarivon. In the 45th sacred verse, Nambiyandar Nambi praises Cheraman Perumal Nayanar, saying that his heart did a good deed by serving that king, Cheraman Perumal Nayanar, who was a warrior who had conquered Kama, and who drove his horse to go before the big, mad white elephant given by Lord Shiva to Nambigal, the Lord of Thennavalur, who was a Chera king. We will see this sacred history at the end of the Periyapuranam. When Sundaramurthy Swamigal ascended to the mountainous country and made a request for liberation from the sacred place of Tiruvanchaikkalam, Lord Shiva sent a white elephant from Tirukkailayam. Sundaramurthy Swamigal ascended to Tirukkailayam on that white elephant, and before him, our Cheraman Perumal Nayanar also reached Tirukkailayam by driving his horse. We will see that history in the Vellana Sarukkam, the concluding Sarukkam. Nambiyandar Nambigal Swamigal praises this in this sacred verse. Thus, the detailed work Tiruttondar Tiruvandadi praises Cheraman Perumal Nayanar. Now, in the extensive work Tiruttondar Puranam, also known as Periyapuranam, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman describes this Puranam of Kalaritrarivar Nayanar in 175 sacred verses. We reflected on the Puranam of Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar, and we saw that it mostly contained the sacred history of Sundaramurthy Swamigal. In this Puranam of Kalaritrarivar Nayanar also, we see the next part of the sacred history of Sundaramurthy Swamigal. Chapter 03 — Kazharitrarivar Nayanar

Tiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Part 939, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Varkonda Vanamulaiyal Sarukkam. Kazharitrarivar Nayanar Puranam. Our Nayanar, Perumakothaiyar, was born. The sixth and seventh sacred verses. The great city, due to the special festival of the sacred birth, was filled with overflowing joy, and as the anointing with ghee was performed, a shower of celestial flowers rained down from the sky, filling it with auspicious sounds, and all living beings on this great earth were filled with increasing joy. Our Nayanar, born to uphold the Saiva path on earth, grew up with the love that came from his previous good deeds, devoted to the feet of the Lord with the shining third eye on his forehead. With inner love, he did not engage in royal duties but performed sacred service at Tiruvanchaikkalam, the sacred place of the Lord with clear water in his matted locks. The people of the wealthy city of Kodungolur, filled with great joy due to the special festival of the sacred birth of our Nayanar, Perumakothaiyar, celebrated with an anointing ceremony using ghee. A shower of celestial flowers rained down from the sky. All living beings on this great earth were filled with increasing joy due to the sacred birth of our Nayanar. Our Nayanar, as the Chera king, not only alleviated the sufferings of humans but also those of non-human beings, and he is going to rule. He wished to understand the language and meaning of non-human beings. Through this, he wanted to ensure that their sufferings were also alleviated under his rule, and that non-human beings also lived happily. For this, our Nayanar prayed to the Lord and received the boon to understand the thoughts of non-human beings, and then he ruled. Therefore, all living beings in this world rejoiced due to the sacred birth of our Nayanar. Due to the sacred birth of our Nayanar, all these beings are going to live and attain salvation by following the Saiva path. Our Nayanar, who took birth for the salvation of beings, was devoted to the feet of Lord Shiva with the love that came from his previous good deeds. His heart was completely filled with love for the Lord. This inherent love is the love that came with him from his previous good deeds. We saw in the Puranam of Tirukkannappadeva Nayanar that the accumulation of past good deeds leads to endless joyful love. Sivagnanabodham states that it is realized through penance. Now, the adhikarana states that knowledge arises in these souls through past good deeds. Although he was born into a royal lineage, our Nayanar did not engage in royal duties but performed sacred service at Tiruvanchaikkalam, where Lord Shiva resides with brilliance. It is very rare to have love for Lord Shiva from a young age. It is even rarer for those born into a royal lineage to perform service to Lord Shiva instead of royal duties. All these things do not come without previous penance. Now, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman sings about the sacred services performed by our Nayanar in the next two sacred verses. The eighth and ninth sacred verses. Knowing that the nature of the world and the nature of kingship are not permanent, he would rise early, bathe in water, and also bathe in the sacred white ash. He would perform many duties in the sacred Nandavanam, bring flowers and buds, arrange them with joy to adorn the Lord, bring sacred water for ablution, apply sacred unguents, and joyfully perform sacred sweeping with love. These two sacred verses, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, describe the sacred service performed by our Nayanar. Our Nayanar realized that this worldly life and royal life are impermanent. He understood that worldly life and royal life are perishable and not permanent, and therefore he performed the permanent sacred service to God. He rises early in the morning, bathes in water, and then bathes in the purifying sacred white ash. Bathing in vibhuti or sacred white ash is called Basma Snanam. Applying sacred ash all over the body is considered bathing in sacred ash. There are eight types of bathing. They are Varunam, which is bathing in water. The second is Agneyam, which is applying sacred ash related to fire, called Basma Snanam. We also see that Basma Snanam is a part of Sandhyavandanam. Now, the third type of bathing is Mantram, which is chanting mantras. The fourth is Manasam, which is Pranava Dhyanam. The fifth type of bathing is called Divya Mahendram. It is walking seven steps eastward in the rain that appears with the sun's rays. That is Divya Mahendram Snanam. The sixth type of bathing is called Marutam or Vayavyam. This is walking against the wind, allowing the dust from the hooves of a cow to fall on one's body. That Snanam is called Maruta Snanam or Vayavya Snanam. The sixth type of bathing is Parthiba Snanam. This is applying mud from a Shiva temple or a river, mixed with water, or sprinkling that mixed water. This is called Parthiba Snanam, the seventh type of bathing. Now, the eighth type of bathing is called Gauravam. Bathing in the sacred water from the feet of the Acharya is called Gaurava Snanam. Bathing in the sacred water from the feet of the Acharya, sprinkling it on oneself, and consuming it are all part of this eighth type of Gaurava Snanam. Thus, there are eight types of Snanam. The first four types of Snanam are considered Nitya (daily), and the next four types are considered Naimittika (occasional). The details of these are clearly explained in the book Saiva Bhushanam. Here, our Nayanar is said to have bathed in the sacred white ash, which refers to Agneya Snanam. Therefore, it is sacred white ash, purifying vibhuti. We have seen the Tiruneetru Tiruppathikam, which states that "cleanliness is ash." Therefore, the message here is that only white ash is to be worn. Thus, after bathing in water in the early morning and then performing Agneya Snanam by bathing in sacred white ash, he performs duties in the sacred Nandavanam. The Nayanar performs gardening duties such as watering trees and plants and weeding. Then, he brings flowers and buds and joyfully arranges them to adorn the Lord with garlands. The flowers should be freshly bloomed. The buds should be picked at the appropriate time for blooming, according to the time they are to be offered to the Lord. "Tirumanjanamum Konarndu" means bringing and adding water for the Lord's sacred ablution. "Tirumezhukku Saathi" means lovingly and joyfully sweeping and cleaning the temple courtyard. This refers to the service performed with the mind. Thus, the Nayanar performs sacred service with the three instruments of mind, speech, and body. The Tevaram composed by Tirunavukkarasu Peruman states, "Nilaiperumara Ennudhiye Nenje Nee Vaa Nithalum Emperan Udaya Koyil Pukku Pularvathan Mun Alagittu Mezhukkum Ittu Poomalai Punainthethi Pugazhndu Padi Talaiyara Kumpittu Koothum Adi Sankara Saya Potri Potri Enrum Alai Punal Ser Senjadaiyam Aathi Enrum Arurara Enrenre Alara Nille." When we read about all the sacred services undertaken by our Nayanar, all these sacred words come to our mind. Now, the tenth sacred verse. The king named Sengolporaiyan, whose generosity was like the clouds, ruled the city, which was surrounded by a moat filled with water and a long mountain range, under the shade of his white umbrella, and then he renounced the world and embraced penance. What does it signify that the king ruled under the white umbrella, which is a special emblem for crowned kings? Just as an umbrella protects the person under it from the troubles of sun, rain, and cold, so too it is the king's duty to protect his country from suffering, increase its joy, and rule it appropriately. This is one of the special emblems for crowned kings. The white umbrella is a symbol that the king alone is entirely responsible for the welfare of the country. We see that Srimat Madhava Sivagnana Munivar, in his commentary on the eighth sutra of Sivagnanabodham, states that the king is like the first cause, the great white umbrella, which is the root cause of all creation and is uniquely his, not belonging to anyone else. This is how this king ruled well under that white umbrella. Now, the shade of generosity. This is the benefit of dharma that comes from generosity. The rules for Andhadhi state that the last letter, syllable, word, or line of a verse should be the first of the next verse. There are many types of Andhadhi. This is a Seyyul Andhadhi. That is, one of the last letter, syllable, word, or line of a verse becomes the first of the next verse. This work is a Kalithurai Andhadhi structured in this way. Andhadhi comes from "Antham" + "Aadhi." This is called Dhirka Sandhi in Sanskrit. We see here that the first word of the first line of the first verse of the work and the last word of the last line of the last verse form a circle, with the end becoming the beginning. The first verse begins with "Ponvannam." Similarly, we see that the hundredth verse ends with "Ponvanname." We also see the Arputhath Thiruvandhadhi, composed by Karaikkal Ammaiyar Perumatti, which has a similar structure as a Venba Andhadhi. Therefore, this Andhadhi is one of the types of Tamil literary works. Its first verse is: "Ponvannam evvannam avvanname meni polinthilangu minvannam evvannam avvanname veezh sadai vellik kundram thanvannam evvannam avvanname maalvidai thannaik kanda envannam evvannam avvanname aagiya Easanukke."
