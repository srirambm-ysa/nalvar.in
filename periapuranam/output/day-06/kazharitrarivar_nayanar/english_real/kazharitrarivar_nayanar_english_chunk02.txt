Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Part 948, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Varkonda Vanamulaiyal Sarukkam, Kazharitrarivar Nayanar Puranam. Cheraman Perumal Nayanar reaches the boundary of the sacred city of Thillai. 53rd verse. When he arrived at the ancient city of Thillai, he bowed at its boundary with joy. As the groups of Brahmins and devotees approached, he bowed in return. He worshipped the fragrant, flower-filled sacred street, joining his hands above his head. With a joyful heart, Cheralanar reached the seven-tiered gopuram. Cheraman Perumal Nayanar reaches the sacred boundary of Thillai. Thillai has a special arrangement where its sacred boundary extends for a distance of a 'katham' (a measure of distance) in all four directions. As he reached this boundary, all the Brahmins of Thillai came forward to greet him with joy. Groups of devotees also arrived. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman states in this verse that the Brahmins of Thillai, due to their unique prominence, first came forward to greet him, and only then did the assembly of Shiva devotees come forward. Cheraman Perumal Nayanar bowed to the Brahmins of Thillai and the groups of devotees who had come to greet and worship him. Entering the sacred city of Thillai, he worshipped the beautiful, flower-adorned sacred street, joining his hands above his head with a joyful heart, and reached the seven-tiered gopuram. 54th verse. He bowed to the ground before the glorious seven-tiered gopuram, his eyes welling up with tears, and entered. He circumambulated the jeweled palace and worshipped the Thiru Perambalam, which illuminates the world. He then approached the Thiru Chitrambalam, where the Lord who created countless universes dances. The glorious seven-tiered gopuram. Cheraman Perumal Nayanar worshipped it according to tradition, entered through the sacred gopuram's entrance, and proceeded with due reverence. It is customary to prostrate on the ground before the sacred gopuram. Thus, prostrating on the ground, with tears welling up in his eyes, he entered and circumambulated the sacred palace. The sacred palace refers to the area within the sacred wall of the Lord's temple, known as the Thirumaligai Patti. After circumambulating it, he worshipped the Thiru Perambalam, which illuminates this world. Perambalam and Chitrambalam are different; they are two distinct halls. Perambalam is the hall where processional deities like Somaskandar always reside. Chitrambalam is the golden hall where Sabhanayagar, the Lord of Dance, resides. Both of these are located south of the main deity's temple in Thillai. After worshipping the Perambalam, Cheraman Perumal Nayanar then approached the Thiru Chitrambalam, where the Lord of Dance, who bestows countless universes, performs his wondrous sacred dance. 55th verse. As the Lord of infinite bliss, the great dancer, showed the anklet He wore for His dance, his inner and outer senses converged and melted in praise, and he found salvation. He praised the Lord's compassion, who offered the world the nectar of the sacred dance, with His anklet placed in the hall, not the nectar that had poison mixed in it. The Lord performs the sacred dance in the Thiru Chitrambalam as the Lord of infinite bliss, the great dancer. The Lord's dance mentioned here is characteristic of the great dance of supreme bliss. Yogis and jnanis perceive it, benefit from it, immerse themselves in it, and experience it. The Lord of Dance shows the anklet He wore for His dance. This is revered as the raised sacred foot, the Kunjitha Padam. As the saying goes, "Seek liberation at the lotus feet," the raised foot is the target for attaining liberation. When the Lord shows it, living beings can see it. Thus, as the Lord showed the anklet He wore for His dance, Cheraman Perumal Nayanar's inner and outer senses converged and melted. "Ullam" refers to the inner instruments: mind, intellect, consciousness, and ego. "Pulanam" refers to the outer senses. "Oruvazhi selluthal" means to unite. In accordance with the sacred words of Thevaram, "Unite and remember," and "Let the five great senses be captivated by the eyes," and "The joy that is not perceived by the five senses," and "The Shiva-bhoga that comes directly from consciousness, effortlessly reaching the five senses without interruption," all these points that we have previously seen come to mind here. Thus, with his inner and outer senses converging and melting, Cheraman Perumal Nayanar praises and finds salvation. The Lord bestowed two kinds of nectar. One was the nectar given to the Devas, with poison held in His throat. The other is the nectar of the sacred dance, which He bestowed upon the world by placing His sacred foot in the Thiru Perambalam. The first was bestowed upon the Devas at one time. Although it saved them from death and granted them longevity, that nectar could not prevent death and did not give them eternal immortality. The Devas could not achieve that through that nectar. But the nectar of this sacred dance, which bestows grace upon the people of the world, is the nectar that always grants living beings a state of non-birth. It is eternal nectar. That sacred dance is the dance that grows with the anklet. "Kazhal vaithu" means to plant one sacred foot. The planted sacred foot is the foundation for the liberation that comes from the dancing, raised sacred foot. Therefore, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman praises it as "the sacred dance that grows with the anklet." Gnana Sastram praises this as "the hidden power that resides in the planted lotus feet." The planted sacred foot acts as the obscuring power, the Tirodhana, and then facilitates the manifestation of the power of grace. There is a reason for this sacred dance performed by the Lord. It is His compassion for living beings. We have already seen Cheraman Perumal Nayanar praise this earlier, saying, "What compassion He bestows upon His devotees!" 56th verse. Immersed in the ocean of endless joy, he praised and bowed at the feet of the Lord, singing the Thiruvandhadhi of Ponvannath, which is full of grace and beautiful colors. He was like the clouds that bring seasonal rain, incomparable in his generosity, Kazharitrarivar. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman praises the generosity of Kazharitrarivar Nayanar in this verse as well. He praises him as "Kazharitrarivar, whose hands are incomparable in generosity, like the clouds that bring seasonal rain." This means that Kazharitrarivar Nayanar possesses hands that bestow generosity, which is superior to the clouds that pour down rain without fail at the right season. Seasonal rain, if it falls at the right time and in the right amount, is beneficial. If it doesn't fall at the right time and in the right amount, even though rain is desirable, if it falls out of season, either too early or too late, it is not only useless but also causes damage and sorrow. Similarly, clouds appear as if they will rain. Sometimes they pass without raining. The generosity of our Cheraman Perumal Nayanar is not like that. Unlike rain that causes harm if it falls out of season, Cheraman Perumal Nayanar's generosity always brings only benefit, without causing harm at any time. This rain only gives water. But Cheraman Perumal Nayanar's generosity gives everything desired, as desired. Therefore, his generosity cannot be compared to these clouds. Such is the greatness of Kazharitrarivar Nayanar, Cheraman Perumal Nayanar. Such a Cheraman Perumal Nayanar, due to his insatiable desire, immersed himself in the ocean of bliss, delighted in it, and by the grace of Lord Shiva, before the Lord of Dance, he sang and praised the Thiruvandhadhi of Ponvannath, which is full of special colors, at the foot of the Thirukalittrupadi, the sacred steps before the Sabhanayagar, so that the world might lovingly praise and enjoy it. This literary work composed by Cheraman Perumal Nayanar is called Ponvannath Thiruvandhadhi. This work was compiled by Nambiyandar Nambigal Swamigal in the eleventh Thirumurai among the twelve Saiva Thirumurais. It begins with "Ponvannam," and therefore it is named Ponvannath Thiruvandhadhi. Just as "Aathichoodi" is named after its beginning, "Aathichoodi," so it is here. Andhadhi is a type of literature. The rules for Andhadhi state that the last letter, syllable, word, or line of a verse should be the first of the next verse. There are many types of Andhadhi. This is a Seyyul Andhadhi. That is, one of the last letter, syllable, word, or line of a verse becomes the first of the next verse. This work is a Kalithurai Andhadhi structured in this way. Andhadhi comes from "Antham" + "Aadhi." This is called Dhirka Sandhi in Sanskrit. We see here that the first word of the first line of the first verse of the work and the last word of the last line of the last verse form a circle, with the end becoming the beginning. The first verse begins with "Ponvannam." Similarly, we see that the hundredth verse ends with "Ponvanname." We also see the Arputhath Thiruvandhadhi, composed by Karaikkal Ammaiyar Perumatti, which has a similar structure as a Venba Andhadhi. Therefore, this Andhadhi is one of the types of Tamil literary works. Its first verse is: "Ponvannam evvannam avvanname meni polinthilangu minvannam evvannam avvanname veezh sadai vellik kundram thanvannam evvannam avvanname maalvidai thannaik kanda envannam evvannam avvanname aagiya Easanukke." This Andhadhi was composed by Cheraman Perumal Nayanar to worship the Lord of Thillai, who accepted his worship and daily let him hear the sound of His anklets. He praised the Lord's abundant grace in many ways and presented this Andhadhi at the foot of the Thirukalittrupadi, the sacred steps before the Sabhanayagar, so that the people of this world might read it and find joy. 57th verse. As he stood before the Lord and offered the garland of Tamil words, the Lord, who dances for the welfare of the celestials, as a reward for that, caused the sound of His sacred anklet, which He had raised in the golden jeweled hall, to be heard by him. As Cheraman Perumal Nayanar stood before his Lord, the Lord of Dance, and offered the Thiruvandhadhi, a garland of words in pure Tamil, the Lord, as a fitting reward for that song, caused the sound of His sacred anklet, which had risen from His sacred foot, which He had raised in the beautiful golden Thiru Ambalam, to be heard by Cheraman Perumal Nayanar. The Lord of Dance made that sound of the anklet audible to Cheraman Perumal Nayanar. It is customary for those who are sung about by poets to give rewards to the poets. Similarly, the best reward for this literary work, a garland of words, is to make a sound audible. Therefore, as sound is the reward for sound, the Lord made the sound of His sacred anklet, the sound of His sacred foot's anklet, audible to Cheraman Perumal Nayanar. That sacred anklet sound produces all supreme joys. Therefore, that is the appropriate reward.

Such is the Lord who shines with the raised sacred foot, performing the sacred dance for the celestials to worship. "Devas to live" means that the Devas, including Brahma and Vishnu, attain well-being, which means that their activities such as creation and preservation occur well. Their activities occur well only through the Lord's fivefold dance. Therefore, the Lord performs the sacred dance for the welfare of the Devas. The Lord thus made the sound of the sacred anklet audible to Cheraman Perumal Nayanar. 58th verse. Cheraman Perumal Nayanar, who heard the sound of the dancing anklet, attained the great blessing of boundless joy and bliss. He worshipped the Lord with such devotion that he prostrated himself repeatedly. After worshipping, he left the temple and stayed outside, bowing to the streets of the grand mansions. Cheraman Perumal Nayanar, who heard the sound of the Lord's dancing anklet, worshipped the Lord, attaining the state of great blessing where boundless joy and bliss converged. He bowed repeatedly and then left the temple, bowing to the streets of the grand mansions, and stayed outside in Thillai. We will continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.

Among these Akathurais, this work predominantly focuses on separation and the reasons for separation, which are the main themes of the Palai Thinai (a genre of love poetry). Other genres are present in a minor way. The core idea of this Thirummanikkkovai is to praise the glories of the Lord of Thiruvarur by embedding them within the Akathurai themes. Taking the one who arose with grace from that place, Vanthondar went out and approached the sacred mansion of Nankai Paravaiyar in Aroor. The beautiful-browed lady, having arranged lamps, full pots, flower garlands, and fragrant incense everywhere, came forward to greet him. Cheraman Peruman Nayanar, having received the Lord's grace before Thyagesa Peruman in the Thiruvarur temple, was led by Nambi Aroorar, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, to the sacred mansion of Nankai Paravai Nachiyar. There, Paravai Ammaiyar greeted him by arranging lamps, full pots, flower garlands, fragrant incense, and other auspicious items everywhere. This kind of welcome for elders is our tradition. We still see this custom in Chera Nadu and Eelam today. The commentator notes that our South Tamil Nadu has forgotten this custom. Paravai Ammaiyar also came forward to greet him at the entrance of the sacred mansion. Welcoming guests who arrive at the home is one of the great duties of household life for the lady of the house. Therefore, she welcomes him. 71st verse. In the jeweled mansion, on a couch with shining golden legs, he seated the great king. Nambi sat with him. Paravaiyar, of faultless character, offered full worship to her husband and his friend, according to the righteous customs. Paravai Ammaiyar performs the worship that should be offered to devotees when they arrive at one's home, seating them on a seat. She seats Cheraman Peruman Nayanar on a couch with shining golden legs. Nambigal, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, sits with him. Nankai Paravai Nachiyar, of faultless character, offers full worship to her husband, Nambigal, and his friend, Cheraman Peruman Nayanar, according to the rules of the Sivagamas, without deviating from righteous conduct. We saw this in the Thirupuranam of Ilaiyankudi Mara Nayanar: "After bringing them into the house, washing their feet, and then, with overflowing love, seating them on the seat and performing archana." Thus, Paravai Nachiyar performs devotee worship. 72nd verse. When the companion (Sundaramoorthy Swamigal) asked the great Chera king, who had leaping horses, to prepare sacred food, Paravaiyar, who was like a lamp with a stimulating flame, quickly prepared various kinds of curries and cooked rice as desired, and served a feast to all who had come with him. When Sundaramoorthy Swamigal asked his consort, Paravai Ammaiyar, who was like a lamp with a stimulating flame, to prepare sacred food for Cheraman Peruman Nayanar, the great Chera king who had leaping horses, Paravai Ammaiyar, whom the divine Sekkizhar Peruman praises in this verse with the epithet "Thoondu Jyoti Vilakkanaiyar" (like a lamp with a stimulating flame), quickly prepared various kinds of curries and cooked rice as desired. "Vilakku anaiya" (like a lamp) refers to her commitment to follow instructions. Thus, Paravai Ammaiyar is praised here as "Thoondu Jyoti Vilakkanaiyar." As Sundaramoorthy Swamigal said this, Paravai Ammaiyar quickly prepared various kinds of curries and cooked rice. She prepared a feast for all who had come with Cheraman Peruman Nayanar, according to their nature. These were food items of different tastes and types. We have seen that food has four types and six tastes. "Ponakam" refers to sacred food, cooked rice. Therefore, "veru veru vidhathu kariyum ponakamum" (various kinds of curries and cooked rice) in the verse can be interpreted as different kinds of curries along with the cooked rice, or as different kinds of cooked rice, such as various mixed rice dishes.

73rd verse. More than the special arrangements made for the king, Paravaiyar, who was like the goddess born from the ocean, prepared the desired sacred food with overflowing love, suitable for a devotee. After that, she came and informed Sundaramoorthy Swamigal that the Chera king, who had elephants with bells, and his entourage were ready to partake of the sacred food. More than the special arrangements made for Cheraman Peruman Nayanar, befitting his status as a king, Paravai Ammaiyar, with overflowing love, prepared the desired sacred food with even greater excellence, suitable for a devotee. The special arrangements made for a king are worldly. The sacred food prepared for a devotee is about the nature of love for Shiva. The latter is far superior and more excellent than the former. Both these qualities are excellent in Cheraman Peruman Nayanar. Although the excellence of being a king and the excellence of being a devotee are both great, here, Paravai Ammaiyar served sacred food based on his quality as a devotee. We also understand that this quality is the underlying principle of Periyapuranam. After preparing the sacred food in this manner, Paravai Ammaiyar informs Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. In this verse, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman praises Paravai Ammaiyar as "Paravai pirandha thiru anaiya Paravaiyar" (Paravaiyar, who is like the goddess born from the ocean). "Paravai" means ocean. "Thiru anaiya" means like the goddess. So, "Paravaiyar, who is like the goddess born from the ocean" is its meaning. Superficially, "Thiru pirandha thiru" (goddess born from the ocean) generally refers to Mahalakshmi, the goddess born from the milk ocean. But "Paravai pirandha thiru" here, "Paravai" refers to the ocean of grace. "Thiru" refers to Umaiyamai, who arises from the ocean of grace. This refers to Mahalakshmi, who is the embodiment of Shiva-Shakti. Such a Mahalakshmi, the embodiment of Shiva-Shakti, is the goddess worshipped in the Vayu Kona (north-west direction) in Shiva worship. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman beautifully explains in this verse the greatness of Paravai Ammaiyar, stating that she attained such a state by serving Umaiyamai and performing sacred service. 74th verse. When the renowned Vanthondar said, "O Paravai, with your flower-laden tresses, by the merit of your past penance, the great Chera king has arrived to partake of sacred food. Therefore, without delay, serve the sacred food," Paravai Ammaiyar, with her beautiful hands, arranged the plates in two separate places. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal says to Paravai Ammaiyar, "O Paravai, with your flower-laden tresses, by the merit of your past penance, we have received the great Chera king here to partake of sacred food. Therefore, without delay, serve the sacred food." Thus, the renowned Vanthondar, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, instructs her. Paravai Ammaiyar, with her beautiful hands, arranges the plates in two separate places. "In two separate places" means in two different locations. She arranges plates in one place for Nambigal and in another place for Cheraman Peruman Nayanar. The Ammaiyar arranges them in this manner, according to the rules of dharma, based on the worldly status that one is a Saiva Brahmin and the other is a king. However, when Sundaramoorthy Swamigal requests Cheraman Peruman Nayanar to partake of the sacred food with him, Cheraman Peruman Nayanar initially refuses. Then, due to the nature of their love, when Sundaramoorthy Swamigal requests again, Cheraman Peruman Nayanar agrees to partake of the sacred food with him. We see here that the nature of love is superior to worldly dharma. Therefore, Paravai Ammaiyar arranges the plates accordingly. Paravai Ammaiyar arranges everything necessary for partaking of the sacred food, such as placing the seats, providing water for washing hands, and so on. 75th verse. When the Lord Nambi asked the great king to partake of the sacred food with him, the king quickly bowed in reverence. But when Nambi, holding his great hand, again earnestly requested him, the king of the Pandyas agreed to partake of the sacred food. When Aludaiya Nambigal, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, asked Cheraman Peruman Nayanar to partake of the sacred food with him, Cheraman Peruman Nayanar quickly bowed in reverence and was startled. However, when Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, holding Cheraman Peruman Nayanar's sacred hands, again earnestly requested him, Cheraman Peruman Nayanar agreed to partake of the sacred food with him. 76th verse. As they partook of the sacred food together, Paravaiyar, of elevated penance, served the sacred food with great delight. She then arranged a feast for all the attendants, with six tastes as they desired, and thus, with overflowing joy, she fulfilled the duty of a pure host. As mentioned before, when Cheraman Peruman Nayanar agreed, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal and Cheraman Peruman Nayanar partook of the sacred food together. Paravai Ammaiyar, of elevated penance, served the sacred food with great delight. Paravai Ammaiyar's previous state of service in Kailash and her past service in Thiruvarur resulted in her receiving the great blessing of serving sacred food to Sundaramoorthy Swamigal and Cheraman Peruman Nayanar together here. Thus, she serves the sacred food. She also serves a feast of six tastes to the attendants, according to their needs, so that they may eat with pleasure. All the attendants are filled with great joy. Thus, Paravai Ammaiyar sweetly fulfills the duty of a pure host. Hospitality is a duty. Among the duties of household life, hospitality is the most excellent. That is why the divine Sekkizhar Peruman refers to it as a "duty" here. Because she served sacred food with love for Shiva, considering the quality of being a devotee of Shiva as supreme, this becomes a pure feast. All other feasts given to worldly people are based on attachment and do not help to cut off rebirth. Therefore, they are not pure feasts. A feast given to Shiva's devotees becomes a pure feast that cuts off the bonds of rebirth. 77th verse. The women prepared and offered fragrant sandalwood paste mixed with green camphor, musk paste that brought joy, and pure, fragrant flower garlands. The golden-creeper-like women then offered sweet Panchavasam and betel nut with betel leaves. In this verse, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman explains the courtesies extended after partaking of the sacred food. They prepared sandalwood paste mixed with green camphor, saffron, rosewater, and other ingredients. The women who were to prepare this sandalwood mixture ground and prepared it. They also offered musk paste, which was made by mixing musk and other rare fragrant ingredients, and was meant to be worn as a tilak. They also offered pure flower garlands, beautifully arranged. Then, Panchavasam and betel nut with betel leaves. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman refers to this here as "Panchavasamudan adaikkai amudu." Panchavasam and betel nut with betel leaves are meant to be offered together. "Adaikkai amudu" refers to betel nut with betel leaves. "Adai" means betel leaf, and "kai" means nut. Therefore, "adaikkai amudu" is betel nut with betel leaves. So, in this verse, the fragrant mixture is for applying to the body. Musk paste and flower garlands are for wearing. Panchavasam and betel nut with betel leaves are for eating. The women assigned to this task stand ready to offer these. 78th and 79th verses. Having accepted all the worship offered with such excellence, he received the sacred ashes on his head, worshipped, and adorned himself with them. As he remained with great delight, Nambi, having attained the true nature of Kazharitrarivar's devotion, bowed at his golden feet. As the great king bowed, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, bowing in return and embracing him, praised the grace of the Lord with the Ganga in His matted locks, who had brought together the victorious king with the bow banner and Paravaiyar, whose face was like the moon, and sat with him. Having lovingly accepted all the worship offered with such excellence as mentioned before, he received the purifying sacred ashes, worshipped, and adorned himself with them on his sacred head. As he remained with great delight, Kazharitrarivar Nayanar, having truly attained the pure nature of devotion, prostrated himself at the golden feet of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. As Cheraman Perumal Nayanar, the great king of the mountain country, bowed, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal also bowed in return and embraced him. He then praised the grace of the Lord with the Ganga in His matted locks, who had brought together Cheraman Perumal Nayanar, the victorious king with the bow banner, and Paravaiyar, whose face was like the moon, and sat with him. 80th verse. Worshipping the feet of the Lord with the mountain as His bow, who joyfully resides in the golden anthill in the prosperous Thiruvarur, and serving the grand procession of Veethividanga Peruman, both great scholars, with enduring love, lived happily day by day. Worshipping the feet of the Lord with the Meru mountain as His bow, who joyfully and lovingly resides in the golden anthill in the prosperous Thiruvarur, and serving the grand procession of Veethividanga Peruman, Thyagesa Peruman, both great scholars, with enduring love, lived happily day by day, having attained great life. The period of Cheraman Perumal Nayanar's arrival here could be during the Panguni festival or the Thiruvathirai festival. The Lord's festival is a festival that brings great life. It is a festival that bestows the ultimate reality of great life. Thus, by witnessing such a festival and worshipping the Lord of Thiruvarur, Cheraman Perumal Nayanar and Sundaramoorthy Swamigal resided in Thiruvarur with great excellence. We will continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 16 — Kazharitrarivar Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Part 952, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Varkonda Vanamulaiyal Sarukkam, Kazharitrarivar Nayanar Puranam. Cheraman Peruman Nayanar resides in Thiruvarur with Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Now, the 81st verse. As they lived in this manner, one day, Vanthondar, adorned with shining jeweled ornaments, remembered in due course to worship the many places where the Lord with the dark, shining throat resided, including Thirumadurai and other ancient cities in the Tamil country, which is called the virgin land, where the Lord with abundant matted locks resides. As they lived immersed in Shiva-bliss in this manner, worshipping the Lord of Thiruvarur and enjoying Shiva-bliss, Vanthondar, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, thought of worshipping the many places where the Lord, the Brahmin, resided. He thought of worshipping Thirumadurai and other sacred places in the Pandiya Nadu, which is called the virgin land and the Tamil country. Divine grace prompted this thought in Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal thought of visiting the sacred places in the Chola country and the Pandiya country, which are located between the Chola country and Madurai, and worshipping all the sacred places in the Pandiya Nadu, including Thirumadurai, which possesses immeasurable glories, and which is praised as the virgin land and the Tamil country. 82nd verse. The Chera king, due to his inseparable attachment to Aroor and his increasing love, and also due to the letter of grace given to him by the valiant Lord residing in Madurai Alavai, and his desire to worship with love, decided to accompany him. At the very moment Sundaramoorthy Swamigal thought of going to Madurai and other sacred places to worship, Cheraman Peruman Nayanar desired to remain with Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, inseparable from him. Moreover, divine grace prompted a thought in Cheraman Peruman Nayanar's heart to go and worship with love the Lord residing in Madurai Thiru Alavai, who had given him a letter of grace, due to his increasing love. Therefore, Cheraman Peruman Nayanar decided to accompany Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Cheraman Peruman Nayanar had undertaken this sacred journey with two intentions: to see and worship the Lord who had given him the sound of the anklet in Thillai, and to worship Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, who had been brought to his mind by the Lord of Thillai. Here, both these objectives were fulfilled. Nevertheless, he decides to worship many sacred places with Sundaramoorthy Swamigal and go to Madurai. There are two reasons for this. The first reason is to always be with Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. The other reason is to worship the Lord of Alavai, who had given him a letter of grace. For these two reasons, Cheraman Peruman Nayanar also accompanies him. 83rd verse. When the hearts of both were thus united, they arose from Thiruvarur, entered the sacred temple, worshipped the Lord's lotus feet, and by His gracious consent, set out. Their numerous devoted attendants, with their boundless services, vehicles, and treasures, accompanied them. As the hearts of both were united as mentioned before, desiring to go on a sacred pilgrimage, they arose from the sacred mansion, entered the sacred temple of the Lord who resides in the anthill in Thiruvarur, worshipped His lotus-like feet, and having received the gracious consent of Thyagaraja Peruman, they came out and began this sacred journey. The devoted people who always surrounded Sundaramoorthy Swamigal and Cheraman Peruman Nayanar, the servants who were privileged to attend to their sacred bodies, the bodyguards, and other attendants, all set out with them. The attendants who carried their belongings, vehicles, and treasures accompanied them. As these two great individuals were embarking on a long pilgrimage, these attendants carried all the necessary items for them. 84th verse. Surrounded by their serving attendants, they worshipped Thiruvarur, crossed the groves of the outer garden, and reached Keezhvelur. There, they worshipped the feet of the Lord, then passed through the sea-coast groves, and entered Nagai, which shone with flowers, and worshipped Karonam. Surrounded by all their serving attendants, who worshipped them, that is, the attendants who served Sundaramoorthy Swamigal and Cheraman Peruman Nayanar, they set out from the city of Thiruvarur and reached the city limits. That is the eastern limit of the city of Thiruvarur. Upon reaching the city limits, they worshipped the sacred place of Thiruvarur itself. After worshipping Thiruvarur, they crossed the groves of the outer garden, which were full of trees, and reached Thirukeezhvelur. There, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal and Cheraman Peruman Nayanar worshipped the feet of the Lord. Keezhvelur is located a short distance east of Thiruvarur. Although Sundaramoorthy Swamigal visited this place and praised the Lord, the sacred hymn composed by Sundaramoorthy Swamigal for this place is not available to us due to our lack of merit. Thus, after worshipping Thirukeezhvelur and receiving the Lord's gracious permission, they set out from Thirukeezhvelur and reached Thirunagappattinam, which was surrounded by sea-coast groves and shone with abundant flowers, and worshipped the Thirukaronam temple. Nagai is the name of the town. Karonam is the name of the temple. 85th verse. The Chera and Aroorar, having offered the garland of pure Tamil words, which melted the hearts of the devotees, saying, "You reside in Thirukaronam," stayed there for a few days. Then, worshipping other places where the Lord with the matted locks, adorned with the great river, resided, they reached Thirumaraikkadu, the sacred forest of the Lord, the cause of grace. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal went to Thirunagappattinam and worshipped the Lord, composing a sacred hymn. It was a sacred hymn with the refrain "You reside in Nagai Karonam." Sundaramoorthy Swamigal composed this sacred hymn, a garland of pure Tamil words, which arose from his eagerness to melt the hearts of the devotees. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman's reference here to "Thirukaronaththu iruppire" (You reside in Thirukaronam) refers to the meaning of the refrain "Kadal Nagai Karonam mevi iruppire" (You reside in Nagai Karonam by the sea). This sacred hymn is composed with eagerness to melt the hearts of devotees. It is a sacred hymn overflowing with love and good qualities. Such a sacred hymn for the sacred place of Thirunagappattinam, composed by Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, is the 46th sacred hymn in the 7th Thirumurai, set to the tune of Kollikkauvanam. The first and last verses of this sacred hymn are as follows: Thiruchitrambalam. "You who reside in many towns, singing many hymns, speaking harshly to women, playing tricks, and wandering about, wearing the bones of the dead, riding a bull, you have hidden your wealth. Have no pity on me for a day. Grant me pearl necklaces, shining jeweled diamond chains, and fragrant musk paste that is sweet to the body. You reside in Kadal Nagai Karonam." "O Lord, who is the support for me and the birds with many-voiced languages, who else do I have? You, who are immersed in me, must remove my suffering. I need shining pearl ornaments, nine kinds of flowers, and fragrant musk paste that is sweet to the eyes. You reside in Kadal Nagai Karonam." These are the rare Tamil words spoken by Nanjavur Arooran, who is close to the Lord. Those who master these will rule the celestial world. Thiruchitrambalam. "I am your servant, who else do I have? You must remove my suffering and grant me joy. You reside in Kadal Nagai Karonam." This is a sacred hymn sung with friendship. The Lord is Kayarohaneswara, and the Goddess is Nilayathakshi.

We have already discussed the details of the sacred place of Thirunagappattinam in the Thirupuranam of Thirunavukkarasu Swamigal. Thus, after worshipping Thirunagappattinam and receiving the Lord's gracious permission, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal and Cheraman Peruman Nayanar set out from Thirunagappattinam and visited places like Poigainallur, Thiruvarinjaiyur, Thirukillikudi, Thirukozhili, Thiruvaimur, Thirumugathalai, and then reached Thirumaraikkadu, the sacred forest of the Lord, which is praised as Vedharanyam. We will continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.
