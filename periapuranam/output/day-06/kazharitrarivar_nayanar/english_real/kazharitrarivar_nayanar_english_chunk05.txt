Chapter 28 — Kazharitrarivar Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 964, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Var Konda Vanamulaiyal Sarukkam, Kazharitrarivar Nayanar Puranam. The attendants of Sundaramurthy Swamigal are carrying all the treasures given by Cheraman Perumal Nayanar and proceeding ahead. Sundaramurthy Swamigal follows behind. As they are traveling along the path to Thirumuruganpoondi, by the grace of the Lord, Shiva's Ganas, disguised as hunters, seize all the wealth from Sundaramurthy Swamigal's attendants and carry it away. Sundaramurthy Swamigal worships the Lord of Thirumuruganpoondi and sings a Thiruppathigam. By the grace of the Lord, the Ganas bring all the seized items and pile them up in front of the temple entrance. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman begins to narrate this wonderful divine event. The 165th verse. Sekkizhar Peruman composes this verse as if he is speaking, saying, "As they were going near Thirumuruganpoondi, we do not know if it was to show that the Lord Himself should give gold to Nambi, who rides the bull, and that no one else should give it, or if it was to take it Himself and then, by His great grace, give it back so that Sundaramurthy Swamigal could receive it." Sekkizhar Peruman sings that when they were near Thirumuruganpoondi, the Lord Himself should bestow gold upon Sundaramurthy Swamigal, and that Sundaramurthy Swamigal should not accept it from anyone else. Perhaps it was to demonstrate this principle, or perhaps it was to take all those items Himself and then, by His divine grace, return them so that Sundaramurthy Swamigal could receive them. Sekkizhar Peruman states that we do not know the reason for this. Whenever Sundaramurthy Swamigal needed it, the Lord Himself bestowed gold upon him. We have seen all that happened in Thiruppugalur, Thiruppachilachiramam, and Thirumudukunram. Therefore, to show that receiving gold from others is not desired, the Lord, perhaps, intended to take all those items Himself, make them His own, and then give them back so that Sundaramurthy Swamigal could receive them. Who can understand the Lord's acts of grace? The sacred words "Aatpal avarukku arulum vannamum Aadhi maanbum ketpaan pugil alavillai kilakka vendaa" all indicate this. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman begins to narrate this sacred history. The 166th verse. "The victorious Ganas, in the form of hunters, went and seized the treasures of Vanthondar, by the grace of the Lord. By the grace of the Lord who burnt the three cities, they formed a hunting party and stood on both sides of the path he was taking, angrily rising up." The victorious Shiva Ganas, whose victory comes from the strength of the Lord's grace, assumed the form of hunters. By the Lord's grace, they were instructed to seize the treasures that Sundaramurthy Swamigal was carrying. Thus, by the Lord's divine grace, these Shiva Ganas transformed into hunting parties and, with anger, rose up on both sides of the path where Sundaramurthy Swamigal's attendants were coming. Here, seizing the wealth becomes an act of grace. Even in worldly life, we sometimes see that actions considered evil turn out to be beneficial. Just as it is not considered theft but a good deed if a compassionate person seizes poison or a knife that someone intended to use to kill himself or others, to prevent such an act, so too should this be understood here. The 167th verse. "Drawing their bows, fitting sharp arrows to the strings, they said, 'Leave these here, or we will kill you,' and with anger, they seized all the boundless treasures. The attendants, robbed and distressed, ran quickly and reached Sundaramurthy Swamigal." These hunting parties bent their bows, fitted arrows to the strings, and angrily told the attendants, "Leave all these bundles here, or we will kill you." With that, they seized all the boundless treasures. Sundaramurthy Swamigal's attendants, having been robbed of these treasures, ran swiftly and in distress to Sundaramurthy Swamigal. We saw that the attendants went ahead, and Sundaramurthy Swamigal followed behind. Accordingly, the attendants went to Sundaramurthy Swamigal and reported all these facts. The 168th verse. "Those hunters did not approach Aroorar, but by the grace of the Lord with the water-laden red matted hair, they departed. They reached Thirumuruganpoondi, his sacred city, and entered the temple of the Lord with the fierce bull." These hunting parties seized the items from Sundaramurthy Swamigal's attendants but did not approach Sundaramurthy Swamigal himself. Even though Nambigal was adorned with jewels and ornaments, the hunters did not approach Sundaramurthy Swamigal and departed immediately. The hunters took all the seized treasures and headed towards Thirumuruganpoondi. Moreover, Thirumuruganpoondi was the first place on that path. Therefore, Sundaramurthy Swamigal went directly to Thirumuruganpoondi, sought out the Lord's temple there, and entered the temple. The 169th verse. "He folded his hands in reverence and worshipped the temple of the Lord with the beautiful eyes. He bowed at the tall entrance that reached the clouds, and with overflowing devotion, he entered, circumambulated, and reached the presence of the Lord who adorns His head with the pure moon." Sundaramurthy Swamigal walks, folding his hands in reverence and worshipping the Lord's temple. He bows at the tall entrance, and as his love for the Lord increases more and more, with great devotion, he enters the temple, circumambulates the sacred structure, and reaches the presence of the Lord. The rules for temple darshan include worshipping the temple as soon as it is seen from a distance, bowing and worshipping at the entrance before entering, and circumambulating the outer courtyard before reaching the inner sanctum. These are the methods of worship. Even at this time, when his bundles of possessions were seized and he was distressed, Sundaramurthy Swamigal did not deviate from these rules of worship, which shows the depth of his devotion. It shows that Sundaramurthy Swamigal was steadfast in his devotion to Shiva, unaffected by worldly circumstances. The truth stated by the scriptures of wisdom, which declare the characteristics of such individuals, is that they consider everything to be Shiva's doing. The 170th verse. "With melting love, he folded his hands and prostrated before his Lord, who is united with Uma, and Vanthondar sang. 'Why were you here nearby in this fierce forest where hunters seized our belongings with fear?' Thus, he sang the beautiful Thiruppathigam beginning with 'Kodugu Venjilai Anjol'." With melting love, he folded his hands over his head and prostrated to the ground. Standing before his Lord, who is united with Uma, he asks the Lord, "Why were you here nearby in this fierce forest where hunters seized our belongings with fear?" Thus, Sundaramurthy Swamigal, the great Vanthondar, sang the beautiful Thiruppathigam beginning with "Kodugu Venjilai," which contains such meaningful words. Throughout this Thiruppathigam, he praises the Lord's state of being united with the Mother. He praises the Lord's state of being with the Mother throughout this Thiruppathigam, using phrases like "with the maiden of slender waist," "with the maiden of adorned breasts," "with the maiden of beautiful breasts," and "He who placed the maiden with bangle-adorned fingers in one part of Himself." Such is the Thiruppathigam for the sacred place of Thirumuruganpoondi, the 49th Thiruppathigam in the Seventh Thirumurai, set to the tune of Pazhampanjuram. The first and concluding verses of this Thiruppathigam are as follows: Thiruchitrambalam. "Kodugu venjilai vaduga veduvarkal viravalamai chollith thidugu mottu enak kuththik koorai kondu aaralaikkum idam mudugu naariya vadugar vazh Muruganpoondi maanagar vaay idugu nunnidai mangai thannodum eththukku ingu irundheer Embiraneere." "Munthi vaanavar thaam thozhu Muruganpoondi maanagar vaay panthanai viral paavai thannaiyor paagam vaiththavanai sinthaiyil sivaththondan Ooran uraiththana paththum kondu entham adigalai eththuvaar idar ondrum thaam ilare." Thiruchitrambalam. By saying "Eththukku ingu arugirundheer" (Why were you here nearby?), this Thiruppathigam is addressed to the Lord, asking why He, who has the duty and compassion to protect all beings in general and devotees in particular, was present there without fulfilling that duty. The implication here, conveyed through the question "Why were you here nearby?", is that if this happens near your domain, it cannot happen without your permission and intention. Therefore, you, who sent your Ganas in the form of hunters to seize the items, must return them. This profound meaning, conveyed through the question, is the greatness of the words of the great Tamil poet. We see a similar instance in the Kandapuranam, in the chapter on the marriage procession, where it is asked, "Eththukku mooththir" (Why are you older?). Thirumuruganpoondi is the second sacred place among the places sung in the Kongu Nadu region. It is one of the places where Lord Muruga worshipped Lord Shiva after the destruction of Soorapadman. Hence, it is named Thirumuruganpoondi. The shrine of Lord Muruga, who performed the worship, is uniquely prominent. As a result of that worship, even today, devotees and the general public worship here and are freed from powerful ailments like Brahmmahatthi and mental derangement. The Brahmma Theertham, which helps in curing these diseases, is located outside the temple. Therefore, separate accommodations have been arranged for those suffering from mental derangement and madness to stay and worship there. Since Durvasa Muni brought the Madhavi creeper from Devaloka and planted it here, this place is also known as Madhavi Vanam and Mullai Vanam. This is mentioned in the Thiruppathigam as "Mullai thadhu manam kamazh" (fragrance of jasmine pollen). The Sthala Purana states that Madhavi is the Kurukkathi tree. This shrine is separate. The shrine of Lord Nataraja, who showed the Brahma Thandavam to Durvasa Muni and others, is specially and separately located. The festival of "Nambigal Vedu Pari" (the seizure by hunters) is celebrated annually. The shrine of Vinayagar, who showed Nambigal the way to the Lord's abode by calling out "Koo Koo" from the place where the wealth was seized, is specially worshipped as "Kooppidu Vinayagar." This place is located a short distance north of this sacred place, on the way to Avinashi. In a small pit on a rock in front of the separate Vinayagar temple opposite the main temple, a spring flows once every 12 years. It is known as "Maamangak Kuzhi." The images of Nambigal being robbed of his possessions and receiving them back are both installed at the entrance of the main temple and worshipped. The Sthala Purana of the temple was composed by Mahavidwan Vasudeva Mudaliar of Chettipalayam, who lived about 300 years ago. He also composed many other works like Bhavani Puranam and Avinashi Karunambikai Sathagam. It has been printed with the original text and commentary. It contains the Kaappu, Payiram, Purana Varalaru, and the first 11 Sarukkams with 618 verses. The "Vedu Pari Sarukkam" in this work narrates the history of this place concerning Nambigal. The Lord's sacred name is Muruganatheswarar, Murugavudaiyar. The Goddess's sacred names are Muyangu Poonmulai Ammai, Alinga Bhushana Sthanambikai, Avudai Nayagi, Mangalambikai. The Theerthams are Brahma Theertham, Gnana Theertham, Subramanya Theertham, Shanmuga Theertham. The Lord and the Goddess have shrines facing west. This sacred place is very close to Avinashi. These are all the Sthala Purana details about Thirumuruganpoondi. Sundaramurthy Swamigal thus prays to the Lord and sings the Thiruppathigam. Now, the 171st verse. "As he sang and praised, by the grace of the Supreme Being, all the items seized by the hunters were piled up high, reaching the sky, in front of the grand entrance. Seeing this, he bowed directly and, by the grace of the Lord who performs the dance of grace, accepted them as they were." As mentioned before, when Sundaramurthy Swamigal prayed and praised the Lord, by the grace of the Supreme Being, the hunters piled up all the items they had seized in front of the grand entrance, reaching up to the sky. Seeing this, Sundaramurthy Swamigal bowed directly and, by the grace of the Lord who performs the dance of grace, accepted them as they were. The 172nd and 173rd verses. "He took them, and having sent his attendants to carry them, he bowed to the Lord with the dark throat and departed from Kongu. With true love, he swiftly traveled and reached Thiruvaroor, surrounded by soft sugarcane and fields of paddy."

"The king of poets, Nambigal, having taken leave by the grace of the Lord, the king Cheraman Perumal Nayanar, constantly remembered Nambi Aroorar, who was united with him in friendship as one's own life. He resided in the city of Makodai, adorned with large flowering groves, and ruled his mountain kingdom as his own rightful domain." Having made his attendants carry all these items, Sundaramurthy Swamigal worshipped the Lord in His temple, took leave by His grace, and departed from Kongu Nadu. He swiftly crossed Kongu Nadu and, with great love, quickly reached Thiruvaroor, which is surrounded by soft sugarcane and dense paddy fields. The path from Kongu Nadu to Thiruvaroor was mentioned earlier. Therefore, crossing Kongu Nadu and reaching Thiruvaroor happened very quickly, almost simultaneously. The reason for this swiftness is indicated as "meykonda kaathalinaal" (with true love). Nambigal, the great poet, refers to King Cheraman Perumal Nayanar, who took leave by divine grace. Cheraman Perumal Nayanar, who had a friendship with Nambi Aroorar as if they were one soul, constantly remembered him. He resided in the city of Makodai, which was adorned with large flowering groves, and ruled the mountain kingdom as his own rightful domain. Just as it is said, "Paarodu visumbotchi enakku umadhu paadhamalar" (The earth and sky are yours, your lotus feet are mine), he considered this kingdom as Sundaramurthy Swamigal's property, according to his command, and ruled it, waiting for Sundaramurthy Swamigal to return. The story of Bharata ruling the kingdom with Rama's sandals until Rama returned comes to mind here. The 174th verse. "This situation of the Uthiyar king, our Lord Vanthondar, leaving the fertile Chola country and entering Makodai, and then, if not for the sacred Kailash, ascending the great mountain on a war-horse, we shall narrate later in the sacred service of driving the war-horse." The sacred history of Cheraman Perumal Nayanar ends here for now. Henceforth, in the Vellana Sarukkam, the concluding chapter of the Periya Puranam, the sacred history of Cheraman Perumal Nayanar continues. It continues along with the sacred history of Sundaramurthy Swamigal. In that concluding chapter, the Vellana Sarukkam, Sundaramurthy Swamigal leaves the fertile Chola country, comes to the city of Makodai, and from there ascends to Thirukkailayam on an elephant from Thiru Anjaikkalam. Cheraman Perumal Nayanar, driving his victorious horse ahead of him, is about to reach Thirukkailayam with Sundaramurthy Swamigal. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman states that he will narrate that sacred history later. "Later" here means that the divine Sekkizhar Peruman states that he will narrate this sacred history in the Vellana Sarukkam, the concluding chapter of the Periya Puranam. Therefore, this section is also extensively described in the Vellana Sarukkam. It also means that the Puranam of Cheraman Perumal Nayanar, known as Kazharitrarivar Nayanar Puranam, is concluded in the same manner, which we see as the divine poetic excellence of Sekkizhar Peruman. The concluding verse of Kazharitrarivar Nayanar Puranam is the 175th verse. "Having praised the feet of Cheralanar, the king of the mountain-rich country, who had armies with banners adorned with bows, like the great ocean, we shall now narrate the glory of Gananathar, who lived in Kaali, a city of stable, beautiful mansions, long streets, and surrounded by the four Vedas, renowned for its artistic excellence." Having praised the sacred feet of Cheraman Perumal Nayanar, the king of the mountain-rich country, who possessed armies with bow-adorned banners like the great ocean, and with the help of those sacred feet, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman concludes the Puranam of Kazharitrarivar Nayanar, which is the Puranam of Cheraman Perumal Nayanar, and introduces the next Puranam, that of Gananathar Nayanar, saying, "We shall now narrate the glory of Gananathar Nayanar, who lived in Sirkazhi, a city of beautiful mansions, long streets where the great Vedas and the arts contained within them were studied and practiced, a city of great fame." We too, thus concluding this sacred Puranam of Cheraman Perumal Nayanar, the Kazharitrarivar Nayanar Puranam, shall now proceed to contemplate the Gananathar Nayanar Puranam. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of our Lord Cheraman Perumal Nayanar. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of Sundaramurthy Swamigal. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of Nambiandar Nambigal. Divine Sekkizhar Peruman.

Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the devotees. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the commentator, sir. Thiruchitrambalam.
