Chapter 32 — Kannappa Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 155, Ilaimalinda Sarukkam, Kannappa Nayanar Puranam. Thinnanar, seeing blood flowing from the right eye of Kudumithevar, was filled with immense sorrow. He clutched Kudumithevar's feet and cried out with tears streaming down his face. "What is this suffering that has come upon this Supreme Being, as I have witnessed? What has happened to our Lord, who is dearer than life itself? What has befallen this pure one, whose devotees, once they reach Him, never part from Him? I don't know what I will do now!" he cried repeatedly. "I don't know what I will do!" he lamented. Thinnanar then declared, "I will search for the medicinal herbs in the valleys of this mountain to cure this disease of blood flowing from Kudumithevar's eye," and set off. Thinnanar believed that Kudumithevar had suffered a "bhagalippuṇ" (an arrow wound). A bhagalippuṇ is a wound caused by arrows. Since Thinnanar was a hunter, he knew that sharp arrows could cause wounds and bleeding. Here, blood was flowing from Kudumithevar's eye. Therefore, he thought it was a wound caused by an arrow, and that the bleeding was a result of it. He set out to find the appropriate herbs. Thinnanar rushed faster than the speed of his mind, gathered the herbs, and returned. He squeezed the collected herbs onto the bleeding eye. Thinnanar, believing that Kudumithevar's wound was caused by a sharp arrow, gathered herbs and squeezed them onto Kudumithevar's bleeding eye. These leaves are called "Salliyakarani" in Sanskrit texts. Salliyakarani is a medicine that heals wounds caused by arrows and weapons. Generally, four types of medicines are mentioned: Salliyakarani, Santhanakarani, Samaniyakarani, and Mrithasanjeevini. Among these, Salliyakarani is the name given to the medicine and herbs that heal wounds caused by arrows and weapons. Furthermore, the Ramayana states that various types of herbs like Salliyakarani are found in the mountains. However, even after Thinnanar squeezed the herbs onto the eye, the blood from Thirukalathiappar's eye continued to flow as before, unabated. All his efforts so far had been in vain. Thinnanar, undeterred, continued to ponder. "Flesh for flesh" is an old proverb. There is also the proverb "Mamsa Mamsena Varthathe." Among hunters, the proverb "flesh for flesh" was used to describe how to cure a disease of the flesh with similar flesh. This was because when hunters engaged in their ancestral profession of hunting, various parts of their bodies could be damaged for various reasons. In such cases, the proverb "flesh for flesh" was a common solution among them. When human body parts were damaged, the practice of taking a similar organ from another human or animal body and attaching it to the affected person, making it functional again, and thereby alleviating the deficiency of that organ, has now developed into modern medicine. It is surprising that even the hunters who lived in the forests of India thousands of years ago knew this.

This proverb came to Thinnanar's mind, and he decided to act as follows: "Now, if I pluck out my own eye with an arrow and apply it to my Lord's eye, it might serve as a medicine to stop the bleeding from the wound." With this surging thought, he rejoiced. He then took a sharp arrow, aimed it at his own eye, and plucked it out. As he was about to apply it to the Lord's eye, he saw the red blood still flowing from the Lord's eye. He then jumped up from the ground, beat his mountain-like shoulders, and danced with joy. "How good is this idea I had!" he exclaimed with a smile, and in his overwhelming joy, he became like one possessed. The above two verses describe what Thinnanar did, as narrated by Sekkizhar Peruman. Didn't he say "flesh for flesh" in the first verse? Therefore, to stop the blood flowing from the right eye of his Lord, Kudumithevar, and to heal the wound, it would be good to replace it with a similar eye. The blood from the Lord's eye would stop, and the Lord's affliction would be cured. Thinnanar decided this. For this, he decided to pluck out his own eye with an arrow and apply it to the Lord. He rejoiced, thinking that if he plucked out his own eye with an arrow and applied it to the Lord, it would be a medicine for the disease in the Lord's eye, and the blood from the wound would stop flowing. According to the principle of "eye for an eye" or "flesh for flesh," to cure the defect in Kudumithevar's eye, an eye was needed. But why not kill a deer or other animal, pluck out its eye, and apply it? Thinnanar thought that if he did that, it would cause further delay. Moreover, by the time Thinnanar went to the mountain, found suitable animals, and brought back their eyes, the heat of the plucked organ would have dissipated, and it would be impossible to apply it to Kudumithevar. This would cause further delay. Therefore, he thought, "We have our own eyes to give. When we, who have worthy eyes to give, are by the Lord's side, where else should we go to seek this? We don't need to seek it anywhere else." He decided this in an instant. He chose a sharp, good arrow from his quiver. He plucked out his right eye with that arrow. He stood still as he plucked out his eye. Immediately, he applied his plucked eye to the Lord's right eye. At that moment, the blood flowing from the Lord's eye stopped. Seeing this, Thinnanar was filled with great joy. He jumped and leaped high from the ground. He beat his mountain-like shoulders. He danced. "The idea that came to me is very good. A good idea occurred to me," he thought, and with a smile, he was in a state of ecstasy, like one possessed. Dancing, forgetting oneself, is a result of immense joy. "Worshipping, prostrating, and dancing are expressions of excessive joy," says Sivagnana Siddhiyar. Thinnanar embraced Lord Shiva. Thinnanar embraced Lord Shiva with love. He made Shiva's work his own work. Standing in the grace of Shiva's inspiration, he worshipped and adored Shiva, forgetting himself. Now, seeing Shiva's disease cured, he danced with joy. "He attained the supreme peace of the Jivanmukta state," is the commentary of Thiru K. Sadasiva Chettiar. Now, as

Thinnanar was in this state of joy, the next event occurred. Sekkizhar Peruman describes it in his verses: "The generous one who applied his own eye to the Lord's right eye, to further demonstrate his goodness, saw blood flowing profusely from the Lord's other eye." He was born into the hunter clan due to their great penance in this world, and his principles were superior to those of the celestials. "After seeing this, I am ruined! My Kalathi Lord's one eye has a wound with blood, and now the other eye is also bleeding profusely. But I am not afraid of this. I have found the medicine! I still have one eye left. I will pluck out that eye and apply it to cure this," he declared. As he pondered the implications of plucking out his own eye and applying it to the Lord's eye, he placed his left foot on the Lord's eye, and with a heart full of love, he took a single arrow. Thinnanar did not hesitate to pierce his own eye with it. The Lord of the red-eyed white bull, the Lord who ruled over Thinnanar, the Lord of Thirukalathi, could not bear to see this wonder. He stretched out his hand, adorned with three layers of serpent bracelets, and with an ambrosial voice, declared, "Stop, Kannappa! Stop!" These are the verses composed by Sekkizhar Peruman. Thinnanar, the generous one, who had applied his own right eye to stop the blood from the Lord's right eye, now stood as a testament to his generosity. Earlier, in a verse, Sekkizhar Peruman referred to Kalathinathar as "Vallalar" (generous one). Now, in this verse, he refers to Thinnanar as "Vallalar." "Vallal" means one who bestows what is desired by those who seek, without any self-interest, out of compassion, as stated in Sivagnanabodha Sittrurai. God possesses the compassion to grant all living beings what they desire, as they desire it. Therefore, He is Vallalar. But because Kannappa Nayanar possessed a generosity similar to that of God, granting what was desired, Sekkizhar Peruman also calls him Vallalar. Other so-called generous people in the world give what they possess outwardly, desiring fame and other such things. But Kannappa Nayanar gave his eye, which is a supreme organ, without desiring anything else. Therefore, he is Vallalar. Even though Thinnanar had given his right eye, and had only one eye remaining, to prevent us from doubting whether he would give that remaining eye as well, the Lord, as the foremost, had another compassionate thought. He now showed a scene where blood flowed from his other eye, his left eye. Thinnanar, born into the hunter clan due to their great penance, and whose principles were superior to those of the celestials, saw blood flowing from the Lord's left eye. Now, Thinnanar was not afraid when he saw blood flowing from the Lord's left eye. Because Thinnanar thought, "I know the medicine for this disease, I have experienced it. And I have that medicine with me now. I still have one eye left. Therefore, I will pluck out that eye and apply it to the Lord's eye to cure this disease of the Lord." Thinnanar was superior to the celestials in his principles. All celestials worship the Lord for some benefit. But Thinnanar worshipped the Lord as the embodiment of love, without desiring anything else.

Therefore, Thinnanar is far superior to the celestials in such principles. Furthermore, doesn't it mention "Umbar Melor" here? "Kolhaiyin Umbar Melor" - superior to the celestials in principle. "Umbar" means celestials. Among all celestials, Thirumal is the foremost. Even Thirumal once offered his eye as an archana to Lord Shiva. Thirumal would daily worship Lord Shiva by reciting Shiva's thousand names and offering a thousand lotus flowers. One day, Lord Shiva made one of those thousand lotus flowers disappear. Thirumal, who had worshipped with 999 flowers and 999 names, found himself short of one flower. So, Thirumal then plucked out his lotus-like eye and offered it to Lord Shiva as a lotus flower. However, Thirumal did this to obtain his Chakra weapon. Lord Shiva also granted Thirumal the Chakra weapon. But here, Thinnanar plucked out his eye and applied it to the Lord purely out of his unadulterated love and devotion for the Lord, without any other benefit in mind. That is why Thinnanar is superior to the celestials in principle. The verse implies that he is even superior to Thirumal, who is considered the foremost among the celestials. Now, Sekkizhar Swamigal here refers to Lord Shiva as "Kan Nuthal Kadavul" (the God with an eye on the forehead). Lord Shiva's right eye removes the bonds of living beings. His left eye is the eye of grace, which makes things Shiva-like. The eye on His forehead is the eye of wisdom, which bestows the experience of Sivananda upon living beings. Thinnanar now resolves to pluck out his remaining eye, his left eye, and apply it to the Lord. If he were to remove his remaining left eye, he would not know where the Lord's left eye was to apply it. Therefore, to identify that spot, he placed his foot on the Lord's left eye as a mark. Furthermore, after an eye is plucked out, it will only adhere to the other flesh, eye to eye, if it is applied immediately, before its heat dissipates and the blood cools. Therefore, as a precautionary measure, Thinnanar placed his foot on the Lord's left eye to mark it, fearing that if he searched for the Lord's left eye after plucking out his own, it would cause delay and he would be unable to apply the eye. Now, Thinnanar tried to bring his sharp arrow near his left eye. At that moment, the Lord could not bear that action. The Lord, who had endured Thinnanar plucking out his right eye and applying it, could not bear it when he tried to pluck out his left eye. This is because the Lord's right eye is on the right side of the Lord. But the Lord's left eye belongs to Ambika. It is the compassionate part of our Mother, who gave birth to us. Therefore, Ambika, who is the embodiment of the Lord's grace, could not bear Thinnanar's attempt to pluck out that left eye, which is the compassionate part of the Mother. So, immediately, the Lord extended His divine hand, grasped Thinnanar's hand, which was ready to pluck out his left eye, and said three times, "Stop, Kannappa! Stop, Kannappa! Stop, Kannappa!" The Lord caused a hand to emerge from the Sivalinga form of Kudumithevar, and with that divine hand, He held Kannappa's hand with the arrow,

and stopped him from performing that action. "Stop, Kannappa! Stop, Kannappa! Stop, Kannappa!" He said three times with an ambrosial voice. "When the Lord of the hunters plucked out his eye and applied it, and when the Lord, who loved the offering of flesh, came forward and held him, the wise sages saw it. The celestials, led by Brahma, showered a rain of fragrant flowers. The Vedas roared." This is the next verse. Thus, Sivakochariyar, who was hidden there, saw the Lord's divine hand holding Thinnanar's hands. This great sage, Sivakochariyar, had received the wisdom to see and understand this event through the grace of the Lord. Therefore, he became a wise sage. Sivakochariyar witnessed the overflowing love of Kannappa Nayanar. Through Sivakochariyar, the Lord also granted us, other souls, the grace to witness the overflowing love and prowess of Kannappa Nayanar. From the heavens, the celestials, led by Brahma, showered a rain of Kalpaka flowers. The celestials praised Him. All the Vedas praised Him. Now, Lord Shiva bestowed His great and enduring grace upon Kannappa Nayanar, saying, "O one with unparalleled love, O one without equal, always stand by my right side!" Is there any greater blessing than this? Is there any other blessing than this? asks Sekkizhar Peruman. This refers to the highest state of Shiva Sayujya. Since Kannappa Nayanar, through his gracious wisdom, became free from the three impurities, became one with the Lord in that path, and performed the Lord's service, what he attained was Paramukti (ultimate liberation). It should not be understood as Samipya Padam (proximity to God) just because the Lord said, "Always stand by my right side." Because Sekkizhar Peruman himself asks from his divine words, "Is there any greater blessing than this, the highest blessing attained by souls?" Doesn't he ask, "Is there any greater blessing than this? Is there anything beyond this?" This refers to the state of Mukti, which is Shiva Sayujya. "What is the use of learning if one does not worship the good feet of the Pure One?" asks Thiruvalluva Deva Nayanar, posing a question. The greatest benefit of education is to worship the feet of the Lord. Is there any other greater benefit from education than worshipping the feet of the Lord? Similarly, Sekkizhar Peruman asks here, "Is there any other benefit to be gained beyond this?" and answers, "No." The highest blessing of Shiva Sayujya, attained by Thinnappan, who rose to become Kannappa Nayanar, is the maximum blessing that all souls can attain. He is Kannappar, the Nambi of abundant glory. Therefore, the meaning of Thirukkural, "He attained the ultimate benefit of learning," is also present here. The idea is that Kannappar received a greater blessing than anyone else. It is for this reason that Kannappa Nayanar has been praised by Paramacharya Murthigal like Thiruvathavur Adigal and all other great people. The Lord grants Kannappa Nayanar, "Stand by my right side." The word "valam" (right) also means superiority and victory. Therefore, the greatest superiority and victory is to become Shiva through love. To become Shiva through love is Shiva's right. So, the implication here is also that the Lord said, "You will stand on my right side." Its meaning is, "You, who are the embodiment of love, stand in non-duality."

My note here is that this is the Shuddha Advaita Siddhanta Mukti described by Saiva Siddhanta. It is because Kannappa Nayanar attained this blessing, which is difficult for anyone else to obtain, that all Saiva Acharyas have praised and adored Kannappa Nayanar. It is here that Thirugnanasambandha Peruman sings with melting devotion, praising Kannappa Nayanar, "Did Kannappa Nayanar walk with his sandals soaked?" Thirunavukkarasu Peruman praises Kannappa Nayanar. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal sings, "I am a servant to Kannappar, the Nambi of abundant glory." Manikkavasagar Peruman sings in Thirukkothumbi in Thiruvachagam, "After seeing that there is no love equal to Kannappan's, my Father, who is incomparable, took me into His grace and commanded me to come, O bee, go and hum to the Lord with the white ash, who is the embodiment of compassion." Nakkirar Deva Nayanar and other Sangam poets also sing about Kannappa Nayanar. The separate work "Thirukkannappa Devar Thirumaram," composed by Kallada Deva Nayanar and Nakkirar Deva Nayanar, is found in the 11th Thirumurai. Not only in Tamil, but also in Sanskrit, the greatness of Kannappa Devar Nayanar has been sung by Adi Shankara in the 63rd verse of his Sivananda Lahari. Adi Shankara praises Kannappa Nayanar. The sandals worn by Kannappa Nayanar, soaked in his path, become as sacred as the darbha grass placed on the head before the abhishekam of Paramashivamurthy. Being moistened by the water gargled from Kannappa Devar Nayanar's mouth becomes like the divine abhishekam that burned down the three cities. The leftover portion of the meat that Kannappa Devar Nayanar tasted a little becomes like a fresh neivedyam. What a wonder! A hunter living in the forest becomes a great devotee. Is this not what "devotion can do anything" means? Thus, Adi Shankara praises and adores Kannappa Devar Nayanar. Similarly, Thirugnanasambandha Peruman's Thevaram also expresses this sentiment. Thirugnanasambandha Peruman praises in Thevaram, "The hunter who worships with his mouth as a kalasam, whose eye is a flower, who plucks it out with an arrow and joins the feet of the Lord, is the mountain of Kalathi." Following Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's praise, "I am a servant to Kannappar, the Nambi of abundant glory," Nambiyandar Nambigal praises him in Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi. Sekkizhar Peruman composes and sings the Puranam of Kannappa Nayanar in 181 verses in Periya Puranam. Thus, Sekkizhar sings the Puranam of Kannappa Devar Nayanar. Sekkizhar Peruman concludes the sacred story of Kannappa Devar Nayanar by stating, "Wearing on my head the sacred feet of Kannappa Nayanar, the chief who had the blessing of replacing the red blood flowing from the wound in the eyes of the Lord residing in Thirukalathi with his own eye, I shall now begin to narrate the sacred service of Kalayanar Nayanar in Thirukkadavur, where Lord Shiva resides." We too shall continue to contemplate the Puranam of Thirukkadavur Kalayanar Nayanar, also known as Kungiliya Kalayanar. I am a servant to Kannappar, the Nambi of abundant glory. Praise to the sacred feet of Kannappa Devar Nayanar. Praise to the sacred feet of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Praise to the sacred feet of Nambiyandar Nambigal. Praise to the sacred feet of Sekkizhar Peruman. Praise to the sacred feet of Sivakavimani Ayya, who wrote the commentary on Periya Puranam. Thiruchitrambalam.
