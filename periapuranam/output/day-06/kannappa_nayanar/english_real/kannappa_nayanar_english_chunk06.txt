Chapter 26 — Kannappa Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 149, Ilai Malinda Sarukkam, Kannappa Nayanar Puranam. Thinnanar, holding meat in one hand and a bow and arrow in the other, with his mouth full of water from the Ponmugali river, and flowers and green leaves tucked into his hair for the Lord, rushes swiftly towards Kudumi Thevar. He hurries, thinking that the Lord might be hungry. Upon arriving, he laments, "My Lord has become weak," and approaches Kudumi Thevar very closely. Thinnanar removes the old flowers from Kudumi Thevar's sacred head with his beautiful sandal-clad foot. The beginning of the daily puja is the removal of the previous day's Nirmalyam (withered flowers and offerings). Therefore, according to that rule, Kannappa Nayanar first removed the flowers from the Lord's head with his sandal-clad foot. Nannan did not mention removing old flowers. What did Nannan say? Nannan only said that a Brahmin would come, place flowers and leaves on the Lord, perform Abhishekam with water, offer food, and then leave. Nannan did not mention removing old flowers. But Thinnanar has the fragrance of the pujas he performed in his previous births, doesn't he? Because of that fragrance, he had the inner urge that old flowers should be removed and Abhishekam performed. So, he removes the old flowers that were already on the Lord's sacred head. Since he held the bow and arrow in one hand and the food for the Lord in the other, he used his foot to remove the old flowers from the Lord's sacred head. After removing the old flowers, it is customary to perform Abhishekam for the Lord. Therefore, he performs Abhishekam for the Lord with the water he brought in his mouth from the Ponmugali river. He spat the sacred water from his mouth onto the Lord. Thinnanar used his mouth as a vessel, filling it with water. He spat the water onto the Lord as if spitting out the love that arose within him. Sekkizhar Swamigal uses love itself as a metaphor for this act, which was born of love. He says that spitting the sacred water from his mouth was like spitting out the love he held. Because, as we saw in the previous verses, Thinnanar is the embodiment of love. Therefore, Sekkizhar Swamigal sings that even what Thinnanar, who was the embodiment of love, spat out, seemed like he was spitting out love itself. Kacchiyappa Sivachariyar later expresses the same idea in the sixth verse of the Aatruppadalam in the Kandapuranam. Kacchiyappa Sivachariyar says in that verse that the dark cloud raining on the mountain was like Thinnanar spitting the sacred water he held in his mouth onto the Lord. Here, Sekkizhar Swamigal sings, "He spat the sacred water from his mouth onto the head of the pure Lord, as if spitting out the love he had produced." In this verse, Sekkizhar refers to the Lord as Vimalanar. The Lord is naturally free from imperfections. Therefore, he is called Amalan, Nirmalan, and Vimalan. So, the Lord is Vimalanar. Therefore, purifying him by performing Abhishekam is not necessary. But the Lord prefers to be immersed in love. That is why he lovingly accepted Thinnanar's sacred mouth-water Abhishekam. Is it inappropriate for Thinnanar to perform Abhishekam for the Lord with his sacred mouth-water? No, it is not inappropriate.

Because the Lord is Vimalan, naturally free from attachments. Thinnanar is the embodiment of love. Therefore, it is not inappropriate. But when the later Sivachariyar, Sivakochariyar, saw all these actions of Thinnanar and worried that it was inappropriate, the Lord appeared in his dream and told him, "All his actions are love towards us," explaining to Sivakochariyar that it was a pure act, not an inappropriate one. Then Thinnanar, with reverence, placed the sacred flowers and leaves (Thiruppallithamam) he had carried on his head, on the Lord's sacred head. The head is the highest part of the body. Above that is the crown. And on the crown is the hair tuft. Therefore, Thinnanar placed the Thiruppallithamam, meant for Kudumi Thevar, even higher than that. Sekkizhar Peruman sings, "He placed the Thiruppallithamam, carried on his head, on the head of the Lord, our Master, on the great Kalatthi mountain, with reverence." Thinnanar held the bow and arrow in one hand and the meat-nectar in the other. Therefore, it was not possible to place them with his hands. So, he bowed, bringing his head close to the Lord's sacred head, and let the Thiruppallithamam (flowers and leaves) he carried on his head fall onto the Lord's sacred head. Sekkizhar Swamigal calls this "Saatthi" (placing). He also says "Vanangi" (bowing). Thinnanar bowed so that the flowers he held on his head would fall onto the Lord's sacred head. This also indicates the method of worship where one should bow when offering flowers to the Lord. After doing this, Thinnanar offers the meat-nectar he brought to the Lord. Thinnanar says to the Lord, "My Lord, for you, I have selected all the good flesh, strung it on my arrow, cooked it over a fire until it reached the right consistency, then tasted it with my teeth, and brought delicious meat. This is very beautiful. Therefore, you must partake of it." Saying this, he offers the meat-nectar to the Lord. A great desire arises in Thinnanar's mind to offer more nectar to the Lord in the same way. At that moment, the sun set below the western mountain. Evening came, followed by night. The sun, knowing Thinnanar's desire to offer more nectar to the Lord, set in the western mountain with the desire to help Thinnanar. It set in the western mountain now, with the desire to rise again, dispel the night, and show Thinnanar the nectar needed for the Lord with its rays. The evening twilight arrives. Thinnanar knows that fierce animals roam here at night. Due to his great love for the Lord, Thinnanar stands like a large dark mountain close to the Lord, so that no harm comes to the Lord from such fierce animals. It is common for fierce animals to roam in the forest at night. Therefore, Thinnanar stands guard close to the Lord, not leaving him for even a moment, with his bow and arrow ready in his hand to prevent any fierce animals from harming the Lord. Not only that, Thinnanar stands guard with the desire for the next day to dawn so that he can offer nectar to the Lord again. Sekkizhar Swamigal sings about how he stands guard: "He stood, looking directly, having seen with overflowing love, with boundless affection, the one whom even sages and celestials, who perform difficult austerities and reach the dark mountain forest, find hard to see."

He sings, "He stood, dispelling the darkness." Who is Kudumi Thevar, the Lord enshrined here? He is Lord Shiva, whom even sages and celestials find difficult to see. Sages (Munivargal) means those who are contemplative. Celestials (Amarargal) means those who prevent death by consuming the elixir of immortality and so on. Thus, Lord Shiva is difficult for both sages and celestials to see. What do these sages do? They perform all kinds of austerities. Sages are those who go to forests and mountains and perform difficult austerities. Austerities (Thavangal) include vows, yogas, etc. Thavam means to burn, to torment. The name Thavam came to refer to these practices, which are performed to torment the body and control the senses. Austerities are performed in many ways, such as staying amidst fires in summer and in water in winter. Pattinatthu Adigal describes how sages perform austerities in Thiruvidaimarudur Mummanikkovai: "Leaving behind the many people, wives, and relatives of this wide world, abandoning homes and other things, entering unimaginable forests and mountains, raising their hands above their heads in summer, bending one leg, standing amidst five types of fire, and then in the rainy season and snowy season, immersing themselves in water for long periods, wearing matted hair, plucking their hair, abandoning clothes, starving themselves, eating fruits, roots, wind-blown leaves, and drinking air and water, sleeping on barren land and stones, they torment their weakening bodies, all to attain liberation." This is from Thiruvidaimarudur Mummanikkovai. Sages perform austerities in these ways. But even with such austerities, our Lord Shiva is difficult to see. Because these austerities are performed with the ego of self-awareness. Therefore, the Lord is rare for austerities performed with self-awareness. When self-awareness is subsumed in Shiva-awareness due to abundant love, the Lord appears easily and bestows grace. Therefore, Lord Shiva, who is difficult for sages to see through their austerities performed with self-awareness, and difficult for celestials to know, was easily found by Thinnanar here. Because Thinnanar's love was boundless love, ever-growing and overflowing. Manifesting this love, Thinnanar stands guarding the Lord, constantly looking at him. He stands guarding, looking directly at the Lord. "Looking directly" means to gaze with absorption, where the seer, the seen, and the act of seeing do not appear as separate but as one. This is explained as Nishthai (meditative absorption). In Sanskrit, this is called the state of Thirupputti, where the distinctions of Gnatru (knower), Gnanam (knowledge), and Gneyam (known) do not arise. In any experience, these three elements exist: the knower, his knowledge, and the object to be known. Here, in the Shiva-experience, where Shiva and the soul are deeply merged without any difference, these three also exist. However, the distinct nature of these three does not appear. That is, the soul, as the knower, is immersed in knowing only Shiva, without knowing itself or its knowledge. In this state, the soul, as Gnatru, will be in the state of Gnana, knowing only Shiva, the Gneya object. In that state,

without separating itself and its knowledge by thinking, "I know this," it remains immersed in the Shiva experience. This clarifies that Shiva-experience is the state where the soul, as Gnatru, without knowing itself and its knowledge, remains immersed in knowing only Shiva, the Gneya. This state of forgetting oneself, being immersed in Shiva, and reveling in Shiva-bliss, while still in this body in this world, is called Nishthai. It is in that state that Thinnanar now stands near the Lord. He is constantly looking at Kudumi Thevar, the Lord. Thinnanar has forgotten that he exists. He has also forgotten that he is looking at the Lord. He is immersed in the Shiva-experience, thinking only of the Lord. How is Thinnanar? How long does he remain? Sekkizhar Peruman sings, "He stood until the long darkness was dispelled." He stood like that until the long night passed. This darkness must be dispelled. Dawn must break. He stood for the long night to pass, with the thought that when dawn breaks, he must offer nectar to the Lord again. Furthermore, in Saiva Siddhanta, darkness (irul) is referred to as the Anava Malam (ego impurity). Therefore, "He stood until the long darkness was dispelled" implies that the long-standing Anava Malam, which has been eternally bound to the soul, was dispelled. Because the Anava Malam was dispelled, and because the soul attained the merciful gaze of Lord Shiva, the previous bond of birth was removed, and thus, with the long darkness dispelled, he remained united with Shiva. This is the implication. Furthermore, another inner meaning for "He stood until the long darkness was dispelled" can be that he stood not only for himself but also for the darkness of other souls to be dispelled. It implies that Thinnanar stood there so that the Anava Malam, which binds other souls, would be dispelled, and other souls in this world would also see the state of love, have their Anava darkness dispelled, and attain salvation. Sekkizhar Swamigal continues to describe how the Kalatthi mountain was during that night when Thinnanar stood guard. Even in that intense night, the moonlight filled the Kalatthi mountain. In the caves of the Kalatthi mountain, the red glow of the rubies emitted by snakes shone on one side. Furthermore, various gems and jewels on the Kalatthi mountain also shone even in the dead of night. On that Kalatthi mountain, there were trees that glowed at night, called Jyoti Vrikshas. These are considered divine trees. It is said that these Jyoti Vrikshas are found in miraculous sacred mountains. They are considered one of the Kayasiddhi herbs. Similarly, it is said that Jyoti Grass (Jyoti Pullu) and Jyoti Creeper (Jyoti Valli) also exist. Thus, the Jyoti Vrikshas on the Kalatthi mountain also glow at night. Pearls and gems kept by female monkeys in their homes to provide light at night also glitter, creating light there. Weaver birds in their nests also have the habit of keeping fireflies and other luminous insects to provide light at night. So, such lights also exist there. Above all these, the ascetic light of the sages who perform austerities, controlling their five senses, glows on that Kalatthi mountain. This is the light of wisdom emanating from those sages. We read in scriptures that even those who see these sages from afar, due to the power of their austerities, perceive their bodies as effulgent. Thus, with various kinds of lights appearing on the Kalatthi mountain in the dead of night, it seemed as if it were not night at all, and the Kalatthi mountain shone like a beacon. Within Thinnanar's heart, there was no inner darkness at all. Similarly, outside, on the Kalatthi mountain, there was no darkness either. Thus, as the entire night passed and the hours dwindled, birds began to chirp and fly. Thinnanar, the brave warrior who stood like a dark sea without sleep, thinking that it was now time to offer the meat-nectar to his Lord, sets out for hunting on the Kalatthi mountain. We will continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 27 — Kannappa Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 150, Ilai Malinda Sarukkam, Kannappa Nayanar Puranam. Thinnanar, who had guarded Kudumi Thevar throughout the night, at the break of dawn, thinking it was the right time for hunting, went hunting. He intended to bring meat-nectar and offer it to the Lord. Thinnanar bowed to Kudumi Thevar and set out for the hunt. At this early hour, as the sun slowly rose, spreading its rays, a sage named Sivakochariyar arrived there. He was a Shiva Brahmin. "He brought flowers, water, and other items according to the puja prescribed in the revered Agamas, to worship the mountain medicine (Lord Shiva) with the blue throat, a sage of great penance, Sivakochariyar." This is a verse by Sekkizhar. Sivakochariyar, a sage of penance, who regularly worshiped Lord Kalatthi, the medicine arising on the mountain, according to the Shiva Agamas, arrived there with flowers, water, and other puja items suitable for the Shiva puja rituals mentioned in the Shiva Agamas. Sivakochariyar, a Shiva Brahmin, regularly performed Parartha Puja (worship for the benefit of others) for Kudumi Thevar, who is enshrined on the Kalatthi mountain, according to the Agamic tradition. He was a devotee of great penance. The name Sivakochariyar means one who always contemplates Shiva. "Kochariththal" means to be the object of knowledge, to meditate. Shiva is beyond speech and mind, yet Sivakochariyar knows that Shiva through Shiva-knowledge, making Shiva an object of his awareness. Sekkizhar refers to him as "the sage Sivakochariyar," adding an epithet. His penance is the penance of worship. It is the penance that serves as the cause for Shiva worship. Worship itself is called Shiva Puja, and Shiva Puja is considered penance. To perform Shiva Puja, one needs prior penance. Thus, Sivakochariyar, a Shiva Brahmin, is a great ascetic who has performed both past and present penance. He performs the daily Parartha Puja for the Lord enshrined on the Kalatthi mountain according to the Shiva Agamas. Sekkizhar Peruman refers to Kudumi Thevar as "the medicine enshrined on the mountain." The medical texts state that medicines found on mountains are excellent and special. Here, the Lord, the medicine enshrined on the Kalatthi mountain, is the medicine that cures the terrible disease of birth, which is the most dreadful of all diseases. The Lord is both the medicine and the physician who uses that medicine to cure the ailment. "He is the mantra, the tantra, and the medicine, capable of curing incurable diseases," says Thiruppullirukkuvelur Thiruthandakam. Thus, Sivakochariyar worships Kudumi Thevar, the medicine on the mountain, according to the Shiva Agamas, as the Lord desires. In the Puranam of Thirukurippuththondar Nayanar, it is said that "the truth desired by the Lord, who spoke countless Agamas, is worship." Accordingly, Sivakochariyar performs the Parartha Puja, which is done according to the Shiva Agamas and is greatly pleasing to the Lord. Generally, the term "Agamas" refers to Shiva Agamas. There are Saiva Agamas, Vaishnava Agamas, and Shakta Agamas. However, the term "Agamas" by convention refers to Shiva Agamas. For Vaishnavites, there are two Agamas: Pancharatra and Vaikhanasa. However,

it is the Vaishnava tradition to refer to Vaikhanasa Agamas as Vaikhanasa Sutras and Pancharatra Agamas as Pancharatra Samhitas. Similarly, it is the Shakta tradition to refer to Shakta Agamas as Tantras. Therefore, "Agamas" generally refers to Shiva Agamas. The Saiva Siddhanta principle is that the Vedas are general, and the Agamas are specific. Saiva Siddhanta states that the Vedas are for all people in the world, and the Agamas are for those who have attained Shakti Nipada (descent of divine grace). The Agamas describe Pati (Lord), Pasu (soul), and Pasam (bondage), and outline the paths for souls, entangled in bondage, to be freed from them, know the Lord, attain liberation (moksha), and experience bliss. Thus, the Agamas are poetic works that explain the three entities of Pati, Pasu, and Pasam, enabling souls to realize the truth and live righteously to attain salvation. In the word "Agama," "A" stands for Shiva Gnanam (divine knowledge), "Ka" for Mukti (liberation), and "Ma" for Mala Nasam (destruction of impurities). Therefore, Agama is a scripture that destroys impurities in souls, produces Shiva Gnanam, and is a means to Mukti. There are a total of 28 Shiva Agamas. Thirumoolar's Thirumandiram, in the 57th verse of the first Tantra, "Agama Sirappu," states, "The Lord with the dark body, who has a part of the Goddess, has 23 Agamas." This refers to the 28 Agamas. These 28 Agamas are as follows: Kamikam, Yogajam, Chintiyam, Karanam, Ajitham, Deeptham, Sookshmam, Sahasram, Amshuman, Suprabhedam, Vijayam, Nisvasam, Svayambhuva, Analam, Veeram, Rauravam, Makutam, Vimalam, Chandragnanam, Mukhabimbam, Protkeetham, Lalitham, Siddham, Santana, Sarvottamam, Paramesvaram, Kiranam, and Vatulam. There are 207 Upagamas (sub-Agamas), from Narasingam to Visvanmikam, for these 28 Agamas. These 207 are Upagamas. It is said that these 28 Agamas and Upagamas were revealed by Lord Shiva through the five faces on the crown of his Maha Sadashiva Murti form. Thiruvachakam's Keerthithiru Agaval states, "He revealed the Agamas spoken on the great Mahendra mountain," and "He received the great Agamas," and "He commanded them through the five faces on Mahendra." Each of these 28 Agamas has four sections: Gnana (knowledge), Yoga (meditation), Kriya (rituals), and Charya (conduct). Both the Vedas and Shiva Agamas are divine pronouncements. Therefore, the Vedas and Shiva Agamas are self-valid, without any higher authority. Shiva Agamas are called "Vedanta Thelivam Saiva Siddhantam" (the clear essence of Vedanta, Saiva Siddhanta). It is the Saiva tradition to refer to the Vedas as Sutras and the Agamas as their commentaries. Shiva Agamas describe all things in such a way as to reveal the Lord to souls and unite souls with the Lord. Therefore, Shiva Agamas are called the ultimate conclusion or Siddhanta. In the Gnana Pada (knowledge section) of these Agamas, the three entities of Pati, Pasu, and Pasam are explained. The Yoga Pada describes Shiva Yoga. The Kriya Pada describes the initiation of mantras, Sandhyavandanam, Puja, Japa, Homa, etc. The Charya Pada describes the religious observances. In Shiva temples built according to the Agamas, daily, occasional, and special pujas, as well as temple construction, consecration, karmic observances for souls, initiation, Atmaratha Puja, etc., must all be performed according to the Agamas. All these matters mentioned above are found only in the Agamas. They are not found in the Vedas or other scriptures. In Periya Puranam, in the Puranam of Poosana Nayanar, the temple built by Poosalar Nayanar in his mind, and the stone temple built by the Kadava king mentioned in his story, along with their consecrations, were all established according to the Agamas. We also saw in Thirunagara Sirappu that Manuneethi Chozhan ruled in such a way that the pujas in Shiva temples were performed according to the Agamas. In Thiruvarur, the Chola kings established many endowments for the Lord who resided in the anthill (Puttidam Konda Nathar), ensuring that the Vedic and Agamic rituals were performed. We can also see in Naminandi Adigal's Puranam that arrangements were made for the special pujas of Panguni Uthiram for Veethi Vidanga Peruman. Furthermore, we will see in the Puranam of Thirukurippu Thonda Nayanar the nature of the Parartha Puja performed by Umaiyamaiyar according to all the Agamic rules. We will also see in the Puranam of Chandeesvara Nayanar the nature of the Atmaratha Puja performed by Chandeesvara Nayanar according to the Agamas. The Lord graciously taught all these special Agamas to Umaiyamaiyar. After listening to them, the Lord also graciously told the Goddess that the truth desired by the Lord, who spoke countless Agamas, is worship. Based on that, the Goddess performs her daily puja, guiding souls to attain worldly pleasures and liberation, and bestowing boons. These can be found in verses 50 to 71 of the Thirukurippu Thonda Nayanar Puranam. Here, in the Kannappa Nayanar Puranam, we will see that Sivakochariyar performed puja and other rituals according to the Agamic rules, and that Thinnanar also followed the same path, performing Abhishekam and other worship, and that the Lord bestowed grace upon both Thinnanar, who became Kannappa Nayanar, and Sivakochariyar. As usual, Sivakochariyar arrived today to perform the Parartha Puja for the Lord. When he approached the Lord and saw roasted meat and bones in front of the Lord's sanctum, he was greatly distressed. He ran away, stepping over them, crying out, "Oh, I am ruined! Who did this inappropriate act?" Immediately, Sivakochariyar thought that hunters must have done this. He thought that hunters, who are not afraid to come directly in front of the Lord, must have done this. The Shiva Agamas, which prescribe temple construction, consecration, and puja, have rules about who should come to the sanctum and to what extent. According to those rules, hunters are not allowed to come directly. But Sivakochariyar was heartbroken that hunters, who are not allowed to come directly in front of the Lord, had come without fear of Shiva's offense. Sivakochariyar performed puja according to the Shiva Agamas, so he was distressed and heartbroken to see an act that violated those rules. Furthermore, Sivakochariyar first wondered who did this act. He only saw the event that occurred here. He did not see the person who did it. There was roasted meat and bones in front of the Lord's sanctum, so he concluded by inference that hunters, who are not afraid to kill, must have done this. Moreover, the footprints of a hunter wearing sandals and the footprints of a dog were also there, which supported his conclusion. Thus, Sivakochariyar, distressed, addressed the Lord, "O Lord of Lords, how can they commit this inappropriate act in front of you and leave? How can you allow them to do such inappropriate things and come and go?" He cried out in great sorrow. Sivakochariyar addresses the Lord as "Deva Devesana" (Lord of Lords), doesn't he?

The Lord is the great Lord of all gods. The Lord, the God of gods, has the nature to punish the gods when they commit offenses against Shiva. Therefore, it implies that the Lord, the God of gods, has the power and nature to inflict appropriate punishment for this offense against Shiva committed by the hunters. Even so, Sivakochariyar concluded that this act could not have happened without the Lord's divine consent. That is why he asks the Lord, "How can you, O Lord, allow such inappropriate things to happen?" He asks the Lord, crying out in distress. If this act had happened without the Lord's divine consent, then both the act and the perpetrator would have been destroyed, like what happened in Daksha's Yagna. But this inappropriate act has happened completely. Therefore, the Lord's divine consent must be there. Sivakochariyar asks the Lord, crying out in distress, "Is it beautiful for you to allow such inappropriate things to happen? Is this becoming of you?" However, the time for Shiva Puja is getting delayed, isn't it? Sivakochariyar is an ascetic sage who performs puja and meditates according to the Agamas. Therefore, he performs both external and internal worship of the Lord. In the internal worship, the Atmaratha Puja, he worships the Lord as a great light. That great light is enshrined here on the Kalatthi mountain as a blazing flame. Parartha Puja must be performed for this blazing light. He is distressed that the time for it is delayed. He thinks that this inappropriate act has happened, and the Lord's puja is being delayed. So, Sivakochariyar decides that he must remove this meat and bones and perform purification. Therefore, he removes the meat and bones that were there. He cleanses the footprints of the hunter wearing sandals and the footprints of the dog with a sacred broom. The author has not mentioned until now that a dog came with Thinnanar. But here, by saying that Sivakochariyar sees the footprints of a dog, we understand that a dog must have come with Thinnanar. The same idea is found in Thirukkannappa Thevar Maram, written by Nakkirar Deva Nayanar. After removing all these inappropriate things, Sivakochariyar descends the mountain and bathes in the sacred waters of the Ponmugali river. He had touched and removed the inappropriate items like meat, bones, and other remains. Therefore, to cleanse himself from the impurity caused by touching them, he again immersed himself in the Ponmugali river and bathed in its sacred waters. This implies that the sacred Ponmugali river has the power to purify both body and soul. After bathing in the sacred waters of the Ponmugali river, he collects the necessary items for the Lord from there and reaches the Lord's sanctum. Although Sivakochariyar had brought all the necessary puja items when he first came, they were now considered impure. Therefore, he discarded them and, after bathing in the Ponmugali river, brought the flowers and water available there. If he were to collect all the puja items again, it would take a long time. So, he took the flowers and leaves available at that moment and came for the puja. He came to the Lord's sanctum. The Agamic rule is that if there are any defects in the daily puja, a ritual to rectify them must be performed before starting the daily puja. Therefore, Sivakochariyar performs purification for Lord Shiva there and then begins the puja. "Pavithram" means purity, sanctity. The actions performed during the Pavithra Puja create purity.

Therefore, it is called Pavithram. Thus, rectifying the faults that occurred in Shiva Puja is called Pavithram. It is also commonly referred to as Prayaschittam (expiation). The rules for this are all stated in the Shiva Agamas. These include Punniyagam, Snapana, Shanti Homam, Japa, etc. These are called Pavithram here. If any deficiency occurs in the daily pujas in Shiva temples, the expiation performed immediately for that deficiency is called Nithyangam. This is done as an expiation for known errors in Shiva Puja, leading to a deficiency in the daily ritual. However, to perform expiation for unknown deficiencies, the Pavithrotsavam festival is also prescribed in the Shiva Agamas annually. This is performed as an expiation for unknown errors that occur in the Lord's Parartha Nithya Puja throughout the year, and it is called Pavithrotsavam. Here, Sivakochariyar performs Nithyangam, which is called Pavithram, as an expiation for these known errors, and then proceeds with the puja. We will continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 28 — Kannappa Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 151, Ilai Malinda Sarukkam, Kannappa Nayanar Puranam. Sivakochariyar, the exalted Shiva Brahmin sage, having realized that inappropriate acts had occurred in his daily Parartha Puja, removed all of them. He then descended from the Thirukalatthi mountain, bathed in the sacred Ponmugali river, collected the available items necessary for the Lord's puja, and again reached the sanctum of Kudumi Thevar. Previously, when he first came, he had brought all the necessary items. However, since they were now considered defiled, he bathed in the Ponmugali river and brought the flowers, leaves, and sacred water available there at that moment. It is also an Agamic rule that if certain items are unavailable, one can perform puja by filling the void with love, using the available items. Sivakochariyar, a highly revered sage who performed puja strictly according to the Agamic principles, as we mentioned earlier, performed purification and is now commencing the Parartha Puja. Sivakochariyar, a Saivachariyar, is a Shiva sage and Shiva Brahmin who has learned and understood all the Agamas. He is fully endowed with the characteristics of an Acharya (preceptor) as described in the Agamas. The Agamas state that Acharyas and Sivacharyas who perform Parartha Puja must be knowledgeable in the Vedas, their auxiliary branches, various mantra sastras, tantra sastras, mudras, kriyas, and Shilpa sastras. Furthermore, the Agamas state that Sivacharyas must be of good conduct, possess good qualities, have a good physique, be auspicious, and that only an Adisaiva Shiva Brahmin, fully endowed with these characteristics, is qualified to perform Parartha Shiva temple worship. Moreover, Adisaiva Sivacharyas must possess philosophical knowledge, contemplate the truths of the six Adhvas, be knowledgeable in Bharata Sastra and Ashtanga Yoga, impart Shiva Gnana, have a pure soul through Acharya Abhishekam, be compassionate towards all beings, be learned in astrological sastras, and bring auspiciousness through their words. The Agamas state that those who possess these qualities are the best among Acharyas. Sivakochariyar is endowed with all these characteristics. Currently, Sivakochariyar is performing purification at the sanctum of Kudumi Thevar and beginning the daily puja. First, he performs the removal of Nirmalyam (withered offerings), and then he performs Thirumanjanam (sacred bath) for Kudumi Thevar. While performing Thirumanjanam, he performs the japa, dhyana, and bhavana associated with it, without any deviation, with unity of mind, speech, and body. Sivakochariyar performs all the rituals mentioned in the Shiva Agamas for Kudumi Thevar, such as adorning the Lord's body with sacred clothes after Thirumanjanam, decorating with leaves and flowers, performing Archana (offering flowers), offering food, circumambulating, bowing, and meditating. He praises Lord Shiva with Vedic mantras, as the Vedas repeatedly declare Shiva to be the supreme God. The Vedas praise Lord Shiva in various ways with Vedic mantras, such as Vishva Karanam (cause of the universe), Vishvarupan (universal form), Vishvadhikan (transcendent to the universe), and Vishva Seviyan (worshiped by the universe). Sivakochariyar praises the Lord with all these. Then, as stated in Namakam and Chamakam, he praises Lord Shiva as the supreme God, stating that Shiva is all, and Shiva is not all. There is no one equal to Shiva or greater than him. Shiva possesses eight qualities. Lord Shiva alone is without birth and death. Shiva is the author of all scriptures, including the Vedas and Agamas. Lord Shiva has countless eyes, feet, and heads. He is called by all names and is all. He is the light of lights, the smallest of the small, the greatest of the great. Shiva is Pasupati. He is the God of gods. Sivakochariyar praises Kudumi Thevar, Lord Shiva, according to the Agamas, reciting these Vedic statements and the secret teachings of Atharva Shikha, Shvetashvatara Upanishad, etc. He praises with the eight names such as Bhavan and at the end of the puja, offers Ashtapushpam (eight flowers). After completing the day's pujas in this manner, Sivakochariyar, with a calmed mind, returns to his hermitage. Sekkizhar Peruman refers to Sivakochariyar as having a "calmed mind." A calmed mind means a mind that has conquered evil qualities like lust, conquered the five senses, and is endowed with profound tranquility. This is the nature of the minds of great sages. This is also one of the characteristics of an Acharya. A question might arise in us: "Didn't Sivakochariyar become agitated and distressed when he saw the inappropriate acts in front of Lord Shiva's sanctum?" Sivakavimani explains that this agitation and distress arose from the love for protecting the Saiva principles. Therefore, they are not faults. "Those who do not know say that love is only for righteousness; it is also a support for trees," says Thirukkural, isn't it? Furthermore, in the 1037th verse of Thirugnanasambandar Puranam, Sekkizhar says, "He shone with the principle of destroying hostile religions that are without attachment." Moreover, Thirunandi Devar asks for a boon from the Lord, "A mind that cannot tolerate the condemnation of Vedas and Saivism." Thus, if there is condemnation or deficiency in the Vedic path or Saiva religion, becoming agitated and distressed upon seeing it is a Saiva principle. These are not faults. Because this agitation and distress arise when the dharmas prescribed in the Vedas and Agamas are affected, and they arise without self-interest. Therefore, these are also Saiva principles. Hence, Sivakavimani explains that saying Sivakochariyar was distressed upon seeing the inappropriate acts in Shiva Puja is not at all contradictory to Sekkizhar now describing him as having a calmed mind. Thus, after performing worship in this manner, bowing to the Lord at the end of the worship, and taking leave of the Lord, Sivakochariyar goes to his hermitage. Now, Sekkizhar Peruman begins to sing about Thinnanar. Thinnanar had already set out for hunting to offer nectar to the Lord. Now, Sekkizhar Peruman begins to describe the hunts performed by Thinnanar. Sekkizhar Swamigal sings, "Thus the great sage departed. Now, I will describe the skill of the dark-haired hunter, the great Lord, who bent his bow and hunted alone in the forest, and I will remove the power of evil deeds." Now, Thinnanar is performing a solo hunt. This is not a hunt like the one Thinnanar performed the previous day with many other hunters. Now, Thinnanar is performing a solo hunt. Moreover, this is not like the hunt performed the previous day. This is a hunt performed by Thinnanar, having lost himself to Kudumi Thevar, completely forgetting himself, thinking only of Kudumi Thevar, and for Kudumi Thevar. It is a hunt performed for that unique Lord. Therefore, it is a solo hunt. This is a hunt that occurs by the grace of the Lord. It is not like the cruel hunt performed by other hunters. It is a hunt performed by remembering the Lord, being subservient to him, and by the grace of the Lord. Therefore, this hunt performed by Thinnanar also becomes an act of grace. The Agamas state that by describing the gracious acts of the Lord, our evil deeds will be removed. The conclusion of Gnana Sastras is that describing or listening to the gracious acts of the Lord is one of the means to remove our evil deeds. Therefore, here, since Thinnanar performs this hunt by the grace of the Lord, Sekkizhar Swamigal says that by singing about this hunt, he will remove all evil deeds. Therefore, this hunt is not an evil act of killing. It becomes a good deed that removes our evil deeds, who listen to this hunt performed by Thinnanar. This hunt happened by grace. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman guarantees that the evil deeds of those who read and listen to the glory of the hunt performed by Thinnanar will all be removed. Sekkizhar Peruman describes the gracious glory of the hunt in this way to make the readers focus on the gracious nature of this hunting act, without thinking about any other matter. This is Sekkizhar Peruman's poetic tradition. Earlier, in the Puranam of Thadutthattkonda, when describing the wedding of Sundaramurthi Swamigal, when describing the wedding attire of Sundaramurthi Swamigal, he says that he is going to sing about the wedding attire of Sundaramurthi Swamigal, and by doing so, he will attain salvation. He says in the 173rd verse, "I will describe what happened there, how our Lord, in his beautiful wedding attire, descended from the horse in front of the wedding pandal, where white conches everywhere roared like a great rain, and I attained salvation." There, Sekkizhar Swamigal says that he attained salvation by singing about the wedding attire of Sundaramurthi Swamigal. Similarly, here, Sekkizhar Peruman sings that he will sing about this gracious hunt performed by Thinnanar, and by doing so, he will remove all evil deeds. Thinnanar begins to hunt for the Lord. He hunts large boars that graze in the clearings on the slopes between two mountains. He hunts deer of all kinds. Thus, Thinnanar kills many kinds of boars and deer. Here, Sekkizhar Peruman uses the word "Aruli" (graciously) for the act of killing. Killing is generally considered a sinful act. But Sekkizhar Peruman says that the act of killing itself became an act of grace here. Because these animals, born in lower forms of life, without intellectual clarity, roam in the forest destroying crops and other living beings, and one day they will die. They will be reborn according to their karma. This is what happens to those who take animal bodies. Since they have taken animal bodies, they cannot perform any other karma by violating their limitations. Just as souls who take human bodies can perform Shiva pujas, Shiva worship, and other services with the clarity of intellect in the human body, animals, due to their lack of intellectual clarity and deficiencies in their inner instruments, cannot perform Shiva worship or Shiva pujas like souls in human bodies. This is a limitation of taking animal bodies.

But here, Thinnanar hunts and kills these animals. These animals are killed by Thinnanar's sacred hands. As a result, the bodies of these animals become sacred food pleasing to Lord Shiva. Because they were killed by Thinnanar's sacred hands, and because Thinnanar offers the bodies of these animals as nectar to Lord Shiva, and through Thinnanar's intention and action, these animal bodies, as meat, serve as sacred food for the Lord, those souls attain Shiva Punya (merit). By attaining Shiva Punya in this way, those souls who took animal bodies attain salvation. If they had not been killed by Thinnanar's sacred hands, the opportunity for those animals to realize Lord Shiva and attain salvation by offering their body, wealth, and life for Lord Shiva would never have occurred. Here, Thinnanar, having lost himself, becoming subservient to Shiva, with all his senses becoming divine instruments, and acting by grace, performs his hunting. Therefore, all the lives killed in this gracious act attain salvation by contributing to Shiva Punya. Therefore, this act of killing performed by Thinnanar becomes an act of grace. That is why Sekkizhar Peruman sings "Konru Aruli" (killed graciously) here. The Shiva Agamas have rules specifying which trees, plants, and creepers, and which flowers, leaves, blossoms, and tender shoots should be plucked and used for Shiva Puja. Therefore, devotees of Shiva use such plants from Nandavanams (flower gardens) and other places dedicated to Lord Shiva, taking their parts like flowers, leaves, tender shoots, and roots for Shiva Puja. By offering their parts for Shiva Puja in this way, all those plants attain salvation. Because their parts serve as causes for Shiva Punya and earn Shiva Punya. Just as plants in Nandavanams and other places that assist in Shiva Puja attain salvation, similarly, all the lives killed in the gracious hunt performed by Thinnanar attain salvation. They earn Shiva Punya. If they had not been killed by Thinnanar's sacred hands, this opportunity would not have been available to those souls. That is why Sekkizhar Swamigal sings "Konru Aruli" at this point. Because the hunt Thinnanar is performing now is not like the hunt he performed the previous day with other hunters. Others hunt out of self-awareness, ego, and individual consciousness. For all their hunting activities, there are sins. But what Thinnanar is performing now is a hunt where all his senses have become divine instruments, subservient to grace and Shiva. That is why, when beginning to read about this hunt, Sekkizhar Peruman sings that by singing about this gracious hunt, all evil deeds will be removed. Thus, Thinnanar gathers all the hunted animals in one place. He draws his sword, cuts down dry trees, makes a fire drill, and starts a fire. He plucks teak leaves, stitches them together to form a vessel. He collects honey from small plants and flowers and gathers it in the teak leaf vessel. He collects firewood, creates fire with a fire drill, and then cooks parts of the animal bodies in the fire he has created, using his arrow to separate them.

Thus, he tastes the cooked meat with his pure sacred mouth, and collects the delicious meat in vessels made of teak leaves. The Havish (offering) meant for the gods is first given to Agni Deva (the fire god). Agni Deva then gives this Havish to the gods. Similarly, Thinnanar first tastes the sacred nectar (meat) meant for Thirukalatthinathar with his sacred mouth, and then offers that sacred nectar to Thirukalatthinathar. In Yagnas (fire rituals), the Havish offered to the gods is offered into the sacrificial fire, Agni. Agni Bhagavan removes all impurities from the items offered in the Yagna, purifies the Havish offered in the Yagna, and then delivers them to the respective gods. Similarly, generally, meat is considered inappropriate for the Lord. But that inappropriate meat becomes nectar to be offered to the Lord after it is placed in Thinnanar's sacred mouth. By being placed in Thinnanar's sacred mouth, all the inappropriateness of the meat is destroyed, and it becomes extremely pure, pure enough for the Lord, and becomes sacred nectar for the Lord. The rule is that Thirumanjanam (sacred bath) should be performed for the Lord only with Ganga water. But Lord Shiva himself will later say that the Thirumanjanam water spat from Thinnanar's sacred mouth is purer than Ganga. Similarly, the inappropriate meat, by touching Thinnanar's sacred mouth, becomes sacred nectar acceptable to the Lord. The gods cannot directly consume the Havish. They can only accept that Havish through Agni Bhagavan. But this Agni is one of the Lord's Ashtamurtis (eight forms). That is why the inner meaning is that Aran (Shiva) acts as the chief of the Yagna, receives the Havish, and gives it to the other gods. Thus, Lord Shiva, as the chief of the Yagna, gives the Havish to all other gods. But it is our Thinnanar who offers nectar to Lord Shiva himself. Just as the Havish offered in Yagnas becomes a Vedic प्रमाण (proof) and sweet food for the gods, the meat offered by Thinnanar, without being a Vedic explanation, became superior and purer than that Havish for Lord Shiva. The meat offered by Thinnanar becomes the most excellent Naivedyam (food offering). This is what Adi Shankara, the Smartha Acharya, also sings in the 63rd verse of Sivananda Lahari: "Kinchit Bakshita Mamsa Sesha Kapalam Navvopaharayate" (The remaining portion of meat, bitten a little, becomes like a new offering). Adi Shankara praises Kannappa Nayanar and his devotion, saying that the remaining portion of meat, bitten a little by the hunter living in the forest, becomes like a new offering. Thus, after preparing the meat and pouring the honey he had collected into it, holding this sacred nectar in the vessel made of teak leaves, and tucking the Thiruppallithamam (flowers and leaves) into his hair as he did the previous day, and taking the necessary Thirumanjanam water for the Lord in his pure sacred mouth, Kannappa Nayanar swiftly proceeds towards Kudumi Thevar's sanctum. Thinnanar, the leader of the forest hunters, just as he did the previous day, removes all the Nirmalyam from the puja performed by Sivakochariyar and performs his own puja for Lord Shiva, the leader of the gods. Thinnanar is the leader of the forest hunters. Lord Shiva is the leader of the gods.

Both are leaders. He is the one who attains, and He is the one to be attained. The hunt and the method of preparing the nectar on the previous day are different now. But Thinnanar's puja performed the previous day and the puja performed now are not different. Therefore, Sekkizhar Swamigal, who explained the different parts in detail, briefly describes Thinnanar's puja, saying that he performed it just as he did the previous day. Just as he removed the Nirmalyam with his sandal-clad foot, performed Thirumanjanam for the Lord with the sacred water he brought in his mouth, placed the flowers from his head on the Lord's sacred head, and offered the nectar he brought to the Lord, he does the same now. Thus, Thinnanar, with ever-increasing love for Thirukalatthi Appar, guards him at night. During the day, he hunts. Then, he offers the sacred nectar he brought from the hunt to the Lord. In the same way, four days passed from the second night to the fifth night. Similarly, on the sixth day, only a day hunt occurred, and Thinnappan became Kannappan and sat on the right side of Thirukalatthi Appar on the path of no return. Sekkizhar Peruman, who described the events of the intervening days from verse 802 to 806, explains the events of the sixth day from verse 809 to 819. Just as he performed puja during the day and guarded at night on the first day, similarly, from the second night to the fifth night, for four nights and three days, Thinnanar hunted during the day and performed puja, and guarded at night. Similarly, Sekkizhar Peruman, after stating that the pujas performed by Sivakochariyar on the third, fourth, and fifth days occurred in the same way as on the second day, is now going to describe the events that occurred on the fifth night. We will continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.
