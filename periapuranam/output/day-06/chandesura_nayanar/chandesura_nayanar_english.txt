Chapter 01 — Chandesura Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Part 245, Mummaiyaal Ulagaanda Sarukkam, Chandesura Nayanar Puranam. In Periyapuranam, following the Puranam of Thirukurippu Thonda Nayanar in the Mummaiyaal Ulagaanda Sarukkam, is the Puranam of Chandesura Nayanar. First, let us reflect on the brief history of Chandesura Nayanar, and then we can delve into the detailed Puranam of the Nayanar. There is a river called Manniyaaru in the Chola country, which is made fertile by the flowing Kaveri river. On the southern bank of the Manniyaaru river is a holy place called Sengalur. This place, Sengalur, was established by Lord Muruga to remain stable before he proceeded to conquer Surapadman, after destroying Krauncha Malai and Tarakasura. Brahmins reside here. The Brahmins in this place are excellent in the truthfulness of the Vedas. There are many sacrificial halls here. There are places where students and teachers learn the Vedas. The streets of that city are filled with herds of cows whose udders are overflowing with milk. Students learning the Vedas walk through the streets of the city, reciting the Vedas they are learning, and carrying bundles of samit (sacred twigs). The holy place of Thiruchengalur is a very prosperous village. Sengalur is one of the five cities where the Chola kings, ancestors of King Anapaya Chola, were crowned. The greatness of this place is immeasurable. In such a glorious place, in the lineage of Brahmins, in the Kashyapa gotra, which is excellent in household life, in a leading family, there was a Brahmin named Echadattan, who seemed to be the embodiment of both good and bad deeds. His holy wife was born into a good family. She was skilled in household duties, caring for her relatives. She was instrumental in detaching from worldly attachments and fostering devotion to God. Her name was Pavithirai. To Echadattan and Pavithirai, our Nayanar was born, named Visara Sarumar. Our Nayanar was born as Visara Sarumar to illuminate the Vedic path, to increase the Brahmin lineage, to uplift the seven worlds, to promote the Saivite truth of Lord Shiva, and to make the victory of great ascetics manifest in the world. When he was five years old, due to the continuation of knowledge from his previous births, the knowledge of the Vedas, Vedangas, and Agamas, as taught by Lord Shiva, shone brightly in Visara Sarumar's mind. When he was seven years old, his parents performed the Upanayanam ceremony according to their tradition. They also arranged for him to be taught various arts. The Brahmins who taught him were astonished by the intellectual prowess of our Visara Sarumar, who understood the Vedas, Agamas, and many arts even before his teacher spoke. Visara Sarumar realized that the limit of all knowledge and arts is the sacred feet of Lord Shiva, who performs the dance of grace in the holy Ambalam in Thillai. As he realized this, the truth that Lord Shiva, the dancer of grace, is our master, always sprang forth in his thoughts. Because of this, the duty of devotion born of love became a natural endeavor in his mind. His love for this endeavor grew more and more. He stood firm in this conviction and shone forth. One day, during those days, Visara Sarumar was walking with a group of Vedic students and herds of cows in the village. At that time, a cow that had just given birth tried to butt the cowherd with its horns.

The cowherd, without any hesitation, hit the cow with the stick he held in his hand. Our Visara Sarumar, out of great love for the cow, became angry with the cowherd who was hitting the cow. He stopped him and prevented him from hitting the cow. Visara Sarumar knew the greatness of cows, which all arts, Vedas, and Agamic texts confirm. Therefore, he spoke to the cowherd who was hitting the cow, saying, "Cows are superior among all species. All pure waters reside in cows. Devas, sages, and divine beings surround and remain with cows without separation. Because of these special qualities, cows have the right to provide Panchagavyam, which Lord Shiva loves to dance with on the very day they give birth. Furthermore, the gomayam (cow dung) that provides the sacred ash worn by Lord Shiva also comes from herds of cows. Not only that, but these herds of cows belong to the lineage of Rishabha Devar, on whom the Lord and Goddess are seated together. Thinking all this, Visara Sarumar told the cowherd, 'Therefore, protecting these cows is the most excellent service. That alone is the path to worship Lord Shiva's feet.' He decided and told the cowherd, 'From now on, you should give up the work of herding cows. I myself will herd all these cows.' The cowherd, who used to herd the cows, bowed to Visara Sarumar and left. Visara Sarumar obtained the approval of the Brahmins in the village and began to herd the cows. He, coming in the guise of a Brahmachari with a staff and a rope, performed the good deed of herding cows that yield milk and benefits. He grazed the cows in grassy areas, went along with them, and fed them as much as they desired. During times of abundant grass, he grazed the cows, and also plucked grass with his hands and fed them, thus satisfying the hunger of the cows. He fed the cows good water at the ghats where there was good, tasty water. He protected the cows from all fears and guided them on good paths. He made the cows sit in the shade and rest. At milking time, he took each cow to its respective owner's house and ensured that the cows gave milk to their owners. In this way, Visara Sarumar protected the cows for many days. The cows protected by Visara Sarumar thus became very beautiful and rejoiced with great happiness. Both morning and evening, the herds of cows were very happy, as milk flowed naturally from their pure and sweet udders. The udders of the cows themselves poured milk. Seeing the beauty of Visara Sarumar protecting the cows in this way, all the Brahmins in the village rejoiced, saying, "This Brahmachari herds the cows well. Therefore, all these cows shine like Kamadhenu." As Visara Sarumar lovingly herded the cows, as soon as they saw him, all the cows went to him, melted with affection, mooed like a mother, and poured milk from their udders without holding back. Seeing this, Visara Sarumar realized that the milk poured by the cows was meant for the sacred anointing of Lord Shiva. As he realized this, he thought about it more and more, became clear, and a great desire to perform Shiva Puja came to him. Visara Sarumar's love for Lord Shiva surged, continuing his Shiva worship from many previous births. His desire to perform Shiva Puja grew. Therefore,

On the banks of the Manniyaaru river, under an Aathi tree, he made a Shiva Lingam out of sand, which was the sacred form of Lord Shiva, and built a Shiva temple, a gopuram, and surrounding shrines. He gathered Aathi flowers and tender shoots, and other things available in that region, in the Mullai land, and brought them in a flower basket, intending to offer them to Shiva. He took good new pots, went to the places where the cows grazed, and as he touched the udders of the cows one by one, they poured milk. He brought all those pots of milk and placed them in the Shiva temple he had made of sand. Due to the continuation of the love he had cultivated in his previous births, he worshipped Lord Shiva and performed the sacred anointing with the milk he had brought. In this way, with great single-mindedness, focusing all his thoughts, he performed the puja, absorbed in Shiva Puja. Visara Sarumar, fulfilling the other requirements for Shiva Puja such as sacred food, incense, sandalwood paste, and lamps, which were not available there, with love, worshipped and rejoiced in Lord Shiva. Even when Visara Sarumar brought the cows' milk in pots for the puja and performed the sacred anointing for the Lord, the cows continued to give milk in full measure at their owners' houses. In this way, Visara Sarumar performed Shiva Puja as a divine sport for many days. A person, unaware of all these glories, told the Brahmins in the village that Visara Sarumar was playfully building a Shiva temple and a Shiva Lingam out of sand and performing ablutions for Lord Shiva with the milk he obtained from the cows he herded. Hearing this, all the Brahmins gathered and said, "This Visara Sarumar, who took our cows promising to herd them well, is deceiving us in this way! Call his father!" Accordingly, Echadattan, Visara Sarumar's father, came there. The Brahmins told him what they had heard. He, fearing, said, "I did not know that this young student was doing such a thing. You must forgive all the mistakes that have happened so far. From now on, this offense will not happen. If this offense happens again, it will be my fault." He gave this assurance to the Brahmins and thought, "Alas, such a blame has come upon me!" That night passed, and the next morning, as usual, Visara Sarumar took the cows to graze. Without Visara Sarumar's knowledge, his father, Echadattan, followed him. As usual, Visara Sarumar led the cows to graze on the banks of the Manniyaaru river. At that time, Echadattan, who was following, climbed a Kura tree and hid there to observe everything that was happening. Visara Sarumar, the Brahmachari, as usual, bathed, made a temple of sand for Lord Shiva as before, gathered all the sacred flowers, and placed them there. As before, he filled pots with milk from the cows and placed them there. As usual, he began his sacred Shiva Puja with a unified mind, offering flowers on the sacred head of the Shiva Lingam. He took all the pots of milk and lovingly performed the sacred anointing. Echadattan, his father, watched all this from above the Kura tree. By the grace of Lord Shiva, Echadattan became angry. He became angry seeing what his son, Visara Sarumar, the Brahmachari, was doing. Sekkizhar Peruman writes that this anger came by the grace of Lord Shiva. Echadattan climbed down from the Kura tree and hit his son on his sacred back with the stick he held in his hand. He spoke harsh and abusive words.

Visara Sarumar, however, kept his desire, thoughts, and contemplation focused on Shiva Puja. Therefore, he was unaware of anything else. Even though his father hit him many times with the stick, Visara Sarumar did not feel anything. He did not realize that his father was hitting him. He did not realize that he was being abused. His entire mind was focused on Shiva Puja, and he was performing the puja for Lord Shiva with great love. Even though his father kept hitting him, he continued to perform the sacred anointing with milk with love. Now, by the grace of Lord Shiva, Echadattan became even more angry and kicked the pot of milk that Visara Sarumar was using for the sacred anointing with his foot. He spilled the milk. When the milk kept for the sacred anointing was spilled, Visara Sarumar realized that it was his father who had done this evil deed. His father Echadattan's feet had kicked the pot of milk for the sacred anointing. Therefore, to punish him, he picked up the staff that was in front of him. As the staff came into Visara Sarumar's hands, it transformed into a battle-axe. It became an axe. Taking it as Lord Shiva's weapon to remove the hindrance to Shiva Puja, he cut off both of his father's legs. As his legs were cut off, Echadattan, Visara Sarumar's father, fell to the ground. Now, the hindrance to Shiva Puja was removed. Therefore, Visara Sarumar proceeded to worship Lord Shiva as before. He continued his Shiva worship. At that time, Lord Shiva, accompanied by Uma Devi, mounted on the Rishabha vahana, surrounded by Bhoota Ganas, Devas, and sages, appeared before Visara Sarumar. Visara Sarumar, the child whose devotion to the sacred feet of Lord Shiva had matured, prostrated before the Lord. Lord Shiva lifted him, cast his graceful gaze upon him, and said, "You cut off your father, who begot you, for my sake. From now on, I myself will be your father." He took the child Visara Sarumar, embraced him, smelled his head, and rejoiced, bestowing his grace. The child, touched by Lord Shiva's sacred lotus hand, was immersed in the surging divine grace of Shiva, shining with Shiva's light. The Lord made the child Visara Sarumar the leader of all his devotees. After that, Lord Shiva said to the child Visara Sarumar, "The position of Chandeesan, where the vessels we eat from, the clothes we wear, and the garlands we adorn belong to you, has been given to you." As a sacred mark of granting the position of Chandeesan, Lord Shiva took the Kondrai garland from his sacred head, from his matted locks, and placed it on Visara Sarumar. In this way, he bestowed upon Visara Sarumar the greatness of being the leader of devotees and the position of Chandeesan, who receives Shiva's Nirmalya (offerings). The Devas showered flowers. The Vedas praised. The whole world rejoiced. The Gana Nathas sang and danced with joy. Divine drums resounded. As the good path of Saivism flourished, Visara Sarumar, worshipping Lord Shiva, assumed the position of Chandeesan. Even though his father Echadattan committed a publicly known offense, by the grace of God, he was punished by the beautiful axe in Visara Sarumar's hand, and thus, freed from sin, he attained Lord Shiva's abode with his relatives. Visara Sarumar, the young Brahmin who attained the position of Chandeesan, became the divine son of Lord Shiva with that very body. Who can comprehend this state? Sekkizhar Peruman explains this holy Puranam by asking, "Are not all the actions performed by devotees who love Lord Shiva and surrender themselves to him, penance?" We have reflected on the summary of the story so far. Now, let us reflect on the Puranam of Chandesura Nayanar in great detail. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 02 — Chandesura Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Part 246, Mummaiyaal Ulagaanda Sarukkam, Chandesura Nayanar Puranam. The Puranam of Chandesura Nayanar is the 20th holy Puranam in Periyapuranam. Sekkizhar Peruman describes the Puranam of Chandesura Nayanar in 59 verses. According to our tradition, we should first reflect on the Puranam of Chandesura Nayanar through the Thogai Nool (summary work), then the Vagai Nool (classified work), and then the Viri Nool (detailed work), which is Periyapuranam. First, we must reflect on the lines from the Thiruthondathogai Thiruppathigam composed by Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, which speaks about Lord Chandesura Nayanar: "Meimmaiye thirumeni vazhipadanikka vegundezhundha thaathai thaal mazhuinaal erindha ammaiyaan adichandi perumaanukku adiyen." These are the lines from Thiruthondathogai by Sundaramoorthy Swamigal about Chandesura Nayanar. Its meaning is as follows: "I am a devotee of the great Chandesa, who, like a mother (Ammaiyaan), threw an axe at the feet of his enraged father who rose up while he was worshipping Lord Shiva in the sacred form of a Shiva Lingam made of sand, standing in truth (meimmaiye), and who held firm to the sacred feet (adichandi)." This is the meaning of that phrase. "Meimmaiye thirumeni vazhipadanikka" should be understood as "worshipping in truth in the sacred form." The sacred form here refers to the Shiva Lingam made of sand. Chandesura Nayanar worshipped in such a Shiva Lingam made of sand. The three places where Lord Shiva can be seen and worshipped are the three sacred forms: Guru, Lingam, and Sangamam. Here, the sacred form referred to is the Lingam form. In such a Shiva Lingam, Chandesura Nayanar performs Shiva worship with great single-mindedness, "meimmaiye vazhipadanikka" (worshipping in truth). His father was watching. His father hit him. He spoke abusive words. Even after all this, Visara Sarumar continued his Shiva Lingam puja without his mind wavering. Furthermore, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal uses the present tense, "meimmaiye thirumeni vazhipattukkonde nirka" (standing and continuously worshipping in truth in the sacred form), to describe the Shiva Puja performed by Visara Sarumar. This is because the Nayanar, with that very body, became the son of Lord Shiva and, seated in the position of Chandesa, continues to worship in Shiva yoga even today. That is why Sundaramoorthy Swamigal refers to it in the present tense as "meimmaiye thirumeni vazhipadanikka." Following this, it says, "meimmaiye thirumeni vazhipadanikka vegundezhundha thaathai thaal mazhuinaal erindha." This means he cut off the feet of his father, who rose up in anger and kicked the pots of milk prepared for the sacred anointing. We will reflect on this in detail. Thus, the great Chandesa, who cut off his father's feet, is referred to as "Ammaiyaan." Here, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal sings about Chandesura Nayanar as "Ammaiyaan." "Ammaiyaan" means one who is like a mother. The feminine noun "Ammai" (mother) has taken a masculine suffix to become "Ammaiyaan." Sundaramoorthy Swamigal refers to him as such a "Adichandi Peruman." "Adichandi Peruman" means Chandesa, the devotee who does not abandon his devotion to the sacred feet of the Lord. The Nayanar, Chandesa Nayanar, continuously holds on to the sacred feet of the Lord without letting go. The rule that one should not cross between the Lord's Nirmalya Theertha Thotti (sacred water tank) and Chandesurar's temple comes from this connection, that Chandesa Nayanar continuously holds on to the Lord.

This is a custom, a rule, that has come about. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal sings, "I am a devotee of such an Adichandi Peruman." To denote the greatness of being the son of the Lord, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal praises the Nayanar's devotion in one and a half lines in his Thiruthondathogai Thiruppathigam. Similarly, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal praises the devotion of Gnanasambandha Peruman, who was the son of Lord Shiva, in one and a half lines in Thiruthondathogai. Thus, Sivakavimani Aiya has interpreted the lines sung by Sundaramoorthy Swamigal in Thiruthondathogai about Chandesura Nayanar: "Meimmaiye thirumeni vazhipada nirka vikandezhundha thaathai thaal mazhuinaal erindha ammaiyaan adichandi perumaanukku adiyen." Now, the verse about Chandesura Nayanar in the Vagai Nool, Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi, composed by Nambiyandar Nambigal, is as follows: "Kulam eriya seynalooril kurisil kurai kadal soozh thalam eriya viral thandi kandeer thanthai thaal irandum valam eriya mazhuvaal erindhu eesan manimudimel nalam eriya paal sorindhu alar soottiya nannadhiye." This is the verse composed by Nambiyandar Nambigal in Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi about Chandesura Nayanar. Now, we begin to reflect on the Puranam of Chandesura Nayanar, which is part of the Thiruthondar Puranam, also known as Periyapuranam, the Viri Nool (detailed work) composed by Sekkizhar Peruman. The Chandesura Nayanar Puranam is the section that narrates the history and character of the Nayanar named Chandesa. In sequential order, it is the sixth holy Puranam in the Mummaiyaal Ulagaanda Sarukkam. Sekkizhar Peruman has composed the Chandesura Nayanar Puranam in 59 verses. The Chola country is a fertile land watered by the Kaveri. The Kaveri does not flow roughly but gently and widely. Therefore, it beautifies every place it passes through. The Kaveri river is a river that never fails to give, always. Even in times of no rain, even in the peak summer, the Kaveri river provides water without its flow drying up. The Kaveri river provides water without fail according to the seasons. "Vaan poiyinum thaan poiyaa Kaveri" (Even if the sky fails, the Kaveri does not fail) is from Pattinappalai. "Thavaa neer Kaveri paavai" (The unfailing water Kaveri, the maiden) is from Manimekalai. Thus, in the Chola country watered by the Kaveri, a river called Manniyaaru flows. Chapter 03 — Chandesura Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Part 247, Mummaiyaal Ulagaanda Sarukkam, Chandesura Nayanar Puranam. The country where Chandesura Nayanar was born is the Chola country. The village where he was born is the holy place of Thiruchengalur. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman sings about the glories of that place. Thiruchengalur is an ancient town where holy Brahmins reside. It is a town filled with Brahmins who follow Saivite principles and Vedic traditions. Sekkizhar Peruman dedicates the second verse to singing about the holy Brahmins living in that town. He has composed this verse using numerical ornamentation, with numbers from one to seven. The verse is as follows: "Semmai vennitru orumai aayinar irandu pirappin sirappinar mummai thazhal ombiya neriyar naangu vedha murai payindrar thammai aindhu pulanum pinchellum thakaiyaar aru thozhilin meimmai ozhukkam ezhu ulagum potrum maraiyor vilanguvathu." Sekkizhar Peruman has composed this verse with numerical ornamentation, using the numbers one, two, three, four, five, six, and seven. Now, its meaning. First, he sings, "Semmai vennitru orumaiyinar." Here, the word "orumai" (oneness) implicitly brings in the number one. This means they are steadfast in the discipline of the sacred white ash, which is a means to Shiva's path. "Semmai vennitru" means the sacred white ash that bestows "semmai." "Semmai" here means Shiva, liberation. "Semmai vennitru" refers to the sacred white ash that is a means to Shiva, bestowing auspiciousness. Sacred ash, like Shiva, is imperishable. It is praised by the Vedas. There are Vedic statements such as "Neeru tharuvathu mukthi" (Ash gives liberation), "Neeru tharuvathu bakthi" (Ash gives devotion), "Neeru arruppathu paavam" (Ash removes sin). The Vedic scholars living in that town were unwavering in their commitment to this path of sacred ash. Next, Sekkizhar Peruman sings, "iru pirappin sirappinar," incorporating the number two. What is the specialty of "iru pirappu" (two births)? It is the excellence of attaining the virtues of two births in a single lifetime. In Sanskrit, they are called "dvija," and in Tamil, "iru pirappalar." Before the Upanayanam ceremony, there is one birth, and after it, there is another birth focused on Brahman. Because Brahmins have these two births, they are called "iru pirappalar" or "dvija." According to Saiva Siddhanta, attaining special diksha (initiation) bestows the nature of two births, as mentioned in Siddhanta Saravali. Now, incorporating the number three, he sings that they live by the path of "mummai thazhal ombiya neriyil." "Mummai thazhal" refers to the three fires, also known as "mukthi," which Brahmins maintain. These three fires are Aahavaneeyam, Gaarhapathyam, and Dakshinagni. Brahmins would constantly maintain these three fires in their homes. In the Puranam of Thillai Vaazh Andhanar, we have seen in detail about these three fires where it is mentioned that the Thillai Vaazh Andhanars maintain three fires in their homes. In Thirunaalaippovar Puranam, we also saw that when the Nayanar looked from outside Chidambaram, three thousand oblations were being offered in the homes of the three thousand Thillai Vaazh Andhanars inside the city of Chidambaram. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman mentions here that the Brahmins living in Thiruchengalur also live by the path of "mummai thazhal ombiya neriyil." He refers to these three fires as "mummai thazhal." "Thazhal" means fire god, Agni Bhagavan. Agni Bhagavan is said to have two faces, seven hands, and three legs. Agni has two faces. He has two faces for "avyam" and "kavyam."

Avyam is for the Devas, and Kavyam is for the ancestors. He has seven hands. These are the hands that carry and give to the seven types of beings, including the Devas, and for the seven crore Mahamantras. Seven crore Mahamantras refer to the seven types of mantras that end with "Svaha" and "Svadha." "Kodi" means at the end. So, "seven kodi" means mantras that end in seven ways. Therefore, mantras that end in seven ways like Svaha and Svadha are called Saptakodi Mahamantras and seven crore mantras. Agni has three legs. He travels to all three worlds. Therefore, he has three legs. "Irumugam, kazhal moondru, ezhu kaiththalam" (two faces, three legs, seven hands) is from the fifth verse of Saattiyakkudi Thiruvisaippa. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman sings about the three fires maintained by the Brahmins. Next, he says that they regularly study the four Vedas. Incorporating the number four, the four Vedas are Rig, Yajur, Sama, and Atharva, as classified by Vyasa. It is also said that before Vyasa classified them this way, they were referred to as four, beginning with Taittiriya. Sekkizhar Peruman sings, "naangu vedha murai payindrar" (they regularly studied the four Vedas). He states that the Brahmins in Thiruchengalur regularly study these four Vedas. "Payiluthal" means to study or practice. The Vedic scholars there regularly study or practice the four Vedas. What does it mean to regularly study or practice the Vedas? It means to study according to the rules prescribed for handling the Vedas. Instead of writing down and reading the Vedas, our Rishis have devised many methods to preserve the Vedas without even a slight error, without writing them down. To ensure that not a single syllable changes, not a single स्वर (pitch) varies in ascent or descent, and that the mantra's power is accurately pronounced to yield its full benefit, they have established many rules for Vedic study. They have a system of "matra ganakku" (time measurement) to determine how much time is needed to pronounce each syllable in each word of the mantras. Furthermore, in the Shiksha, a Vedanga, they have even determined how to breathe so that the pure syllable is produced by vibration in a specific part of the body. They have also guided on how to correctly pronounce Vedic pitches. To prevent words and syllables from changing, they have devised a great method. What is it? It is to arrange the words in a mantra in many ways, such as Vakyam, Padam, Kramam, Jata, Mala, Shikha, Rekha, Dhvajam, Dandam, Ratham, and Ganam. Studying in these specified methods is the correct way to study the Vedas, the correct way to practice them. Our Rishis have established so many methods for what purpose? To ensure that not even a single syllable of the Vedic mantras changes, and to interweave them back and forth. Vakya Patham or Samhita Patham is to recite the mantras exactly as they are. When mantras come in the form of sentences, the words in them combine at the sandhi. The individual words are not recognizable in their original form, but are joined together. To clearly separate and recite the words in each Vedic sentence is called Pada Patham. First, reciting the mantras as they are is called Samhita Patham. What we just saw is Pada Patham. Next is Krama Patham. In this, the first word of a mantra is joined with the second, the second with the third,

The third with the fourth, and the fourth with the next, and so on, until the mantra ends. In ancient inscriptions and charters, some names of village dignitaries would end with "Kramavithan." "Kramavithan" means one who knows how to recite the Vedas in the Krama method of study. Just as we generally refer to those who know the Vedas as "Vedavith," similarly, those who knew how to recite the Kramas in their prescribed method of study were called "Kramavith." After this is Jata Patham. In this, after joining the first word with the second, it should be reversed, joining the second with the first. Then again, the first with the second. Then the second word with the third, and reversing it, the third with the second, and again the second with the third, and so on, continuously joining the subsequent words in reverse. Those who can recite in this manner are called "Jata Vallabhars." In this method, two words are interwoven. Similarly, reversing three words back and forth is Shikha Patham. More difficult than this is Ghana Patham. There are also four types of this. In these ways, our ancestors established methods for studying the Vedas. The Vedas are transmitted only orally. They are transmitted without being written down. Therefore, their form should not change even a little. Therefore, they have stipulated that there must be so many types of recitation methods for them. Furthermore, one must know the appropriate time for study and study accordingly. That too is called studying according to the method. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman mentions that the Brahmins living in Thiruchengalur regularly study and practice the four Vedas. Now, incorporating the number five, Sekkizhar Peruman sings that they possess the quality of having their five senses follow them. He sings about the Brahmins there, stating that they do not follow the path of the five senses, but rather made the five senses follow their own path. "Uran ennum thottiyaan or aindhum kaappan varan ennum vaippirkor viththu" (He who guards the five senses with the goad of firmness is a seed for the treasure of liberation) is from Thirukkural, chapter on the Greatness of Renunciation, verse 24. Here, "Uran ennum thottiyaan or aindhum kaappan" means, as Parimelazhagar interprets, "He who guards the five senses, which are like elephants, from going astray on their respective sense objects, with the goad of firmness." Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman sings that the Vedic scholars living in Thiruchengalur possess the quality of not letting their five senses follow the path of the five senses, but rather making them follow themselves. "Maari nindru ennai mayakkidum vanjap pulan aindhin vazhi adaiththu" (Blocking the path of the five deceitful senses that stand and delude me) is from Thiruvachagam, Koyil Thiruppathigam, verse one. Now, incorporating the number six, Sekkizhar Peruman sings that the Brahmins of Thiruchengalur possess the true discipline of the six professions. Brahmins have six professions. The six professions are studying the Vedas, teaching the Vedas, performing sacrifices, causing sacrifices to be performed, giving alms, and receiving alms. Sekkizhar Peruman sings that they live by the true discipline of these six professions. "Meimmai ozhukkam" (true discipline) means to adhere to the truth in these six professions. That is, they perform these six professions not for food or other such needs or reasons, but solely to protect that discipline as a testimony to God. In this way, Sekkizhar Peruman sings, incorporating the number seven, that those Brahmins who live in this manner are praised by the seven worlds. A city is a city where people of many different communities live. Therefore, it will have separate areas where people of those respective communities live. However, small villages are villages that are exclusively established for people of a single community to live. Establishing villages exclusively for a particular community has been a custom in the past and even in the present. Sengalur is a village where Brahmins reside exclusively. This is called an Agrahara. It is also called Agaram. Establishing villages with houses exclusively for Vedic scholars was one of the charities performed by kings and others in the past. Such villages given to Brahmins are referred to as "Chathurvedi Mangalam" in ancient inscriptions. Visara Sarumar, the Nayanar whose history this is, Chandesura Nayanar, was a young Brahmin from the Vedic tradition. That is why Sekkizhar Peruman sings about the special qualities of the Brahmins in six verses, describing the glory of the town and the glory of the Brahmins. In this particular second verse, he sings about their greatness using numerical ornamentation, with numbers from one to seven. Sekkizhar Peruman sings that they are steadfast in the discipline of sacred ash, possess two births, maintain three fires, regularly study the four Vedas, have the quality of making their five senses follow them, regularly perform the six professions, and are praised by the seven worlds. Let us continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 04 — Chandesura Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Part 248, Mummaiyaal Ulagaanda Sarukkam, Chandesura Nayanar Puranam. Brahmins reside in Thiruchengalur. Therefore, there are Vedic schools there. These schools are so tall that clouds touch them. In those Vedic schools, teachers are teaching the Vedas to their students. Young students, adorned with faultless deer skin and the triple sacred thread on their chests, and with their hair tied in a tuft, are learning the Vedas. Venerable teachers, who teach the Vedas, are seated in those Vedic schools. As they teach, the sound of many students gathered there reciting the Vedas resonates louder than the sound of thunder in the clouds. Nishadam is a high note, resembling the trumpeting of an elephant. The Vedas, mixed with high notes like Nishadam, are recited by the students learning the Vedas there. Therefore, the sound of many students reciting the Vedas together resonates louder than the sound of thunder in the clouds. There is one teacher there. He is teaching the students. There are many students there. Sekkizhar Peruman describes this with a beautiful simile. He sings that just as there are stars in the sky, there is one teacher and many students seated in that place where the Vedas are recited. There will be many students. Therefore, he compares them to stars, and the one teacher to the moon. Sekkizhar Peruman sings that just as stars surround the moon, students surround the teacher. Furthermore, the moon has cool rays and a white color. Similarly, teachers will be full of compassion and purity. The moon is compared to the teacher. Therefore, the cool rays of the moon are compared to the teacher's compassion, and the white light of the moon is compared to the teacher's purity. The stars are compared to the students. Although stars appear small, they, like the moon, are self-luminous objects. Therefore, the comparison of students to stars implies that students, like teachers, are living beings with individual intellectual light. Furthermore, some stars emit more light, while others emit less. Similarly, the greatness and smallness of students are also conveyed through this simile. Moreover, when the moon appears, we can see the stars by the moon's light. Similarly, the simile beautifully suggests that students will gain intellectual enlightenment through the intellectual illumination of the teacher. Furthermore, the moon is one of the planets. The moon continuously emits light. Stars, on the other hand, emit light intermittently. Therefore, even in this sense, the beauty of comparing the teacher to the moon and the students to stars gives us joy. Moreover, the light of the moon and the light of the stars are together in the sky. This signifies the blending of the teacher's intellectual light and the students' intellectual light. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman sings about that place where the Vedas are recited. There, young students wear deer skin and the triple sacred thread on their chests. Sekkizhar Peruman refers to the deer skin they wear as "Kothil maan thol," meaning faultless deer skin. Deer skin is taken from a dead animal, a deer. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman refers to it with the adjective "faultless skin." Furthermore, deer skin is traditionally considered pure. Thus, these young boys wear deer skin and the triple sacred thread on their chests. They have a tuft of hair. These are all the symbols of Brahmachari boys in the Brahmin community. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman sings about the presence of Vedic schools in Thiruchengalur. He continues to sing about the Brahmins living in that holy place. There are sacrificial halls there. Sacrifices are performed in those sacrificial halls. In those sacrifices, oblations are offered to Brahma, Vishnu, Indra, and other deities. Vishnu, Brahma, and Indra come there to partake of the offered oblations. When they come, they arrive on their respective vehicles. The Garuda on which Vishnu rides, the swan on which Brahma rides, and the Airavata elephant on which Indra rides, are parked and tied to the Yupa Stambhas (sacrificial posts) located next to the sacrificial halls in that village. Those who come to the sacrificial halls for the sacrifices in their vehicles must park them next to the sacrificial halls. Therefore, the Yupa Stambhas serve this purpose. A Yupa is a pillar to which the sacrificial animal is tied. In Tamil, it is called "Omaththari." Sekkizhar Peruman sings, "Eendha avi unavin bhaagam," meaning the portion of the offered oblation. He sings that Vishnu, Indra, and Brahma have come there to receive the remaining portions of the oblation. He does not mention Lord Shiva here. This is because Lord Shiva is the chief of all sacrifices. Sacrifices performed without Lord Shiva, like the sacrifice performed by Daksha, will be destroyed and incomplete. Therefore, it is customary to first offer oblations to Lord Shiva and invite him before offering food to other deities. Furthermore, Vishnu, Brahma, and Indra will be present to protect the sacrifices dedicated to Shiva. Therefore, the oblation offered to Lord Shiva is understood without explicit mention. This is the underlying meaning. Moreover, since Sekkizhar Peruman mentions "Eendha avi unavin bhaagam" (the portion of the offered oblation), it is implied that what is given to them is the remainder after offering to Shiva. This is also an underlying meaning. Thus, at the end of the sacrifice, oblations are offered to them. Therefore, they must wait until then. Their vehicles must also wait until then. Therefore, they need a place for those vehicles to park. Sekkizhar Peruman sings in this verse that the Yupa Stambha serves this purpose. Now, Sekkizhar Peruman sings about the streets of Thiruchengalur. The streets of Thiruchengalur are very long. They are wide streets. They are streets without ups and downs. They are beautiful streets without bends, curves, or narrow alleys. There is good shade on both sides of the streets. Herds of cows walk along these streets throughout the day. As these cows walk along the streets, their udders are so full that milk flows out without being milked. The unhindered flow of milk is a sign of the cows' healthy growth. Furthermore, the milk flows unhindered because the cows are thinking of their calves as they walk along the streets. These cows are raised in the homes of Vedic scholars for the Panchagavyam (five products of the cow) needed for the homas (fire rituals) performed by the Vedic scholars. All of them are walking along those streets. It is implied here that if cows are raised healthily, they will yield milk without holding back. Furthermore, the story of Chandesura Nayanar is also mentioned here, as it resulted from the cows yielding milk without holding back. Moreover, in the streets of Thiruchengalur, students learning the Vedas gather samit (sacred twigs) for the homa. Bringing samit for the daily fire ritual is one of the duties assigned to students studying the Vedas. This was a custom among Brahmins of that era. Vedic schools are located outside the village. Students go there to learn the Vedas. When they return home in the evening after learning the Vedas, they bring back the necessary samit. These samit are for the daily fire ritual maintained in Brahmin homes. Thus, students who learn the Vedas in the Vedic schools outside the village and return home in the afternoon bring back the gathered samit. As they walk along the streets, they repeatedly recite and practice the portions of the Vedas they have learned. This is called "kaniththal" (calculating or reckoning). "Kaniththal" is the method of repeatedly reciting and practicing the learned portions of the Vedas and reckoning them. As we have already seen, because the measurements of pitches, words, Jata, Krama, and other methods, and the measurements of time are calculated and practiced, this is called "kanippathu" (calculating), and the young boys who do this are called "kanippor" (calculators). Thus, in the afternoon, the Vedic boys in the streets of Thiruchengalur memorize and practice the Vedas as they bring the gathered samit. The women in Brahmin homes go to the flower ponds outside the village, bathe, and bring water back to their homes. Sacrificial halls are set up in suitable places outside the village. Generally, there are specific places where sacrificial halls should be built. They should be outside the village, and next to them should be a river, fields, and ponds. Sacrificial halls should be built on their banks. Furthermore, there should be large areas around the sacrificial halls for chariots to stand. In Thiruchengalur, the Manniyaaru river flows outside the village, filled with water, with waves rolling. There are fields on the banks of the Manniyaaru river. Next to the field boundaries are banks with large open spaces. Next to these banks is a very large area. Beyond that is the city. Thus, sacrificial halls are built in the wide areas between the city and the field banks. There are open spaces around the sacrificial halls. Those spaces are for parking chariots and other vehicles. The leaders of the sacrifices, after completing the rituals in the sacrificial halls, mount their chariots parked there and depart. Similarly, the celestial vehicles and conveyances of the celestials who came to receive oblations are parked in those places. They, the celestials, receive the oblations and depart in their vehicles. The leaders of the sacrifices, after completing the sacrificial rituals, mount their chariots and come into the streets of Thiruchengalur and go to their respective homes. This implies that the leaders who performed sacrifices in those times were very wealthy. Now, Sekkizhar Peruman sings about the prosperity of Thiruchengalur. Red water lilies bloom in the irrigation channels. The fields are fertile, surrounded by water. There are bundles of red paddy stalks in the fields. Next to the fields are areca nut trees. Due to abundant water, the areca nut trees have bloomed with flower spathes. Lotus flowers with many leaves have bloomed there. Carp fish sleep in the large lotus flowers. Along the path between the fields, delicate jasmine creepers spread like a canopy. Kanchi trees with spreading branches stand on both sides of the street, allowing them to spread. Sekkizhar Peruman continues to sing about the glories of Thiruchengalur. Let us continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 05 — Chandesura Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Part 249, Mummaiyaal Ulagaanda Sarukkam, Chandesura Nayanar Puranam. Sekkizhar Peruman continues to sing about the glories of Thiruchengalur. Thiruchengalur is one of the five cities where Chola kings were crowned. Chola kings traditionally crowned themselves in five cities within their country. These five cities are Kaveri Poompattinam, also known as Poompuhar, Thiruvarur, Uraiyur, Sengalur, and Karur. While singing about the Chola lineage that underwent such coronations, Sekkizhar Peruman sings that King Anapaya Chola was born into the Chola lineage, and Thiruchengalur is one of the five places where the Chola kings of that lineage, to which Anapaya Chola belonged, were crowned. Anapaya Chola was the king who commissioned Sekkizhar Peruman to compose Periyapuranam. Therefore, out of gratitude for commissioning this Puranam, Sekkizhar Peruman has praised Anapaya's devotion in eleven places in Periyapuranam. This place is one of the most significant of those eleven places. Anapaya Chola, also known as Abayan and Kulothunga Chola in the Chola lineage, ruled with great distinction. He was the Chola king who covered the sacred boundary of Thillai with gold. Sekkizhar Peruman sings that Thiruchengalur possesses the quality of being one of the five holy places where the ancient Chola kings, of the lineage in which the glorious Anapaya Chola was born, were crowned. Sekkizhar Peruman notes here that Anapaya was known by two names: Abayan and Kulothungan. He also mentions that he covered the sacred boundary of Thillai with gold. We have reflected on the story of Anapaya Chola requesting Sekkizhar Peruman to compose Periyapuranam in the introduction to Periyapuranam. Such was the glorious king Anapaya Chola. Sekkizhar Peruman refers to Anapaya here as Kulothunga Chola. Kulothunga Chola is a name adopted by many Chola kings. There have been many Chola kings with the name Kulothunga Chola. However, Anapaya is the special name of Anapaya Chola. That is why Sekkizhar Peruman has praised this king by using the name Anapaya in many places. Anapaya has many glories. The most prominent of all these glories is that he lived with great devotion to sacred ash. He covered the Thillai boundary with gold. These two virtues captivated Sekkizhar Peruman's heart. Therefore, he praises these two sacred services in many places. Anapaya Chola excelled in his devotion to sacred ash. Therefore, Anapaya also has the name Thiruneetru Chola (Chola of Sacred Ash). The Chola king who covered the great temple of Thillai, its temple and gopuram, with gold, the Thiruneetru Chola, covered it with gold for the flourishing of the Saivite path. This is mentioned in the Chidambara Puranam. The Koyil Puranam also states the same. Furthermore, Abayan and Kulothunga Chola are names borne by many Chola kings. But Anapayar is his special name. That is why Sekkizhar Peruman praises this king using the name Anapayar in many places. Moreover, in this verse, Sekkizhar Peruman refers to Anapaya Chola as "ennrum puvi kaakkum mannan" (the king who forever protects the earth). This conveys Sekkizhar Peruman's blessing that the reign of his beloved king may last long. It was at Anapaya Chola's request that Sekkizhar Peruman composed Thiruthondar Puranam, also known as Periyapuranam, and even today, the world thrives in the Saivite path because of this Thiruthondar Puranam. It would not be an exaggeration to say that this is due to the strength of Anapaya's rule, according to Saivite scholars. Having sung thus, Sekkizhar Peruman continues to sing about the glories of Thiruchengalur. It is a very beautiful verse. The benefit of a tune is good music. The benefit of milk is sweet taste. The benefit of having eyes is that they have light. The benefit of thought is the sacred five letters. The benefit of the sky is the rain it pours. The benefit of the Veda is Saivism. Similarly, what is the benefit of this earth? It is the holy place of Thiruchengalur. Can its greatness be measured? Sekkizhar Peruman sings that its greatness is immeasurable. The author has conveyed many great truths in this verse using similes. The author's teaching and imparting these truths for the benefit of the world are noteworthy and commendable in this verse. Now, the detailed meaning of this verse. First, he states that the benefit of a tune is good music. "Pann" refers to tunes like Kurinji Pann, Mullai Pann, Paalai Pann, etc. This is a musical arrangement unique to Tamil. What we now call "ragam" was called "pann" in Tamil in ancient times. "Pann" is said to be the creation of a melodious raga by appropriately placing the seven notes according to one's mental state. From the period of Tholkappiyam until the end of the Chola empire, Tamil music was entirely based on "pann." Texts state that for a song, its corresponding "pann" is essential. Our ancestors have established rules for the hymns in the Thevarams of the Three Saints, specifying which "pann" should be used for each hymn. According to this, even today, Oduvar Moorthigal (reciters) in all temples recite those hymns in their respective "panns" and offer them to the Lord. Furthermore, we have reflected on the information about "pann" in the Thirumalai Sarukkam, in the Thaduthaatkonda Puranam, where Sekkizhar Peruman states that Sundaramoorthy Swamigal sang the hymn "Piththa Piraisoodi" to the Lord in Indalappann. We can recall all those details here and now reflect on the meaning of Sekkizhar Peruman's statement, "Pannin payanaam nal isai" (The benefit of a tune is good music). Sekkizhar Peruman says, "Pannin payanaam nal isai." This means that a tune yields benefit only when it is accompanied by music. If there is no music, the tune will not yield any benefit. Music is a connection that includes the praise of God. We can also relate this to Thiruisaippa, which is found in the ninth Thirumurai. Music should be sung with objects that are full of God's praise. A mere tune without God's praise will only be a pleasant sound to the ears. At the same time, tunes, songs, and hymns that praise God will enter the mind, melt the mind and soul, and bestow upon us the sacred feet of God. Songs that do not praise God will only provide sensory pleasure, bringing both joy and sorrow to living beings. Songs that praise God will provide strength to the soul. This is what Appar Swamigal mentioned in Thiruppazhanam Thevaram, "Pann porundha isai paadum." Thirugnanasambandha Peruman said in Pullirukkuvelur Thiruppathigam, "Pann ondrai isai paadum adiyargal kudiyaga manninri vin kodukkum Manigandan" (Manigandan, who gives heaven without earth to the devotees who sing with a tune). Sundaramoorthy Swamigal mentioned this in Kurukkavur Thevaram as "Pannidai Tamil oppaay." Therefore, only music that is combined with the praise of God will yield true benefits. Mere music without the praise of God will only be sensory pleasure. This is the meaning of Sekkizhar Peruman's statement. Therefore, Sivakavimani Aiya says that those who engage in mere music without the praise of God and spend money and time on it should realize this. This is the meaning of "Pannin payanaam nallisai." Now, Sekkizhar Peruman sings, "Paalin payanaam insuvai" (The benefit of milk is sweet taste). What is the benefit of milk? He sings that it is sweet taste. In this Puranam, Chandesura Nayanar worships by anointing the Lord with milk milked from cows. This is how this story unfolded. Sekkizhar Swamigal implicitly suggests that this story resulted from the sweetness of the milk offered to the Lord for anointing, and thus states, "Paalin payan insuvai." Furthermore, in the Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam, in the chapter on carrying earth, it is said in the 23rd verse, "Annai mulai theempalin ariya suvai idhu" (This is the rare taste of mother's sweet breast milk). This verse provides another explanation for the benefit of milk being sweet taste. The Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam describes how the Vaigai river overflowed in Madurai, and the Pandyan king ordered one person from each house to block the banks of the Vaigai. When Vandippatti, a woman selling pittu (a sweet dish), lamented that it would be good to have a hired laborer, Lord Shiva himself came forward as a laborer for Vandippatti. The author of Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam sings that he came eagerly to eat the pittu that Vandippatti had. How did the Lord come? He came before Vandippatti with a spade on his sacred shoulder. Sages lovingly offer oblations in sacrifices to the Lord. Even after eating that, his hunger was not satisfied. Even after sweetly eating the nectar fed by Uma Devi, his hunger was not satisfied. Paranjothi Munivar sings in that verse that the Lord came to eat the pittu that Vandippatti was about to give, like a child eagerly coming to drink its mother's breast milk with unquenchable hunger. One phrase in that verse is "Annai mulai theempalin ariya suvai idhu." Therefore, the meaning that the benefit of milk is sweet taste is well understood through this verse as well. Now, Sekkizhar Peruman sings that the benefit of the eye is light. The eye here refers to the organ of sight. In the external world, there are great luminous objects like the sun. Nevertheless, the benefit for the eye is that there must be light for it to see. Having that light to see is what constitutes the benefit for the eye. Next, Sekkizhar Peruman sings that the benefit of thought is the sacred five letters. The conclusion of the Jnana Shastras is that the mind is given to humans only for contemplating the sacred five letters. "Karuththu" here means heart or mind. Humans are given a heart, mind, and consciousness. For what purpose? Only for remembering the sacred five letters, only for contemplating the sacred five letters. We can see this in the Thevaram, "Vaazhththa vaayum ninaikka madanenjum" (A mouth to praise and a foolish mind to remember). There are many other such sacred sayings. Remembering Lord Shiva in the mind is the benefit of having a mind. If one does not remember, it means that the benefit from the mind has not been obtained. The five letters are the sacred name of God. What does it mean to remember these sacred five letters with the mind? It means that the message conveyed to us through the sacred five letters is the great compassion that Lord Shiva bestows upon living beings. Therefore, the inherent message of Sri Panchaksharam is the great compassion that Lord Shiva bestows upon living beings. Meditating on such great compassion and melting with devotion is what it means to remember the sacred five letters. Now, he sings that the benefit of the sky is the rain it pours. The sky provides space for everything. Linguists say that it is called "akasam" because it provides "avakasam" (opportunity or space) for everything. Such a sky gives space for rain and causes it to pour. Therefore, causing rain to pour is considered the benefit of the sky or akasam. Regarding this, Thirukkural mentions in the chapter on the Glory of Rain, "Vaanindru ulagam vazhangi varuthalaal" (Because the world thrives from the sky) and "Visumbin thuli veezhin allaal" (Unless a drop falls from the sky). Now, Sekkizhar Peruman sings that the benefit of the Veda is Saivism. The ultimate meaning of the Veda is Saivism. Saivism refers to the meaning of Shiva Agamas. If we consider the meaning conveyed by the Shiva Agamas mentioned in the Atharva Shikha and other Upanishads, the Vedas are like sutras, and the Shiva Agamas are like commentaries. Following the Thoola Arundhati method (gross to subtle), the Vedas are called general texts, and the Agamas are called special texts. "Vedantha thelivaam siddhanthath thiran ingu therikkalutram" (Here we shall explain the essence of Siddhanta, which is the clarity of Vedanta) is from Sivaprakasam, preface seven. Therefore, if we consider all these truths from the Jnana Shastras, the benefit of the Veda being Saivism becomes clear to us. Madhava Sivagnana Yogigal has explained these facts in the special preface to Sivagnana Mapadiyam. Now, Sekkizhar Peruman sings that the benefit of the earth is Thiruchengalur. Earth here refers to the world made of earth. This is called "Prithvi Andam" (earthly sphere). The benefit of this earth is Thiruchengalur itself. If this holy place of Thiruchengalur did not exist, all the earthly worlds would be useless. Therefore, this holy place is what gives benefit to the earthly world. Unlike other higher and lower worlds, which are realms of enjoyment, this earthly world is a world where living beings are born, worship Lord Shiva, and attain liberation. This place is blessed with Chandeesa Nayanar, who helps living beings who are born here and perform Shiva Puja to attain the benefit of that Shiva Puja. That is why Sekkizhar Peruman sings that this place is the "mannin payanaam pathi" (the place that is the benefit of the earth). We saw in the first verse that Lord Muruga completed this path by worshipping Lord Shiva, showing that this is the meaning for all living beings in the world. Sekkizhar Peruman, by stating the ability to bestow the benefit of such Shiva Puja, sings, "Is there any other place equal to this prosperous place? Can its glories be described?" Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman sings, using six similes: the benefit of a tune is music, the benefit of milk is taste, the benefit of the eye is light, the benefit of thought is the sacred five letters, the benefit of the sky is rain, and the benefit of the Veda is Saivism, and then states that the benefit of the earth is this holy place of Sengalur. The inner meaning of this is that by singing with appropriate tunes, anointing the Lord with milk, seeing Him with one's eyes, remembering His sacred five letters with one's thoughts, worshipping Lord Shiva with cool holy water filled by rain from the sky, and performing Shiva Puja according to Vedic and Agamic texts, this place of Thiruchengalur possesses the glory of bestowing the benefit of that Shiva Agamic Puja upon the people of the earthly world. Let us continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 06 — Chandesura Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Part 250. Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam, Chandesura Nayanar Puranam. Sekkizhar Perumaan continues to sing the glories of Thiruchengalur. In this glorious Thiruchengalur, in the Brahmin lineage of the Kashyapa Gotra, lived a Brahmin named Echadattan. Among Brahmins, there are many sub-divisions called Gotras. Among them, the Kashyapa Gotra is connected to the sage Kashyapa. Echadattan was a Vedic scholar who lived in a small sub-division within such a Gotra. A small sub-division within a Gotra is called Pravaram. The name of that Brahmin was Echadattan. The word "Echam" in Tamil comes from "Yajnam" in Sanskrit. They came from a tradition of performing sacrifices, so the name "Echam" was given to them. "Dattan" was one of the special names adopted by Brahmins in ancient times. Therefore, his name was Echadattan. This Echadattan was one who embodied both good and bad karma. Just as a snake can yield both gems and poison, Echadattan was blessed with the good karma of begetting a virtuous son named Visarasarumar. At the same time, he was also destined to commit the evil deed of obstructing the Shiva Puja performed by Visarasarumar, which is a Shiva Aparatham (offense against Shiva). Therefore, Sekkizhar sings that Echadattan embodied both good and bad karma, and he uses the analogy of a snake. A snake has both gems and poison. Similarly, Sekkizhar Swamigal says that Echadattan lived. This Echadattan was engaged in domestic life and led an excellent household. The greatness of domestic life, according to Thirukkural, is "Nallaatrin nindra thunai" (a good support for the three natural stages of life) and "Thurandhaarkkum thuvvaathavarkkum irandhaarkkum thunaiyaavadhu" (a support for ascetics, the poor, and the deceased). It also involves protecting the five groups: "Thenpulathaar, Deivam, Virundhu, Okkal, Thaan" (ancestors, gods, guests, relatives, and oneself). These are the virtues of householders mentioned in Thirukkural. Our elders ordained that household life should not be for oneself, but for helping others. Echadattan had a virtuous wife named Pavithirai. She was born into a lineage suitable for marriage, in accordance with Echadattan's tradition. Earlier, when describing Echadattan, it was said that he embodied both good and bad karma. Here, when describing his wife, Pavithirai, Sekkizhar Perumaan sings that she was not in such a dual state, but stood in a single state, supporting her husband in attaining Shiva's feet. For a woman, the excellence of her birthplace, lineage, and qualities is very important. Pavithirai was born into such a distinguished and virtuous lineage. In her household life, she loved, protected, and nurtured all her relatives. Nurturing relatives is a great duty and profession for a wife in domestic life. This is a special quality a woman should possess in her marital home. Such was Pavithirai. Pavithirai means "one who is pure." Pavithiram means "purity." Therefore, Pavithirai has the meaning of "one who is pure" and also "one who has the quality of purifying living beings." Thus, Echadattan and Pavithirai lived their domestic life. Pavithirai was blessed with the penance of begetting a virtuous son. Begetting virtuous sons is the penance of wives in domestic life.

It is beneficial. Thirukkural states, "Mangalam enba mana maatchi matru adhan nankalam nanmakkal perru" (A good wife is a blessing, and her best ornament is virtuous children). Raising virtuous children is the fruit of penance. Those who do not beget and raise good children do not attain the benefit of household life. What is a good son? He is one who not only helps his parents prosper in this world but also helps them avoid hell in the afterlife. Such a one is a good son. But here, Pavithirai is about to give birth to our Chandesura Nayanar, Visarasarumar. Our Nayanar, who came as their son, not only bestowed good upon his parents in this world and the next but also helped them attain the ultimate world of liberation. Pavithirai is the one who possesses such penance to beget such a Nayanar. Pavithirai, the woman, is about to attain the ultimate benefit of liberation, which is the greatest of all blessings attained by those who beget sons in the world, through the son she is about to bear. She had performed penance before to beget Visarasarumar, who is Chandesura Nayanar, as her son. The fruit of all the penances she performed in previous births is to be realized in this birth by begetting Visarasarumar as her son. Such a Pavithirai is one who has abandoned all other attachments and holds only to the attachment to Shiva's feet. Thirukkural states, "Patruka patratraan patrinai appatrai patruka patru vidarku" (Cling to the attachment of the one who is unattached; cling to that attachment to shed all other attachments). We saw the verse where Visarasarumar, also known as Chandesa Nayanar, instructs the cowherd about the greatness of cows. We also saw all the greatness of cows. Furthermore, Sekkizhar Perumaan sings that Visarasarumar states that cows alone have the right to provide the Panchagavya necessary for the sacred ablution of our Lord Shiva, who dances in the Thiruchitrambalam, on His sacred crown. We first saw how cows possess a pure body. Such pure-bodied cows alone have the right to provide the Panchagavya for the Lord's sacred ablution. Panchagavya consists of cow ghee, milk, curd, cow dung, and cow urine. We have contemplated the details of these in the Puranam of Arivattaya Nayanar. Cows alone have the right to provide such Panchagavya. This right has been granted to cows by God. Visarasarumar instructs that herds of cows possess such greatness. Not only that, Visarasarumar also states that these cows are the very forms that provide the cow dung, called Gomayam, which is the raw material for the sacred ash (Thiruneeru) that Lord Shiva adorns. Lord Shiva is the prime cause who protects all gods and all worlds throughout time. The epithet "prime cause" here means that Lord Shiva is the ultimate cause who has no superior. He is capable of creating all protecting deities, protecting them, and finally absorbing them into Himself. Therefore, he is referred to as the "protecting prime cause" in this verse by Sekkizhar Perumaan. Thiruvachagam states, "Padaipon padaikkum pazhaiyavan kaappon kaakkum kadavul" (The creator creates, the ancient one protects, the protecting God). The sacred ash that such a Lord Shiva adorns is imperishable even at the time of ultimate dissolution, because it resides on the Lord's sacred body. Even during the Mahasamhara (great dissolution), Thiruneeru is imperishable. The Thiruneeru mentioned here is what devotees use to adorn the Lord's sacred body. The cow dung, called Gomayam, is the source for obtaining this Thiruneeru. And cows are the source from which such Gomayam is produced, are they not? Therefore, Visarasarumar states that these cows, which benefit living beings, are the abode of all gods, sages, and sacred waters, provide the Panchagavya necessary for the Lord's sacred ablution, and provide Gomayam, the raw material for the sacred ash that adorns the Lord's sacred body. He further states that Lord Shiva, along with the Divine Mother, grants divine vision while seated on the Rishabha (divine bull). And is not this cow lineage the lineage of that Rishabha Devar? asks Visarasarumar. Lord Shiva holds a leaping deer in the tips of the fingers of His sacred hand. The deer calf, which came to destroy this world from the Abhichara Yagna performed by the Rishis, dances on the tips of the two fingers of Lord Shiva's left hand. We can see this story in detail in the Skanda Puranam. Lord Shiva, who holds such a leaping deer in His sacred hand, also has a glorious matted hair that holds the Ganga water, which contains the jewels of snakes. He, along with Uma Devi, the Empress of the Devas, is seated on the Rishabha. Here, Sekkizhar Perumaan refers to Uma Devi as "Devargal Thampiraatti" (Empress of the Devas). This means that Umaiyamman is the leader and queen of all the Devas. "Thampiraan" refers to Lord Shiva.

The feminine form of the word "Thampiraan" is "Thampiraatti." This word "Thampiraatti" is still in use in Malayalam country today. It is an ancient Tamil usage. Thus, Lord Shiva and Uma Devi, the Thampiraan and Thampiraatti, grant divine vision while seated on Rishabha Devar. Visarasarumar asks, "Is not this cow lineage the lineage of such a glorious Rishabha Devar?" Why has Sekkizhar arranged for Visarasarumar to say this here? It is because herds of cows exist on this earth for the salvation of souls. How did they come to this earth? They came to earth by the grace of Lord Shiva to help souls. Their history is as follows: In Shivaloka, Rishabha Devar resides in the presence of Lord Shiva. Beside Rishabha Devar are five types of cows: Nandai, Pathirai, Surabhi, Susilai, and Sumani. Among them, Nandai is of Kapil (brownish-yellow) color. Subathirai is of black color. Surabhi is of white color. Susilai is of smoke color. Sumani is of red color. Lord Shiva, by His grace, ordained that these five types of cows should go from Shivaloka to Bhuloka (earth) so that living beings on earth could perform Shiva Pujas, and so that the Panchagavya and Thiruneeru necessary for those Shiva Pujas would be available to living beings. Therefore, these five types of cows first appear in Thiruppaarkadal (the ocean of milk) by the grace of Lord Shiva. Then, they come from Thiruppaarkadal to Bhuloka. They all come to Bhuloka to assist in Shiva Pujas. This is the history of how herds of cows came to Bhuloka. Thus, these herds of cows came through the five types of cows that surround Rishabha Devar, on whom Lord Shiva and Uma Devi are seated. Therefore, Visarasarumar bestows grace and instructs, "Is not this cow lineage the lineage of Rishabha Devar, on whom the Lord and the Goddess are seated?" Having thus instructed, he says that the duty of grazing and protecting cows with their calves, so that they may be happy, is the most supreme duty. There is no other duty to be performed in this world above this duty. This is also the way to praise the sacred feet of Lord Shiva. He tells the cowherd, "From now on, you should give up the profession of grazing cows. I will graze these herds of cows myself." There are many duties that people must perform. Among all those duties, protecting cows is a great duty. It not only involves protecting cows but also becomes a way to praise the sacred feet of God. People have many duties. However, not all of them are ways to serve the sacred feet of Lord Shiva. Other duties are worldly and pertain to this body, and they will pass away. But the duty of protecting cows is not only the foremost among people's duties but also a way to worship the sacred feet of Lord Shiva. This is because cows provide all the materials needed for yagnas performed for Lord Shiva. Therefore, protecting cows becomes a Shiva Punya (meritorious act) that provides the means for Shiva sacrifices. Lord Shiva delights in Panchagavya and Thiruneeru. Therefore, protecting those exalted cows that provide Panchagavya and Thiruneeru, which are necessary for Lord Shiva's sacred ablution, becomes a Shiva Punya. Furthermore, Lord Shiva loves herds of cows. Therefore, Shiva will be pleased with those who protect cows. He will bestow grace. Therefore, protecting cows is also a way to please Lord Shiva. Among the ways to praise Lord Shiva, Shiva Archana (worship of Shiva) and yagna are excellent. Thus, protecting cows, which provide the means for Shiva Puja and Shiva yagna, is the best path. It is also the best duty. Having said all this, he tells the cowherd,

"From now on, I will graze these cows. You no longer need to graze them." He dismisses him. Hearing this, the cowherd was frightened and bowed to Visarasarumar, abandoning the profession of cow grazing. Thiruchengalur was a village inhabited by Brahmins. They were the leaders in that village. Visarasarumar was one of those Brahmins. Therefore, the cowherd considered Visarasarumar's command to be the command of the village community and abandoned the profession of cow grazing. Visarasarumar said, "I will now graze these cows myself," didn't he? As soon as the cowherd left, he himself undertook the task of cow grazing. Grazing cows is the traditional profession of cowherds. Grazing cows is not one of the professions prescribed for Brahmins. Brahmins are not enjoined to graze cows. It is a traditional profession belonging to cowherds. Nevertheless, here, the cowherd, whose traditional profession was grazing cows, ceases to graze them. Visarasarumar, who came from the Brahmin tradition and was not engaged in the profession of cow grazing, now decides to graze cows. Why did this happen? The answer to this is found in the previous verse. It happens according to the rules we saw, that grazing cows is a superior duty to any other profession or duty, and that this duty of cow grazing is also a path to Shiva worship. Having said this, Visarasarumar obtained the approval of the other Brahmins in the village and began to graze cows. This is because the cows in that village belonged to various Brahmin families. Therefore, he needed their consent to perform this duty of protecting cows. Furthermore, cow grazing is not a traditional profession for Brahmins. Therefore, to do something that is not a traditional profession for Brahmins, he needed the approval of the Brahmins of that village. Therefore, for these two reasons, Visarasarumar obtained the approval of the other Brahmins in the village and began to perform the duty of protecting cows, saying, "I will now graze these cows myself." Furthermore, the word "Pasu" (cow) also has the meaning of "soul" here. Therefore, when Visarasarumar said, "I will now graze these cows myself," it was not limited to cows but also had the inner meaning that "I will save all of us, who are souls." This is because, while the Nayanar was engaged in this task of grazing cows, the Shiva Puja he performed, the Chandesa position he attained through that Shiva Puja, and the benefit of Shiva Puja that the world would receive by worshipping him, were all yet to happen. Therefore, when the Nayanar says, "I will graze cows," it has the inner meaning that he will save the great multitude of souls. Thus, Sekkizhar Perumaan praises Visarasarumar, also known as Chandesura Nayanar, as "Dheiva Marai Siruvar" (the divine Vedic child) who came to protect the herds of cows. We will continue to contemplate how Visarasarumar grazed cows and how he protected them. Thiruchitrambalam.

Visarasarumar, also known as Chandesa Nayanar, takes on the duty of grazing cattle himself and begins to graze them. As we have already seen, Visarasarumar was aware of the greatness of cows. He knew and understood how the greatness of cows was described in the Vedas, Agamas, and other scriptures. Therefore, he grazed the cows accordingly. The Shiva Dharmottaram, a text, describes how cows should be protected. According to it, Visarasarumar protected the cows. In the Shiva Dharmottaram, the qualities of protecting cows are specially explained under the chapter "Gopuraviyal." The Shiva Dharmottaram is a text in Tamil, compiled by Maraignanasambandha Nayanar, which is the essence of the Shivagamas. In the Gopuraviyal section of this text, Maraignanasambandha Perumaan has graciously explained the methods of protecting cows. Let us see them as follows: When protecting and grazing cows, one must protect and graze them without causing them any harm. One must protect cows from fear. One must gently stroke them with a stick made of palasa wood and urge them forward with "Go, go." One should not harm them by getting angry, scolding, or beating them without compassion. Those who harm them in such a way will go to hell. Those who understand the greatness of cows, worship them, and offer them grass will attain Shivaloka. We can see in Thirumooladeva Nayanar's Thirumandiram that one should offer a mouthful of grass to a cow, which is beneficial for all. Thirumoolar, who was a Shivayogi, did this very act to alleviate the suffering of cows. The Shivayogi left his body and entered the body of Moolan, a cowherd, and grazed the cows. Thus, the Shivayogi entered the body of Moolan, the cowherd, through Parakaya Pravesam (entering another's body), protected the cows, and made them happy. We will see all these stories in the Puranam of Thirumooladeva Nayanar. Furthermore, the Shiva Dharmottaram states that even touching cows will remove all our evil deeds and increase all good deeds. Cows bestow both worldly and otherworldly benefits upon people. By providing the means for Shiva worship, cows also help us attain Shiva's feet. To protect cows, cow sheds (goshalas) must be built according to rules. The floor of the goshala should be made of pure soil, such as river sand, ant-hill soil, stream soil, soil from the base of a peepal tree, or soil from the base of a bilva tree. In the goshala, separate places should be arranged for young calves, mature calves, and sick calves. We have contemplated this briefly in the Puranam of Anaya Nayanar. Every day, the cow urine and cow dung in the goshala should be removed and cleaned. To prevent mosquitoes, incense should be offered, lamps should be lit, and the goshala should be decorated with flowers. Cows should be gently led into it with the word "Swasthi" and offered grass with love. Sick cows should be kept in a separate place, given medicine, and protected. It is a great meritorious act to have compassion for weak cows and buy and raise them. On every Ashtami day, cows should be bathed, adorned with flowers, offered rice and water, shown incense and lamps, worshipped, and revered. During summer, cows should be grazed in good groves. During the rainy season, they should be grazed in forest areas with light rain, and during winter, in sunny places, and protected from suffering. They should be grazed in lands with good grass and allowed to drink water from pure and good water sources. Those who do not protect cows from suffering, those who do not worship cows, and kings who do not punish those who do not protect cows will go to hell. Stones and pillars should be erected for cows to rub against. That is, stones should be erected at various places on the roads where cows walk. Thus, grazing cows will rub their bodies against these stones and find comfort. Such stones and pillars should be erected at various places. Cows should be donated to Lord Shiva and to acharyas. A bull with good characteristics should be donated to the Shiva shrine. Donating bulls for Lord Shiva's service is also a great Shiva Punya. Those who load burdens on barren cows, those who load burdens on bulls, and those who cause suffering to bulls by riding on them will fall into hell and suffer. Shivakavimani Ayya writes that Sekkizhar Nayanar has indicated in many places the tradition that bulls should not be used even for plowing. Therefore, it is implicitly understood that burdens should not be loaded on cows. Offering the five sacred ablutions (Panchagavya) given by cows to Lord Shiva is a supreme Shiva Punya. When a cow calves, the calf should be allowed to drink its mother's milk for two months, and only then should the milk be offered to Lord Shiva for sacred ablution. When the calf has drunk milk and left the udder, the udder should be washed with water before milking. Those who milk the mother cow out of greed, without leaving milk for the calf, will fall into hell and suffer for a long time, then be born on earth, suffer from severe hunger, and beg from house to house. Even if they beg, they will suffer without getting it. The milk of a Kapil (brownish-yellow) cow should be given only to Lord Shiva. Those who drink that milk without doing so will fall into hell. Untouchables should not enter cow sheds. If they do, they will fall into the hell of burning mouths for countless ages and suffer without release. If enemies seize cows, they should be resisted and protected. If it is not possible to protect them, those who sacrifice their lives in the attempt to protect cows will attain Shivapadam. Sekkizhar Perumaan, in the Periyapuranam, instructs us on the methods of protecting cows and their greatness through the stories of three Nayanars: Anaya Nayanar, Chandesura Nayanar, and Thirumooladeva Nayanar. The Vedas and Agamas speak of this special quality. These are extensively discussed in the Shiva Dharmottaram. The worship of cows is also prescribed for Saivites in the Nitya Karma Vidhi Paddhatis. These are all the methods of protecting cows mentioned in the Shiva Dharmottaram. Shrimath Arumuga Navalar has also mentioned these in his commentary on the Puranam of Chandesura Nayanar. Furthermore, Shivakavimani Ayya writes the following commentary: The Vedas, Agamas, and many other texts speak uniformly about the greatness of cow protection and the evil of not protecting cows. Those who live according to the teachings of these texts are from our sacred land of Bharata Khanda. However, in this era, all these disciplines have changed. Today, it is said that it is a great meritorious act to simply not slaughter cows for food, let alone protect them. Is this not the cruelty of time? Scriptures state that even if a single drop of cow's blood falls on the ground, millions of asuras will arise from it and destroy the world. But every day, the blood of cows slaughtered to fill human stomachs flows like a river.

No. What a chaotic state this country is in now! The wise believe that the day this terrible sin began in our sacred land was the day countless sufferings began for our country. There are people of other religions who are merciless towards cows, who slaughter cows, and who eat cow meat. It is a great sin to sell cows or calves to them, or to killers, untouchables, and so on. The one who sells cows to someone who commits such a sin is also responsible for the sin committed by the buyer. This is a truth stated in the scriptures of wisdom. Even selling sick cows, old cows, weak cows, and considering the small profit from them as wealth, is a sin. Those who own such sick cows, old cows, weak cows, and cows that have stopped giving milk, should themselves keep and protect those cows. That is their duty. If they cannot protect them, it is proper to entrust them to those who can protect cows. All those who cannot protect cows individually should gather together and establish cow protection sheds and try to protect them through that, which is also a great duty and a Shiva Punya. It is also meritorious to create lands with grass for cows to graze in outside the village. Conversely, seizing lands with grass where cows graze is a great sin. If the people of today understand and try to reform these things, it will be beneficial for the country and for us. Thus, Shivakavimani Ayya writes his commentary. Chapter 11 — Chandesura Nayanar

Sekkizhar Peruman describes the form that Chandesura Nayanar took to graze the cows. Visarasarmanar, also known as Chandesura Nayanar, appears with a staff and a rope in his hand for grazing the cows. It is essential for those who graze cows to have a rope and a staff in their hands. A rope is needed to tie cows and calves when necessary. A staff or stick helps to guide the cows. Previously, the cowherd who grazed the cows also carried a staff in his hand. Now, Visarasarmanar also takes on the form of a cowherd with a staff in his hand to graze the cows. The staff used for grazing cows should only be used to guide them, not to harm them as the earlier cowherd did. We can recall here that the very staff held by Visarasarmanar later became an axe, with which he severed his father's feet. Visarasarmanar, who set out to graze the cows, goes with the appearance that signifies his brahmacharya (celibate student) state: a topknot with a tuft of hair, a deer skin, a triple sacred thread, and a loincloth. Brahmacharis should have a topknot with a tuft of hair. This small tuft of hair is a distinguishing mark of brahmacharis. It is called Panchasikai. Next, Sekkizhar sings, "deer skin and triple sacred thread adorning his small chest." Tying a deer skin to the sacred thread and wearing it is a custom for brahmacharis. He wears a loincloth around his waist. Wearing a loincloth around the waist is also a brahmachari custom. That loincloth should be pure and white. Visarasarmanar wears such a loincloth. Thus, Visarasarmanar, with the staff and rope for grazing cows in his hand, with his small tuft of hair swaying, with the deer skin and sacred thread adorning his small chest, and with a shining loincloth around his waist, grazes the cows so that milk and calves may increase. The benefits derived from cows are milk and calves. Of these, milk is an immediate benefit, available daily. A calf is considered a long-term, great benefit. These are the two benefits of grazing cows. Visarasarmanar grazes the cows so that both of these may increase. What does it mean for milk and calves to increase? It means that both should increase equally, without one diminishing the other. In these times, many, out of greed for milk, milk the cows without leaving any for the calves, thus ruining the calves' growth. We also see the cruel sight today of people making a fake calf out of straw and showing it to the cow to milk it. Others might only want calves and waste the milk. However, here it is implied that one should graze cows in such a way that both milk and calves increase, not like these examples. Thus, he grazes the cows so that both milk and calves increase. He gives the cows as much grass as they need, allowing them to eat. During times of abundant grass, he lets the cows graze in those areas and eat the grass. He plucks grass with his own hands and gives it to the cows. When cows are able to graze, they should be allowed to eat the grass by grazing. When that is not possible, grass should be plucked and given to the cows to eat. There are times when cows cannot graze. If it rains, cows cannot go out to graze. Even if cows are not well, they cannot go out to graze.

They cannot. In places where cows graze, there might be obstacles like mounds, pits, mud, and other natural features, preventing them from grazing in many directions. Due to such difficulties, when cows are unable to go and graze, grass should be plucked and fed to them. The Nayanar feeds them in this manner. Furthermore, cows should be made to drink water in water bodies where there is good water. The places where cows drink water should not be muddy. The water bodies should not be deep where cows enter. There should be no reeds or other bushes. Cows should be made to drink water in such water bodies that are free from these obstacles and have good, clean water. Moreover, the water should be pure, free from decaying matter, silt, and the like, and cows should be made to drink in such pure water sources. Not only that, but the fears of cows should also be removed. The fear that cows have from tigers and other animals should be removed. Cows should not be carried away by enemies or others. Such fears should also be removed. Cows should be led along paths that are free from obstacles like pits, mounds, stones, thorns, and where there is no possibility of attack by dangerous animals. After cows have eaten, they should be allowed to rest so that the food they ate can be digested. For this, after the cows have grazed, they should be seated in the shade. Diseases affecting cows should be cured. Visarasarmanar also does this. He provides delicious food that helps in the physical growth of cows and nourishes them. In all these ways, Visarasarmanar cares for and grazes the cows, ensuring they suffer no harm. Not only that, but he also ensures that the sweet, nectar-like milk provided by the cows reaches the homes of their rightful owners. Sekkizhar Peruman here refers to cow's milk as "amuda madhurappaal" (nectar-like sweet milk). Nectar is a delicious food that removes disease and aids in bodily growth. Similarly, cow's milk removes disease, aids in our bodily growth, and is also a delicious food. It is sweet. Cow's milk is naturally sweet without adding sugar or other sweetening agents. Furthermore, this milk serves as an offering for Lord Shiva's yagna (sacrificial ritual). This milk given by the cow. Therefore, cow's milk bestows the imperishable, immortal state of Shiva, making people attain permanence. Even in this story, we can see that this milk itself brings nectar to Visarasarmanar. Thus, Visarasarmanar grazes and protects the cows on the banks of the Manniyaaru river, near the Mullai land (forest region) and the low-lying areas, and near the groves of the Marutham land (agricultural region) that yield cool pearls. In this verse, Sekkizhar Peruman describes all the places where Visarasarmanar grazes the cows. The Marutham land, which is the land area on the river bank, the groves on the river bank, the low-lying areas formed by silt accumulation on the river bank—such low-lying areas have abundant grass and plants. In the Marutham land, due to abundant water resources, there are many groves. Visarasarmanar takes the cows to all these types of places and grazes them. By grazing the cows in this manner, they thrive. Thus, after grazing the cows during the day, Visarasarmanar also collects samithu (sacred firewood) on his way back. Collecting samithu for the daily agnikarya (fire ritual) in the homes of Brahmins is one of the duties of brahmacharis. Therefore, he collects such samithu and returns home before evening. In this way, for many days, Visarasarmanar grazed the cows,

He used to graze them in such a way that they themselves yielded abundant milk, and he would also collect samithu, returning home before evening. Let us continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 12 — Chandesura Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 256. The section on "Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam" (The Chapter of Ruling the World with Threefold Virtues). The Puranam of Chandesura Nayanar. We have contemplated all the wonders of Visarasarmanar grazing the cows. Because Visarasarmanar grazed the cows, all those cows received good grass and good water, thriving without fear and feeling very happy. Therefore, the cows' bodies were well-nourished and beautiful. Since the cows were well-nourished, beautiful, and happy, their udders swelled, and their teats secreted milk. If cows are protected with love, they become happy. When cows are happy, they yield a lot of milk. It is also true that some cows, pleased by listening to songs, yield more milk. This is why Gopalan, who came as a cowherd, plays the flute as Venugopalan. We saw the custom of cowherds playing the flute in the story of Aanaya Nayanar. Thus, the idea is that if cows are happy, they will yield a lot of milk. The proverb says, "Milk a dancing cow by dancing, milk a singing cow by singing." Even if cows have abundant milk, they generally secrete milk only when calves suckle. The secretion of milk in cows occurs due to the joyful state of their love for their calves. This is why the vatsalya (affection) of cows for their calves is compared to God's grace for living beings. That is why God is called Bhaktavatsalan. Seeing Visarasarmanar, who grazes them well, and recognizing his mother-like nature, all the cows yield milk without holding back. Generally, cows secrete milk in the morning and evening. Here, because Visarasarmanar grazes the cows so well, and due to his mother-like nature, the cows, filled with great love, yield milk even at noon and at night. That is, morning and evening are the times for milking. Therefore, after morning and evening, at noon and at night, the cows' udders would normally be empty of milk. Even udders that have been milked now swell with milk again at noon and at night, secreting it. This is because of the way Visarasarmanar grazes them and because of their love for Visarasarmanar. All the Brahmins are very happy to see that the sacrificial cows, which aid in their yagnas, have grown well. Compared to before, these sacrificial cows are now thriving and yielding many times more milk. The Brahmins of Thiruchengalur rejoice, saying that because the brahmachari Visarasarmanar grazes the cows with such care and love, following the proper methods for protecting and grazing them, the cows are thriving and yielding milk in such abundance. Even the cows, though separated from their calves at home, seeing Visarasarmanar as their secret calf, and considering themselves to have become mothers to the calf that is Visarasarmanar, mooed and secreted milk from their udders without holding back. When cows go out to graze, it is customary to leave their calves at home and take only the cows for grazing. Even though the cows came alone to graze, leaving their calves at home, they were not sad. Instead, seeing Visarasarmanar as their own calf, they yielded milk spontaneously. Normally, cows separated from their calves would naturally feel sad. But here, not only were they not sad, but their love increased,

And they stood with swollen udders, yielding milk. This is because, contrary to nature, there was a beloved calf, Visarasarmanar, present there. Seeing Visarasarmanar, the cows considered him their calf, thought of themselves as his mother, and approached him, melting with affection, mooing, and secreting milk from their udders. These are all characteristics of mother cows. They not only desired to be mothers to their own calves but also eagerly accepted the state of being mothers to Visarasarmanar, the Vedic calf. Thus, in this state of motherhood, milk flowed from their udders. The story of Mangaiyarkkarasi Ammaiyar, whose breasts secreted milk upon hearing the state of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, is also worthy of remembrance here. "The queen of the southern Pandya land, with a humble heart, whose breasts secreted milk when she heard the word 'child' - such was her love," says the 54th verse of Thirukalittruppadiyar. The spontaneous yielding of milk upon remembering or seeing a calf is a sign of immense love and abundant milk. Such cows can still be seen in some places today. This very act of spontaneously yielding milk becomes the starting point for the Nayanar's story. In those days, the cows that came to him spontaneously yielded milk from their udders without holding back. Seeing this, Visarasarmanar was delighted. In the mind of Visarasarmanar, who stood firm in the righteous path of Shiva, the milk that the cows spontaneously yielded was an offering for Lord Shiva's sacred bath (Thirumanjanam), as realized by Visarasarmanar, our benevolent master. Thus, having thought and understood this, within that clarity arose in him the yearning to perform Shiva Puja, which is the true worship of Lord Shiva. The clear mind, which understood that Shiva Puja is the means to liberation, desired and yearned to perform Shiva Puja. The arising of this yearning in Visarasarmanar's mind to perform Shiva Puja happened by the grace of God, according to the inclinations of his previous birth. The thought that this milk, spontaneously yielded by the cows, would be an offering for Lord Shiva's sacred bath arose in Visarasarmanar's mind. However, a question first arises: can one use the milk of cows for Lord Shiva's sacred bath without the consent of the cow owners? Would it not be a transgression? But here, the cows themselves yield milk without the consent of their owners. Therefore, using this spontaneously yielded milk for Lord Shiva's sacred bath is appropriate. If this were not done, the spontaneously yielded milk, which would not be useful for yagnas, would be wasted, spilling onto the ground. The owners of the cows are Brahmins. The cows they raise provide milk for their yagnas. These Brahmins raise cows specifically to obtain the milk needed for their homas and yagnas. Now, the cows themselves are yielding milk. This spontaneously yielded milk is spilling onto the ground and being wasted. It is not being used for the Brahmins' yagnas. Therefore, he realizes that using this spontaneously yielded milk for Lord Shiva's sacred bath is perfectly right. For, among all yagnas, Shiva Puja, the yagna for Shiva, is the most excellent, is it not? Thus, having understood that this milk is for Lord Shiva's sacred bath, the yearning to perform Shiva Puja now arises in Visarasarmanar, as a continuation of the penance he performed in all his previous births. Generally, worldly people, upon seeing milk, would realize that it is useful for themselves, for their children, and as good food for others.

Those with evil minds, even when seeing milk that could be used for God's sacred bath, would argue that it is being wasted instead of being used for people, and thus incur hell. We see such people who think and argue in this way even today. But Visarasarmanar, due to the continuity of his previous births, due to the continuation of the Shiva Puja he performed in all his previous births, upon seeing the spontaneously yielded milk, had the thought arise in him, by the grace of God, that it should be used for Lord Shiva's sacred bath. This is the difference between him and other worldly people, and those who stand firm in the righteous path of Shiva. When Pattinattar was given a box with a needle without an eye, what did others think? They thought that even a useless needle without an eye should be kept and protected in a box. But what did the great Pattinattar think? He realized the truth that "even a needle without an eye will not accompany one to the final journey." Therefore, he renounced nine crore possessions in a single moment and became a lion among ascetics. We can recall that story here as well. This is the difference between those who stand firm in the righteous path of Shiva and those who stand in the worldly path. In Visarasarmanar, from the seed of the inclinations cultivated in previous births, arose the yearning to perform Shiva Puja. As this yearning arose, just as children playfully build houses in the sand, so too, due to the overflowing love for Shiva in Visarasarmanar's mind, he fashioned a Shiva Lingam from the sand of the Manniyaaru river, under an Aathi tree. Then, he also created a Shiva temple from the sand. Using that sand, he built a Shiva Lingam in the sand, and around it, a Shiva temple, and around that, a large circumambulatory path and a gopuram (temple tower). The continuation of Shiva worship performed in previous births came to Visarasarmanar in this birth in the form of a child's playful building of houses in the sand. Therefore, he thus fashioned a Shiva Lingam from sand and also built a temple and gopuram. Lord Shiva accepts and bestows grace upon the worship performed by souls in two places: within (aham) and without (puram). Lord Shiva, residing within souls, accepts the worship performed by them and bestows grace. Lord Shiva, standing outside, in the form of Shiva Lingams and other sacred images, and in the form of Gurus and Jangaamas (wandering ascetics), accepts the worship performed by souls and bestows grace. Therefore, the places where souls worship Lord Shiva externally are of two types: Sthavaram (immovable, like a lingam) and Jangamam (movable, like a Guru). The 118th verse of Sivagnana Siddhiyar states: "Standing in two forms, Sthavaram and Jangamam, the great Lord accepts worship and bestows grace upon the living beings of the earth. If you contemplate the supreme being in your heart, perform complete worship. Worship the supreme being daily with love." This is the verse. What is its meaning or explanation? The Lord, who is in the Sadasiva Tattva, stands outside in the form of the Shiva Lingam inside the temple, and in the form of the Guru and Shiva devotees, who are Jangamas (moving forms), and accepts the worship performed by living beings and bestows grace. These are the places where Lord Shiva stands and bestows grace externally. Internally, He bestows grace by residing within the soul. He accepts the worship performed by souls. Therefore, the meaning is to worship in those respective places. Lord Shiva is beyond speech and mind. The place where He takes form is the Shuddha Tattva. If the Lord, who is beyond speech and mind, takes form only in the Shuddha Tattva, how can those here see and worship Him? Therefore, the Lord, who is in the Sadasiva Tattva, has two ways of bestowing grace upon us: external (puram) and internal (aham). The external, that is, the sacred image inside the temple, is called Sthavaram. Besides that, those who wear sacred ashes and rudraksha are called Jangamas. Therefore, the places of worship outside are of two types: Sthavaram and Jangamam. The same Lord bestows grace by residing within the soul. Therefore, if one worships the Lord who stands in the external forms of Sthavaram and Jangamam, such as the Lingam, the Guru, and the devotees, and also within the soul, the Lord will accept such worship and bestow grace. Therefore, it means that one should worship the Lord with devotion in those respective places every day. It is stated here that there are two places where the Lord bestows grace externally. They are the Lingam and the sacred form of the devotees. Among these, Visarasarmanar worshipped the Lord in the Linga form, which is the sacred image inside the temple. In worshipping the Shiva Lingam externally, there is a rule that one can create a Shiva Lingam from river sand, rudraksha, sandalwood, and many other materials and perform puja. Arumuga Navalar Peruman, in his book "Saiva Vinavidai," in the section on "Shiva Linga Iyal," under the category of "Ishta Lingam," and specifically under "Kshanika Lingam," has stated that a Shiva Lingam can be created and worshipped using the following 12 materials: earth, rice, cooked food, river sand, cow dung, butter, rudraksha, sandalwood, kurcham (a bundle of sacred grass), flower garland, sugar, and flour. Therefore, here, Visarasarmanar worshipped the Shiva Lingam created from river sand. Other children would playfully build houses and other things from river sand. But Visarasarmanar, as a continuation of the Shiva Puja performed in his previous births, as a young boy, desired to perform puja to a Shiva Lingam made of river sand and accordingly fashioned the Shiva Lingam. Not only that, but he also built the circumambulatory path (prakaram), gopuram, and other parts of the temple around the Shiva Lingam from river sand and worshipped. Let us continue to reflect. Chapter 13 — Chandesura Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 257. The section on "Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam" (The Chapter of Ruling the World with Threefold Virtues). The Puranam of Chandesura Nayanar. As the yearning to perform Shiva Puja arose, Visarasarmanar first fashioned a Shiva Lingam from the river sand on the banks of the Manniyaaru river, then the circumambulatory paths, and then the gopuram. The proper method is to first place the Shiva Lingam and, centering it, then build the Shiva temple, gopuram, circumambulatory paths, and other structures accordingly. We see this here. Sekkizhar Peruman also indicates here that it is not the rule to first build the temple, gopuram, etc., and then install the Shiva Lingam inside. Visarasarmanar first fashions the Shiva Lingam from river sand. After that, he builds the Shiva temple. After the Shiva Lingam is installed, a Shiva temple should be built for it. A Shiva temple is a temple dedicated to Lord Shiva. Around it, a circumambulatory path, called the Thiruchuttru, should also be built. The Shiva temple is the temple where Lord Shiva resides. The area around it, for the subsidiary deities (Parivara Moorthigal) such as Ganesha, Muruga, Nandi, Dakshinamurthy, Chandesa, Bhairava, Surya, Chandra, etc., is called the circumambulatory path. It is also the rule to place them in the first prakaram (courtyard) or the first circuit. The Shiva Lingam and Shiva temple built by Chandesura Nayanar are now located in the village of Thiruvaipaadi as a Shiva Lingam and a temple. We saw that Chandesura Nayanar built this under an Aathi tree. This Aathi tree is currently located in the southwest part of the Thiruvaipaadi temple. This is a type of Aathi tree called Kaattaathi (wild Aathi). Similarly, the Uttarapathiyar, who came to bestow grace upon Siruthonda Nayanar, is enshrined under an Aathi tree in the Thiruchengaattangudi temple. That tree is also available for darshan there now. Building the Shiva Lingam, Shiva temple, gopuram, and circumambulatory path for Shiva Puja was the first act performed by Visarasarmanar after the yearning for Shiva Puja arose. For Shiva Puja, the Shiva Lingam and temple are indispensable and are needed first. Therefore, he first built these two. Then, flowers are needed for Shiva Puja. So, Visarasarmanar gathers Aathi flowers and the best of the flowers that bloomed in the Mullai region, considering them as prime flowers. He himself plucks many such sacred flowers (Thiruppallithamam) suitable for adorning Lord Shiva's matted locks, strings them with leaves, and brings them in a flower basket, placing them there. Visarasarmanar brings selected flowers, tender leaves, etc., prescribed by the Agamas for the Lord's puja. These are Aathi flowers and lush tender leaves. It is said that they grew and bloomed naturally in the nearby Mullai region. Sacred flowers for Shiva Puja can be gathered from Nandavanams (flower gardens) cultivated by humans. Here, Chandesura Nayanar gathered flowers that grew naturally in trees and plants in the Mullai region. This is one of the glories of our sacred southern land. This southern land has the glory that all the flowers needed for Shiva Puja bloom naturally on trees and plants. When they are on trees, plants, and creepers, they are called flowers. When they are selected for Lord Shiva's puja, they are called Thiruppallithamam. Thus, Visarasarmanar, knowing the rules for Shiva Puja, plucks the Thiruppallithamam himself and collects them in a basket. How to select flowers

We have extensively discussed the details of selecting flowers in the Puranams of Eripattar Nayanar and Muruga Nayanar. We should recall them here. To carry the plucked Thiruppallithamam, a flower basket is needed. Therefore, Visarasarmanar himself makes a flower basket from leaves. Thus, he collects and brings the flowers in a flower basket made by weaving leaves. Flowers for Lord Shiva's puja should not be carried in bare hands. They should not be carried in cloth or other materials. It is the rule that they should be carried in proper flower baskets. Visarasarmanar, who had built the Shiva Lingam, Shiva temple, gopuram, and circumambulatory path for Shiva Puja, then needed materials for Shiva Puja, including sacred bath (Thirumanjanam) and flowers. Sekkizhar Peruman, after explaining that Visarasarmanar gathered the necessary flowers for Shiva Puja, now explains how he sought the sacred bath material for Shiva Puja. For Lord Shiva's sacred bath in Shiva Puja, milk is needed. Therefore, Visarasarmanar takes nine new pots (Navakumbams) and collects milk from the cows. Sekkizhar Peruman here mentions "good Navakumbams." The Kamika Agama states that one can install Navakumbams and perform abhishekam (sacred bath) for Atmarttha Puja (self-worship). Installing nine pots and performing Thirumanjanam is one of the Agamic rules. It is stated in the book "Saiva Bhushanam," authored by Sri Panchakshara Yogigal of Kanchipuram, that one should install nine pots and worship in Atmarttha Shiva Puja. In the 104th shloka of this book "Saiva Bhushanam," Sri Panchakshara Yogigal has explained the method mentioned in the Kamika Agama that one can install Navakumbams and perform abhishekam for Atmarttha Puja. They have also explained how to do it. One should draw a square mandala of two cubits length, pouring one measure of paddy, and create nine divisions (Navakoshtas) within it. Spread darpa grass in it and install the Shiva Kumbham in the center. To the north of the Shiva Kumbham, install the Vardhini Kumbham, and in the eight directions starting from the east, place eight kalashas (pots). In these, invoke Lord Shiva in the Shiva Kumbham, Parameswari in the Vardhini Kumbham, and the Ashtavidyeswaras in the other eight kumbhams, offering dhoopa (incense) and deepa (lamp) and worshipping them. Furthermore, Navakumbams also means new pots. Thus, Visarasarmanar went to the place where the cows were grazing to obtain the Navakumbams. According to the rule that milk should be obtained from one teat of a cow, he touched one teat, and the cows yielded milk. Earlier, the cows, considering Visarasarmanar as their calf, spontaneously yielded milk. Now, Visarasarmanar approached the cows, and as soon as he slightly touched their teats, the cows, overflowing with love, secreted and yielded milk. Thus, Visarasarmanar did not milk the cows; merely by touching their teats, the cows, filled with love, secreted milk. He collected the milk yielded by the cows in pots and installed those pots inside the temple he had built from white sand. We had already seen that he had collected and kept the sacred flowers (Thiruppallithamam). Thus, Visarasarmanar, having filled the pots with the milk needed for the sacred bath and having collected the sacred flowers, performs Shiva Puja, which is called Archana, with the love that is a continuation of his previous births. What is meant by Shiva Puja, also called Archana, mentioned here? It means that in the Shiva Lingam that Visarasarmanar fashioned from sand, he establishes the Vidya Deham (knowledge body) of Lord Shiva and performs the mantra, bhavana (meditation), and kriya (ritual actions) prescribed in the Agamas to make Shiva manifest in it.

This includes performing Linga Shuddhi (purification of the Lingam), placing the Adhara Shakti (base energy) to the Adi Shakti (primordial energy) on the Linga Peetha (pedestal), performing Shivaasana Pujas (worship of Shiva's seat), then invoking the Vidya Deham (knowledge body) as the Moorthi (deity) on the Lingam, and then meditating on the Paramasiva, who is the Moorthiman (possessor of form) and the life force within that body, above the Dvadashanta (twelve-ended point), and invoking Him. All these are described. He performs the Shiva Avahanam (invocation) as described in the Agamas. Then, he requests Lord Shiva, who has been invoked in the Shiva Lingam, to mercifully reside in the Shiva Lingam until the puja is completed. After that, he offers flowers with appropriate mantras, folds his hands in prayer, praises, and bows. For Svayambhu Lingams (self-manifested Lingams) and Daivika Lingams (divinely created Lingams), this Nyasa Vidhi (ritual of placing mantras on the body) and Avahana Vidhi (invocation ritual) are not performed. However, for other types of Lingams, such as Manuda Lingams (human-made Lingams) and the six types of Lingams, the Shivagamas prescribe that Nyasa and Avahanam should be performed. Therefore, here, Visarasarmanar, also known as Chandesura Nayanar, installs the Shiva Lingam in river sand. Thus, according to the rules of Atmarttha Shiva Puja prescribed in the Agamas, he performs Nyasa, Avahanam, and other rituals, and sees Lord Shiva manifest in that Shiva Lingam. Then, he performs Thirumanjanam (sacred bath) for Lord Shiva there. In the Puranam of Thirukuripputhonda Nayanar, Kamakshi Ambal worships the Lord under a mango tree on the banks of the Kampa river. That Lord is a Svayambhu Moorthi, who manifested Himself by His own grace. Therefore, there, without performing the Avahana ritual, Ammaiyar performed puja by starting with Thirumanjanam, applying sacred paste, offering incense, lamps, and other pujas according to Agamic rules. What Kamakshi Ambal did there was for the welfare of the world, performed on the self-manifested deity. Therefore, it is called Pararttha Puja (worship for others). What Chandesura Nayanar does here is considered Atmarttha Puja (self-worship). Thus, according to the method prescribed by the Shivagamas, according to the rules of Atmarttha Puja, and as a result of his experience of performing Shiva Puja in all his previous births, the young boy Visarasarmanar performs the puja by performing Shuddhis (purifications), Asana Puja (worship of the seat), Nyasa, Avahanam, and other rituals, following the procedures prescribed in the Agamas, from Avahanam to Pushpadanam (offering of flowers), with ten actions and five services. Regarding this method of puja, Shivakavimani Aiya writes, "See actions 23 and 24 of Siddhanta Saraavali Kriyapada." Furthermore, how to perform Atmarttha Puja and how to receive Diksha (initiation) can be learned by receiving Diksha from qualified Shivacharyas and through their Upadesha (instruction). Sekkizhar mentions in the 1218th verse, "due to the continuation of previous knowledge." According to this, these Agamic rules were made known to Visarasarmanar's mind by the grace of Lord Shiva, due to the practice he had done before. The yearning for Shiva Puja that Visarasarmanar had was born of love. It ripened into mature devotion. As this mature devotion ripened, he repeatedly poured the white milk, performing the sacred bath. Thus, with great love, with ripe love, as he performed Thirumanjanam for Lord Shiva, Lord Shiva, residing within the Shiva Lingam, accepted the Shiva Puja performed by Visarasarmanar. We saw that he installed the Shiva Lingam from white sand, from river sand. After performing Nyasa and Avahanam, this Shiva Lingam became the Shiva Moorthi in which Lord Shiva resided.

Within it, Lord Shiva, who resided within the Shiva Lingam, accepted Chandesura Nayanar's puja. Even though Lord Shiva is omnipresent, He stood manifest in that Shiva Lingam and accepted the puja performed by Visarasarmanar. How does Visarasarmanar perform Shiva Puja? He does it with great absorption (Layam). Layam is a Sanskrit word, and Sekkizhar Peruman here refers to it as "with a unified thought in his heart." What is unified thought or Layam? It is explained as the worshipper and the worshipped Lord Shiva, these two entities, not being seen as separate, but merging into one in thought. Sekkizhar Peruman refers to this as "with a unified thought." In Sanskrit, this is called Layam. Such worship is the true worship desired by God. The great child of Sengalur, whose glory shines, performs Shiva Puja by filling with love those parts of the puja, such as the sacred bath, that he did not seek out. We saw that Visarasarmanar arranged for leaves and flowers, which are parts of the puja, and milk for the sacred bath. Visarasarmanar did not seek out other things needed for the sacred bath, such as water, sandalwood, incense, and offerings. He performs the puja by filling these unobtained items with love. It is a rule that when the necessary materials for Shiva Puja are unavailable, one should fill them with love and perform the puja. This is called Bhavana Puja (mental worship). When the necessary materials for puja are unavailable, one should imagine that those materials are present and perform the puja. Sekkizhar Peruman refers to this as "filling with love those parts of the sacred bath and other initial items that were not sought." Thus, Chandesura Nayanar, also known Visarasarmanar, performs the puja according to the Shivagamas, adorning with clothes, sandalwood, flower garlands, ornaments, etc., performing Archana with appropriate mantras, offering Dhoopa (incense), Deepa (lamp), Arghyam (water offering), offering food, performing Avarana Puja (worship of surrounding deities), praising, bowing, chanting mantras, and following other rituals. Even after performing the puja, the joy that the Nayanar felt remains undiminished. Lord Shiva accepts the Shiva Puja performed by Visarasarmanar and bestows grace with delight. Here, Shivakavimani Aiya writes an important note: "Even when the instruments and materials for puja are sought but not found, the puja should not stop. It is a rule that the puja should be performed without interruption or hindrance." Therefore, in times when the necessary materials for Shiva Puja are unavailable, it is also a rule that one should fill them with mental imagination (Bhavana) and perform Shiva Puja without interruption. The details of these are found in the Agamas. Flowers and water, which are essential for puja, are available everywhere in this sacred land. But even when these are unavailable, the puja should be performed mentally without hindrance. Thus writes Shivakavimani Aiya. Let us continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.

This endeavor and practice of performing excellent puja, which requires many days of effort and practice, is very rare and difficult even for elders. But for the young Visarasarmanar, it happened naturally, as a form of play. For Visarasarmanar, Shiva Puja was a game, and the game itself was Shiva Puja. He told the other Brahmins in the village, who owned the cows, that Visarasarmanar was taking all their milk and performing Shiva Puja. It is the duty of parents to correct children. This is the way of the world. We should also remember that Visarasarmanar was a boy of seven years. Echadattan, Visarasarmanar's father, came and stood before the assembly of Brahmins. Then all the Brahmins addressed Echadattan, saying,

"Listen to the evil deed your son is doing, claiming to graze all our cows," and they recounted all the events that had transpired. The assembly members told Echadattan that Visarasarmanar, with a joyful heart and great love, was taking all the cows that yielded milk for the Brahmins' yagnas, as if to graze them, but then milking them on the banks of the Manniyaaru river, spilling the milk on the sand, and acting according to his own whims. They also made it clear that the one who had directly witnessed all these actions of Visarasarmanar was standing there as a witness. Therefore, this was a firsthand account, not something heard from others. Upon hearing this from the assembly of Brahmins, Echadattan, Visarasarmanar's father, was greatly afraid. He decided that he must personally witness his son's actions. That night passed. The next day dawned. Visarasarmanar, as usual, went to graze the cows. Echadattan, who had not slept all night due to great worry, now that day had broken and Visarasarmanar had gone to graze the cows, decided that he must see it directly. Echadattan decided to hide and watch his son's actions. In truth, after the Brahmin assembly made such accusations, Echadattan could have directly called and questioned his son, or he could have openly gone with his son and observed his actions directly. Instead of doing all this, Echadattan decided to hide and watch. Echadattan, who was born in the Kashyapa gotra, had studied the Vedas and lived according to them, being a Brahmin, a Vedic scholar, and an elder. However, he had not realized the inner meaning of the Vedas, which is Shiva Gnanam (knowledge of Shiva). That is why he thought of hiding and watching his son's actions. Only those with deceit in their hearts would hide themselves in this manner. Avvai Perumatti's Moothurai, 25th verse, sings: "A snake, knowing it has poison, hides and dwells; a fearless water snake swims openly. Those with deceit in their hearts hide themselves; those without deceit in their hearts do not hide." That is, a poisonous snake, knowing its venom, lives hidden. A non-poisonous water snake lives openly, swimming for all to see. Similarly, those with deceit in their hearts will not speak openly; they will hide. Those without deceit in their hearts will speak openly and act openly. Therefore, in accordance with "a snake, knowing it has poison, hides and dwells," Echadattan, Visarasarmanar's father, instead of directly asking his son or going with him to find out, decided to hide and watch. Visarasarmanar, the son of Echadattan, took all the cows of the village and grazed them on the banks of the Manniyaaru river. Echadattan, his father, followed him secretly to the banks of the Manniyaaru river and climbed a Kura tree there, hiding to watch what would happen next. That day was the day Echadattan was freed from unexpiated Shiva-aparaadha (offense against Shiva) and attained salvation. Moreover, it was the unique auspicious day when the world would attain the benefit of Shiva Puja through the events of that story. Echadattan hid in a Kura tree. This is a type of tree. Its flower is very dear to Lord Shiva. We saw in the 240th verse of Periya Puranam that the Kura tree should also be planted in the Nandavanams (flower gardens) created for Lord Shiva's worship. In such a Kura tree, Echadattan hid and was about to witness the events unfolding there. At that time, the brahmachari Visarasarmanar, according to his daily custom, bathed, built a Shiva temple and a circumambulatory path from sand for Lord Shiva, plucked the flowers and buds that bloomed that day, collected the milk that the cows themselves yielded in pots, arranged all these, and began to perform that rare Shiva Puja even that day. According to the rules of the Saiva Agamas, and out of the maturity of the love that arose in his heart, he began to perform Shiva Puja even that day, as if in play. He offered the sacred flowers (Thiruppallithamam) he had collected on Lord Shiva's sacred head and performed Archana. He lovingly took the sweet pots of milk and performed Thirumanjanam for Lord Shiva. At that moment, an auspicious event occurred, as if by the grace of Lord Shiva. Let us continue to reflect. Chapter 15 — Chandesura Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 259. The section on "Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam" (The Chapter of Ruling the World with Threefold Virtues). The Puranam of Chandesura Nayanar. As usual, Chandesura Nayanar was performing Shiva Puja that day. He offered sacred flowers (Thiruppallithamam) and performed Archana. Then, he took the pots of milk and lovingly performed Thirumanjanam for Lord Shiva. At that moment, the event occurred by the grace of Lord Shiva. Echadattan, Visarasarmanar's father, who was on the Kura tree, saw his son Visarasarmanar performing Thirumanjanam with milk and became angry. Why is it mentioned here that Echadattan's anger occurred by divine grace? Because if Echadattan had not become angry, that day's event would not have happened. If it had not happened, Visarasarmanar's greatness would not have been revealed to the world. Therefore, the implication here is that divine grace itself caused him to lose his wisdom. Grace is the form of wisdom. Does it cause wisdom to be lost? The implication is that divine grace acted in this way for Visarasarmanar's greatness to be revealed. Echadattan, Visarasarmanar's father, came down from the Kura tree, and, angered by seeing his son performing Thirumanjanam, struck his son's back with the staff he held in his hand. He struck him while uttering very harsh words. Echadattan stood behind Visarasarmanar and struck Visarasarmanar's back. But Visarasarmanar, the great little devotee who was performing the sacred service of Shiva Puja, had his intense love firmly fixed on Lord Shiva during the Archana. Therefore, he did not feel the blows. He was completely absorbed in Shiva Puja, performing it with intense love and great concentration (Layam). So, he did not feel his father striking his back. Echadattan, growing angrier and angrier, struck him many times. Because of his anger, he had lost his wisdom. So he struck him many times. But Visarasarmanar, who was greater than all, remained steadfast in his sacred duty of performing Thirumanjanam and continued to do it. Seeing Visarasarmanar, who continued to perform Shiva Puja by pouring milk for Thirumanjanam without reacting in the slightest, no matter how much he was beaten, Echadattan became even angrier, lost his wisdom, became bewildered, and was filled with extreme rage. Consequently, Echadattan kicked the pot of milk for Thirumanjanam, spilling it. Even after striking him many times with the staff he held, it was of no avail. Therefore, not knowing the truth and having lost his wisdom, Echadattan began to commit evil with his feet, just as he had done with his hands. Echadattan kicked the pot of milk for Thirumanjanam, which Visarasarmanar had kept for performing Thirumanjanam for the Lord, the pot filled with milk for Thirumanjanam, which had been set up to perform Thirumanjanam for the Lord. Echadattan kicked and spilled that milk. Thus, Echadattan committed a great Shiva-aparaadha (offense against Shiva). He struck Visarasarmanar, a devotee of Shiva. Now he kicked the pot of milk kept for Lord Shiva's Thirumanjanam. This is a Shiva-aparaadha. An offense against Shiva's devotees is considered even greater than an offense against Shiva. Thus, Echadattan committed two great Shiva-aparaadhas. Whenever he was struck, Visarasarmanar did not feel it, being absorbed in Shiva Puja. But when he saw the pot of milk for Lord Shiva's puja being kicked, Visarasarmanar then focused on what was happening. He did not feel the blows when he was struck, because Visarasarmanar's intense love was entirely focused on Shiva Archana. Therefore, Visarasarmanar did not feel the blows falling on him. But now, Echadattan kicked the pots of milk meant for Shiva Puja. When the pot of milk for Shiva Archana, on which Visarasarmanar had placed all his intense love, was scattered, Visarasarmanar's gaze now fell upon it. Visarasarmanar's external and internal senses were not directed outwards but were entirely absorbed in Shiva Puja. Therefore, the obstruction that came to that Shiva Puja changed his state of absorption (Layam) and made him see the spilled milk pot. Visarasarmanar observed the act of the Thirumanjanam milk pot being spilled. His gaze then fell upon the perpetrator, and he looked to see who had kicked and scattered the milk pot. He realized that it was his own father who had done it. Even though he realized it was his father, only the evil deed his father had committed remained in Visarasarmanar's mind. Only the thought of him being the evil person who had committed the Shiva-aparaadha of kicking the Thirumanjanam milk pot meant for the Lord remained in Visarasarmanar's mind. Echadattan's feet kicked the Thirumanjanam milk pot meant for Lord Shiva, and the milk spilled. Therefore, by spilling the milk meant for Lord Shiva, those feet became feet deserving of punishment. Visarasarmanar decided to punish that transgression. Visarasarmanar was a cowherd. Therefore, he always carried a staff with him. Now, Visarasarmanar took that staff to punish Echadattan's feet. The moment he took it, the staff transformed into an axe (Mazhu). To punish the crime committed by Echadattan's feet, he took the staff, and it transformed into an axe, a tool capable of delivering appropriate punishment. This was by divine command. Visarasarmanar, the great elder whose senses had all become Shiva-senses, therefore, the small staff he took to punish Echadattan's feet transformed into an axe in his hand by Lord Shiva's divine command. He threw it. The axe went and severed Echadattan's feet. The son, Visarasarmanar, severed the two feet of his father, who had obstructed Shiva Puja, with that axe, which served as an instrument to remove the obstruction that occurred in Shiva Puja. The instrument that removes obstacles in Shiva Puja is the Pashupata Astra. One of the components of Shiva Puja prescribed in the Agamas is the removal of obstacles to Shiva Puja. This is called Vighnochcharanam. It is the method of removing obstacles arising from the three worlds—the upper world, the middle world, and the lower world—through appropriate mantras, visualizations, and actions, and then entering the puja. The staff taken by Visarasarmanar transformed into an axe, and when he threw it, Echadattan's feet were severed, and Echadattan fell to the ground. Only one of Echadattan's feet had kicked the milk pot. But both his feet were mutilated and severed. This is because, even though Echadattan kicked the milk pot with one foot, the other foot, which was firmly planted, assisted it. With the support of the other firmly planted foot, the second foot kicked and spilled the milk pot. Therefore, both feet were severed, including the one that assisted the kicking foot, and Echadattan fell to the ground. According to the rules of justice, both the perpetrator and the accomplice should be punished. Thus, having removed the obstruction to his Shiva Puja, Visarasarmanar now began to perform Shiva Puja again. Immediately, Lord Shiva and Uma Devi appeared to Visarasarmanar, riding on the bull. Is it not true that God manifests only when worldly attachments are severed? Until now, Lord Shiva, who had been accepting the puja from within the Shiva Lingam fashioned from sand by Visarasarmanar, now, in that place where Visarasarmanar's love had melted and ripened, manifested very clearly with Uma Devi on the Rishabha Vahana (bull vehicle). Is it not our custom to see the Lord come with the Goddess on the bull whenever He comes to bestow grace upon devotees and grant them His world? With the Bhutaganas (celestial attendants) surrounding Him, and the sages and gods praising the Lord with Vedic chants, Lord Shiva, the immaculate form, manifested with overflowing divine compassion. Visarasarmanar, the young boy whose devotion had matured, saw Lord Shiva, worshipped Him, was filled with immense joy, and fell at His lotus feet. Lord Shiva, with matted locks adorned with a Kondrai garland, addressed Visarasarmanar, the young boy who lay at His feet, saying, "For Our sake, you struck down your own father. Henceforth, We Ourselves shall be your next father," and bestowed His grace. He embraced him, caressed him with grace-filled compassion, and smelled his head with delight, bestowing His grace. Sekkizhar Peruman says, "the young boy who fell at the feet of the Lord." The Lord's feet are two feet. They are the sacred feet that serve as a support and refuge for those who reach Him. The sacred feet are the fullness of divine grace. The shadow of the sacred feet is the state of manifestation for a person within that fullness of grace. The experience of divine grace occurs only after it has manifested. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman says "under the shadow of the sacred feet" instead of "under the sacred feet." The Lord then lifted Visarasarmanar, the young boy who had fallen. This concept also applies to the idea that those who wish to receive grace from elders and God should fall at their feet. He fell at His feet. Therefore, it became His duty to lift the one who had fallen. We should recall here the story of Kulachirrai Nayanar, who wished to uplift the fallen Pandya kingdom, and lay prostrate on the path where Thirugnanasambandha Peruman was coming, remaining fallen until Peruman himself came and lifted him with his sacred hands. Thus, the Lord lifted Visarasarmanar, the young boy who lay at His sacred feet. He embraced Visarasarmanar, bestowed grace, and caressed him. These were actions performed by Lord Shiva with His sacred hands. Lifting Visarasarmanar, who lay fallen, signifies the removal of his bonds (Pasa Neekkam). Embracing him signifies granting him Shiva-peru (the state of Shiva-hood). "Having removed the defilement of my mind and made me Shiva, He ruled me," says the Achcho Pathikam in Thiruvachakam. By embracing, he is made Shiva. By caressing, he is immersed in Shiva and appears in the light of Shiva. Vidwan Thiru Sa. Periyasamy Pillai writes, following the commentary on the 11th Sutra of Sivagnana Mapadiyam, that embracing signifies the manifestation of grace, and caressing signifies the manifestation of bliss. Therefore, embracing with hands is the manifestation of grace. Caressing with hands is the manifestation of bliss. Here, Lord Shiva looks at Visarasarmanar, who lay fallen, bestows grace, embraces him, and caresses him. These signify the Dikshas of Nayana Diksha (initiation through sight), Vakku Diksha (initiation through word), and Sparisa Diksha (initiation through touch). Sri Na. Sivaprakasa Desikar writes that since these Dikshas are indicated, it can be understood that other Dikshas also occurred by implication. Then Lord Shiva says, "For My sake, you struck down your father, causing him to fall." Lord Shiva says, "For Our sake." What does that mean? It means that he did so for the sake of the sacred service, the puja, to Lord Shiva, to be completed without hindrance or interruption. Thus, Lord Shiva addressed Visarasarmanar, saying, "For My sake, for the sake of the Shiva Puja you perform for Me to be completed without hindrance, you struck down your father, who begot you, raised you, and performed your Brahmopadesham (sacred thread ceremony), with a sword. Therefore, henceforth, We shall be your next father." The father who begot Visarasarmanar was struck down and perished. Therefore, he becomes an unworthy father to you. Therefore, henceforth, We Ourselves shall be your next father, says Lord Shiva. The meaning of this is that just as Lord Shiva is the father of all living beings in a general sense, so too was He the father of Visarasarmanar. Now, without that general sense, He says that He will be very close to him in a special sense, as a father. Therefore, He lifts Visarasarmanar, embraces him, bestows grace, caresses him, and smells his head with delight. These are all actions a father performs out of love for his son. Lord Shiva, by saying, "I am your next father," bestows grace in a fatherly manner. Therefore, He performs these actions just like a father. Visarasarmanar's body, which was made of Maya (illusion), when touched by Lord Shiva's sacred hands, became a Shiva-body. His senses all became Shiva-senses. The meaning of this is that his individual senses all became divine senses. By the touch of Lord Shiva's sacred hands, the body made of Maya was transformed into a Shiva-body of immeasurable exaltation. Just as the philosopher's stone (Parisavedhi) transforms whatever it touches into gold, Visarasarmanar's body became Shiva-maya (permeated with Shiva). Shiva-maya here means Shiva-svarupam (the very form of Shiva). Thus, immersed in the abundance of Lord Shiva's divine grace, Visarasarmanar lost his self and was filled with and revelled in divine grace. All the gods, including Brahma, who is seated on the lotus flower, saw this and praised him. In accordance with the saying, "How the earth is saved by Shiva," Brahma and other gods come to the earth and perform Shiva Puja. To receive the benefit of that Shiva Puja, they praise Chandisa. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman has composed it in this way. Thus, Visarasarmanar shone in the Shiva light that enveloped him. He shone in the Shiva Prakasam (divine effulgence). This Shiva Prakasam is seen with the eye of wisdom. Lord Shiva, having thus bestowed grace, made Visarasarmanar the chief of all Shiva devotees and said, "We have given you the position of Chandisa, so that all the food We eat, the clothes We wear, the garlands We adorn, and the ornaments, all shall belong to you." And Lord Shiva took the Kondrai flower garland from His matted locks and adorned Visarasarmanar with it. We will continue to contemplate this in detail. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 16 — Chandesura Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam Part 260. Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam. Chandesura Nayanar Puranam. We meditated on the words of Lord Shiva, who, after lifting Visarasarmanar, embraced him, caressed his head, and declared, "From now on, I shall be your father." Furthermore, Lord Shiva appoints Visarasarmanar as the chief of all devotees. He makes him the leader of all devotees. Sekkizhar Peruman composes a sacred verse stating that the Lord of the celestials made him the chief of the devotees and said, "We have given you the position of Chandikeswara, so all the vessels we have eaten from, the clothes we wear, and the garlands we adorn are for you." He then took the garland of konrai flowers from his own crescent-moon-adorned matted locks and placed it on Visarasarmanar's golden head. Thus, Lord Shiva makes Visarasarmanar the chief of all devotees. Having done so, Lord Shiva tells Visarasarmanar, "All the vessels I have eaten from, the clothes I wear, the garlands, ornaments, and everything else belong to you, and I have bestowed upon you the position of Chandikeswara." Not only that, Lord Shiva takes the konrai garland from his own sacred head and places it on the head of Visarasarmanar, who is Chandikeswara Nayanar. The grace bestowed by Lord Shiva upon Visarasarmanar is the position of Chandikeswara. Chandikeswara is a position or status. For Lord Shiva, Goddess Ambika, Lord Ganesha, Lord Muruga, and the Sun God, there is a Chandikeswara position associated with each deity, close to them. Those who hold these positions are the ones who bestow the benefits of worship upon those who worship these respective deities. In this kalpa, the Nayanar known as Visarasarmanar holds the Chandikeswara position of Lord Shiva. Therefore, since Visarasarmanar was appointed by Lord Shiva to the position of Chandikeswara, he is called Chandesura Nayanar after that position. Those who hold the Chanda position of Lord Shiva are called Dhvani Chandas. Similarly, the Chanda of the Sun God is called Tejas Chanda. Likewise, for Ambika, there is Neeli Chandan; for Ganesha, Ambo Chandan; and for Lord Muruga, Matru Chandan, all holding the Chandikeswara position. Lord Shiva bestows such a Chandikeswara position upon Visarasarmanar. Therefore, Visarasarmanar is called Chandesura Nayanar. Not only that, Lord Shiva ensures that all the vessels he has eaten from, the clothes he wears, the garlands, ornaments, and everything else belong exclusively to Chandikeswara. The Shiv Nirmalyas, such as Lord Shiva's sacred food, clothes, and garlands, belong to Chandikeswara. The shrine of Chandeswara will be built next to the ablution tank in a Shiva temple. He is one who constantly meditates on Lord Shiva without interruption. Therefore, one of the rules of Shiva temple darshan is not to cross between Chandeswara's shrine and the Shiva ablution tank. Shiva darshan is completed with the prayer of Chandikeswara darshan. The rule is to reach Chandikeswara's shrine, worship Chandikeswara, gently clap one's hands, offer the Shiv Nirmalya prasadam received, pray for the benefit of Shiva darshan, and then apply the sacred ash received from him. It is a rule that those who do not have Shiva initiation should not consume Shiv Nirmalyas. It is also stipulated in various Shiva Agamas that no one should consume Chandikeswara Nirmalya. Sivakavimani Ayya writes that it is the duty of good Saivite people to learn and follow these rules from good teachers. Lord Shiva tells Chandikeswara Nayanar, "All the vessels I have eaten from, the clothes I wear, and the garlands I adorn are for you." Therefore, the food offered to Lord Shiva, which is the remaining food, is called "parikala shesham" in Sanskrit. This means the food left over after Lord Shiva has eaten.

Therefore, this means that the sacred food offered to Lord Shiva is suitable for Chandikeswara to consume. It also means that the clothes worn by Lord Shiva should be worn by Chandikeswara Nayanar. By "garlands and ornaments," it is implied that Chandikeswara Nayanar should be adorned with the very flowers and garlands that were offered to Lord Shiva. In Shiva temples, there is a bad practice seen nowadays where devotees, after worshiping, take threads from their clothes and leave them at the shrine of Chandeswara Murthy. This is a great Shiva offense. The clothes that were offered as Nirmalya to Lord Shiva should be offered to Chandikeswara. It is highly improper and sinful for people to leave threads from their own worn and impure clothes. The shrine of Chandeswara is found in all Shiva temples. He is one of the Pancha Murthies (five deities). In the five separate temples of Shiva, Muruga, Ganesha, Surya, and Ambika, Chandeswara manifests with different names and forms. In Shiva temples, Chandeswara is known as Dhvani Chanda or Veera Chanda. In Muruga temples, he is Sumitra Chanda. In Ganesha temples, he is Kumbha Chanda. In Surya temples, he is Tejas Chanda. In Ambika temples, he is Chandikeswari or Yamuni Chandi or Dharmaneethi Neeli. In all Shiva temples, there is a small, separate shrine for Lord Chandeswara. This shrine is located outside the sanctum sanctorum, near the gomukhi (spout) from which the abhisheka water flows. After performing Shiva darshan and worship in the temple, we should conclude our prayers with Chandeswara darshan. The Agamas state that if Chandeswara darshan is not completed, Shiva darshan is not complete, and its benefits are not attained. As we have already seen, since Chandeswara Nayanar is always in Shiva meditation, one should not cross between him and the ablution tank. Since he is always receiving Shiva Nirmalya, one should also not circumambulate the Chandeswara shrine. In Shiva temples, after completing all the necessary pujas for the main deity, the Linga Murthy, one should go to the Chandeswara temple, bring the Nirmalyas of Lord Shiva (such as leaves, flowers, garlands, clothes, and offerings), establish them at the Chandeswara shrine, perform abhishekam to the image of Chandeswara with pure water, worship him, and meditate on him. This is stated in the Agamas. This is the Chandeswara Puja performed by the Sivacharyas as part of the Parartha Puja in Shiva temples. During the Mahotsava (grand festival) periods, the Chandeswara Utsavam (festival) is held at the end. The next day, the Acharya Utsavam is held. At that time, Chandeswara should be adorned with clothes, ornaments, garlands, and other Shiva Nirmalyas according to the rules, worshipped, and then taken out in a palanquin, shield, or on a bull, with umbrellas, chamaras (whisks), and other decorations, accompanied by all musical instruments, in a circumambulation of the streets. After circumambulating, he should be re-established in his place, and prayers should be offered for the benefits of the Shiva Utsavam. Only those who perform this Utsavam with firm resolve will attain the benefits of the Shiva Utsavam. In Shiva temples, the Pancha Murthies (five deities) revered are Ganesha, Muruga, Shiva, Ambika, and Chandeswara. Their main shrines and processional images are properly established and can still be seen in temples today. It is our tradition to keep the accounts of Shiva temples in the name of Chandeswara. Devotees who worship Shiva, after worshiping Shiva, finally receive the benefits of Shiva temple worship through Chandeswara Murthy at his shrine. Chandeswara Nayanar built a Shiva Lingam and a Shiva temple in the sand at Thiruvayppadi. In that temple, Lord Chandeswara is enshrined in a separate temple on a raised platform within the sanctum sanctorum of the main deity, facing south. It is believed that the entire administration of the Shiva temple is carried out according to his command. We have now meditated on the sacred history of Visarasarmanar, to whom Lord Shiva bestowed the position of Chandikeswara.

Similarly, Agamic texts state that there have been many who attained the Chandikeswara position in various yugas preceding the Kali Yuga. Chandeswara is known by many names: Chanda, Veera Chanda, Chandeswara, Chandikeswara, Chandeswara Nayanar, Sengalur Pillaiyar, Chandanatha, Thondar Nayagar, Sivaganathalaivar, Adi Chanda, Sadaryuga Chandeswaras, Chandisan, Dandeswara, Prachanda, Vikranta Chanda, Vibhuchanda, and Brahma Chandi. In the Krita Yuga, Chandeswara Nayanar, bearing the name Prachanda, will appear with a white body, 16 arms, riding an elephant, holding a trident, axe, vajra, kadaka, kosha, surikai, danda, and abhaya in his right hands, and a shield, pindipalam, pasam, mani, vyaghra mudra, suchi mudra, naga, and varada mudra in his left hands. In the Treta Yuga, he will have eight arms, a blood-red complexion, riding a horse, holding a trident, pasam, sword, and axe in his right hands, and varada, shield, palam, and mani in his left hands. In the Dvapara Yuga, he will have four arms, a golden complexion, matted locks, protruding fangs, holding an axe and trident in his right hands, and a pasam and ankusam in his left hands, seated on a bhadra peetha. In the Kali Yuga, he will have matted locks, two arms, holding a danda or axe (parasu) in his right hand, and his left hand resting on his thigh or in simha karna hastam, with a serene face, a dark or reddish complexion, seated on a bhadra peetha. Chandeswara's consort is called Chanda Shakti. Chanda Shakti is serene, green-hued, adorned with all ornaments, and known as Dharmaneethi. She has two arms, holding a blue lotus in one hand and making the varada mudra with the other. The Deepta Agama describes the types of Chandeswara: Chanda, Apachanda, Vikranta, and Chandesa, stating there are four types. They are described as possessing great strength, great knowledge, being Rudras, and having a radiance equal to Rudra. They have successively held the Chandikeswara position in each yuga. Therefore, the Agamas state that Chanda Puja is essential to attain the wealth of Shiva Preeti (Shiva's love). In the Parartha Puja, which is the temple worship, Chandeswara Puja should be performed according to the Agamic rules of that temple. In Atmaratha Puja (self-worship), Chandeswara Puja and other rituals should be performed according to the instructions of one's Gurus and Acharyas; this is considered excellent and traditional. The Chandesa Anugraha Murthy in Gangaikonda Cholapuram is very special and famous. Chandesa Anugraha Murthy is one of the Ashtashta Murthams. When Visarasarmanar, who became Chandeswara, cut off his father's legs and then continued his Shiva meditation and Shiva worship, Lord Shiva and Goddess Uma appeared as Uma Sahita Murthy. Lord Shiva, with his sacred hands, took the konrai garland that adorned his matted locks and placed it on Chandeswara's sacred head. This scene is depicted in the Chandesa Anugraha Murthy sculpture. A small sculpture of Chandesa Anugraha Murthy is found at the base of the gopuram of the Sthanunatha Swami Temple in Suchindram. In the Meenakshi Sundareswarar Temple in Madurai, in the Kambathadi Mandapam (Nandi Mandapam), there are twenty-five forms of Lord Shiva. Among them, the Chandesa Anugraha Murthy sculpture is very beautiful and artistically excellent. Here, Chandeswara is depicted in a standing posture. Similarly, Chandesa Murthies are found in many temples in various parts of Tamil Nadu, each with its unique significance. Thus, Lord Shiva bestows upon Visarasarmanar the highly exalted position of Chandikeswara. Therefore, Visarasarmanar is revered as Chandeswara Nayanar. While all beings in all worlds rejoiced with cries of "Hara Hara," and a shower of flowers rained everywhere, and thousands of Shiva Ganas sang and danced in delight, and the Vedas offered praises filled with words, and various musical instruments resounded on all four sides, our Lord Nayanar, bowing to Lord Shiva, joined his Chandikeswara position as the leader of Shiva devotees, so that the good path of Saivism might flourish. Sekkizhar Peruman sings, "While all the world rejoiced, and showers of flowers rained everywhere, and thousands of Ganas sang and danced in delight, and the eloquent Vedas offered praises, and various instruments resounded, our Lord, bowing, joined his position, so that the good path of Saivism might flourish." The benefit of the Nayanar attaining the position of leader of devotees is that the world should understand and benefit from the greatness of Shiva Puja. Now, Echadattan, the aged Brahmin, who committed a Shiva offense known to the world, was freed from that sin by the grace of Lord Shiva, by being struck by the beautiful axe held in the sacred hand of Sengalur Pillaiyar, who embodies the good conduct of the four Vedas. He attained the blessing of joining the Shiva Loka of the Primeval Lord with his relatives. Echadattan committed a Shiva offense, an offense against Shiva devotees and Shiva. There is no atonement for such an offense. Yet, that sin left him because his legs were severed by the axe in the sacred hand of Visarasarmanar, who is Sengalur Pillaiyar, Chandikeswara Nayanar. By attaining that blessing, he reached Shiva Loka. In this verse, Sekkizhar Peruman refers to Visarasarmanar, Chandeswara Nayanar, as Sengalur Pillaiyar. This means the son of Shiva who was born in Sengalur. Generally, we refer to Lord Ganesha as the elder Pillaiyar, Lord Muruga as the younger Pillaiyar, and Thirugnanasambandar as Aludaiya Pillaiyar, because they are the children of Lord Shiva. Here, since Lord Shiva himself said that he became the father of Visarasarmanar, Sekkizhar Peruman refers to him as Sengalur Pillaiyar. We will continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 17 — Chandesura Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam Part 261. Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam. Chandesura Nayanar Puranam. We have extensively meditated on the Puranam of Chandesura Nayanar. Sri Arumuga Navalar Peruman of Sri Lanka, in his commentary on the Puranam of Chandesura Nayanar, with the discourse of Sivakavimani Ayya, provides the following insight: Due to the primordial impurity (Anava Mala) in souls, four types of births occur according to karma. These are Andajam, Svedajam, Udbhijam, and Jarayujam. Andajam refers to creatures born from eggs. Svedajam refers to those born from sweat and warmth. Udbhijam refers to those that sprout by breaking open seeds. Jarayujam refers to those born from the womb. If these are expanded, they amount to 8.4 million types of births (Yoni Bhedas). Among these myriad births, it is extremely rare and a very difficult feat to transcend all other births through enjoyment or atonement and attain human birth. Even after attaining such a rare human birth, it is a great merit to be born in lands where scriptures flourish, rather than in mountains and forests where the fragrance of scriptures does not reach, and where one might be born as a hunter or tribal. Moreover, it is a great merit to be born in the Aryan lands, that is, Bharatadesa, where the Vedas and Agamas are prevalent, leaving aside the Mleccha (barbarian) lands where they are not. Furthermore, it is a great merit to be born among the four varnas, such as Brahmins, rather than in other divisions. Beyond all this, it is an immeasurable and immense merit to embrace the Saiva religion, which clearly recognizes Lord Shiva as the Supreme Being, without straying into other religions. There is scriptural evidence for this in Sivagnana Siddhiyar: "It is extremely rare to attain the grace to leave the delusion of life, escape the meanness of poverty, and with humility, embrace the Saiva religion. Those who worship with the highest Shiva-knowledge, like a full moon, have received grace." This is from Sivagnana Siddhiyar. This precious human birth was created by Pashupati, Lord Shiva, solely for us to meditate on Him in our minds, praise Him with our words, and worship Him with our hands. We should circumambulate Shiva temples with our feet. We should bow to Lord Shiva with our heads. We should listen to the glories of Lord Shiva with our ears. We should behold the sacred form of Lord Shiva with our eyes. It is for these purposes that Lord Shiva created this human birth. Even Brahma, Vishnu, Indra, and other celestials from the upper worlds come to this earth to worship Shiva. Therefore, this human body is extremely rare to obtain. If one does not perform Shiva Puja and attain liberation while possessing this body, then liberation will be very difficult to attain later. What is the ignorance of those souls who, even after obtaining such a good body, waste their time wandering without investigating or reflecting on any of these matters? "Only those eyes that behold the temple of the three-eyed Lord are true eyes. Only those feet that enter the temple of the blue-throated Lord are true feet. Only those heads that bow to the Lord who shares half his body with the Goddess are true heads. Only those hands that worship the Lord with matted locks are true hands. Only that tongue that sings the praises of the Lord is a true tongue. Only those ears that listen to the stories of the Supreme Lord are true ears. Only that heart that meditates on the golden anklets of the Lord is a true heart. Only that servitude that seeks refuge at His feet is true servitude." This is from the Brahmottara Khanda. Therefore, Shiva Puja, which involves serving Lord Shiva in this manner, is the natural and supreme act to be performed by a human being. Shiva Puja involves using implements such as flowers and sacred water, performing the five purifications (Atma Shuddhi, Sthana Shuddhi, Dravya Shuddhi, Mantra Shuddhi, Linga Shuddhi), and then, on the pedestal of the Shiva Lingam, worshipping the Shiva Asana, which is a lotus from Shakti to Adi Shakti. Above that, the Murthy, which is the Vidya Deha (body of knowledge), is invoked in the Lingam. The Nishkala Rupam (formless aspect), which is the life of that Vidya Deha, the all-pervading, all-doing, blissful, and knowledgeable Paramasiva, is meditated upon above the Dvadashanta. He is then invoked into the Vidya Deha that was previously placed. One then prays, "O Lord, You are the Lord of all worlds. Please remain present in this Lingam with great pleasure until the end of this Puja." After worshipping, praising, circumambulating, and prostrating, the Puja is concluded. This is Shiva Puja. The word "Lingam" means "that which causes to manifest." Therefore, the glory of Parameswara, who manifests the world through creation, preservation, and so on, is called Lingam. The manifest forms of that Lingam, such as those made of stone, crystal, sand, or metal, are also called Lingam by courtesy. Vidya Deha refers to the power that presides over the five cosmic functions. Since Shiva is free from primordial impurities, He does not have a physical body like other beings but possesses a body of power. This body is conceived from the five mantras, such as Ishana, which are useful for the five cosmic functions, from head to toe. It is said that Shiva, being a formless entity, took on this conceptual body, which is a manifestation of His power, for the sake of His devotees' grace, as it is difficult for souls to meditate on and worship Him in His formless state. If worshipped with such love, the compassionate Shiva will accept the worship in the Lingam and bestow His grace upon those who perform it. This worship is of two types: sthavara (immovable) and jangama (movable). In this, Lord Shiva, being the Supreme Being, accepts worship and bestows grace upon all living beings. Therefore, Sivagnana Siddhiyar states, "Perform such Shiva Puja with abundant love." The underlying meaning is that worship performed without love will not yield results. One should understand the words of Pattinatthu Pillaiyar: "If my hand says one thing, my mind thinks another, my tongue speaks deceitful lies, my body seeks carnal pleasures, and my ears desire to hear other things, how will you accept the puja I perform, O Remover of karma?" Visarasarmanar, who became Chandesura Nayanar, in his previous birth, studied and understood the Vedas and Agamas and performed Shiva Puja without fail. That is why, in this birth, at a very young age, he easily understood the essence of all scriptures and realized that Shiva is our master. Through true realization, his extraordinary and ever-increasing devotion to Lord Shiva became his very form. The Vedas and Agamas were easily understood by him due to the continuity of knowledge from his previous birth. The conclusion of the gnana shastras is that the soul is the possession of Lord Shiva. Chandesura Nayanar, in his previous birth, performed Puja without fail, and due to that continuity, in this birth, at a very young age, as soon as he thought that cow's milk was worthy of being the sacred ablution for Lord Shiva, he, with great desire for Shiva Puja, performed it. That Puja seemed like a play to others. But because it was performed with constant true devotion, it became very pleasing to Lord Shiva, who is the life of all lives. This Nayanar, whenever he thought of, heard, or saw the truth of Shiva, his mind melted, and he stood in devotion, without expecting any benefit, finding joy in that love itself, forgetting himself. When he was performing Shiva Puja, his father, Echadattan, beat him many times on his back and spoke harsh words. But he was unaware of all that. However, when he saw Echadattan kick the pot of milk and spill it, he realized that his father had committed an offense against his Supreme Father, Lord Shiva, and he cut off his father's legs. Then, he continued his Shiva Puja as before. Because of this devotion, the compassionate Lord Shiva appeared on His bull, and with His sacred hands, lifted him and said, "You cut off your own father for My sake. From now on, I am your father." He embraced him, caressed his body, and smelled his head, bestowing upon him His own Sarupyam (similarity of form) in that very body, and placed him in the Chandesa position, making him the chief of all devotees. Chandesura Nayanar cut off the legs of his father, Echadattan. Echadattan was first a Brahmin. He was Visarasarmanar's father and also his Guru who initiated him into Brahmopadesam. Chandeswara Nayanar cut off such an Echadattan with an axe.

Would the sins of Brahmicide (for killing a Brahmin), patricide (for killing his father), and Gurucide (for killing his Guru who initiated him into Brahmopadesam) apply to Visarasarmanar for cutting Echadattan? No, they would not. This is because there is no greater merit than Shiva Puja, and no greater sin than an offense against Shiva. Echadattan obstructed Visarasarmanar's Shiva Puja, which was an offense against Shiva. Visarasarmanar cut Echadattan out of the abundance of his love for Lord Shiva, who is his Supreme Father and Supreme Guru. Furthermore, Visarasarmanar was at that time in a state of self-forgetfulness, completely absorbed in Shiva. Even if an act committed by one who is absorbed in Shiva is considered a sin, Shiva will consider it as His own work, and therefore, that sin would not apply here. "The sin committed by a devotee of Hara becomes righteousness. The merit of one who has no love for the feet of the Supreme Lord becomes a sin. The great sacrifice performed by the arrogant Daksha became evil, while the sin committed by the child among men became good." This is the 119th verse of Sivagnana Siddhiyar. The merit or sin of an act depends on the strength or weakness of the one who accepts it, not on the strength or weakness of the doer or the act itself. Therefore, a sinful act committed by one who loves the feet of the Primeval Lord will result in merit. Even meritorious acts performed by one who has no love for the feet of Lord Shiva will result in sin. Daksha performed a great sacrifice, which was a meritorious act. However, because it was performed without love for the feet of Lord Shiva, that sacrifice ended in evil. Even though some might say that Chandikeswara Nayanar's act was a sinful act of murder, it was performed out of love for the feet of Lord Shiva, having lost his individual consciousness and become one with Shiva, completely absorbed in Shiva. Therefore, it resulted in merit. Thus, it is understood that one should worship the Supreme Lord Shiva with love. Furthermore, Echadattan was punished immediately by Visarasarmanar, who became Chandikeswara Nayanar. Therefore, Echadattan, along with his relatives, attained Shiva Loka without suffering in hell due to that offense against Shiva. Thus, the greatness of Chandeswara Nayanar is praised in the first Thirumurai (sacred collection) in the Thiruchengalur Thirupathigam, Thirukollili Thirupathigam, and Thiruooral Pathi Thirupathigam by Thirugnanasambandar Peruman. In the second Thirumurai, in the Thirupullirukkuvelur Thirupathigam, and in the third Thirumurai, in Thirupasuram, Thiruvettakudi Thirupathigam, and Thiruvalavai Thiru Yamakam, Thirugnanasambandar Peruman has praised Visarasarmanar, who is Chandeswara Nayanar. In the fourth Thirumurai, Thirunavukkarasu Swamigal has praised him in the Thiruvappadi Thirupathigam, Thirukkurukkai Veerattam Thirupathigam, Thiruchaikkadu Thirupathigam, and Thirucherai Thirupathigam. Furthermore, Thirunavukkarasu Peruman has praised Chandeswara Nayanar in the fifth Thirumurai in the Koyil Thirupathigam, Thirukondisaram Thirupathigam, and Thirumangalakudi Thirupathigam, and in the sixth Thirumurai in the Thirupuvanam Thirupathigam. Chapter 18 — Chandesura Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam Part 262. Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam. Chandesura Nayanar Puranam. As we have seen so far, Sekkizhar Peruman, who extensively sang the Puranam of Chandesura Nayanar, further states the following: Echadattan, the father of Chandesura Nayanar, committed an improper act. Visarasarmanar, the young Brahmin boy who became Chandesura Nayanar, cut off his legs with an axe. That young Brahmin boy, Visarasarmanar, became the beloved son of Lord Shiva. He attained the position of Chandesa. He became the leader of Shiva devotees. Who are those who understood this state? Furthermore, Sekkizhar Peruman sings this as the 59th verse: "Are not the actions performed by the devotees who offered love to Lord Shiva themselves penance?" This is from Thiruvachagam, Thiruthol Nokkam, sixth verse: "The Father whose mind is Shiva, whatever He does becomes penance." Even those actions that are not prescribed as penance in the world, or even those that go against rules, become penance if they are performed by those who have love for the feet of Lord Shiva. Therefore, truly speaking, whatever Shiva devotees do is penance. Even if it is not prescribed as penance in the scriptures, if a devotee performs it with intense devotion to Lord Shiva, it will be considered penance. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman concludes the Puranam of Chandesura Nayanar. We are now meditating on the Puranam of Chandesura Nayanar in the Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam of the Thiruthondar Puranam, known as Periya Puranam, which has now concluded. In the Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam, we have meditated on the Puranams of Murthy Nayanar, Muruga Nayanar, Rudra Pasupathi Nayanar, Thirunalaipovar Nayanar, Thirukuripputhonda Nayanar, and Chandesura Nayanar. We know that this fourth Sarukkam corresponds to the third verse of Thiruthondar Thogai composed by Sundaramurthy Swamigal. "To Murthy who ruled the world with triple purity, I am a servant; to Muruga, I am a servant; to Rudra Pasupathi, I am a servant; to Thirunalaipovar, I am a servant; to the devotees of Thirukuripputhondar, I am a servant; to the motherly Chandikeswara Peruman who, in truth, worshipped the sacred form and, when his father angrily rose, cut off his father's legs with an axe, I am a servant; I am a slave to the Lord of Arur, Aruran." This is the third verse of Thiruthondar Thogai. In this, "To Murthy who ruled the world with triple purity, I am a servant" refers to the Puranam of Murthy Nayanar. "To Muruga, I am a servant" refers to the Puranam of Muruga Nayanar. "To Rudra Pasupathi, I am a servant" refers to the Puranam of Rudra Pasupathi Nayanar. "To Thirunalaipovar, I am a servant" refers to the Puranam of Thirunalaipovar Nayanar. "To the devotees of Thirukuripputhondar, I am a servant" refers to the Puranam of Thirukuripputhondar Nayanar. "To the motherly Chandikeswara Peruman who, in truth, worshipped the sacred form and, when his father angrily rose, cut off his father's legs with an axe, I am a servant" refers to the Puranam of Chandesura Nayanar. Now comes the concluding line, "I am a slave to the Lord of Arur, Aruran." The structure of Thiruthondar Puranam as arranged by Sekkizhar is well known to us. Sekkizhar Peruman composed each Sarukkam for each verse of Thiruthondar Thogai, incorporating the Puranams of the devotees mentioned in that verse. Then, at the end of each Sarukkam, Sekkizhar Peruman included a praise of Sundaramurthy Swamigal, in accordance with the concluding line "Aruran, I am a slave to the Lord of Arur." This structure is well known to us. Accordingly, Sekkizhar Peruman has composed a praise of Sundaramurthy Swamigal in accordance with the line "Aruran, I am a slave to the Lord of Arur" in this verse. Firstly, the compendium states, "Aruran, I am a slave to the Lord of Arur." This, in the sequence of the Thiruthondar Thogai, which begins with "Mummaiyaal Ulaganda," comes after the narrative of "I am a servant to the motherly Chandikeswara Peruman." Its meaning is, "I, Sundaramurthy, am a slave to the Lord of Arur, that is, the Lord of Thiruvarur." To explain further, the six devotees praised in this verse, which begins with "Mummaiyaal Ulaganda," are six individuals. Sundaramurthy Swamigal concludes by saying, "I, Aruran, who am a separate servant to each of these six, am also a slave to the Lord of Thiruvarur." That is, Sundaramurthy Swamigal concludes by saying, "I, who am a servant to Murthy Nayanar, I who am a servant to Muruga Nayanar, I who am a servant to Rudra Pasupathi Nayanar, I who am a servant to Thirunalaipovar Nayanar, I who am a servant to Thirukuripputhondar Nayanar, I who am a servant to Chandesura Nayanar, I, Aruran, am also a slave to the Lord of Thiruvarur." Alternatively, it can be concluded that Aruran, who is a slave to Lord Shiva in Arur, is a separate servant to each of these six devotees mentioned earlier. Now, in accordance with this phrase "Aruran, I am a slave to the Lord of Arur," Nambiyandar Nambigal has composed a verse in the Thiruthondar Thiruvandathi, which is a genre of literature. "He is the one who received grace from those who knew the meaning of the words of grace, 'Do not forget the mind that is like the southern well of Thuruthi, and remove the blemish that was placed as a sign for me.' He is the good one known as the Lord of the fertile fields, surrounded by abundant water." This is from Thiruthondar Thiruvandathi. Now, Sekkizhar Peruman, following the compendium's line "Aruran, I am a slave to the Lord of Arur," has composed a praise of Sundaramurthy Nayanar in the concluding section of this Sarukkam. The praise of Sundaramurthy Nayanar is as follows: "We worship this birth that came to worship the fragrant, flower-like soft feet of the sacred one who, with a heart full of love, for the greatness of the devotees of the Lord with the dark, beautiful throat, took up the task for all beings to worship and for the whole land to prosper, and presented the Thiruthondar Thogai." This is the concluding verse of this Sarukkam, composed by Sekkizhar Peruman as a praise of Sundaramurthy Nayanar. This is the 1265th verse. Its meaning is as follows: "We worship this birth that has been blessed to worship the fragrant, flower-like soft feet of Aludaiya Nambigal, who is Sundaramurthy Swamigal, the sacred one who, with a heart full of love, for the greatness of the devotees of Lord Shiva, the Lord with the beautiful throat containing poison, took up the task for all beings to know, worship, and praise, and for the whole land to prosper, and presented the Thiruthondar Thogai." This is the meaning of that verse. Sundaramurthy Swamigal sings the greatness of the Lord and the greatness of the devotees in the Thiruthondar Thogai, which begins with "I am a servant to the Brahmins of Thillai." Lord Shiva is also one among the Brahmins of Thillai. Therefore, Sundaramurthy Swamigal has sung both the greatness of the Lord and the greatness of the devotees in the Thiruthondar Thogai. We have also learned that Sundaramurthy Swamigal incarnated here in the South from Mount Kailash solely to compose the Thiruthondar Thogai. Thus, Sundaramurthy Swamigal composed the Thiruthondar Thogai, in which he sang the glories of individual devotees and groups of devotees. As a result, the sacred text of Thiruthondar Puranam, known as Periya Puranam, emerged. Through this, all beings receive the divine grace of meditating on the greatness and sacred history of the 63 Nayanmars and nine groups of devotees by reading this Periya Puranam, and thereby attain salvation. This is an expression of the compassion of God and Sundaramurthy Swamigal towards all living beings. "He who performed great penance to make the South prosper, the one who came to give the flawless Thiruthondar Thogai," as it is said, we have seen that Nambigal incarnated by divine grace to bestow the Thiruthondar Thogai for the world to prosper by knowing and worshipping the greatness of the devotees. This birth has been blessed to worship the flower-like soft lotus feet of such a sacred Sundaramurthy Swamigal. Generally, others would say that this birth is evil. But to worship the sacred lotus feet of Sundaramurthy Swamigal,

we have been blessed with the fortune of worshipping. Therefore, that birth becomes a worshipable birth, free from any evil to be avoided. "Human birth is indeed desirable in this great world," is the sacred saying of Thirunavukkarasu Swamigal. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman concludes the fourth Sarukkam of Thiruthondar Puranam, known as Periya Puranam, by praising Sundaramurthy Swamigal and saying, "We worship this birth that came to worship the sacred feet of Sundaramurthy Swamigal." We have so far meditated on 1265 verses in four Sarukkams. In these four Sarukkams, we have meditated on the histories of 19 Nayanmars out of the total 63 Nayanmars. We have meditated on the history of one group of devotees out of the nine groups of devotees. We have meditated on the Puranams of the Brahmins of Thillai, Thiruneelakandathu Kuyavanar, Iyarpagai Nayanar, Ilaiyankudi Mara Nayanar, Meypporul Nayanar, Viranminda Nayanar, Amarnithi Nayanar, Eripathar Nayanar, Enathinatha Nayanar, Kannappa Nayanar, Kalaya Nayanar, Manakkanjara Nayanar, Arivattaya Nayanar, Anaya Nayanar, Murthy Nayanar, Muruga Nayanar, Rudra Pasupathi Nayanar, Thirunalaipovar Nayanar, Thirukuripputhonda Nayanar, and Chandesura Nayanar. Without the grace of God and the Guru, such a great Shiva merit is very rare to obtain. Therefore, bowing to the sacred feet of God and the sacred feet of the Guru, and praying for the opportunity to meditate on the histories of the remaining Nayanmars, we will continue to meditate.
