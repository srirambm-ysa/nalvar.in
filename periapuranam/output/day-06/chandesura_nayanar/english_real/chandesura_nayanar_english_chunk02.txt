Chapter 11 — Chandesura Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 255. The section on "Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam" (The Chapter of Ruling the World with Threefold Virtues). The Puranam of Chandesura Nayanar. Sekkizhar Peruman describes the form that Chandesura Nayanar took to graze the cows. Visarasarmanar, also known as Chandesura Nayanar, appears with a staff and a rope in his hand for grazing the cows. It is essential for those who graze cows to have a rope and a staff in their hands. A rope is needed to tie cows and calves when necessary. A staff or stick helps to guide the cows. Previously, the cowherd who grazed the cows also carried a staff in his hand. Now, Visarasarmanar also takes on the form of a cowherd with a staff in his hand to graze the cows. The staff used for grazing cows should only be used to guide them, not to harm them as the earlier cowherd did. We can recall here that the very staff held by Visarasarmanar later became an axe, with which he severed his father's feet. Visarasarmanar, who set out to graze the cows, goes with the appearance that signifies his brahmacharya (celibate student) state: a topknot with a tuft of hair, a deer skin, a triple sacred thread, and a loincloth. Brahmacharis should have a topknot with a tuft of hair. This small tuft of hair is a distinguishing mark of brahmacharis. It is called Panchasikai. Next, Sekkizhar sings, "deer skin and triple sacred thread adorning his small chest." Tying a deer skin to the sacred thread and wearing it is a custom for brahmacharis. He wears a loincloth around his waist. Wearing a loincloth around the waist is also a brahmachari custom. That loincloth should be pure and white. Visarasarmanar wears such a loincloth. Thus, Visarasarmanar, with the staff and rope for grazing cows in his hand, with his small tuft of hair swaying, with the deer skin and sacred thread adorning his small chest, and with a shining loincloth around his waist, grazes the cows so that milk and calves may increase. The benefits derived from cows are milk and calves. Of these, milk is an immediate benefit, available daily. A calf is considered a long-term, great benefit. These are the two benefits of grazing cows. Visarasarmanar grazes the cows so that both of these may increase. What does it mean for milk and calves to increase? It means that both should increase equally, without one diminishing the other. In these times, many, out of greed for milk, milk the cows without leaving any for the calves, thus ruining the calves' growth. We also see the cruel sight today of people making a fake calf out of straw and showing it to the cow to milk it. Others might only want calves and waste the milk. However, here it is implied that one should graze cows in such a way that both milk and calves increase, not like these examples. Thus, he grazes the cows so that both milk and calves increase. He gives the cows as much grass as they need, allowing them to eat. During times of abundant grass, he lets the cows graze in those areas and eat the grass. He plucks grass with his own hands and gives it to the cows. When cows are able to graze, they should be allowed to eat the grass by grazing. When that is not possible, grass should be plucked and given to the cows to eat. There are times when cows cannot graze. If it rains, cows cannot go out to graze. Even if cows are not well, they cannot go out to graze.

They cannot. In places where cows graze, there might be obstacles like mounds, pits, mud, and other natural features, preventing them from grazing in many directions. Due to such difficulties, when cows are unable to go and graze, grass should be plucked and fed to them. The Nayanar feeds them in this manner. Furthermore, cows should be made to drink water in water bodies where there is good water. The places where cows drink water should not be muddy. The water bodies should not be deep where cows enter. There should be no reeds or other bushes. Cows should be made to drink water in such water bodies that are free from these obstacles and have good, clean water. Moreover, the water should be pure, free from decaying matter, silt, and the like, and cows should be made to drink in such pure water sources. Not only that, but the fears of cows should also be removed. The fear that cows have from tigers and other animals should be removed. Cows should not be carried away by enemies or others. Such fears should also be removed. Cows should be led along paths that are free from obstacles like pits, mounds, stones, thorns, and where there is no possibility of attack by dangerous animals. After cows have eaten, they should be allowed to rest so that the food they ate can be digested. For this, after the cows have grazed, they should be seated in the shade. Diseases affecting cows should be cured. Visarasarmanar also does this. He provides delicious food that helps in the physical growth of cows and nourishes them. In all these ways, Visarasarmanar cares for and grazes the cows, ensuring they suffer no harm. Not only that, but he also ensures that the sweet, nectar-like milk provided by the cows reaches the homes of their rightful owners. Sekkizhar Peruman here refers to cow's milk as "amuda madhurappaal" (nectar-like sweet milk). Nectar is a delicious food that removes disease and aids in bodily growth. Similarly, cow's milk removes disease, aids in our bodily growth, and is also a delicious food. It is sweet. Cow's milk is naturally sweet without adding sugar or other sweetening agents. Furthermore, this milk serves as an offering for Lord Shiva's yagna (sacrificial ritual). This milk given by the cow. Therefore, cow's milk bestows the imperishable, immortal state of Shiva, making people attain permanence. Even in this story, we can see that this milk itself brings nectar to Visarasarmanar. Thus, Visarasarmanar grazes and protects the cows on the banks of the Manniyaaru river, near the Mullai land (forest region) and the low-lying areas, and near the groves of the Marutham land (agricultural region) that yield cool pearls. In this verse, Sekkizhar Peruman describes all the places where Visarasarmanar grazes the cows. The Marutham land, which is the land area on the river bank, the groves on the river bank, the low-lying areas formed by silt accumulation on the river bank—such low-lying areas have abundant grass and plants. In the Marutham land, due to abundant water resources, there are many groves. Visarasarmanar takes the cows to all these types of places and grazes them. By grazing the cows in this manner, they thrive. Thus, after grazing the cows during the day, Visarasarmanar also collects samithu (sacred firewood) on his way back. Collecting samithu for the daily agnikarya (fire ritual) in the homes of Brahmins is one of the duties of brahmacharis. Therefore, he collects such samithu and returns home before evening. In this way, for many days, Visarasarmanar grazed the cows,

He used to graze them in such a way that they themselves yielded abundant milk, and he would also collect samithu, returning home before evening. Let us continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 12 — Chandesura Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 256. The section on "Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam" (The Chapter of Ruling the World with Threefold Virtues). The Puranam of Chandesura Nayanar. We have contemplated all the wonders of Visarasarmanar grazing the cows. Because Visarasarmanar grazed the cows, all those cows received good grass and good water, thriving without fear and feeling very happy. Therefore, the cows' bodies were well-nourished and beautiful. Since the cows were well-nourished, beautiful, and happy, their udders swelled, and their teats secreted milk. If cows are protected with love, they become happy. When cows are happy, they yield a lot of milk. It is also true that some cows, pleased by listening to songs, yield more milk. This is why Gopalan, who came as a cowherd, plays the flute as Venugopalan. We saw the custom of cowherds playing the flute in the story of Aanaya Nayanar. Thus, the idea is that if cows are happy, they will yield a lot of milk. The proverb says, "Milk a dancing cow by dancing, milk a singing cow by singing." Even if cows have abundant milk, they generally secrete milk only when calves suckle. The secretion of milk in cows occurs due to the joyful state of their love for their calves. This is why the vatsalya (affection) of cows for their calves is compared to God's grace for living beings. That is why God is called Bhaktavatsalan. Seeing Visarasarmanar, who grazes them well, and recognizing his mother-like nature, all the cows yield milk without holding back. Generally, cows secrete milk in the morning and evening. Here, because Visarasarmanar grazes the cows so well, and due to his mother-like nature, the cows, filled with great love, yield milk even at noon and at night. That is, morning and evening are the times for milking. Therefore, after morning and evening, at noon and at night, the cows' udders would normally be empty of milk. Even udders that have been milked now swell with milk again at noon and at night, secreting it. This is because of the way Visarasarmanar grazes them and because of their love for Visarasarmanar. All the Brahmins are very happy to see that the sacrificial cows, which aid in their yagnas, have grown well. Compared to before, these sacrificial cows are now thriving and yielding many times more milk. The Brahmins of Thiruchengalur rejoice, saying that because the brahmachari Visarasarmanar grazes the cows with such care and love, following the proper methods for protecting and grazing them, the cows are thriving and yielding milk in such abundance. Even the cows, though separated from their calves at home, seeing Visarasarmanar as their secret calf, and considering themselves to have become mothers to the calf that is Visarasarmanar, mooed and secreted milk from their udders without holding back. When cows go out to graze, it is customary to leave their calves at home and take only the cows for grazing. Even though the cows came alone to graze, leaving their calves at home, they were not sad. Instead, seeing Visarasarmanar as their own calf, they yielded milk spontaneously. Normally, cows separated from their calves would naturally feel sad. But here, not only were they not sad, but their love increased,

And they stood with swollen udders, yielding milk. This is because, contrary to nature, there was a beloved calf, Visarasarmanar, present there. Seeing Visarasarmanar, the cows considered him their calf, thought of themselves as his mother, and approached him, melting with affection, mooing, and secreting milk from their udders. These are all characteristics of mother cows. They not only desired to be mothers to their own calves but also eagerly accepted the state of being mothers to Visarasarmanar, the Vedic calf. Thus, in this state of motherhood, milk flowed from their udders. The story of Mangaiyarkkarasi Ammaiyar, whose breasts secreted milk upon hearing the state of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, is also worthy of remembrance here. "The queen of the southern Pandya land, with a humble heart, whose breasts secreted milk when she heard the word 'child' - such was her love," says the 54th verse of Thirukalittruppadiyar. The spontaneous yielding of milk upon remembering or seeing a calf is a sign of immense love and abundant milk. Such cows can still be seen in some places today. This very act of spontaneously yielding milk becomes the starting point for the Nayanar's story. In those days, the cows that came to him spontaneously yielded milk from their udders without holding back. Seeing this, Visarasarmanar was delighted. In the mind of Visarasarmanar, who stood firm in the righteous path of Shiva, the milk that the cows spontaneously yielded was an offering for Lord Shiva's sacred bath (Thirumanjanam), as realized by Visarasarmanar, our benevolent master. Thus, having thought and understood this, within that clarity arose in him the yearning to perform Shiva Puja, which is the true worship of Lord Shiva. The clear mind, which understood that Shiva Puja is the means to liberation, desired and yearned to perform Shiva Puja. The arising of this yearning in Visarasarmanar's mind to perform Shiva Puja happened by the grace of God, according to the inclinations of his previous birth. The thought that this milk, spontaneously yielded by the cows, would be an offering for Lord Shiva's sacred bath arose in Visarasarmanar's mind. However, a question first arises: can one use the milk of cows for Lord Shiva's sacred bath without the consent of the cow owners? Would it not be a transgression? But here, the cows themselves yield milk without the consent of their owners. Therefore, using this spontaneously yielded milk for Lord Shiva's sacred bath is appropriate. If this were not done, the spontaneously yielded milk, which would not be useful for yagnas, would be wasted, spilling onto the ground. The owners of the cows are Brahmins. The cows they raise provide milk for their yagnas. These Brahmins raise cows specifically to obtain the milk needed for their homas and yagnas. Now, the cows themselves are yielding milk. This spontaneously yielded milk is spilling onto the ground and being wasted. It is not being used for the Brahmins' yagnas. Therefore, he realizes that using this spontaneously yielded milk for Lord Shiva's sacred bath is perfectly right. For, among all yagnas, Shiva Puja, the yagna for Shiva, is the most excellent, is it not? Thus, having understood that this milk is for Lord Shiva's sacred bath, the yearning to perform Shiva Puja now arises in Visarasarmanar, as a continuation of the penance he performed in all his previous births. Generally, worldly people, upon seeing milk, would realize that it is useful for themselves, for their children, and as good food for others.

Those with evil minds, even when seeing milk that could be used for God's sacred bath, would argue that it is being wasted instead of being used for people, and thus incur hell. We see such people who think and argue in this way even today. But Visarasarmanar, due to the continuity of his previous births, due to the continuation of the Shiva Puja he performed in all his previous births, upon seeing the spontaneously yielded milk, had the thought arise in him, by the grace of God, that it should be used for Lord Shiva's sacred bath. This is the difference between him and other worldly people, and those who stand firm in the righteous path of Shiva. When Pattinattar was given a box with a needle without an eye, what did others think? They thought that even a useless needle without an eye should be kept and protected in a box. But what did the great Pattinattar think? He realized the truth that "even a needle without an eye will not accompany one to the final journey." Therefore, he renounced nine crore possessions in a single moment and became a lion among ascetics. We can recall that story here as well. This is the difference between those who stand firm in the righteous path of Shiva and those who stand in the worldly path. In Visarasarmanar, from the seed of the inclinations cultivated in previous births, arose the yearning to perform Shiva Puja. As this yearning arose, just as children playfully build houses in the sand, so too, due to the overflowing love for Shiva in Visarasarmanar's mind, he fashioned a Shiva Lingam from the sand of the Manniyaaru river, under an Aathi tree. Then, he also created a Shiva temple from the sand. Using that sand, he built a Shiva Lingam in the sand, and around it, a Shiva temple, and around that, a large circumambulatory path and a gopuram (temple tower). The continuation of Shiva worship performed in previous births came to Visarasarmanar in this birth in the form of a child's playful building of houses in the sand. Therefore, he thus fashioned a Shiva Lingam from sand and also built a temple and gopuram. Lord Shiva accepts and bestows grace upon the worship performed by souls in two places: within (aham) and without (puram). Lord Shiva, residing within souls, accepts the worship performed by them and bestows grace. Lord Shiva, standing outside, in the form of Shiva Lingams and other sacred images, and in the form of Gurus and Jangaamas (wandering ascetics), accepts the worship performed by souls and bestows grace. Therefore, the places where souls worship Lord Shiva externally are of two types: Sthavaram (immovable, like a lingam) and Jangamam (movable, like a Guru). The 118th verse of Sivagnana Siddhiyar states: "Standing in two forms, Sthavaram and Jangamam, the great Lord accepts worship and bestows grace upon the living beings of the earth. If you contemplate the supreme being in your heart, perform complete worship. Worship the supreme being daily with love." This is the verse. What is its meaning or explanation? The Lord, who is in the Sadasiva Tattva, stands outside in the form of the Shiva Lingam inside the temple, and in the form of the Guru and Shiva devotees, who are Jangamas (moving forms), and accepts the worship performed by living beings and bestows grace. These are the places where Lord Shiva stands and bestows grace externally. Internally, He bestows grace by residing within the soul. He accepts the worship performed by souls. Therefore, the meaning is to worship in those respective places. Lord Shiva is beyond speech and mind. The place where He takes form is the Shuddha Tattva. If the Lord, who is beyond speech and mind, takes form only in the Shuddha Tattva, how can those here see and worship Him? Therefore, the Lord, who is in the Sadasiva Tattva, has two ways of bestowing grace upon us: external (puram) and internal (aham). The external, that is, the sacred image inside the temple, is called Sthavaram. Besides that, those who wear sacred ashes and rudraksha are called Jangamas. Therefore, the places of worship outside are of two types: Sthavaram and Jangamam. The same Lord bestows grace by residing within the soul. Therefore, if one worships the Lord who stands in the external forms of Sthavaram and Jangamam, such as the Lingam, the Guru, and the devotees, and also within the soul, the Lord will accept such worship and bestow grace. Therefore, it means that one should worship the Lord with devotion in those respective places every day. It is stated here that there are two places where the Lord bestows grace externally. They are the Lingam and the sacred form of the devotees. Among these, Visarasarmanar worshipped the Lord in the Linga form, which is the sacred image inside the temple. In worshipping the Shiva Lingam externally, there is a rule that one can create a Shiva Lingam from river sand, rudraksha, sandalwood, and many other materials and perform puja. Arumuga Navalar Peruman, in his book "Saiva Vinavidai," in the section on "Shiva Linga Iyal," under the category of "Ishta Lingam," and specifically under "Kshanika Lingam," has stated that a Shiva Lingam can be created and worshipped using the following 12 materials: earth, rice, cooked food, river sand, cow dung, butter, rudraksha, sandalwood, kurcham (a bundle of sacred grass), flower garland, sugar, and flour. Therefore, here, Visarasarmanar worshipped the Shiva Lingam created from river sand. Other children would playfully build houses and other things from river sand. But Visarasarmanar, as a continuation of the Shiva Puja performed in his previous births, as a young boy, desired to perform puja to a Shiva Lingam made of river sand and accordingly fashioned the Shiva Lingam. Not only that, but he also built the circumambulatory path (prakaram), gopuram, and other parts of the temple around the Shiva Lingam from river sand and worshipped. Let us continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 13 — Chandesura Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 257. The section on "Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam" (The Chapter of Ruling the World with Threefold Virtues). The Puranam of Chandesura Nayanar. As the yearning to perform Shiva Puja arose, Visarasarmanar first fashioned a Shiva Lingam from the river sand on the banks of the Manniyaaru river, then the circumambulatory paths, and then the gopuram. The proper method is to first place the Shiva Lingam and, centering it, then build the Shiva temple, gopuram, circumambulatory paths, and other structures accordingly. We see this here. Sekkizhar Peruman also indicates here that it is not the rule to first build the temple, gopuram, etc., and then install the Shiva Lingam inside. Visarasarmanar first fashions the Shiva Lingam from river sand. After that, he builds the Shiva temple. After the Shiva Lingam is installed, a Shiva temple should be built for it. A Shiva temple is a temple dedicated to Lord Shiva. Around it, a circumambulatory path, called the Thiruchuttru, should also be built. The Shiva temple is the temple where Lord Shiva resides. The area around it, for the subsidiary deities (Parivara Moorthigal) such as Ganesha, Muruga, Nandi, Dakshinamurthy, Chandesa, Bhairava, Surya, Chandra, etc., is called the circumambulatory path. It is also the rule to place them in the first prakaram (courtyard) or the first circuit. The Shiva Lingam and Shiva temple built by Chandesura Nayanar are now located in the village of Thiruvaipaadi as a Shiva Lingam and a temple. We saw that Chandesura Nayanar built this under an Aathi tree. This Aathi tree is currently located in the southwest part of the Thiruvaipaadi temple. This is a type of Aathi tree called Kaattaathi (wild Aathi). Similarly, the Uttarapathiyar, who came to bestow grace upon Siruthonda Nayanar, is enshrined under an Aathi tree in the Thiruchengaattangudi temple. That tree is also available for darshan there now. Building the Shiva Lingam, Shiva temple, gopuram, and circumambulatory path for Shiva Puja was the first act performed by Visarasarmanar after the yearning for Shiva Puja arose. For Shiva Puja, the Shiva Lingam and temple are indispensable and are needed first. Therefore, he first built these two. Then, flowers are needed for Shiva Puja. So, Visarasarmanar gathers Aathi flowers and the best of the flowers that bloomed in the Mullai region, considering them as prime flowers. He himself plucks many such sacred flowers (Thiruppallithamam) suitable for adorning Lord Shiva's matted locks, strings them with leaves, and brings them in a flower basket, placing them there. Visarasarmanar brings selected flowers, tender leaves, etc., prescribed by the Agamas for the Lord's puja. These are Aathi flowers and lush tender leaves. It is said that they grew and bloomed naturally in the nearby Mullai region. Sacred flowers for Shiva Puja can be gathered from Nandavanams (flower gardens) cultivated by humans. Here, Chandesura Nayanar gathered flowers that grew naturally in trees and plants in the Mullai region. This is one of the glories of our sacred southern land. This southern land has the glory that all the flowers needed for Shiva Puja bloom naturally on trees and plants. When they are on trees, plants, and creepers, they are called flowers. When they are selected for Lord Shiva's puja, they are called Thiruppallithamam. Thus, Visarasarmanar, knowing the rules for Shiva Puja, plucks the Thiruppallithamam himself and collects them in a basket. How to select flowers

We have extensively discussed the details of selecting flowers in the Puranams of Eripattar Nayanar and Muruga Nayanar. We should recall them here. To carry the plucked Thiruppallithamam, a flower basket is needed. Therefore, Visarasarmanar himself makes a flower basket from leaves. Thus, he collects and brings the flowers in a flower basket made by weaving leaves. Flowers for Lord Shiva's puja should not be carried in bare hands. They should not be carried in cloth or other materials. It is the rule that they should be carried in proper flower baskets. Visarasarmanar, who had built the Shiva Lingam, Shiva temple, gopuram, and circumambulatory path for Shiva Puja, then needed materials for Shiva Puja, including sacred bath (Thirumanjanam) and flowers. Sekkizhar Peruman, after explaining that Visarasarmanar gathered the necessary flowers for Shiva Puja, now explains how he sought the sacred bath material for Shiva Puja. For Lord Shiva's sacred bath in Shiva Puja, milk is needed. Therefore, Visarasarmanar takes nine new pots (Navakumbams) and collects milk from the cows. Sekkizhar Peruman here mentions "good Navakumbams." The Kamika Agama states that one can install Navakumbams and perform abhishekam (sacred bath) for Atmarttha Puja (self-worship). Installing nine pots and performing Thirumanjanam is one of the Agamic rules. It is stated in the book "Saiva Bhushanam," authored by Sri Panchakshara Yogigal of Kanchipuram, that one should install nine pots and worship in Atmarttha Shiva Puja. In the 104th shloka of this book "Saiva Bhushanam," Sri Panchakshara Yogigal has explained the method mentioned in the Kamika Agama that one can install Navakumbams and perform abhishekam for Atmarttha Puja. They have also explained how to do it. One should draw a square mandala of two cubits length, pouring one measure of paddy, and create nine divisions (Navakoshtas) within it. Spread darpa grass in it and install the Shiva Kumbham in the center. To the north of the Shiva Kumbham, install the Vardhini Kumbham, and in the eight directions starting from the east, place eight kalashas (pots). In these, invoke Lord Shiva in the Shiva Kumbham, Parameswari in the Vardhini Kumbham, and the Ashtavidyeswaras in the other eight kumbhams, offering dhoopa (incense) and deepa (lamp) and worshipping them. Furthermore, Navakumbams also means new pots. Thus, Visarasarmanar went to the place where the cows were grazing to obtain the Navakumbams. According to the rule that milk should be obtained from one teat of a cow, he touched one teat, and the cows yielded milk. Earlier, the cows, considering Visarasarmanar as their calf, spontaneously yielded milk. Now, Visarasarmanar approached the cows, and as soon as he slightly touched their teats, the cows, overflowing with love, secreted and yielded milk. Thus, Visarasarmanar did not milk the cows; merely by touching their teats, the cows, filled with love, secreted milk. He collected the milk yielded by the cows in pots and installed those pots inside the temple he had built from white sand. We had already seen that he had collected and kept the sacred flowers (Thiruppallithamam). Thus, Visarasarmanar, having filled the pots with the milk needed for the sacred bath and having collected the sacred flowers, performs Shiva Puja, which is called Archana, with the love that is a continuation of his previous births. What is meant by Shiva Puja, also called Archana, mentioned here? It means that in the Shiva Lingam that Visarasarmanar fashioned from sand, he establishes the Vidya Deham (knowledge body) of Lord Shiva and performs the mantra, bhavana (meditation), and kriya (ritual actions) prescribed in the Agamas to make Shiva manifest in it.

This includes performing Linga Shuddhi (purification of the Lingam), placing the Adhara Shakti (base energy) to the Adi Shakti (primordial energy) on the Linga Peetha (pedestal), performing Shivaasana Pujas (worship of Shiva's seat), then invoking the Vidya Deham (knowledge body) as the Moorthi (deity) on the Lingam, and then meditating on the Paramasiva, who is the Moorthiman (possessor of form) and the life force within that body, above the Dvadashanta (twelve-ended point), and invoking Him. All these are described. He performs the Shiva Avahanam (invocation) as described in the Agamas. Then, he requests Lord Shiva, who has been invoked in the Shiva Lingam, to mercifully reside in the Shiva Lingam until the puja is completed. After that, he offers flowers with appropriate mantras, folds his hands in prayer, praises, and bows. For Svayambhu Lingams (self-manifested Lingams) and Daivika Lingams (divinely created Lingams), this Nyasa Vidhi (ritual of placing mantras on the body) and Avahana Vidhi (invocation ritual) are not performed. However, for other types of Lingams, such as Manuda Lingams (human-made Lingams) and the six types of Lingams, the Shivagamas prescribe that Nyasa and Avahanam should be performed. Therefore, here, Visarasarmanar, also known as Chandesura Nayanar, installs the Shiva Lingam in river sand. Thus, according to the rules of Atmarttha Shiva Puja prescribed in the Agamas, he performs Nyasa, Avahanam, and other rituals, and sees Lord Shiva manifest in that Shiva Lingam. Then, he performs Thirumanjanam (sacred bath) for Lord Shiva there. In the Puranam of Thirukuripputhonda Nayanar, Kamakshi Ambal worships the Lord under a mango tree on the banks of the Kampa river. That Lord is a Svayambhu Moorthi, who manifested Himself by His own grace. Therefore, there, without performing the Avahana ritual, Ammaiyar performed puja by starting with Thirumanjanam, applying sacred paste, offering incense, lamps, and other pujas according to Agamic rules. What Kamakshi Ambal did there was for the welfare of the world, performed on the self-manifested deity. Therefore, it is called Pararttha Puja (worship for others). What Chandesura Nayanar does here is considered Atmarttha Puja (self-worship). Thus, according to the method prescribed by the Shivagamas, according to the rules of Atmarttha Puja, and as a result of his experience of performing Shiva Puja in all his previous births, the young boy Visarasarmanar performs the puja by performing Shuddhis (purifications), Asana Puja (worship of the seat), Nyasa, Avahanam, and other rituals, following the procedures prescribed in the Agamas, from Avahanam to Pushpadanam (offering of flowers), with ten actions and five services. Regarding this method of puja, Shivakavimani Aiya writes, "See actions 23 and 24 of Siddhanta Saraavali Kriyapada." Furthermore, how to perform Atmarttha Puja and how to receive Diksha (initiation) can be learned by receiving Diksha from qualified Shivacharyas and through their Upadesha (instruction). Sekkizhar mentions in the 1218th verse, "due to the continuation of previous knowledge." According to this, these Agamic rules were made known to Visarasarmanar's mind by the grace of Lord Shiva, due to the practice he had done before. The yearning for Shiva Puja that Visarasarmanar had was born of love. It ripened into mature devotion. As this mature devotion ripened, he repeatedly poured the white milk, performing the sacred bath. Thus, with great love, with ripe love, as he performed Thirumanjanam for Lord Shiva, Lord Shiva, residing within the Shiva Lingam, accepted the Shiva Puja performed by Visarasarmanar. We saw that he installed the Shiva Lingam from white sand, from river sand. After performing Nyasa and Avahanam, this Shiva Lingam became the Shiva Moorthi in which Lord Shiva resided.

Within it, Lord Shiva, who resided within the Shiva Lingam, accepted Chandesura Nayanar's puja. Even though Lord Shiva is omnipresent, He stood manifest in that Shiva Lingam and accepted the puja performed by Visarasarmanar. How does Visarasarmanar perform Shiva Puja? He does it with great absorption (Layam). Layam is a Sanskrit word, and Sekkizhar Peruman here refers to it as "with a unified thought in his heart." What is unified thought or Layam? It is explained as the worshipper and the worshipped Lord Shiva, these two entities, not being seen as separate, but merging into one in thought. Sekkizhar Peruman refers to this as "with a unified thought." In Sanskrit, this is called Layam. Such worship is the true worship desired by God. The great child of Sengalur, whose glory shines, performs Shiva Puja by filling with love those parts of the puja, such as the sacred bath, that he did not seek out. We saw that Visarasarmanar arranged for leaves and flowers, which are parts of the puja, and milk for the sacred bath. Visarasarmanar did not seek out other things needed for the sacred bath, such as water, sandalwood, incense, and offerings. He performs the puja by filling these unobtained items with love. It is a rule that when the necessary materials for Shiva Puja are unavailable, one should fill them with love and perform the puja. This is called Bhavana Puja (mental worship). When the necessary materials for puja are unavailable, one should imagine that those materials are present and perform the puja. Sekkizhar Peruman refers to this as "filling with love those parts of the sacred bath and other initial items that were not sought." Thus, Chandesura Nayanar, also known Visarasarmanar, performs the puja according to the Shivagamas, adorning with clothes, sandalwood, flower garlands, ornaments, etc., performing Archana with appropriate mantras, offering Dhoopa (incense), Deepa (lamp), Arghyam (water offering), offering food, performing Avarana Puja (worship of surrounding deities), praising, bowing, chanting mantras, and following other rituals. Even after performing the puja, the joy that the Nayanar felt remains undiminished. Lord Shiva accepts the Shiva Puja performed by Visarasarmanar and bestows grace with delight. Here, Shivakavimani Aiya writes an important note: "Even when the instruments and materials for puja are sought but not found, the puja should not stop. It is a rule that the puja should be performed without interruption or hindrance." Therefore, in times when the necessary materials for Shiva Puja are unavailable, it is also a rule that one should fill them with mental imagination (Bhavana) and perform Shiva Puja without interruption. The details of these are found in the Agamas. Flowers and water, which are essential for puja, are available everywhere in this sacred land. But even when these are unavailable, the puja should be performed mentally without hindrance. Thus writes Shivakavimani Aiya. Let us continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 14 — Chandesura Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 258. The section on "Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam" (The Chapter of Ruling the World with Threefold Virtues). The Puranam of Chandesura Nayanar. We have extensively contemplated how Chandesura Nayanar built a Shiva Lingam, a circumambulatory path, and a gopuram from sand on the banks of the Manniyaaru river and performed Shiva Puja under an Aathi tree. The temple built by Chandesura Nayanar is now known as Thiruvaipaadi, near Thirucheygnallur, where a very beautiful Shiva temple stands. The Lord's name there is Balukandanathar, Balukandeeswarar. The reference to Chandesura Nayanar bathing the Lord with milk is evident in the Lord's name. The Goddess's name is Birugannayaki, Periyanayaki. The Sthala Vriksham (sacred tree) there is the Aathi tree. We also saw that Chandesura Nayanar built that Shiva temple in the shade of an Aathi tree. Accordingly, the Sthala Vriksham here is an Aathi tree, a wild Aathi tree. Thirunavukkarasu Peruman has composed a Thiruppathikam (hymn) at this sacred place. Thus, Visarasarmanar, who went to graze the cows, collects the milk yielded by the cows in pots, performs Thirumanjanam (sacred bath), and performs Shiva Puja. Even after pouring the milk needed for the sacred bath for Shiva Puja, the cows continue to yield abundant milk with full udders, without any reduction. Therefore, the Brahmins, who are the owners of those cows, receive an undiminished supply of milk in their homes. Consequently, the prosperity derived from it also flourishes without reduction. Visarasarmanar performs Shiva Puja in this manner for many days. The paths of Saivism are called Chariyai, Kiriyai, Yogam, and Gnanam. Among these four, the Shiva Puja performed by Visarasarmanar is Kiriyai, the second path, which is special. This is also called Putthira Margam (the path of the son). This endeavor and practice of performing excellent puja, which requires many days of effort and practice, is very rare and difficult even for elders. But for the young Visarasarmanar, it happened naturally, as a form of play. For Visarasarmanar, Shiva Puja was a game, and the game itself was Shiva Puja. Therefore, he did it naturally, not artificially. Worship of Shiva should be natural for humans. While this was happening, someone observed Visarasarmanar performing Shiva Puja. Even though he directly witnessed the excellent Shiva Puja performed by Visarasarmanar, lacking the ability to comprehend its true nature, he acted out of ignorance. He told the other Brahmins in the village, who owned the cows, that Visarasarmanar was taking all their milk and performing Shiva Puja. The Shiva Puja performed by Visarasarmanar was great among the great. But the observer, who saw something great but possessed a very small intellect, told this news to the Brahmins in the village. Upon hearing what he said, all the Brahmins became angry. "This boy Visarasarmanar, who said he would graze our cows well, is taking them out as if to graze them, but then milking them and doing this Shiva Puja!" they exclaimed. "Call Visarasarmanar's father, Echadattan, to report his mischief!" everyone said. This is because Visarasarmanar was a child, and parents are responsible for the transgressions of children. It is the duty of parents to correct children. This is the way of the world. We should also remember that Visarasarmanar was a boy of seven years. Echadattan, Visarasarmanar's father, came and stood before the assembly of Brahmins. Then all the Brahmins addressed Echadattan, saying,

"Listen to the evil deed your son is doing, claiming to graze all our cows," and they recounted all the events that had transpired. The assembly members told Echadattan that Visarasarmanar, with a joyful heart and great love, was taking all the cows that yielded milk for the Brahmins' yagnas, as if to graze them, but then milking them on the banks of the Manniyaaru river, spilling the milk on the sand, and acting according to his own whims. They also made it clear that the one who had directly witnessed all these actions of Visarasarmanar was standing there as a witness. Therefore, this was a firsthand account, not something heard from others. Upon hearing this from the assembly of Brahmins, Echadattan, Visarasarmanar's father, was greatly afraid. Then he told the assembly, "I was unaware of this act committed by a young boy. You must forgive the harm that has occurred so far," and he bowed and spoke. He further stated, "If this act happens again, that fault will be mine," said Echadattan. Having said this, Echadattan took leave of the Brahmins, completed his evening rituals, and went home, thinking, "This son has brought shame upon me." He decided that he must personally witness his son's actions. That night passed. The next day dawned. Visarasarmanar, as usual, went to graze the cows. Echadattan, who had not slept all night due to great worry, now that day had broken and Visarasarmanar had gone to graze the cows, decided that he must see it directly. Echadattan decided to hide and watch his son's actions. In truth, after the Brahmin assembly made such accusations, Echadattan could have directly called and questioned his son, or he could have openly gone with his son and observed his actions directly. Instead of doing all this, Echadattan decided to hide and watch. Echadattan, who was born in the Kashyapa gotra, had studied the Vedas and lived according to them, being a Brahmin, a Vedic scholar, and an elder. However, he had not realized the inner meaning of the Vedas, which is Shiva Gnanam (knowledge of Shiva). That is why he thought of hiding and watching his son's actions. Only those with deceit in their hearts would hide themselves in this manner. Avvai Perumatti's Moothurai, 25th verse, sings: "A snake, knowing it has poison, hides and dwells; a fearless water snake swims openly. Those with deceit in their hearts hide themselves; those without deceit in their hearts do not hide." That is, a poisonous snake, knowing its venom, lives hidden. A non-poisonous water snake lives openly, swimming for all to see. Similarly, those with deceit in their hearts will not speak openly; they will hide. Those without deceit in their hearts will speak openly and act openly. Therefore, in accordance with "a snake, knowing it has poison, hides and dwells," Echadattan, Visarasarmanar's father, instead of directly asking his son or going with him to find out, decided to hide and watch. Visarasarmanar, the son of Echadattan, took all the cows of the village and grazed them on the banks of the Manniyaaru river. Echadattan, his father, followed him secretly to the banks of the Manniyaaru river and climbed a Kura tree there, hiding to watch what would happen next. That day was the day Echadattan was freed from unexpiated Shiva-aparaadha (offense against Shiva) and attained salvation. Moreover, it was the unique auspicious day when the world would attain the benefit of Shiva Puja through the events of that story. Echadattan hid in a Kura tree. This is a type of tree. Its flower is very dear to Lord Shiva. We saw in the 240th verse of Periya Puranam that the Kura tree should also be planted in the Nandavanams (flower gardens) created for Lord Shiva's worship. In such a Kura tree, Echadattan hid and was about to witness the events unfolding there. At that time, the brahmachari Visarasarmanar, according to his daily custom, bathed, built a Shiva temple and a circumambulatory path from sand for Lord Shiva, plucked the flowers and buds that bloomed that day, collected the milk that the cows themselves yielded in pots, arranged all these, and began to perform that rare Shiva Puja even that day. According to the rules of the Saiva Agamas, and out of the maturity of the love that arose in his heart, he began to perform Shiva Puja even that day, as if in play. He offered the sacred flowers (Thiruppallithamam) he had collected on Lord Shiva's sacred head and performed Archana. He lovingly took the sweet pots of milk and performed Thirumanjanam for Lord Shiva. At that moment, an auspicious event occurred, as if by the grace of Lord Shiva. Let us continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 15 — Chandesura Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 259. The section on "Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam" (The Chapter of Ruling the World with Threefold Virtues). The Puranam of Chandesura Nayanar. As usual, Chandesura Nayanar was performing Shiva Puja that day. He offered sacred flowers (Thiruppallithamam) and performed Archana. Then, he took the pots of milk and lovingly performed Thirumanjanam for Lord Shiva. At that moment, the event occurred by the grace of Lord Shiva. Echadattan, Visarasarmanar's father, who was on the Kura tree, saw his son Visarasarmanar performing Thirumanjanam with milk and became angry. Why is it mentioned here that Echadattan's anger occurred by divine grace? Because if Echadattan had not become angry, that day's event would not have happened. If it had not happened, Visarasarmanar's greatness would not have been revealed to the world. Therefore, the implication here is that divine grace itself caused him to lose his wisdom. Grace is the form of wisdom. Does it cause wisdom to be lost? The implication is that divine grace acted in this way for Visarasarmanar's greatness to be revealed. Echadattan, Visarasarmanar's father, came down from the Kura tree, and, angered by seeing his son performing Thirumanjanam, struck his son's back with the staff he held in his hand. He struck him while uttering very harsh words. Echadattan stood behind Visarasarmanar and struck Visarasarmanar's back. But Visarasarmanar, the great little devotee who was performing the sacred service of Shiva Puja, had his intense love firmly fixed on Lord Shiva during the Archana. Therefore, he did not feel the blows. He was completely absorbed in Shiva Puja, performing it with intense love and great concentration (Layam). So, he did not feel his father striking his back. Echadattan, growing angrier and angrier, struck him many times. Because of his anger, he had lost his wisdom. So he struck him many times. But Visarasarmanar, who was greater than all, remained steadfast in his sacred duty of performing Thirumanjanam and continued to do it. Seeing Visarasarmanar, who continued to perform Shiva Puja by pouring milk for Thirumanjanam without reacting in the slightest, no matter how much he was beaten, Echadattan became even angrier, lost his wisdom, became bewildered, and was filled with extreme rage. Consequently, Echadattan kicked the pot of milk for Thirumanjanam, spilling it. Even after striking him many times with the staff he held, it was of no avail. Therefore, not knowing the truth and having lost his wisdom, Echadattan began to commit evil with his feet, just as he had done with his hands. Echadattan kicked the pot of milk for Thirumanjanam, which Visarasarmanar had kept for performing Thirumanjanam for the Lord, the pot filled with milk for Thirumanjanam, which had been set up to perform Thirumanjanam for the Lord. Echadattan kicked and spilled that milk. Thus, Echadattan committed a great Shiva-aparaadha (offense against Shiva). He struck Visarasarmanar, a devotee of Shiva. Now he kicked the pot of milk kept for Lord Shiva's Thirumanjanam. This is a Shiva-aparaadha. An offense against Shiva's devotees is considered even greater than an offense against Shiva. Thus, Echadattan committed two great Shiva-aparaadhas. Whenever he was struck, Visarasarmanar did not feel it, being absorbed in Shiva Puja. But when he saw the pot of milk for Lord Shiva's puja being kicked, Visarasarmanar then focused on what was happening. He did not feel the blows when he was struck, because Visarasarmanar's intense love was entirely focused on Shiva Archana. Therefore, Visarasarmanar did not feel the blows falling on him. But now, Echadattan kicked the pots of milk meant for Shiva Puja. When the pot of milk for Shiva Archana, on which Visarasarmanar had placed all his intense love, was scattered, Visarasarmanar's gaze now fell upon it. Visarasarmanar's external and internal senses were not directed outwards but were entirely absorbed in Shiva Puja. Therefore, the obstruction that came to that Shiva Puja changed his state of absorption (Layam) and made him see the spilled milk pot. Visarasarmanar observed the act of the Thirumanjanam milk pot being spilled. His gaze then fell upon the perpetrator, and he looked to see who had kicked and scattered the milk pot. He realized that it was his own father who had done it. Even though he realized it was his father, only the evil deed his father had committed remained in Visarasarmanar's mind. Only the thought of him being the evil person who had committed the Shiva-aparaadha of kicking the Thirumanjanam milk pot meant for the Lord remained in Visarasarmanar's mind. Echadattan's feet kicked the Thirumanjanam milk pot meant for Lord Shiva, and the milk spilled. Therefore, by spilling the milk meant for Lord Shiva, those feet became feet deserving of punishment. Visarasarmanar decided to punish that transgression. Visarasarmanar was a cowherd. Therefore, he always carried a staff with him. Now, Visarasarmanar took that staff to punish Echadattan's feet. The moment he took it, the staff transformed into an axe (Mazhu). To punish the crime committed by Echadattan's feet, he took the staff, and it transformed into an axe, a tool capable of delivering appropriate punishment. This was by divine command. Visarasarmanar, the great elder whose senses had all become Shiva-senses, therefore, the small staff he took to punish Echadattan's feet transformed into an axe in his hand by Lord Shiva's divine command. He threw it. The axe went and severed Echadattan's feet. The son, Visarasarmanar, severed the two feet of his father, who had obstructed Shiva Puja, with that axe, which served as an instrument to remove the obstruction that occurred in Shiva Puja. The instrument that removes obstacles in Shiva Puja is the Pashupata Astra. One of the components of Shiva Puja prescribed in the Agamas is the removal of obstacles to Shiva Puja. This is called Vighnochcharanam. It is the method of removing obstacles arising from the three worlds—the upper world, the middle world, and the lower world—through appropriate mantras, visualizations, and actions, and then entering the puja. The staff taken by Visarasarmanar transformed into an axe, and when he threw it, Echadattan's feet were severed, and Echadattan fell to the ground. Only one of Echadattan's feet had kicked the milk pot. But both his feet were mutilated and severed. This is because, even though Echadattan kicked the milk pot with one foot, the other foot, which was firmly planted, assisted it. With the support of the other firmly planted foot, the second foot kicked and spilled the milk pot. Therefore, both feet were severed, including the one that assisted the kicking foot, and Echadattan fell to the ground. According to the rules of justice, both the perpetrator and the accomplice should be punished. Thus, having removed the obstruction to his Shiva Puja, Visarasarmanar now began to perform Shiva Puja again. Immediately, Lord Shiva and Uma Devi appeared to Visarasarmanar, riding on the bull. Is it not true that God manifests only when worldly attachments are severed? Until now, Lord Shiva, who had been accepting the puja from within the Shiva Lingam fashioned from sand by Visarasarmanar, now, in that place where Visarasarmanar's love had melted and ripened, manifested very clearly with Uma Devi on the Rishabha Vahana (bull vehicle). Is it not our custom to see the Lord come with the Goddess on the bull whenever He comes to bestow grace upon devotees and grant them His world? With the Bhutaganas (celestial attendants) surrounding Him, and the sages and gods praising the Lord with Vedic chants, Lord Shiva, the immaculate form, manifested with overflowing divine compassion. Visarasarmanar, the young boy whose devotion had matured, saw Lord Shiva, worshipped Him, was filled with immense joy, and fell at His lotus feet. Lord Shiva, with matted locks adorned with a Kondrai garland, addressed Visarasarmanar, the young boy who lay at His feet, saying, "For Our sake, you struck down your own father. Henceforth, We Ourselves shall be your next father," and bestowed His grace. He embraced him, caressed him with grace-filled compassion, and smelled his head with delight, bestowing His grace. Sekkizhar Peruman says, "the young boy who fell at the feet of the Lord." The Lord's feet are two feet. They are the sacred feet that serve as a support and refuge for those who reach Him. The sacred feet are the fullness of divine grace. The shadow of the sacred feet is the state of manifestation for a person within that fullness of grace. The experience of divine grace occurs only after it has manifested. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman says "under the shadow of the sacred feet" instead of "under the sacred feet." The Lord then lifted Visarasarmanar, the young boy who had fallen. This concept also applies to the idea that those who wish to receive grace from elders and God should fall at their feet. He fell at His feet. Therefore, it became His duty to lift the one who had fallen. We should recall here the story of Kulachirrai Nayanar, who wished to uplift the fallen Pandya kingdom, and lay prostrate on the path where Thirugnanasambandha Peruman was coming, remaining fallen until Peruman himself came and lifted him with his sacred hands. Thus, the Lord lifted Visarasarmanar, the young boy who lay at His sacred feet. He embraced Visarasarmanar, bestowed grace, and caressed him. These were actions performed by Lord Shiva with His sacred hands. Lifting Visarasarmanar, who lay fallen, signifies the removal of his bonds (Pasa Neekkam). Embracing him signifies granting him Shiva-peru (the state of Shiva-hood). "Having removed the defilement of my mind and made me Shiva, He ruled me," says the Achcho Pathikam in Thiruvachakam. By embracing, he is made Shiva. By caressing, he is immersed in Shiva and appears in the light of Shiva. Vidwan Thiru Sa. Periyasamy Pillai writes, following the commentary on the 11th Sutra of Sivagnana Mapadiyam, that embracing signifies the manifestation of grace, and caressing signifies the manifestation of bliss. Therefore, embracing with hands is the manifestation of grace. Caressing with hands is the manifestation of bliss. Here, Lord Shiva looks at Visarasarmanar, who lay fallen, bestows grace, embraces him, and caresses him. These signify the Dikshas of Nayana Diksha (initiation through sight), Vakku Diksha (initiation through word), and Sparisa Diksha (initiation through touch). Sri Na. Sivaprakasa Desikar writes that since these Dikshas are indicated, it can be understood that other Dikshas also occurred by implication. Then Lord Shiva says, "For My sake, you struck down your father, causing him to fall." Lord Shiva says, "For Our sake." What does that mean? It means that he did so for the sake of the sacred service, the puja, to Lord Shiva, to be completed without hindrance or interruption. Thus, Lord Shiva addressed Visarasarmanar, saying, "For My sake, for the sake of the Shiva Puja you perform for Me to be completed without hindrance, you struck down your father, who begot you, raised you, and performed your Brahmopadesham (sacred thread ceremony), with a sword. Therefore, henceforth, We shall be your next father." The father who begot Visarasarmanar was struck down and perished. Therefore, he becomes an unworthy father to you. Therefore, henceforth, We Ourselves shall be your next father, says Lord Shiva. The meaning of this is that just as Lord Shiva is the father of all living beings in a general sense, so too was He the father of Visarasarmanar. Now, without that general sense, He says that He will be very close to him in a special sense, as a father. Therefore, He lifts Visarasarmanar, embraces him, bestows grace, caresses him, and smells his head with delight. These are all actions a father performs out of love for his son. Lord Shiva, by saying, "I am your next father," bestows grace in a fatherly manner. Therefore, He performs these actions just like a father. Visarasarmanar's body, which was made of Maya (illusion), when touched by Lord Shiva's sacred hands, became a Shiva-body. His senses all became Shiva-senses. The meaning of this is that his individual senses all became divine senses. By the touch of Lord Shiva's sacred hands, the body made of Maya was transformed into a Shiva-body of immeasurable exaltation. Just as the philosopher's stone (Parisavedhi) transforms whatever it touches into gold, Visarasarmanar's body became Shiva-maya (permeated with Shiva). Shiva-maya here means Shiva-svarupam (the very form of Shiva). Thus, immersed in the abundance of Lord Shiva's divine grace, Visarasarmanar lost his self and was filled with and revelled in divine grace. All the gods, including Brahma, who is seated on the lotus flower, saw this and praised him. In accordance with the saying, "How the earth is saved by Shiva," Brahma and other gods come to the earth and perform Shiva Puja. To receive the benefit of that Shiva Puja, they praise Chandisa. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman has composed it in this way. Thus, Visarasarmanar shone in the Shiva light that enveloped him. He shone in the Shiva Prakasam (divine effulgence). This Shiva Prakasam is seen with the eye of wisdom. Lord Shiva, having thus bestowed grace, made Visarasarmanar the chief of all Shiva devotees and said, "We have given you the position of Chandisa, so that all the food We eat, the clothes We wear, the garlands We adorn, and the ornaments, all shall belong to you." And Lord Shiva took the Kondrai flower garland from His matted locks and adorned Visarasarmanar with it. We will continue to contemplate this in detail. Thiruchitrambalam.
