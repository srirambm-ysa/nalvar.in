Chapter 16 — Chandesura Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam Part 260. Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam. Chandesura Nayanar Puranam. We meditated on the words of Lord Shiva, who, after lifting Visarasarmanar, embraced him, caressed his head, and declared, "From now on, I shall be your father." Furthermore, Lord Shiva appoints Visarasarmanar as the chief of all devotees. He makes him the leader of all devotees. Sekkizhar Peruman composes a sacred verse stating that the Lord of the celestials made him the chief of the devotees and said, "We have given you the position of Chandikeswara, so all the vessels we have eaten from, the clothes we wear, and the garlands we adorn are for you." He then took the garland of konrai flowers from his own crescent-moon-adorned matted locks and placed it on Visarasarmanar's golden head. Thus, Lord Shiva makes Visarasarmanar the chief of all devotees. Having done so, Lord Shiva tells Visarasarmanar, "All the vessels I have eaten from, the clothes I wear, the garlands, ornaments, and everything else belong to you, and I have bestowed upon you the position of Chandikeswara." Not only that, Lord Shiva takes the konrai garland from his own sacred head and places it on the head of Visarasarmanar, who is Chandikeswara Nayanar. The grace bestowed by Lord Shiva upon Visarasarmanar is the position of Chandikeswara. Chandikeswara is a position or status. For Lord Shiva, Goddess Ambika, Lord Ganesha, Lord Muruga, and the Sun God, there is a Chandikeswara position associated with each deity, close to them. Those who hold these positions are the ones who bestow the benefits of worship upon those who worship these respective deities. In this kalpa, the Nayanar known as Visarasarmanar holds the Chandikeswara position of Lord Shiva. Therefore, since Visarasarmanar was appointed by Lord Shiva to the position of Chandikeswara, he is called Chandesura Nayanar after that position. Those who hold the Chanda position of Lord Shiva are called Dhvani Chandas. Similarly, the Chanda of the Sun God is called Tejas Chanda. Likewise, for Ambika, there is Neeli Chandan; for Ganesha, Ambo Chandan; and for Lord Muruga, Matru Chandan, all holding the Chandikeswara position. Lord Shiva bestows such a Chandikeswara position upon Visarasarmanar. Therefore, Visarasarmanar is called Chandesura Nayanar. Not only that, Lord Shiva ensures that all the vessels he has eaten from, the clothes he wears, the garlands, ornaments, and everything else belong exclusively to Chandikeswara. The Shiv Nirmalyas, such as Lord Shiva's sacred food, clothes, and garlands, belong to Chandikeswara. The shrine of Chandeswara will be built next to the ablution tank in a Shiva temple. He is one who constantly meditates on Lord Shiva without interruption. Therefore, one of the rules of Shiva temple darshan is not to cross between Chandeswara's shrine and the Shiva ablution tank. Shiva darshan is completed with the prayer of Chandikeswara darshan. The rule is to reach Chandikeswara's shrine, worship Chandikeswara, gently clap one's hands, offer the Shiv Nirmalya prasadam received, pray for the benefit of Shiva darshan, and then apply the sacred ash received from him. It is a rule that those who do not have Shiva initiation should not consume Shiv Nirmalyas. It is also stipulated in various Shiva Agamas that no one should consume Chandikeswara Nirmalya. Sivakavimani Ayya writes that it is the duty of good Saivite people to learn and follow these rules from good teachers. Lord Shiva tells Chandikeswara Nayanar, "All the vessels I have eaten from, the clothes I wear, and the garlands I adorn are for you." Therefore, the food offered to Lord Shiva, which is the remaining food, is called "parikala shesham" in Sanskrit. This means the food left over after Lord Shiva has eaten.

Therefore, this means that the sacred food offered to Lord Shiva is suitable for Chandikeswara to consume. It also means that the clothes worn by Lord Shiva should be worn by Chandikeswara Nayanar. By "garlands and ornaments," it is implied that Chandikeswara Nayanar should be adorned with the very flowers and garlands that were offered to Lord Shiva. In Shiva temples, there is a bad practice seen nowadays where devotees, after worshiping, take threads from their clothes and leave them at the shrine of Chandeswara Murthy. This is a great Shiva offense. The clothes that were offered as Nirmalya to Lord Shiva should be offered to Chandikeswara. It is highly improper and sinful for people to leave threads from their own worn and impure clothes. The shrine of Chandeswara is found in all Shiva temples. He is one of the Pancha Murthies (five deities). In the five separate temples of Shiva, Muruga, Ganesha, Surya, and Ambika, Chandeswara manifests with different names and forms. In Shiva temples, Chandeswara is known as Dhvani Chanda or Veera Chanda. In Muruga temples, he is Sumitra Chanda. In Ganesha temples, he is Kumbha Chanda. In Surya temples, he is Tejas Chanda. In Ambika temples, he is Chandikeswari or Yamuni Chandi or Dharmaneethi Neeli. In all Shiva temples, there is a small, separate shrine for Lord Chandeswara. This shrine is located outside the sanctum sanctorum, near the gomukhi (spout) from which the abhisheka water flows. After performing Shiva darshan and worship in the temple, we should conclude our prayers with Chandeswara darshan. The Agamas state that if Chandeswara darshan is not completed, Shiva darshan is not complete, and its benefits are not attained. As we have already seen, since Chandeswara Nayanar is always in Shiva meditation, one should not cross between him and the ablution tank. Since he is always receiving Shiva Nirmalya, one should also not circumambulate the Chandeswara shrine. In Shiva temples, after completing all the necessary pujas for the main deity, the Linga Murthy, one should go to the Chandeswara temple, bring the Nirmalyas of Lord Shiva (such as leaves, flowers, garlands, clothes, and offerings), establish them at the Chandeswara shrine, perform abhishekam to the image of Chandeswara with pure water, worship him, and meditate on him. This is stated in the Agamas. This is the Chandeswara Puja performed by the Sivacharyas as part of the Parartha Puja in Shiva temples. During the Mahotsava (grand festival) periods, the Chandeswara Utsavam (festival) is held at the end. The next day, the Acharya Utsavam is held. At that time, Chandeswara should be adorned with clothes, ornaments, garlands, and other Shiva Nirmalyas according to the rules, worshipped, and then taken out in a palanquin, shield, or on a bull, with umbrellas, chamaras (whisks), and other decorations, accompanied by all musical instruments, in a circumambulation of the streets. After circumambulating, he should be re-established in his place, and prayers should be offered for the benefits of the Shiva Utsavam. Only those who perform this Utsavam with firm resolve will attain the benefits of the Shiva Utsavam. In Shiva temples, the Pancha Murthies (five deities) revered are Ganesha, Muruga, Shiva, Ambika, and Chandeswara. Their main shrines and processional images are properly established and can still be seen in temples today. It is our tradition to keep the accounts of Shiva temples in the name of Chandeswara. Devotees who worship Shiva, after worshiping Shiva, finally receive the benefits of Shiva temple worship through Chandeswara Murthy at his shrine. Chandeswara Nayanar built a Shiva Lingam and a Shiva temple in the sand at Thiruvayppadi. In that temple, Lord Chandeswara is enshrined in a separate temple on a raised platform within the sanctum sanctorum of the main deity, facing south. It is believed that the entire administration of the Shiva temple is carried out according to his command. We have now meditated on the sacred history of Visarasarmanar, to whom Lord Shiva bestowed the position of Chandikeswara.

Similarly, Agamic texts state that there have been many who attained the Chandikeswara position in various yugas preceding the Kali Yuga. Chandeswara is known by many names: Chanda, Veera Chanda, Chandeswara, Chandikeswara, Chandeswara Nayanar, Sengalur Pillaiyar, Chandanatha, Thondar Nayagar, Sivaganathalaivar, Adi Chanda, Sadaryuga Chandeswaras, Chandisan, Dandeswara, Prachanda, Vikranta Chanda, Vibhuchanda, and Brahma Chandi. In the Krita Yuga, Chandeswara Nayanar, bearing the name Prachanda, will appear with a white body, 16 arms, riding an elephant, holding a trident, axe, vajra, kadaka, kosha, surikai, danda, and abhaya in his right hands, and a shield, pindipalam, pasam, mani, vyaghra mudra, suchi mudra, naga, and varada mudra in his left hands. In the Treta Yuga, he will have eight arms, a blood-red complexion, riding a horse, holding a trident, pasam, sword, and axe in his right hands, and varada, shield, palam, and mani in his left hands. In the Dvapara Yuga, he will have four arms, a golden complexion, matted locks, protruding fangs, holding an axe and trident in his right hands, and a pasam and ankusam in his left hands, seated on a bhadra peetha. In the Kali Yuga, he will have matted locks, two arms, holding a danda or axe (parasu) in his right hand, and his left hand resting on his thigh or in simha karna hastam, with a serene face, a dark or reddish complexion, seated on a bhadra peetha. Chandeswara's consort is called Chanda Shakti. Chanda Shakti is serene, green-hued, adorned with all ornaments, and known as Dharmaneethi. She has two arms, holding a blue lotus in one hand and making the varada mudra with the other. The Deepta Agama describes the types of Chandeswara: Chanda, Apachanda, Vikranta, and Chandesa, stating there are four types. They are described as possessing great strength, great knowledge, being Rudras, and having a radiance equal to Rudra. They have successively held the Chandikeswara position in each yuga. Therefore, the Agamas state that Chanda Puja is essential to attain the wealth of Shiva Preeti (Shiva's love). In the Parartha Puja, which is the temple worship, Chandeswara Puja should be performed according to the Agamic rules of that temple. In Atmaratha Puja (self-worship), Chandeswara Puja and other rituals should be performed according to the instructions of one's Gurus and Acharyas; this is considered excellent and traditional. The Chandesa Anugraha Murthy in Gangaikonda Cholapuram is very special and famous. Chandesa Anugraha Murthy is one of the Ashtashta Murthams. When Visarasarmanar, who became Chandeswara, cut off his father's legs and then continued his Shiva meditation and Shiva worship, Lord Shiva and Goddess Uma appeared as Uma Sahita Murthy. Lord Shiva, with his sacred hands, took the konrai garland that adorned his matted locks and placed it on Chandeswara's sacred head. This scene is depicted in the Chandesa Anugraha Murthy sculpture. A small sculpture of Chandesa Anugraha Murthy is found at the base of the gopuram of the Sthanunatha Swami Temple in Suchindram. In the Meenakshi Sundareswarar Temple in Madurai, in the Kambathadi Mandapam (Nandi Mandapam), there are twenty-five forms of Lord Shiva. Among them, the Chandesa Anugraha Murthy sculpture is very beautiful and artistically excellent. Here, Chandeswara is depicted in a standing posture. Similarly, Chandesa Murthies are found in many temples in various parts of Tamil Nadu, each with its unique significance. Thus, Lord Shiva bestows upon Visarasarmanar the highly exalted position of Chandikeswara. Therefore, Visarasarmanar is revered as Chandeswara Nayanar. While all beings in all worlds rejoiced with cries of "Hara Hara," and a shower of flowers rained everywhere, and thousands of Shiva Ganas sang and danced in delight, and the Vedas offered praises filled with words, and various musical instruments resounded on all four sides, our Lord Nayanar, bowing to Lord Shiva, joined his Chandikeswara position as the leader of Shiva devotees, so that the good path of Saivism might flourish. Sekkizhar Peruman sings, "While all the world rejoiced, and showers of flowers rained everywhere, and thousands of Ganas sang and danced in delight, and the eloquent Vedas offered praises, and various instruments resounded, our Lord, bowing, joined his position, so that the good path of Saivism might flourish." The benefit of the Nayanar attaining the position of leader of devotees is that the world should understand and benefit from the greatness of Shiva Puja. Now, Echadattan, the aged Brahmin, who committed a Shiva offense known to the world, was freed from that sin by the grace of Lord Shiva, by being struck by the beautiful axe held in the sacred hand of Sengalur Pillaiyar, who embodies the good conduct of the four Vedas. He attained the blessing of joining the Shiva Loka of the Primeval Lord with his relatives. Echadattan committed a Shiva offense, an offense against Shiva devotees and Shiva. There is no atonement for such an offense. Yet, that sin left him because his legs were severed by the axe in the sacred hand of Visarasarmanar, who is Sengalur Pillaiyar, Chandikeswara Nayanar. By attaining that blessing, he reached Shiva Loka. In this verse, Sekkizhar Peruman refers to Visarasarmanar, Chandeswara Nayanar, as Sengalur Pillaiyar. This means the son of Shiva who was born in Sengalur. Generally, we refer to Lord Ganesha as the elder Pillaiyar, Lord Muruga as the younger Pillaiyar, and Thirugnanasambandar as Aludaiya Pillaiyar, because they are the children of Lord Shiva. Here, since Lord Shiva himself said that he became the father of Visarasarmanar, Sekkizhar Peruman refers to him as Sengalur Pillaiyar. We will continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 17 — Chandesura Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam Part 261. Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam. Chandesura Nayanar Puranam. We have extensively meditated on the Puranam of Chandesura Nayanar. Sri Arumuga Navalar Peruman of Sri Lanka, in his commentary on the Puranam of Chandesura Nayanar, with the discourse of Sivakavimani Ayya, provides the following insight: Due to the primordial impurity (Anava Mala) in souls, four types of births occur according to karma. These are Andajam, Svedajam, Udbhijam, and Jarayujam. Andajam refers to creatures born from eggs. Svedajam refers to those born from sweat and warmth. Udbhijam refers to those that sprout by breaking open seeds. Jarayujam refers to those born from the womb. If these are expanded, they amount to 8.4 million types of births (Yoni Bhedas). Among these myriad births, it is extremely rare and a very difficult feat to transcend all other births through enjoyment or atonement and attain human birth. Even after attaining such a rare human birth, it is a great merit to be born in lands where scriptures flourish, rather than in mountains and forests where the fragrance of scriptures does not reach, and where one might be born as a hunter or tribal. Moreover, it is a great merit to be born in the Aryan lands, that is, Bharatadesa, where the Vedas and Agamas are prevalent, leaving aside the Mleccha (barbarian) lands where they are not. Furthermore, it is a great merit to be born among the four varnas, such as Brahmins, rather than in other divisions. Beyond all this, it is an immeasurable and immense merit to embrace the Saiva religion, which clearly recognizes Lord Shiva as the Supreme Being, without straying into other religions. There is scriptural evidence for this in Sivagnana Siddhiyar: "It is extremely rare to attain the grace to leave the delusion of life, escape the meanness of poverty, and with humility, embrace the Saiva religion. Those who worship with the highest Shiva-knowledge, like a full moon, have received grace." This is from Sivagnana Siddhiyar. This precious human birth was created by Pashupati, Lord Shiva, solely for us to meditate on Him in our minds, praise Him with our words, and worship Him with our hands. We should circumambulate Shiva temples with our feet. We should bow to Lord Shiva with our heads. We should listen to the glories of Lord Shiva with our ears. We should behold the sacred form of Lord Shiva with our eyes. It is for these purposes that Lord Shiva created this human birth. Even Brahma, Vishnu, Indra, and other celestials from the upper worlds come to this earth to worship Shiva. Therefore, this human body is extremely rare to obtain. If one does not perform Shiva Puja and attain liberation while possessing this body, then liberation will be very difficult to attain later. What is the ignorance of those souls who, even after obtaining such a good body, waste their time wandering without investigating or reflecting on any of these matters? "Only those eyes that behold the temple of the three-eyed Lord are true eyes. Only those feet that enter the temple of the blue-throated Lord are true feet. Only those heads that bow to the Lord who shares half his body with the Goddess are true heads. Only those hands that worship the Lord with matted locks are true hands. Only that tongue that sings the praises of the Lord is a true tongue. Only those ears that listen to the stories of the Supreme Lord are true ears. Only that heart that meditates on the golden anklets of the Lord is a true heart. Only that servitude that seeks refuge at His feet is true servitude." This is from the Brahmottara Khanda. Therefore, Shiva Puja, which involves serving Lord Shiva in this manner, is the natural and supreme act to be performed by a human being. Shiva Puja involves using implements such as flowers and sacred water, performing the five purifications (Atma Shuddhi, Sthana Shuddhi, Dravya Shuddhi, Mantra Shuddhi, Linga Shuddhi), and then, on the pedestal of the Shiva Lingam, worshipping the Shiva Asana, which is a lotus from Shakti to Adi Shakti. Above that, the Murthy, which is the Vidya Deha (body of knowledge), is invoked in the Lingam. The Nishkala Rupam (formless aspect), which is the life of that Vidya Deha, the all-pervading, all-doing, blissful, and knowledgeable Paramasiva, is meditated upon above the Dvadashanta. He is then invoked into the Vidya Deha that was previously placed. One then prays, "O Lord, You are the Lord of all worlds. Please remain present in this Lingam with great pleasure until the end of this Puja." After worshipping, praising, circumambulating, and prostrating, the Puja is concluded. This is Shiva Puja. The word "Lingam" means "that which causes to manifest." Therefore, the glory of Parameswara, who manifests the world through creation, preservation, and so on, is called Lingam. The manifest forms of that Lingam, such as those made of stone, crystal, sand, or metal, are also called Lingam by courtesy. Vidya Deha refers to the power that presides over the five cosmic functions. Since Shiva is free from primordial impurities, He does not have a physical body like other beings but possesses a body of power. This body is conceived from the five mantras, such as Ishana, which are useful for the five cosmic functions, from head to toe. It is said that Shiva, being a formless entity, took on this conceptual body, which is a manifestation of His power, for the sake of His devotees' grace, as it is difficult for souls to meditate on and worship Him in His formless state. If worshipped with such love, the compassionate Shiva will accept the worship in the Lingam and bestow His grace upon those who perform it. This worship is of two types: sthavara (immovable) and jangama (movable). In this, Lord Shiva, being the Supreme Being, accepts worship and bestows grace upon all living beings. Therefore, Sivagnana Siddhiyar states, "Perform such Shiva Puja with abundant love." The underlying meaning is that worship performed without love will not yield results. One should understand the words of Pattinatthu Pillaiyar: "If my hand says one thing, my mind thinks another, my tongue speaks deceitful lies, my body seeks carnal pleasures, and my ears desire to hear other things, how will you accept the puja I perform, O Remover of karma?" Visarasarmanar, who became Chandesura Nayanar, in his previous birth, studied and understood the Vedas and Agamas and performed Shiva Puja without fail. That is why, in this birth, at a very young age, he easily understood the essence of all scriptures and realized that Shiva is our master. Through true realization, his extraordinary and ever-increasing devotion to Lord Shiva became his very form. The Vedas and Agamas were easily understood by him due to the continuity of knowledge from his previous birth. The conclusion of the gnana shastras is that the soul is the possession of Lord Shiva. Chandesura Nayanar, in his previous birth, performed Puja without fail, and due to that continuity, in this birth, at a very young age, as soon as he thought that cow's milk was worthy of being the sacred ablution for Lord Shiva, he, with great desire for Shiva Puja, performed it. That Puja seemed like a play to others. But because it was performed with constant true devotion, it became very pleasing to Lord Shiva, who is the life of all lives. This Nayanar, whenever he thought of, heard, or saw the truth of Shiva, his mind melted, and he stood in devotion, without expecting any benefit, finding joy in that love itself, forgetting himself. When he was performing Shiva Puja, his father, Echadattan, beat him many times on his back and spoke harsh words. But he was unaware of all that. However, when he saw Echadattan kick the pot of milk and spill it, he realized that his father had committed an offense against his Supreme Father, Lord Shiva, and he cut off his father's legs. Then, he continued his Shiva Puja as before. Because of this devotion, the compassionate Lord Shiva appeared on His bull, and with His sacred hands, lifted him and said, "You cut off your own father for My sake. From now on, I am your father." He embraced him, caressed his body, and smelled his head, bestowing upon him His own Sarupyam (similarity of form) in that very body, and placed him in the Chandesa position, making him the chief of all devotees. Chandesura Nayanar cut off the legs of his father, Echadattan. Echadattan was first a Brahmin. He was Visarasarmanar's father and also his Guru who initiated him into Brahmopadesam. Chandeswara Nayanar cut off such an Echadattan with an axe.

Would the sins of Brahmicide (for killing a Brahmin), patricide (for killing his father), and Gurucide (for killing his Guru who initiated him into Brahmopadesam) apply to Visarasarmanar for cutting Echadattan? No, they would not. This is because there is no greater merit than Shiva Puja, and no greater sin than an offense against Shiva. Echadattan obstructed Visarasarmanar's Shiva Puja, which was an offense against Shiva. Visarasarmanar cut Echadattan out of the abundance of his love for Lord Shiva, who is his Supreme Father and Supreme Guru. Furthermore, Visarasarmanar was at that time in a state of self-forgetfulness, completely absorbed in Shiva. Even if an act committed by one who is absorbed in Shiva is considered a sin, Shiva will consider it as His own work, and therefore, that sin would not apply here. "The sin committed by a devotee of Hara becomes righteousness. The merit of one who has no love for the feet of the Supreme Lord becomes a sin. The great sacrifice performed by the arrogant Daksha became evil, while the sin committed by the child among men became good." This is the 119th verse of Sivagnana Siddhiyar. The merit or sin of an act depends on the strength or weakness of the one who accepts it, not on the strength or weakness of the doer or the act itself. Therefore, a sinful act committed by one who loves the feet of the Primeval Lord will result in merit. Even meritorious acts performed by one who has no love for the feet of Lord Shiva will result in sin. Daksha performed a great sacrifice, which was a meritorious act. However, because it was performed without love for the feet of Lord Shiva, that sacrifice ended in evil. Even though some might say that Chandikeswara Nayanar's act was a sinful act of murder, it was performed out of love for the feet of Lord Shiva, having lost his individual consciousness and become one with Shiva, completely absorbed in Shiva. Therefore, it resulted in merit. Thus, it is understood that one should worship the Supreme Lord Shiva with love. Furthermore, Echadattan was punished immediately by Visarasarmanar, who became Chandikeswara Nayanar. Therefore, Echadattan, along with his relatives, attained Shiva Loka without suffering in hell due to that offense against Shiva. Thus, the greatness of Chandeswara Nayanar is praised in the first Thirumurai (sacred collection) in the Thiruchengalur Thirupathigam, Thirukollili Thirupathigam, and Thiruooral Pathi Thirupathigam by Thirugnanasambandar Peruman. In the second Thirumurai, in the Thirupullirukkuvelur Thirupathigam, and in the third Thirumurai, in Thirupasuram, Thiruvettakudi Thirupathigam, and Thiruvalavai Thiru Yamakam, Thirugnanasambandar Peruman has praised Visarasarmanar, who is Chandeswara Nayanar. In the fourth Thirumurai, Thirunavukkarasu Swamigal has praised him in the Thiruvappadi Thirupathigam, Thirukkurukkai Veerattam Thirupathigam, Thiruchaikkadu Thirupathigam, and Thirucherai Thirupathigam. Furthermore, Thirunavukkarasu Peruman has praised Chandeswara Nayanar in the fifth Thirumurai in the Koyil Thirupathigam, Thirukondisaram Thirupathigam, and Thirumangalakudi Thirupathigam, and in the sixth Thirumurai in the Thirupuvanam Thirupathigam. Sundaramurthy Swamigal has praised him in the seventh Thirumurai in the Thirukalayanallur Thirupathigam, Thirupunkur Thirupathigam, and Thiruveezhimizhalai Thirupathigam. Manikkavasagar Swamigal has praised Chandeswara Nayanar in the eighth Thirumurai, in Thiruvachagam, in Thiruthol Nokkam. Chandeswara Nayanar, at the end of Shiva Puja, is authorized to accept the food, drink, betel, garlands, sandalwood, and other Nirmalyas accepted by Shiva and bestow the benefits of Shiva Puja. Chandeswara Puja is also an essential part of Atmaratha Puja (self-worship) that must be performed at the end. In Saiva Siddhanta, there are rules regarding where and when Chandeswara should be worshipped. Chandesa Puja is prescribed in the Shiva Agamas that appeared during the time of creation. Therefore, it is understood that Chandeswara Puja existed even before this Nayanar. Just as one of the Ashtavidyeswaras attains ultimate liberation or a higher position, and another takes that position, so too did this Nayanar, Visarasarmanar, attain the position of Chandeswara that existed before him. Thus, from what has been said so far, it is understood that there is no greater merit than Shiva Puja and no greater sin than an offense against Shiva. Therefore, while this body exists, those who are eligible for Shiva Linga worship should approach a Saivacharya.

Chapter 18 — Chandesura Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam Part 262. Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam. Chandesura Nayanar Puranam. As we have seen so far, Sekkizhar Peruman, who extensively sang the Puranam of Chandesura Nayanar, further states the following: Echadattan, the father of Chandesura Nayanar, committed an improper act. Visarasarmanar, the young Brahmin boy who became Chandesura Nayanar, cut off his legs with an axe. That young Brahmin boy, Visarasarmanar, became the beloved son of Lord Shiva. He attained the position of Chandesa. He became the leader of Shiva devotees. Who are those who understood this state? Furthermore, Sekkizhar Peruman sings this as the 59th verse: "Are not the actions performed by the devotees who offered love to Lord Shiva themselves penance?" This is from Thiruvachagam, Thiruthol Nokkam, sixth verse: "The Father whose mind is Shiva, whatever He does becomes penance." Even those actions that are not prescribed as penance in the world, or even those that go against rules, become penance if they are performed by those who have love for the feet of Lord Shiva. Therefore, truly speaking, whatever Shiva devotees do is penance. Even if it is not prescribed as penance in the scriptures, if a devotee performs it with intense devotion to Lord Shiva, it will be considered penance. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman concludes the Puranam of Chandesura Nayanar. We are now meditating on the Puranam of Chandesura Nayanar in the Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam of the Thiruthondar Puranam, known as Periya Puranam, which has now concluded. In the Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam, we have meditated on the Puranams of Murthy Nayanar, Muruga Nayanar, Rudra Pasupathi Nayanar, Thirunalaipovar Nayanar, Thirukuripputhonda Nayanar, and Chandesura Nayanar. We know that this fourth Sarukkam corresponds to the third verse of Thiruthondar Thogai composed by Sundaramurthy Swamigal. "To Murthy who ruled the world with triple purity, I am a servant; to Muruga, I am a servant; to Rudra Pasupathi, I am a servant; to Thirunalaipovar, I am a servant; to the devotees of Thirukuripputhondar, I am a servant; to the motherly Chandikeswara Peruman who, in truth, worshipped the sacred form and, when his father angrily rose, cut off his father's legs with an axe, I am a servant; I am a slave to the Lord of Arur, Aruran." This is the third verse of Thiruthondar Thogai. In this, "To Murthy who ruled the world with triple purity, I am a servant" refers to the Puranam of Murthy Nayanar. "To Muruga, I am a servant" refers to the Puranam of Muruga Nayanar. "To Rudra Pasupathi, I am a servant" refers to the Puranam of Rudra Pasupathi Nayanar. "To Thirunalaipovar, I am a servant" refers to the Puranam of Thirunalaipovar Nayanar. "To the devotees of Thirukuripputhondar, I am a servant" refers to the Puranam of Thirukuripputhondar Nayanar. "To the motherly Chandikeswara Peruman who, in truth, worshipped the sacred form and, when his father angrily rose, cut off his father's legs with an axe, I am a servant" refers to the Puranam of Chandesura Nayanar. Now comes the concluding line, "I am a slave to the Lord of Arur, Aruran." The structure of Thiruthondar Puranam as arranged by Sekkizhar is well known to us. Sekkizhar Peruman composed each Sarukkam for each verse of Thiruthondar Thogai, incorporating the Puranams of the devotees mentioned in that verse. Then, at the end of each Sarukkam, Sekkizhar Peruman included a praise of Sundaramurthy Swamigal, in accordance with the concluding line "Aruran, I am a slave to the Lord of Arur." This structure is well known to us. Accordingly, Sekkizhar Peruman has composed a praise of Sundaramurthy Swamigal in accordance with the line "Aruran, I am a slave to the Lord of Arur" in this verse. Firstly, the compendium states, "Aruran, I am a slave to the Lord of Arur." This, in the sequence of the Thiruthondar Thogai, which begins with "Mummaiyaal Ulaganda," comes after the narrative of "I am a servant to the motherly Chandikeswara Peruman." Its meaning is, "I, Sundaramurthy, am a slave to the Lord of Arur, that is, the Lord of Thiruvarur." To explain further, the six devotees praised in this verse, which begins with "Mummaiyaal Ulaganda," are six individuals. Sundaramurthy Swamigal concludes by saying, "I, Aruran, who am a separate servant to each of these six, am also a slave to the Lord of Thiruvarur." That is, Sundaramurthy Swamigal concludes by saying, "I, who am a servant to Murthy Nayanar, I who am a servant to Muruga Nayanar, I who am a servant to Rudra Pasupathi Nayanar, I who am a servant to Thirunalaipovar Nayanar, I who am a servant to Thirukuripputhondar Nayanar, I who am a servant to Chandesura Nayanar, I, Aruran, am also a slave to the Lord of Thiruvarur." Alternatively, it can be concluded that Aruran, who is a slave to Lord Shiva in Arur, is a separate servant to each of these six devotees mentioned earlier. Now, in accordance with this phrase "Aruran, I am a slave to the Lord of Arur," Nambiyandar Nambigal has composed a verse in the Thiruthondar Thiruvandathi, which is a genre of literature. "He is the one who received grace from those who knew the meaning of the words of grace, 'Do not forget the mind that is like the southern well of Thuruthi, and remove the blemish that was placed as a sign for me.' He is the good one known as the Lord of the fertile fields, surrounded by abundant water." This is from Thiruthondar Thiruvandathi. Now, Sekkizhar Peruman, following the compendium's line "Aruran, I am a slave to the Lord of Arur," has composed a praise of Sundaramurthy Nayanar in the concluding section of this Sarukkam. The praise of Sundaramurthy Nayanar is as follows: "We worship this birth that came to worship the fragrant, flower-like soft feet of the sacred one who, with a heart full of love, for the greatness of the devotees of the Lord with the dark, beautiful throat, took up the task for all beings to worship and for the whole land to prosper, and presented the Thiruthondar Thogai." This is the concluding verse of this Sarukkam, composed by Sekkizhar Peruman as a praise of Sundaramurthy Nayanar. This is the 1265th verse. Its meaning is as follows: "We worship this birth that has been blessed to worship the fragrant, flower-like soft feet of Aludaiya Nambigal, who is Sundaramurthy Swamigal, the sacred one who, with a heart full of love, for the greatness of the devotees of Lord Shiva, the Lord with the beautiful throat containing poison, took up the task for all beings to know, worship, and praise, and for the whole land to prosper, and presented the Thiruthondar Thogai." This is the meaning of that verse. Sundaramurthy Swamigal sings the greatness of the Lord and the greatness of the devotees in the Thiruthondar Thogai, which begins with "I am a servant to the Brahmins of Thillai." Lord Shiva is also one among the Brahmins of Thillai. Therefore, Sundaramurthy Swamigal has sung both the greatness of the Lord and the greatness of the devotees in the Thiruthondar Thogai. We have also learned that Sundaramurthy Swamigal incarnated here in the South from Mount Kailash solely to compose the Thiruthondar Thogai. Thus, Sundaramurthy Swamigal composed the Thiruthondar Thogai, in which he sang the glories of individual devotees and groups of devotees. As a result, the sacred text of Thiruthondar Puranam, known as Periya Puranam, emerged. Through this, all beings receive the divine grace of meditating on the greatness and sacred history of the 63 Nayanmars and nine groups of devotees by reading this Periya Puranam, and thereby attain salvation. This is an expression of the compassion of God and Sundaramurthy Swamigal towards all living beings. "He who performed great penance to make the South prosper, the one who came to give the flawless Thiruthondar Thogai," as it is said, we have seen that Nambigal incarnated by divine grace to bestow the Thiruthondar Thogai for the world to prosper by knowing and worshipping the greatness of the devotees. This birth has been blessed to worship the flower-like soft lotus feet of such a sacred Sundaramurthy Swamigal. Generally, others would say that this birth is evil. But to worship the sacred lotus feet of Sundaramurthy Swamigal,

we have been blessed with the fortune of worshipping. Therefore, that birth becomes a worshipable birth, free from any evil to be avoided. "Human birth is indeed desirable in this great world," is the sacred saying of Thirunavukkarasu Swamigal. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman concludes the fourth Sarukkam of Thiruthondar Puranam, known as Periya Puranam, by praising Sundaramurthy Swamigal and saying, "We worship this birth that came to worship the sacred feet of Sundaramurthy Swamigal." We have so far meditated on 1265 verses in four Sarukkams. In these four Sarukkams, we have meditated on the histories of 19 Nayanmars out of the total 63 Nayanmars. We have meditated on the history of one group of devotees out of the nine groups of devotees. We have meditated on the Puranams of the Brahmins of Thillai, Thiruneelakandathu Kuyavanar, Iyarpagai Nayanar, Ilaiyankudi Mara Nayanar, Meypporul Nayanar, Viranminda Nayanar, Amarnithi Nayanar, Eripathar Nayanar, Enathinatha Nayanar, Kannappa Nayanar, Kalaya Nayanar, Manakkanjara Nayanar, Arivattaya Nayanar, Anaya Nayanar, Murthy Nayanar, Muruga Nayanar, Rudra Pasupathi Nayanar, Thirunalaipovar Nayanar, Thirukuripputhonda Nayanar, and Chandesura Nayanar. Without the grace of God and the Guru, such a great Shiva merit is very rare to obtain. Therefore, bowing to the sacred feet of God and the sacred feet of the Guru, and praying for the opportunity to meditate on the histories of the remaining Nayanmars, we will continue to meditate. May the rain of divine grace fall. Praise to the sacred feet of Chandesura Nayanar. Praise to the sacred feet of Sundaramurthy Swamigal. Praise to the sacred feet of Nambiyandar Nambigal. Praise to the sacred feet of Sekkizhar Peruman. Praise to the sacred feet of Sivakavimani Ayya, who composed the commentary on Periya Puranam. Thiruchitrambalam.
