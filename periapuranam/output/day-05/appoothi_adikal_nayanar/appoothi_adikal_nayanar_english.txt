Chapter 01 — Appoothi Adikal Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Part 504, composed by Sekkizhar Swamigal. Thirunindra Sarukkam, Appoothi Adikal Nayanar Puranam. In this Sarukkam called Thirunindra Sarukkam in Periyapuranam, the Puranam of Appoothi Adikal Nayanar is placed after the Puranam of Karaikkal Ammaiyar. The fourth verse in the Thiruthondathogai Thiruppathigam composed by Sundaramurthy Swamigal begins with "Thirunindra Semmaiye". In that verse, Sundaramurthy Swamigal sings, "Thirunindra Semmaiye Semmaiya Konda Thirunavukkaraiyan than adiyaarkkum adiyen, Perunambi Kulachirai than adiyaarkkum adiyen, Perumizhalai Kurumbarkkum Peyarkkum adiyen." Following this, he sings, "Oru Nambi Appoothi Adiyaarkkum Adiyen." Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman places the Puranam of Appoothi Adikal Nayanar after the Puranam of Karaikkal Ammaiyar. It is one of the unique specialties of our Sekkizhar Peruman that when he completes one Puranam in a Sarukkam and begins the next, he sets the stage for the upcoming Puranam in the concluding part of the previous Puranam. In that manner, in the concluding verse of Karaikkal Ammaiyar Puranam, Sekkizhar Peruman sets the stage for the upcoming Puranam of Appoothi Adikal Nayanar. In the 66th concluding verse of Karaikkal Ammaiyar Puranam, praising Ammaiyar's lotus feet, he sings, "Seetha Neer Vayal Soozh Thingaloorin Appoothiyaaram Bodhama Munivar Seitha Thiruthondu Pugalalutren." Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman sets the stage by saying, "I begin to narrate the sacred service performed by Appoothiyar, the great sage of wisdom, who lived in Thingalur, surrounded by cool, water-rich fields, after worshipping Ammaiyar's sacred feet." The Puranam of Appoothi Adikal Nayanar is a Puranam that comes along with the Puranam of Thirunavukkarasu Nayanar. In the Puranam of our Thirunavukkarasu Nayanar, Sekkizhar Peruman briefly sings about Appoothi Adikal Nayanar's Puranam as well, so that it can be contemplated together. As needed, that is, as required for Thirunavukkarasu Nayanar's Puranam, he narrates the history of Appoothi Adikal Nayanar there. Here, as Sundaramurthy Swamigal sang "Oru Nambi Appoothi Adiyaarkkum Adiyen," Sekkizhar Peruman presents the Puranam of Appoothi Adikal Nayanar separately here. In the Puranam of Thirunavukkarasu Nayanar, we briefly saw the sacred name and sacred service of Appoothi Adikal Nayanar. We also saw that Thingalur was the village where Appoothi Adikal Nayanar lived. Sekkizhar Peruman mentions it here. He begins by saying, "I begin to narrate the sacred service performed by Appoothiyar, the great sage of wisdom, in Thingalur, surrounded by cool fields." Here, Sekkizhar Peruman refers to Appoothi Adikal Nayanar as Bodhama Munivar. Bodhama Munivar means a great sage of wisdom. "Tham muthal guruvumai thavathinil unartha vittu anniyam inmavin Aran kazhal selume" is the eighth sutra of Sivagnanabodham. According to that, Appoothi Adikal Nayanar was one who revered guru worship. Appoothi Adikal Nayanar performed sacred services, revering Thirunavukkarasu Peruman himself as his guru. Through the knowledge of the Lord's feet obtained by worshipping such a guru, he sought and found the Lord and was freed from attachments. Therefore, Appoothi Adikal Nayanar becomes Bodhama Munivar. He is praised by Sekkizhar Peruman as a great sage of wisdom. The incidents in his sacred history serve as examples of Appoothi Adikal Nayanar being freed from attachments. His son, who went to pluck banana leaves, falls due to poison. Even then, Appoothi Adikal Nayanar tries to perform his sacred service without mental agitation. Similarly, when his son comes back to life,

he does not rejoice but laments that there has been a slight interruption to the sacred service he performs for our Lord Thirunavukkarasu Nayanar. Similarly, when Thirunavukkarasu Peruman himself says that Thirunavukkarasu Peruman's sacred name is another name, Appoothi Adikal Nayanar becomes angry in his mind. Through these histories, through these incidents, Appoothi Adikal Nayanar was freed from attachments and shone as one who possessed knowledge of the Lord. It will be understood that he was performing sacred service by seeking and finding the Lord through that knowledge of the Lord's feet. Even though Appoothi Adikal Nayanar lived a householder's life with his wife and children, Appoothi Adikal Nayanar shone as a great sage who had renounced attachments in his mind and embraced spiritual detachment. Therefore, based on this foundation, we should begin to learn the history of Appoothi Adikal Nayanar. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman praises Appoothi Adikal Nayanar as Bodhama Munivar here, guiding the people of the world. It would be enough to just say "Munivar". Sekkizhar Peruman praises him as Bodhama Munivar. This is because our Appoothi Adikal Nayanar possessed the wisdom revealed to him in his penance, considering it his foremost guru. The word "Ma" in "Bodhama Munivar" explains that Appoothi Adikal Nayanar possessed the firmness in that wisdom and was thus distinguished. Sekkizhar Peruman sings, "I begin to narrate the sacred service performed by Appoothi Adikal Nayanar, who shone as such a Bodhama Munivar, a great sage of wisdom." We saw that Sekkizhar Peruman sang about the character of Appoothi Adikal Nayanar and the history of his service in the Puranam of Thirunavukkarasu Nayanar, from verse 1465 to verse 1477. When narrating the Nayanar's sacred service in the history of Thirunavukkarasu Peruman, Sekkizhar Peruman expanded on the actions of Thirunavukkarasu Nayanar and briefly narrated the history of Appoothi Adikal Nayanar there. Now, in the upcoming Puranam of Appoothi Adikal Nayanar, Sekkizhar Peruman is going to narrate the sacred services performed by Appoothi Adikal Nayanar in great detail. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman, who sets the stage for the Puranam of Appoothi Adikal Nayanar in the concluding verse of Karaikkal Ammaiyar Puranam, now continues the Puranam of Appoothi Adikal Nayanar as the 25th Puranam in Periyapuranam. In this Sarukkam, Thirunindra Sarukkam, Sekkizhar Peruman begins to narrate the history of Appoothi Adikal Nayanar as the fifth Puranam. The section that describes the historical account and character of the Nayanar named Appoothi Adikal is the Appoothi Adikal Nayanar Puranam. Sekkizhar Peruman narrates the Puranam of Appoothi Adikal Nayanar in detail in 45 verses. Now, according to the order of Thogai Nool (summary text), Vagai Nool (classified text), and Viri Nool (detailed text), let us first look at the phrase composed by Sundaramurthy Swamigal about Appoothi Adikal in the Thogai Nool. "Oru Nambi Appoothi Adiyaarkkum Adiyen" is the utterance of Sundaramurthy Swamigal in Thiruthondathogai. Its meaning is, "I am a servant to the devotees of Appoothi Adikal, who is a Nambi." Here, Sundaramurthy Swamigal adds "um" (also) in "Oru Nambi Appoothi Adiyaarkkum." What does this mean? It means, "I am a servant to the devotees of Appoothi Adikal, in addition to the great personalities mentioned before." Furthermore, this "um" also conveys the meaning that "I am a servant not only to Appoothi Adikal but also to his devotees." Sundaramurthy Swamigal praises Appoothi Adikal Nayanar as "Oru Nambi" when he sings "Oru Nambi Appoothi Adiyaarkkum Adiyen." "Oru" means incomparable. "Nambi" means

the best among men. "Nambi" means one who possesses goodness, good qualities, is free from evil, and has no bad qualities. Therefore, the word "Nambi" means the best among men. For this reason, in earlier days, the custom of calling the bridegroom "Nambi" was prevalent in Tamil. Furthermore, there is also a historical note about Appoothi Adikal Nayanar here, stating that "Nambi" refers to one who firmly believed that the sacred feet of Thirunavukkarasu Nayanar were the only certainty. Here, Appoothi is the sacred name of the Nayanar. Thus, in the Thogai Nool, Sundaramurthy Swamigal sings "Oru Nambi Appoothi Adiyaarkkum Adiyen" with this meaning. Now, let us continue to contemplate the Nayanar's sacred history through the Vagai Nool and Viri Nool. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 02 — Appoothi Adikal Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Part 505, composed by Sekkizhar Swamigal. Thirunindra Sarukkam, Appoothi Adikal Nayanar Puranam. We saw the sacred utterance about Appoothi Adikal Nayanar by Sundaramurthy Swamigal in the Thogai Nool, Thiruthondathogai. Now, in the Vagai Nool, Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi, Nambiyandar Nambi composes the following verse about Appoothi Adikal Nayanar. This is the 29th verse in Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi. The verse is: "Dhanamavadhu Thirunavukkarasin saranam enna manamar punal pandhar vazhththi vaiththu aangavan vanthamizhkke inama thanathu peyar idapperravan engal piraan anamar vayal Thingaloorinil Vedhiyan Appoothiye." Its meaning is: Appoothi Adikal Nayanar is the one who, with a full heart, established water pandals and other charities, praising the sacred feet of Thirunavukkarasu Nayanar, believing that the imperishable great wealth is the sweet sacred feet of Thirunavukkarasu Nayanar. For this reason, Appoothi Adikal Nayanar's name is sung in the rich Tamil Devara sacred utterances of Thirunavukkarasu Peruman. Such Appoothi Adikal Nayanar is our leader. Appoothi Adikal Nayanar, the Brahmin who was born in Thingalur, surrounded by fields where swans reside. He is our leader. This is the meaning of that sacred verse. In this verse, it is stated that the sacred feet of Thirunavukkarasu Peruman are the imperishable great wealth. The meaning is that this is the great wealth, the fruit of having attained other wealth. The Nayanar's name and historical notes are shown in the Thogai Nool. The Nayanar's village, lineage, name, the history of his sacred services, and the fame he attained are all classified and shown in the Vagai Nool. These have been sung in detail by Sekkizhar Peruman in the Viri Nool, Periyapuranam. As we have already seen, the Nayanar's village, lineage, and some special characteristics were described in 13 verses, from the 200th to the 211th verse, in the Puranam of Thirunavukkarasu Nayanar. The rest are elaborated upon in this Puranam. What we saw earlier in the Puranam of Thirunavukkarasu were elaborated upon in relation to the history of Thirunavukkarasu Peruman. What is narrated here is elaborated upon mainly in relation to the history of Appoothi Adikal Nayanar. Therefore, the Nayanar's Puranam has been sung in detail both there and here, as appropriate for the context. Now, let us contemplate the Puranam of Appoothi Adikal Nayanar according to the Viri Nool, Periyapuranam. The first verse in the Puranam of Appoothi Adikal Nayanar is as follows: "Thandavam puriyavalla Thampiranarukku anbar Eendiya pugazhin paalar Ellaiyil thavathin mikkar Aanda seer arasinpatham adainthu avar ariya munne Kaandhagu kaadhal koora kalandha anbinaraai ullar." This is the first verse. Appoothi Adikal Nayanar was devoted to the sacred feet of Lord Shiva. He was devoted to Lord Shiva, who is skilled in performing the sacred dance that executes the five cosmic functions. This sacred dance of the five functions is unique to Lord Shiva. It cannot be performed by others. Moreover, it is a simple play for Lord Shiva. Appoothi Adikal Nayanar was devoted to the sacred feet of such a Lord Shiva. Sekkizhar Peruman mentions in this verse, "Thampiranarukku anbar" (devoted to the Lord). Sekkizhar Peruman, who had previously sung about the depth of Appoothi Adikal Nayanar's devotion to Thirunavukkarasu Nayanar in the Puranam of Thirunavukkarasu Nayanar, here, at the beginning of Appoothi Adikal Nayanar Puranam, first states how the Nayanar was devoted to Lord Shiva. Sivagnana Siddhiyar states, "Adiyavarkku anbu illar Eesanukku anbu illar" (those who have no love for devotees have no love for God). According to this,

Sekkizhar Peruman, who had previously sung about Appoothi Adikal Nayanar's love for Thirunavukkarasu Nayanar, now begins his Puranam by singing about Appoothi Adikal Nayanar's love for the Lord. Regarding this nature of Appoothi Adikal Nayanar, his greatness is praised in Thevaram as "Appoothi Kunjip Poovai Nindra Sevadiyai." Such Appoothi Adikal Nayanar was approached by fame. Fame sought him, rather than him seeking fame. Appoothi Adikal Nayanar was "Ellaiyil thavathin mikkar" (exceeding in boundless penance). He was exalted by immeasurable past penances. Here, "thavam" refers to past penance. Boundless penance. His penance was boundless in its greatness. The extent of his penance was also boundless, immeasurable. All those penances abounded in him. Hence, "thavathin mikkar." That is why it is stated here as the reason that he attained the feet of Thirunavukkarasu Swamigal and became devoted even before knowing him. That is, Appoothi Adikal Nayanar had immense love and devotion for Thirunavukkarasu Peruman even before seeing him directly with his eyes. Such love for Thirunavukkarasu Peruman was the cause of the Nayanar's historical outcome. Appoothi Adikal Nayanar was a person of such greatness. He had eradicated all faults such as theft, falsehood, lust, and anger. Theft means taking others' possessions. Falsehood is the opposite of truth. Anger is inappropriate rage. Lust is desire for evil things. Thus, Appoothi Adikal Nayanar had eradicated all faults, including theft, falsehood, anger, and lust. In the first verse, Sekkizhar Peruman praised all the good qualities that Appoothi Adikal Nayanar possessed. In the second verse, he sings about how Appoothi Adikal Nayanar was free from evil qualities. This is because even those who possess many good qualities may have a few evil qualities. But Appoothi Adikal Nayanar possessed many good qualities, and at the same time, he had eradicated all evil qualities. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman has sung in this manner. Furthermore, Sundaramurthy Swamigal praised Appoothi Adikal Nayanar as "Oru Nambi." We saw that "Nambi" there meant one who possesses good qualities and is free from evil qualities. Therefore, to elaborate on this, Sekkizhar Peruman first sings about Appoothi Adikal Nayanar's good qualities and then, secondly, sings about how he was free from evil qualities. Appoothi Adikal Nayanar lived a prosperous householder's life. Appoothi Adikal Nayanar had immense love and devotion for Thirunavukkarasu Peruman and was a leader in guru worship. Therefore, Appoothi Adikal Nayanar named all the items in his house after Thirunavukkarasu Nayanar. Appoothi Adikal Nayanar named all the measuring instruments in his house, such as 'padi' (a measure of capacity) and 'uzhakku' (a smaller measure), the linear measuring instruments used for measuring length (like one cubit, two cubits), and weighing scales, after Thirunavukkarasu Nayanar. Appoothi Adikal Nayanar also named his sons after Thirunavukkarasu Peruman. Thirukkural states, "Thakkar thagavilar enbadhu avaravar echchaththaal kaanappadum" (The worthy and the unworthy are known by their offspring). Accordingly, Appoothi Adikal Nayanar named his sons, who were like a scale to determine his worthiness, after Thirunavukkarasu Peruman. The custom of naming measuring instruments like 'uzhakku', 'padi', and scales was prevalent in those days.

As further evidence, Sivakavimani Ayya cites some examples from the inscriptions of the great King Rajaraja Chola. It is mentioned in the inscriptions of Rajaraja's time, "Datchinameru Vidangan ennum kallal niraiyeduththum Ponnadavaallan ennum kallal niraiyeduththum." Therefore, these inscriptions also show us the custom of naming measuring instruments as "Datchinameru Vidangan" and "Ponnadavaallan." Furthermore, Appoothi Adikal Nayanar even named the cows and buffaloes in his house after Thirunavukkarasu Peruman. The measuring instruments mentioned earlier are inanimate objects. The cows and buffaloes mentioned now are animate beings. Thus, Appoothi Adikal Nayanar named all inanimate and animate beings after Thirunavukkarasu Peruman. Appoothi Adikal Nayanar, who had such boundless devotion to Thirunavukkarasu Peruman, had never seen Thirunavukkarasu Peruman until then. Appoothi Adikal Nayanar had so much devotion to Thirunavukkarasu Peruman without ever seeing him. Even though he had not seen Thirunavukkarasu Peruman directly, he had heard everyone speak of Thirunavukkarasu Peruman's enduring greatness and the depth of his sacred service, and as a result, he had boundless devotion to Thirunavukkarasu Peruman. Appoothi Adikal Nayanar heard the devotees of Shiva speak of Thirunavukkarasu Peruman's greatness and thus developed devotion to the Peruman. Therefore, Shiva devotees are accustomed to speaking only of the grace of Lord Shiva and the greatness of Shiva devotees. They do not speak of other things. This is understood to be the conduct of devotees. "Ennai aanda nikarila vannangalum sittan sivan adiyarai seerattum thirangalumae sindhithu" (Thinking only of the incomparable ways in which He ruled me and the ways in which the Lord praises Shiva's devotees) is from Thirupallandu. Accordingly, hearing about the greatness of Lord Shiva, the greatness of Lord Shiva's devotees, and Lord Shiva's grace, and not hearing about other things, are the actions of devotees. Thus, Appoothi Adikal Nayanar, who had immense devotion to Thirunavukkarasu Peruman, performed many charities such as monasteries and water pandals in the name of Thirunavukkarasu Peruman. He continues to do so. Earlier, we saw that Appoothi Adikal Nayanar named all the measuring instruments, cows, and buffaloes in his house after Thirunavukkarasu Peruman. He did so due to his past penance and the general knowledge he had gained about Thirunavukkarasu Peruman. Here, he establishes monasteries, alms-houses, water pandals, etc., in the name of Thirunavukkarasu Peruman and lives by performing charities. This is due to his special knowledge about Thirunavukkarasu Peruman's devotion to Lord Shiva and the grace Lord Shiva bestowed upon Thirunavukkarasu Peruman. He lives by performing charities in the name of Thirunavukkarasu Peruman. The love that arose earlier grew and matured more and more as he heard more and more, leading to more and more devotional acts. According to Thirunavukkarasu Peruman's Thiruthandagam, "Munnam avanudaiya naamam kettal" (If one hears His name first), we can see how the Nayanar's sacred utterance explains the state where love grows and matures through hearing. Appoothi Adikal Nayanar names all the items in his house, including cows and buffaloes, after Thirunavukkarasu Peruman and calls them by that name. This is so that Appoothi Adikal, he himself, can benefit by repeatedly chanting the sacred name of Thirunavukkarasu Peruman. Here, he performs many charities in the name of Thirunavukkarasu Peruman. Why is this? It is so that others, like himself, may also benefit. Thus, we see the expanding nature of Appoothi Adikal Nayanar's love.

He writes the sacred name of Thirunavukkarasu Peruman in all the alms-houses he establishes, stating, "This is the charity of Thirunavukkarasu Peruman," and thus establishes alms-houses and performs charities. The charities performed by Appoothi Adikal Nayanar were endless and enduring, such as monasteries and water pandals. All these charities are enduring acts that yield the lasting benefit of attachment to Shiva's feet. The monasteries established by the Peruman were for devotees to stay overnight. The water pandal established by Appoothi Adikal Nayanar was for those who came in the sun to rest and quench their thirst. Similarly, he established roads, ponds, groves, paths, and water channels. Generally, monasteries for lodging, water pandals, roads, ponds, groves, etc., are called 'pasu punniyangal' (merits related to individual souls). These 'pasu punniyangal' grant pleasures like heaven. After granting them, they become the cause of rebirth. This is because one has to experience those good merits again, thus leading to future births. However, the monasteries and other acts established by Appoothi Adikal Nayanar were performed due to his devotion to Thirunavukkarasu Peruman, his guru, the sage Vageesa, and due to guru worship and devotion to Shiva. Therefore, these charities performed by Appoothi Adikal Nayanar, unlike those performed by others, are not 'pasu punniyangal' but transform into 'pathi punniyangal' (merits related to the Lord) and become the cause of Appoothi Adikal Nayanar's liberation. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman sings of them as "endless charities." Thus, it is true that what Appoothi Adikal Nayanar did was Shiva dharma, Pathi dharma. Let us continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 03 — Appoothi Adikal Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Part 506, composed by Sekkizhar Swamigal. Thirunindra Sarukkam, Appoothi Adikal Nayanar Puranam. In Thingalur, Appoothi Adikal Nayanar performs many charities. At this same time, our Thirunavukkarasu Peruman sets out from Thirunallur on a pilgrimage towards Thirupazhanam. Thirunavukkarasu Peruman proceeds to worship and perform service at Thirupazhanam, the sacred place of Lord Shiva, who is accompanied by Parvati Ammaiyar, who is like a young elephant, the daughter of the mountain king. Thus, Thirunavukkarasu Peruman, who is proceeding from Thirunallur to Thirupazhanam, passes through Thingalur, which is on the way. Thirupazhanam and Thingalur are sacred places near Thiruvaiyaru, near Thanjavur in Tamil Nadu. Thus, Thirunavukkarasu Peruman arrives near Thingalur on his way to Thirupazhanam. There, he arrives near the water pandal established by Appoothi Adikal Nayanar on the Thingalur road. The water pandal established by Appoothi Adikal Nayanar is located on a very important road, at a significant spot. Countless people are constantly traveling on that road, coming and going. Sekkizhar Peruman composes the following verse here: "Alavil sanam selavozhiya vazhikkaraiyil aruludaiyar ulamanaiya thannaliththaai uruvenil parivu agatri kulaniraindha neertthadam pol kulir thoongum parappinathaai valamaruvem nizhaltharu thanneer pandhar vanthu anainthaar." This sacred verse describes the characteristics of how water pandals should be established. Sekkizhar Peruman composes this verse in such a way that the excellence of the water pandal made by Appoothi Adikal Nayanar appears as a painting before our eyes. Not only that, Sekkizhar Peruman composes this verse so excellently that those who establish water pandals can read this verse, learn how to establish a water pandal, and set it up accordingly. First, one must choose a suitable and appropriate location for establishing a water pandal. A water pandal is a place where thirsty people and those who need shade after walking in the sun come. Therefore, this water pandal should be located on an important road, at a significant spot. It should be located on the path where people travel. It should not be located far away from the road. The water pandal should be easily identifiable to people. It should be easily accessible. Going to the beneficiaries and performing charity, and doing what they need without them even asking, are the specialties of charity. Here, our Lord Appoothi Adikal Nayanar has established that water pandal on that important road where crowds of people travel. He has established that water pandal in a very easily accessible place. He has established that water pandal so clearly and visibly that those traveling on that path cannot pass by without seeing it. People traveling at night is a common sight on major roads. Therefore, the water pandal should be set up in such a way that those traveling at night can stop and rest. Our Appoothi Adikal Nayanar has set it up in the same way. In addition, this water pandal should also serve as a place for those walking in the sun during the day to rest for a while. Therefore, Appoothi Adikal Nayanar has set up the water pandal accordingly. He has not set up this water pandal near his house but at an important location on the road. Such a water pandal, the one established by Appoothi Adikal Nayanar, is described by Sekkizhar Peruman with a beautiful simile: it is as cool as the hearts of compassionate people.

Who are the compassionate ones? Who are the merciful ones? Those who treat all beings with kindness are righteous people, Brahmins. Therefore, by alleviating the suffering of all beings, that water pandal shines with the cool serenity of the hearts of great, compassionate people. How does that water pandal compassionately remove suffering? All the items in that water pandal alleviate the suffering of those who reach it. It quenches thirst. It provides a place to rest. That water pandal is naturally cool. Therefore, it is compared to the hearts of great people. That water pandal is as cool as a pond that removes all the distress of heat during summer, and like a water-filled reservoir. A pond is a large natural body of water. A 'thadam' is a small pool filled with water from time to time. A pond helps crops. A 'thadam' helps people and other living beings. Thus, that water pandal is very cool. Not only water, but also 'panakam' (a sweet drink) is served during summer. Water mixed with jaggery is called 'panakam'. The custom of serving this jaggery-mixed 'panakam' in water pandals during summer has been prevalent. Therefore, that water pandal helps quench the thirst of travelers and provides shade to relieve their fatigue. That water pandal is set up in such a way that travelers cannot pass by without coming there. Therefore, our Thirunavukkarasu Peruman, who travels on pilgrimage without minding thirst, fatigue, etc., on the way, arrives near this water pandal with the desire to worship the Lord. Sekkizhar Peruman composes the next verse: "Vandhadaindha Vageesar mandhamarutha seethappandharudan amudhamam thanneerum paarththaruli sindhai viyappura varuvaar Thirunavukkarasu enum per sandhamura varaindhadhanai emmarungum thaam kandaar." Thirunavukkarasu Peruman, who arrived at that place, sees the cool pandal with gentle, cool breeze blowing, and the ambrosial water. Thirunavukkarasu Peruman, approaching with wonder in his sacred mind, sees the name "Thirunavukkarasar" written clearly on all sides of the water pandal. A very gentle, cool breeze blows near that water pandal. There is ambrosial water there. That water quenches the thirst caused by the summer heat and saves lives. Therefore, that water is ambrosial. Moreover, that water is ambrosial in taste as well. Thirunavukkarasu Peruman sees the air, shade, and water not as mere objects but with a divine insight. He is greatly amazed by that pandal. He is amazed by the pandal's excellences, structure, benefits, and utility. In addition, he sees the name "Thirunavukkarasar" written very clearly and largely there. He sees it written very clearly, largely, and prominently, so that this sacred name is visible from any direction. Just as devotees see the Lord in their minds, Appoothiyar sees Thirunavukkarasu Nayanar in his sacred mind. He also sees him in his water pandal. He has written the name "Thirunavukkarasar" there for others to see as well. Thirunavukkarasu Nayanar, who always forgets himself and remembers only Lord Shiva, sees himself there. Thirunavukkarasu Peruman asks, "Who established this water pandal and named it this?" Thirunavukkarasu Peruman asks this, thinking why this good person, who established this excellent charity of a water pandal, did not perform this charity in his own name but in another's name. All the people there knew that water pandal and its history. Therefore, they tell Thirunavukkarasu Peruman about this excellent service being performed by Appoothi Adikal Nayanar. Indeed, everything they said was not flattery; it was all truth. Thus, the people there tell Thirunavukkarasu Peruman about Appoothi Adikal Nayanar and his greatness. What a blessing these people had, to see and speak directly with our Thirunavukkarasu Peruman! Sekkizhar Peruman composes the verse: "Ippandhar ippeyarittu ingu amaiththaar yaar endraarkku appandhar arindhaargal aanda arasu enum peyaraal sepparunseer Appoothi Adigalaar seythu amaiththaar thappindri engumula saalai kulam ka endraar." This is the seventh verse in the Puranam of Appoothi Adikal Nayanar. All those people tell Thirunavukkarasu Peruman that the one who established this water pandal is the greatly renowned Appoothi Adikal. This sacred verse describes the mental state Appoothi Adikal Nayanar attained and the emotional expressions that appeared in him when he realized this great blessing that he suddenly received. It is stated here that he brought everyone, including relatives, to worship Thirunavukkarasu Peruman. Therefore, he also brought his household servants and others to worship there. Following 'paathyam', 'aasamanam' and 'arkkiyam' are the rituals in worship. Following these prescribed rituals, he first cleanses Thirunavukkarasu Peruman's feet with water, which is called 'paathyam'. Sekkizhar Peruman refers to this as 'malar punai neer' (water adorned with flowers). It is customary to cleanse the feet of the Lord and elders with water mixed with fresh flowers and fragrant substances for 'paathyam' and other rituals. Such water, used to cleanse the feet, becomes sacred water. Therefore, Appoothi Adikal Nayanar purified himself internally and externally with that sacred water. This is called 'pooriththal' (filling oneself with purity). Now the next verse. "Aasanaththil poosanaigal amarviththu viruppinudan vaasanirrai thiruneettruk kaappu endhi manam thazhaippa thesam uyya vandhavarai thiruvamudhu seivikkum nesamuravinnappam seyya avarum adhu nerndhaar." This is called 'aasanam samarppiththal' in the worship procedure. This is the first part of the worship procedure. This is the method of placing the deity on the seat with appropriate mantras and bhavanas. Thus, Appoothi Adikal Nayanar performs worship by seating him on the asana. Offering incense, lighting lamps, performing archana, etc., are all prescribed as parts of Maheswara Puja. Appoothi Adikal Nayanar performs them with love. After thus placing him on the asana and performing these rituals, he holds up the sacred ash casket. The fragrance of civet, green camphor, etc., mixed with the sacred ash, is present there. Along with that, the natural fragrance of sacred ash is also present. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman refers to this as 'vaasanirrai thiruneeru' (fragrant sacred ash). Thus, holding up the sacred ash casket before Thirunavukkarasu Peruman, Appoothi Adikal Nayanar, with overflowing love, requests Thirunavukkarasu Peruman, who has come for the salvation of the world, to partake of the sacred food. Bound by Appoothi Adikal Nayanar's love, Thirunavukkarasu Peruman also agrees. Now the next verse. "Sey thavar isaindha podhu thirumanaiyaarai nokki eydhiya peru nambaal irundhavaaru ennea endru maithigazh midattrinaan than arulinaal vandhathendre uyndhum endru vandhukondu thiruvamudhu aakkal utraar." Thirunavukkarasu Peruman is a great ascetic. Appoothi Adikal Nayanar considered his consent to partake of the sacred food as a great blessing. Thirunavukkarasu Peruman, the great ascetic, gracing his home was one blessing. These two great blessings were received by Appoothi Adikal Nayanar without any expectation. Therefore, he praises the grace of Lord Shiva, saying that these could not have been obtained without divine grace. Appoothi Adikal Nayanar is utterly devoid of any egoistic thought that this blessing was obtained as a result of his many days of meditation. Appoothi Adikal Nayanar praises the grace of Lord Shiva, saying that this great blessing occurred due to divine grace, without any egoistic thought that he meditated on Thirunavukkarasu Peruman for so long, and therefore Thirunavukkarasu Peruman has graced his home as a result of that meditation. We have already seen that Appoothi Adikal Nayanar was utterly devoid of ego, as he performed charities in the name of Thirunavukkarasu Peruman without writing his own name in the alms-houses he established. Now it is further confirmed. Therefore, Appoothi Adikal Nayanar considered that this blessing came through divine grace. This is because penance itself does not yield results. It is the Lord who must bring about the fruit of the penance performed, which is the truth of Jnana Sastras. In the Puranam of Sakkiya Nayanar, the subtlety of "seyvinaiyum adhan payanum serppanum" (the deed, its fruit, and the one who bestows it) is elaborately explained. "Thooya narkarigalana aruvagai suvaiyaal aakki aaya in amudhum aakki amudhu seythu arula thangal seyavar thammil mooththa Thirunavukkarasai vaazhai meya porkkuruththu kondu va ena viraindhu vittar." Vegetables, etc., used for Shiva Puja or Maheswara Puja, should be grown in pure soil. They should not be grown with impure manure, water, etc. Also, they should not be placed in impure vessels. Besides these, there are many other rules. All these are for the vegetables to be pure. Sekkizhar sings, "Thooya narkarigalal ana innamudhu" (sweet food made of pure good curries). To indicate that they should be according to all these rules, Sekkizhar Peruman mentions "Thooya narkarigalal ana." That is, just as those who consume nectar do not suffer from hunger and do not die, good food is referred to as "like divine nectar." "Vaazhai meya porkkuruththu" means a young banana leaf. That banana leaf should be large, long, and not fully mature. These are its meanings. Chapter 08 — Appoothi Adikal Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Part 511. Thiruneetru Sarukkam, Appoothi Adikal Nayanar Puranam. Appoothi Adikal Nayanar and his wife send their eldest son, the elder Thirunavukkarasu, to pluck a banana leaf shoot for Thirunavukkarasu Peruman to partake of the sacred food. This is the verse composed by Sekkizhar Peruman. In the first part of this verse, Sekkizhar Peruman shows what the elder Thirunavukkarasu thinks in his mind as he goes to pluck the banana leaf. The elder Thirunavukkarasu rejoices in his heart and goes quickly, thinking, "I have received this blessing to obey the command of my good mother and father and perform this task." He thinks his mother and father are good parents because they engaged him in this service to the Lord's devotees and guided him. Therefore, they are doing good for him. So, the elder Thirunavukkarasu rejoices, thinking his mother and father are good parents. Generally, the primary duty of parents is to guide their children to the right path. Among them, the mother is the foremost in guiding her child to the right path. "Annaiyum pithavum munnari deivam" (Mother and father are the first known deities) is a moral saying. We should reflect on why the mother is mentioned first and the father second. Although "Eva makkal moova marundhu" (Children who obey are an unfailing medicine) is also a moral saying, it is a general rule. A parent may have many children. In such cases, it is the duty of parents to understand the time, place, the children's qualifications, and everything else, and to nurture and guide each child according to their needs. It is the duty of children to fulfill the commands given by their parents and follow that path. The elder Thirunavukkarasu here feels proud and happy. This is because Appoothi Adikal Nayanar and his wife have many children, and among them, he, the elder Thirunavukkarasu, has received this opportunity. This is the kind of elevated pride, "Aththan enakku aruliyavaaru aar peruvaar achchuve" (Who else will receive the grace my Lord bestowed upon me?), as in Thiruvachagam. This is not the egoistic pride that arises from "I." This is pride related to the specialty of devotion to the Lord. This pride will weaken ego. The task assigned to him by his parents is to bring the serving leaf for Thirunavukkarasu Peruman to partake of the sacred food. It is the task of plucking the leaf that will be placed for serving the sacred food. No matter how excellent the sacred food items are, the banana leaf, which serves as the seat and base for the sacred food, is what helps. In the worship procedure, the asana mantra is recited first, and we can compare the idea that the asana mantra comes first here. Therefore, the elder Thirunavukkarasu rejoices, considering its importance. Thus, with various thoughts, he goes to the task of plucking the banana leaf, rejoicing in himself. How does he go? Sekkizhar Peruman sings, "Ollaiyil viraindhu" (quickly and swiftly). "Ollai" also means swiftness. He has used two words for swiftness, "ollaiyil viraindhu." This is to indicate that the child goes very quickly. He goes very quickly and enters the garden next to his house. This is a common arrangement in the Chola country. Having a garden next to the house is a common arrangement in the Chola country. This is because the parents of the elder Thirunavukkarasu send him to bring the banana leaf after the sacred food is cooked, so that the serving leaf, the banana leaf, does not wilt and remains fresh. The elder Thirunavukkarasu thus enters the banana garden and sees a beautiful banana tree with good, abundant growth, and its beautiful banana shoot. He decides that this is the best and begins to cut it. At that moment, a snake bit him on his hand. It bit him on his palm. "Vaal araa ondru" is mentioned in this verse. "Vaal" means light. The eyes of a snake are luminous. Some types of snakes in Sri Lanka have 'drishti visham' (poison by sight). That is, just by looking, poison enters those who are looked at. Similarly, the fangs of a snake are very luminous. Moreover, snakes have multi-colored upper and lower skins on their bodies. Such a snake was on that banana tree. The snake was on the shoot of the banana tree from which the elder Thirunavukkarasu tried to cut the leaf. So, when he tried to cut the banana leaf, the snake that was there bit the elder Thirunavukkarasu's hand. Poisonous creatures sometimes reside in banana leaves, and their venomous properties can be present in the leaves. That is why there has been a tradition of washing leaves with water before eating. Sekkizhar Peruman then composes what happens next in the following three verses. Sekkizhar Peruman sings what happened there in these three verses: "Kaiyinil kavarndhu sutri kan neri kaandhukindra pai aravu udhari veezhththu padaippudan paanthal patrum veyya vegaththaal veezha munnam vegaththaal eythi koydha ikkuruththai sendru koduppan endru odi vandhaan. Porundhiya vida vegaththil podhuvaan vegam muntha varundhiye anaiyum pozhudhu maasunnam kavarndhadhu yaarukkum arunthavar amudhu seyya thaazhkaiyan araiye endru thirundhiya karuththinodum sezhumanai sendru pukkaan. Erividam muraiye eri thalaikkonda ezhaam vegam therivura eyirum kannum meniyum karugi theendhu viriyurai kulari aavi vidak kondu mayangi veezhvaan parikkalak kuruththai thaayaar paal vaiththu padimel veezhndhaan." Sekkizhar Peruman sings about the actions of the elder Thirunavukkarasu in three verses: "vandhaan" (he came), "pukkaan" (he entered), "veezhndhaan" (he fell). The snake that bit the elder Thirunavukkarasu's hand did not stop there but coiled around his hand, with its eyes burning with anger. Just as the heat of a fire affects those near it, the heat of the angry fire from the snake's eyes blazed like a fiery wind. The snake bit the elder Thirunavukkarasu, thinking he was trying to harm it, and coiled around his hand. Moreover, its anger did not subside, and the fire of anger continued to blaze in its eyes. Generally, snakes bite out of fear and anger for self-defense. Otherwise, they do not usually bite. Their venomous fangs naturally fold back into the upper jaw. So, they are not prone to biting. When these snakes feel fear or anger, their folded venomous fangs straighten up. Then, through the hollow of their tube-like venomous fangs, the venom from the venom sac at the base of the fangs is injected into the bodies of others, causing harm with their sharp fangs. The venom from the venom sac at the base of the snake's fangs passes through the hollow fangs and enters the body of the bitten person through the puncture made by the bite. The venom then mixes with their blood and enters their system. This is the general characteristic of venomous snakes. Some snakes not only bite but also coil around. This is also a special characteristic of some types of venomous snakes. The elder Thirunavukkarasu, realizing that the snake had coiled around his hand, shook his hand very quickly. As he shook it, the snake slipped and fell down. Due to this sudden and rapid danger, the elder Thirunavukkarasu's body trembled. He became greatly agitated. Thinking, "Before I fall due to the terrible speed of the poison caused by the snakebite, I will quickly go and deliver this banana leaf I plucked," the elder Thirunavukkarasu runs very fast. He thinks, "Even if I am bitten by a snake, it doesn't matter. No obstacle should occur to Thirunavukkarasu Peruman's sacred service. So, I will go very quickly and give this banana leaf." Thus, he goes very quickly. His bravery, mental fortitude, determination in action, and depth of love are conveyed here. He goes faster than the speed of the poison. As he goes fast, his blood circulation increases greatly. This increases the severity and suffering of the poison mixed in his blood. Therefore, he starts running, thinking, "I will go and deliver it quickly before I fall." He also thinks that he will not tell his parents that he was bitten by a snake, and with that determination, he quickly enters the house. This is because if he tells his parents that he was bitten by a snake, they will try to treat him. That will cause delay. There will be a delay in Thirunavukkarasu Peruman partaking of the sacred food. That will result in a sin against Shiva. Therefore, he thinks, "That sin against Shiva should not happen because of me. So, I will not tell anyone about this," and with his mind focused on his love for the devotee, he quickly goes home. He resolves not to tell anyone, not just his parents, even in that situation. At the same time he enters the house, the poison in his body reached the seventh stage, as Sekkizhar Peruman mentions. He sings, "Erividam muraiye eri thalaikkonda ezhaam vegam therivura." Poison burns. What does it burn? It destroys the vitality from the seven dhatus (constituents) in the body. Sekkizhar Peruman refers to this as the seventh stage because it destroys the nature of these seven dhatus. The spread of poison through each dhatu is considered the first stage, second stage, and so on, and destroying the seventh dhatu is called the seventh stage. Sekkizhar Peruman mentions it here. The seven dhatus in the body are tvak (skin), rakta (blood), mamsa (flesh), meda (fat), asthi (bone), and the seventh dhatu, shukla (semen). These seven, starting from blood, are interconnected. The poison spreads in this interconnected manner. First, in the blood, then that blood circulation goes into the heart, and from there, the poison spreads throughout the body one, two, three times. Thus, each time it spreads, the poison spreads in each dhatu: skin, muscle, fat, bone, in each circulation. In the seventh stage, it reaches its peak. In each circulation, that is, in each round of blood circulation, one dhatu is destroyed, and thus, when the blood circulates seven times in the body, all seven dhatus are destroyed.

Due to the completion of the seven stages, all seven dhatus become toxic and turn black. That black color also begins to appear externally. In that seventh round, the teeth, eyes, and body begin to blacken due to the advanced poison, so a black color appears. After this, consciousness is lost, and life ends. Thus, the elder Thirunavukkarasu reaches the seventh stage. The state of nearing death has arrived. Even though the state of nearing death has arrived, the elder Thirunavukkarasu stops that life from leaving with his determination. He possesses such mental strength. He was determined and resolved that he must deliver the plucked banana leaf and hold onto his life until then. Thus, the elder Thirunavukkarasu, who entered the house, placed the banana leaf he had plucked with his mother and then, overcome by poison, collapsed. He fell to the ground. As soon as he gave the banana leaf to his mother, he fainted because the nature of the nerves and other dhatus supporting the body was destroyed. Therefore, his body fell down. It is said that he "placed" the banana leaf with his mother, not "gave" it. That was his action in that state as he was falling. So, he extends the hand holding the banana leaf towards his mother, unable to give it properly. He stumbles and falls before his mother can take the leaf. So, he places the leaf with his mother and then stumbles and falls. The elder Thirunavukkarasu falls to the ground, thinking that he has completed the task assigned to him by his parents. Therefore, similarly, it must be performed in Maheswara Puja. So, they clean the place with water mixed with cow dung. This cow dung is praised in Vedas and scriptures. This is because cow dung is the source for producing sacred ash. Cow dung is used for cleaning and purifying the temples where Lord Shiva resides. Not only that, cow dung is also one of the components of 'panchagavya' (five products of the cow). Therefore, they clean the place with water mixed with such special cow dung. They draw patterns with white chalk paste, that is, they draw patterns with dissolved flour. They light lamps. They light incense. Just as in Shiva Puja, incense and lamps are essential for Maheswara Puja. After making all these arrangements, they wash and clean the banana leaf thoroughly and place it there. Then, they place the leaf so that the side where the leaf is cut is to the right of the person eating. Here, Sekkizhar Peruman explains to our Shaiva devotees how Maheswara Puja should be performed. Sekkizhar Peruman describes through the actions of Appoothi Adikal Nayanar to explain and emphasize to the Shaiva devotees that cleaning the ground with cow dung water, drawing patterns, lighting lamps, preparing the serving plate, placing the serving plate, and offering good water for washing hands are all essential parts of Maheswara Puja in Shaiva tradition. Appoothi Adikal Nayanar offers good fragrant water for Thirunavukkarasu Peruman to wash his sacred hands. Washing hands before eating is a religious and ethical practice. It is also a practice of external purity. Moreover, the water used for washing hands and drinking should be good water. Water from springs, wells, rivers, and ponds would have been exposed to soil, air, etc. Other creatures would also have touched it. Therefore, all this water should be passed through sand, cloth, etc., to remove impurities. Then it should be placed in the sun to cool. These are the methods our ancestors discovered for purifying water. Not only that, fragrant roots like vetiver, flowers, green camphor, saffron, etc., should be added to that water. These are good substances that are not only for fragrance but also for medicinal properties. After Appoothi Adikal Nayanar does all this, Thirunavukkarasu Peruman washes his sacred hands with that good water and then, looking at Appoothi Adikal and his sons, graciously says, "You and your dear sons, please sit near me and partake of the sacred food." The compassionate gaze of Thirunavukkarasu Peruman looks at everyone with eyes of grace, bestows Shiva's grace, performs Shiva diksha, makes them Maheswaras, and settles there. Moreover, Thirunavukkarasu Peruman also addresses the sons as "dear sons." What is their dearness? They are special ones who are endowed with the quality of devotion to Shiva, befitting their parents. Therefore, they are praised by Thirunavukkarasu Peruman as "dear sons." Therefore, looking at their dearness, he first calls the sons and graciously says, "You and your sons, partake of the sacred food." Let us continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Part 515. Thirunindra Sarukkam, Appoothi Adikal Nayanar Puranam. As Thirunavukkarasu Peruman requested them to partake of the sacred food with him, Appoothi Adikal Nayanar and his sons sit with Thirunavukkarasu Peruman and partake of the sacred food. Along with them, all the devotees also join them, and Maheswara Puja takes place in Appoothi Adikal Nayanar's house. Appoothi Adikal Nayanar's wife serves everyone with great desire and devotion. At this point, Appoothi Adikal Nayanar is a Brahmin, and Thirunavukkarasu Peruman is a Vellalar. Therefore, some people will research this event of all of them eating together, focusing on caste, lineage, and so on. But all these researches are unnecessary and should be discarded. This history occurred based on the elevated religious discipline of the soul concerning love for Shiva. Therefore, there is absolutely no connection between these and ordinary worldly customs and researches about caste and lineage. Thus, in Appoothi Adikal Nayanar's sacred house, Thirunavukkarasu Peruman, Appoothi Adikal Nayanar, Appoothi Adikal Nayanar's sons, and other Shiva devotees performed Maheswara Puja and partook of the sacred food. Appoothi Adikal Nayanar, the great ascetic Brahmin, had performed great penances before. He is still performing great penances. Therefore, as a result of these penances, Appoothi Adikal Nayanar received the great blessing of guru's grace. Because of this, his house became a house of wealth.
