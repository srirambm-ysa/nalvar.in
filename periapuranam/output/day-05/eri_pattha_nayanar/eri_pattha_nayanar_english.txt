Chapter 01 — Eri-Pattha Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. We have contemplated the Thirumalai Sarga and Thillaivaazh Andhanar Sarga, the first two chapters of the Periya Puranam authored by Sekkizhar Swamigal, and now we enter the third chapter, the Ilaimalindha Sarga. According to the order of the Thiruthondathogai Thiruppathikam, its first song begins with "Thillaivaazh Andhanar tham adiyarkkum adiyen." The second song begins with "Ilaimalindha vel nambi Eripaththarkku adiyen." Sekkizhar Swamigal has elaborated this third chapter in the Periya Puranam as the chapter corresponding to the second song of the Thiruthondathogai Thiruppathikam. Since this song starts with "Ilaimalindha vel nambi," Sekkizhar has named this chapter "Ilaimalindha Sarga." Let us now look at that second song of the Thiruthondathogai. "Ilaimalindha vel nambi Eripaththarkku adiyen, Enaathi Naathan than adiyarkkum adiyen, Kalaimalindha seer nambi Kannapparkku adiyen, Kadavooril Kalayandhan adiyarkkum adiyen, Malaimalindha thol vallal Maanakkangchaaran enjaadha vaattaayan adiyarkkum adiyen, Alaimalindha punal mangai Aanaayarkku adiyen, Aarooran Arooril Ammanukku aale." This is the second song of the Thiruthondathogai. In this song, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal has mentioned and sung about seven Nayanmars: Eripaththa Nayanar, Enaathi Naatha Nayanar, Kannappa Nayanar, Kungiliya Kalaya Nayanar, Maanakkangchaara Nayanar, Arivattaaya Nayanar, and Aanaaya Nayanar. Therefore, in this Ilaimalindha Sarga, Sekkizhar Swamigal has individually sung the puranas of these seven Nayanmars. Among these seven Nayanmars, the purana of Eripaththa Nayanar is presented as the first. So, we are now going to contemplate the purana of Eripaththa Nayanar. As usual, let us first contemplate the summary of Eripaththa Nayanar's purana, and then we can contemplate it in detail. Among the seven Thevara Paadal Petra Sthalams located in the Kongu region, Karuvoor is a significant Thiruthalam. Is it not currently known as Karur near Tiruchirappalli? This city of Karur was formerly known as Karuvoor. In this Karuvoor, Lord Shiva is enshrined in a temple with the name Pasupatheeswarar. This temple is called Thiru Aanilay. The Nayanar whom we are about to see, Eripaththa Nayanar, is a devotee who regularly worships the Lord in this temple and serves His devotees. Eripaththa Nayanar was full of Saiva discipline. Do you know what service he performed? If any obstacles or sufferings came to Shiva's devotees, he would appear like a lion and remove those obstacles and sufferings that befell the devotees. To assist him, he always carried a mazu aayudham, an axe, in his hands. In this holy city of Karuvoor, there lived a virtuous Brahmana sage named Sivakami Aandaar. This sage, Sivakami Aandaar, who had immense love for the Lord, lovingly performed the service of offering flower garlands to the Lord. Our devotee, Sivakami Aandaar, lovingly performed the service of offering flower garlands according to the rules prescribed in Saiva scriptures on how to offer flower garlands to the Lord. One day, as usual, he woke up early, bathed, and went to the Nandavanam (flower garden). After going to the Nandavanam, he tied his mouth, picked fresh flowers for the Lord, filled them in the flower baskets he carried, and then carried those flower baskets on a koozhil (a long pole) with great love, heading towards the temple to offer flower garlands to Pasupatheeswarar, who is enshrined in the Thiru Aanilay temple in Karuvoor. That day was the day before Maha Navami. The king who ruled Karuvoor was King Pugazhchozhar. He was also a great devotee of Shiva. This Pugazhchozhar is also one of the Nayanmars. His history comes later, as a separate purana in the latter part of the Periya Puranam. But here, in the purana of Eripaththa Nayanar, Pugazhchozha Nayanar also appears. The devotee Sivakami Aandaar is carrying the flowers and baskets and going to the temple. At that time, King Pugazhchozhar's royal elephant, after bathing in the river, is joyfully walking through the street, exuding mada (rut). Two mahouts are riding on the elephant, and three parikkol kaarar (men with ankus) are running in front and on both sides of the elephant, guiding it. This royal elephant, as it went, followed Sivakami Aandaar, who was walking slowly in front of it, snatched the flower basket carried on his koozhil, and scattered the flowers on the ground. Seeing this, the mahouts quickly drove the elephant away. Sivakami Aandaar, seeing his flower basket snatched and the flowers scattered on the ground, was distressed. He ran after the elephant and tried to hit it with his staff. But the elephant quickly moved away. Sivakami Aandaar was a very old devotee, wasn't he? So, unable to run after the elephant, he fell to the ground, hit the ground with his hand, and with great sorrow, cried out to the Lord, "Sivada! Sivada!" lamenting that the flowers meant for the Lord had been scattered. Eripaththa Nayanar, who was coming from the opposite direction, witnessed this. He became very angry. He asked Sivakami Aandaar, "Where has that elephant gone that caused trouble to a devotee like you? I will cut it down." He held his mazu (axe) and asked Sivakami Aandaar where it had gone. Sivakami Aandaar replied that the elephant had gone this way. Immediately, the enraged Eripaththar declared, "Let me see how that elephant remains alive!" He rose swiftly like a great fire and a great wind, and like a lion, he approached the elephant. The enraged Eripaththa Nayanar, with the mazu (axe) he held in his hand, cut off the elephant's trunk and threw it to the ground. After that, he killed the two mahouts and the three parikkol kaarar, a total of five people. This was done by our Eripaththa Nayanar. Some people who witnessed this event ran and reported it to the guards at the king's palace. Hearing this, the king became very angry. He rose in anger and set out. The king mounted a horse and reached the spot where the elephant and the mahouts lay dead. He thought that some enemies had done this to his royal elephant. But when he reached the spot, there were no enemies. Instead, he saw Eripaththa Nayanar, a devotee of Shiva, holding an axe. He thought, "This is a devotee of Shiva. Therefore, he would not have killed the elephant without reason." He loudly asked him, "Who did this?" Immediately, those nearby said that Eripaththar, who held the axe, had killed the elephant. Immediately, King Pugazhchozha Nayanar thought to himself, "Devotees of Shiva would only kill if someone committed a mistake or a wrong. Therefore, the elephant must have committed a wrong." With this thought, he approached Eripaththa Nayanar. The king humbly asked Eripaththa Nayanar, "O devotee, what mistake occurred that made you so angry?" He further added, "Is killing the elephant and its mahouts sufficient atonement for the elephant's crime? Please grant your grace." The king stood bowing before Eripaththa Nayanar. Hearing this, Eripaththar said, "O King, this elephant snatched the Thiruppallithamam (garland) that the devotee Sivakami Aandaar was bringing to offer to the Lord and scattered it on the ground. That is why I cut it down. Even after seeing the elephant doing such an improper act, its mahouts did not try to stop it. Therefore, I killed them too. This is what happened here," said our Eripaththa Nayanar. Hearing this, King Pugazhchozha Nayanar thought, "The atonement for this sacrilege committed against a Shiva devotee cannot end here. This alone is not enough. I too must be killed. But I am not worthy of being killed by the auspicious mazu (axe) in your holy hands." Saying this, King Pugazhchozhar drew his sword and extended it to Eripaththa Nayanar. Eripaththa Nayanar, seeing this, was frightened and thought, "Oh, I am ruined! Only now have I understood the immeasurable love of this King Pugazhchozhar." He was deeply moved. He did not have the heart to accept the sword offered by the king. However, fearing that the king might harm himself if he did not accept the sword, he took the sword from the king's hand. As soon as Eripaththar took the sword from the king's hand, the king became very happy. King Pugazhchozha Nayanar rejoiced, "I have received the great fortune of having him kill me with my own sword and thus atone for my fault." Seeing this, Eripaththa Nayanar was even more frightened. Reflecting on Pugazhchozha Nayanar's boundless devotion to Shiva, he thought, "Even though I killed his elephant and mahouts, after I killed them, he offers his sword and asks me to kill him too. I have thought ill of this King Pugazhchozha Nayanar, who possesses such great love!" Eripaththa Nayanar was deeply distressed and said, "As an atonement for this, the only solution is for me to take my own life." Saying this, Eripaththa Nayanar placed the sword he had taken from the king on his own neck and tried to kill himself. Seeing this, King Pugazhchozha Nayanar was greatly frightened. He rushed to Eripaththa Nayanar and held both the sword in his hand and his hand itself, standing there in great sorrow. At that moment, a divine voice from the sky (Asareeri) was heard, resolving all the troubles that had occurred. The Asareeri said, "O devoted servants, the act of the elephant scattering the flowers happened only to show the world the greatness of your devotion. This happened by divine grace."

The Asareeri spoke. After this, the royal elephant and the mahouts came back to life. Eripaththa Nayanar dropped the sword and prostrated at the feet of Pugazhchozha Nayanar. Pugazhchozha Nayanar threw the sword away and praised and worshipped the feet of Eripaththa Nayanar. Both of them stood up and praised the divine voice that had emerged as an Asareeri. The flowers in Sivakami Aandaar's flower basket were again filled. Seeing this, Sivakami Aandaar also stood overflowing with joy. At the request of Eripaththa Nayanar, Pugazhchozha Nayanar mounted the royal elephant, which had come back to life, and proceeded to his palace. Sivakami Aandaar went to the temple for the Lord's service. Eripaththa Nayanar, marveling at the devotees of Shiva, continued his service. Thus, Eripaththa Nayanar, who performed his service for many years, finally attained the blessing of becoming a Gananayaka in Thirukailai. In this way, Sekkizhar Swamigal concludes this purana, saying, "Who else but the Lord can measure the greatness of Eripaththa Nayanar?" This is the summary of the purana of Eripaththa Nayanar. Now, according to our tradition, we will extensively contemplate this history through the verses found in the Thiruthondathogai, Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi, and then the Periya Puranam, which contains the purana of Eripaththa Nayanar. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 02 — Eri-Pattha Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. In the Periya Puranam, in the third chapter known as Ilaimalindha Sarga, we are going to contemplate the purana of Eripaththa Nayanar. First, let us contemplate the phrase sung by Sundaramoorthy Swamigal about this Nayanar in the Thiruthondar Thogai, which is a collection of verses. "Ilaimalindha vel nambi Eripaththarkku adiyen" is from the Thiruthondathogai. Its meaning is, "I am a servant to Eripaththa Nayanar, the Nambi (excellent man) who holds the mazu weapon, which is renowned like a leaf-shaped spear." Eripaththa Nayanar always carried an axe, a mazu weapon, in his hand. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal here refers to that mazu weapon, the axe, as "Ilaimalindha vel." The word "Nambi" refers to an excellent man among men. Therefore, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal sings, "Ilaimalindha vel nambi Eripaththarkku adiyen." Now, let us look at the verse composed by Nambiyandar Nambigal in the Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi, which is a genre of literature. "Oor madhil moonru attra uththamarukku endru or uyar thavaththon thar malar koyya varubavan thandin malar pariththa oor malai merkolum paagar udal thuni aakkum an ner mali mamadhil soozh Karuvooril Eripaththane." This is the verse sung by Nambiyandar Nambigal about Eripaththa Nayanar in the Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi. Now, let us contemplate the purana of Eripaththa Nayanar in the Periya Puranam, which is an elaborate work, through the sacred verses of Sekkizhar Swamigal. The title given by Sekkizhar Peruman to this purana is "Eripaththa Nayanar Puranam." In this Ilaimalindha Sarga, the historical accounts of seven Nayanmars are narrated. Among them, the purana of Eripaththa Nayanar is presented first. This purana is a section that describes the history and characteristics of Eripaththa Nayanar. Sekkizhar Swamigal begins the purana of Eripaththa Nayanar as follows: "Although the greatness of Eripaththa Nayanar, who is renowned for making it his sacred duty to remove any suffering that befalls the devotees of Lord Shiva in this water-surrounded world, is beyond my words, I begin to narrate his purana out of a desire to do so." Thus, Sekkizhar Swamigal begins to narrate the purana of Eripaththa Nayanar. We contemplated seven puranas in the second chapter, didn't we? At the end of each purana, Sekkizhar Swamigal would conclude that purana and introduce the next Nayanar to begin the next purana. It was Sekkizhar Swamigal's tradition, as we saw, to conclude the history of a Nayanar in an Upasangara (concluding) manner at the end of each purana and then introduce the history of the next Nayanar. However, in the previous chapter, the Thillaivaazh Andhanar Sarga, the purana of Amarneethi Nayanar was the concluding purana. That chapter ended with the purana of Amarneethi Nayanar. Therefore, there was no introduction to the history of the next Nayanar there. Eripaththa Nayanar, the next Nayanar, was not introduced there. So, now, Sekkizhar Swamigal, who is about to begin the purana of Eripaththa Nayanar in this new third chapter, the Ilaimalindha Sarga, introduces it at the beginning of this purana and sings this verse as the first one.

Lord Shiva is one who comes riding on a Mazhai Vidai. Mazhai Vidai means a bull that is eternally youthful. The Rishabha vahana (bull mount) on which the Lord rides is the embodiment of Dharma. Therefore, it does not experience old age. That is why the Lord's Rishabha vahana is called Mazhai Vidai, meaning the bull vehicle, the embodiment of Dharma, which is eternally youthful. Our Lord is one who comes riding on such a vehicle. If any obstacle arises for His devotees anywhere, at any time, Eripaththa Nayanar will appear there immediately and remove that suffering. This is the nature and characteristic of Eripaththa Nayanar. Sekkizhar Peruman says that he cannot express the purana of such an Eripaththa Nayanar in words. The fame of Eripaththa Nayanar is beyond words. Therefore, even though he cannot fully describe his fame, he begins to narrate his purana out of a desire to do so. When desire becomes strong, one does not think whether the task can be done or not. Even if the task is impossible, the desire that arises in us will make us strive for it. Generally, desire is something to be avoided and discarded. But what is the desire that Sekkizhar Peruman had here? It is the desire to narrate the purana of Eripaththa Nayanar, a devotee of Shiva. This is not like other desires; it is a desire that is cherished by the wise and the learned. That is why Sekkizhar Swamigal says, "Aasaiyal sollalutren" (I began to narrate out of desire). To encourage us to desire such Shiva Punya (meritorious deeds), Manikkavasagar Peruman has blessed us with a whole pathikam called "Aasaippatthu" in Thiruvachagam. "Nin pala adiyaar koottam aththa kaana aasaippatten kandaay ammanae" (O Lord, I desired to see your many devotees) is from Thiruvachagam. This desire that arose in Sekkizhar Peruman was a desire that arose with the intention of guiding the world. Thus, Sekkizhar Swamigal begins the purana of Eripaththa Nayanar. The Chola Emperor Anabaya Chola Chakravarti was the reason for Sekkizhar Swamigal to compose the Periya Puranam. This Chola lineage, from which Anabaya Chakravarti emerged, is a very glorious Chola lineage. These Cholas were powerful and famous, having led armies up to the Himalayas, expanded their kingdom to the Himalayas, and inscribed their tiger emblem on the summit of the Himalayas. The Chola lineage produced countless famous kings, from Karikal Chola to Anabaya Chola. Anabaya Chola patronized Sekkizhar Peruman. Therefore, Sekkizhar, who came to sing the glory of this Chola lineage, praised the ancestors of Anabaya Chola and then praised the Chola lineage up to Anabaya Chola, who was Sekkizhar Swamigal's contemporary king. Anabaya Chola was the great king who was instrumental in the creation of the Periya Puranam. The Saiva world flourished because of the Periya Puranam, didn't it? Therefore, Sekkizhar Swamigal praises Anabaya Chola in 11 places in this Periya Puranam for his great service to the Saiva world by having Sekkizhar Peruman compose this Periya Puranam. This place is one of those 11 places. The Chola kings, who possessed such greatness, had five capital cities where they performed their coronations. They are Kaveri Poombattinam (Poompuhar), Thiruvarur, Uraiyur, Sengalur, and Karuvoor. Thus, Karuvoor is one of the five capital cities where the Chola kings were crowned. This Karur is currently known as Karur. It is located near Tiruchirappalli. This Karur is one of the seven Thevara Paadal Petra Sthalams in the Kongu region. This Thiruthalam of Karur appears in many places in the Periya Puranam. Similarly, references to Thevara Paadal Petra Sthalams and their details appear in many places in the Periya Puranam. Sivakkavimani, who wrote a commentary on this Periya Puranam, would elaborate on the special features of any Thevara Sthalam the first time it was mentioned in the Periya Puranam, even if it was mentioned multiple times later. Following that tradition, we too, when we encounter a Thiruthalam for the first time in the Periya Puranam, contemplate its Sthala Puranas and Sthala Viseshangal (special features) as much as possible before continuing with the purana. For example, in the purana of Amarneethi Nayanar, the Thirunallur Thiruthalam appeared. Later, in the history of Thirunavukkarasu Swamigal in the Periya Puranam, the Thirunallur Sthalam appears. Nevertheless, since the reference to Thirunallur first appeared in the purana of Amarneethi Nayanar, we contemplated the Sthala Purana of Thirunallur before contemplating the purana of Amarneethi Nayanar. Similarly, now, in the purana of Eripaththa Nayanar, the reference to Karur (Karuvoor) appears for the first time. Therefore, we will contemplate the Sthala Purana of this Karur (Karuvoor) Thiruthalam before continuing with the purana of Eripaththa Nayanar. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 03 — Eri-Pattha Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. We are contemplating the purana of Eripaththa Nayanar in the third chapter of Periya Puranam, the Ilaimalindha Sarga. The place where Eripaththa Nayanar was born is the holy place called Karuvoor. It is currently called Karur. This city of Karur is a metropolis near Tiruchirappalli in Tamil Nadu. The Sthala Mahatmyam (glory of the place) of Karur is described in the 109th chapter of the Sivakshetra Kanda of the Brahma Vaivarta Purana, one of the 18 Mahapuranas, as revealed by Thiru Nandi Devar to Sanatkumara Maharishi, by Sanatkumara Maharishi to Vedavyasa Muni, and by Vedavyasa to Suta Puranika. Among the seven Thevara Paadal Petra Sthalams of the Kongu region, this holy place called Karur is one. Karur is blessed with the Thevara Thiruppathikam songs of Thirugnanasambandha Swamigal. The glory of Karur is mentioned not only in the Puranas but also in Sangam literature. The glories of Karur are sung and described in texts such as Brahmanda Purana, Bhavishya Purana, Brahma Vaivarta Purana, Narasinga Purana, Tholkappiyam, Silappathikaram, Purapporul Panniru Padalam, Purananooru, Agananooru, Patittrupattu, Pattuppattu, Periya Puranam, Agasthiya Bhakta Vilasam, Upamanyu Bhakta Vilasam, Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi, Thiruthondar Purana Saram, and Thevaram. However, the Karur Mahatmyam we are about to see describes the glories mentioned in chapters 108 and 109 of the Sivakshetra Kanda of the Brahma Vaivarta Mahapurana. As we have already seen, this Karur Mahatmyam was taught by Lord Shiva to Thiru Nandi Devar, and from Thiru Nandi Devar to the Naimisaranya Munis, this purana was narrated. A poet has sung this purana in Tamil as Karuvoor Puranam. This Karur has many sacred names such as Karuvoor, Garbhapuri, Kovoor, Aavoor, Paarkkarapuram, Shanmangala Kshetram, Aadhimapuram, Karapuram, Veerasozhapuram, Vanjularanyam, Vanjivanam, Vanji, Poova Vanji, Vaada Vanji, Thaayoor, Mukundha Nagaram, Kadambu Erindhaan Pathi, and Brahmapuram. The reasons for these sacred names are as follows. During the great deluge, this place held all the mobile and immobile objects within itself like a fetus (karu) and manifested them again at the beginning of creation. Therefore, it is called Garbhapuri in Sanskrit and Karuvoor in Tamil. Since Brahma Devar came here in every Kalpa (cosmic cycle), performed penance, and used the footprints of mobile and immobile objects in the southeast direction of this place as a guide for his creation work, it is named Karppapuri. Since Kamadhenu in Swarga worshipped Aadhi Nayagar and received grace to perform the act of creation, it is named Kovoor and Aavoor. The Lord enshrined here is Pasupatheeswarar. Even though Pasupatheeswarar is prominently enshrined here, since Vishnu Murthy, the tall one who measured the seven worlds, is in a state of deep sleep (Harithuyil) here, it is also named Paarkkarapuram. Since it possesses six auspicious elements: mountain, forest, river, sacred water (theertham), city, and temple, it is named Shanmangala Kshetram. Since it appeared first before all other holy places in the world, it is named Aadhimapuram. Since it absorbs within itself many holy places, sacred waters, and idols created by Pasupatheeswarar Peruman, it is named Karapuram. Since it was rebuilt by a king named Veerasozhan, it is named Veerasozhapuram. Since it is a forest filled with Vanji trees, it is named Vanjularanyam, Vanjivanam, and Vanji.

Since it is a Vanji that does not blossom and does not wither, it is named Poova Vanji and Vaada Vanji. Since Soundarya Nayaki Ammaiyar, the Universal Mother, worshipped here, it is named Thaayoor. It is the city ruled by Musukundha Chakravarti, who was born in the lineage of King Harichandra and received the grace of Lord Subramanya. Therefore, it is named Musukundha Nagaram. Once, a king who ruled Karuvoor cut down a Kadappa tree that had grown out of deceit in the middle of the ocean and removed the suffering. Therefore, it is named Kadambu Erindhaan Pathi. Since countless Brahma Devas worshipped here, it is named Brahmapuram. The boundary of this Karuvoor is five krosha (a measure of distance) in all four directions from the Thiru Aanilay temple where Pasupatheeswarar Peruman is enshrined. This is the boundary of this holy place. Krosha means a calling distance. The all-powerful Shiva Paramapoorna (Supreme Being) is enshrined in countless Shiva Sthalams. Among them, 1008 Shiva Sthalams are very excellent. Among these 1008 Shiva Sthalams, Thirukailai, Mandiragiri, Madurai, Kasi, Kanchi, Prayagai, Naimisaranyam, Sethu, and Karuvoor are nine very special places. Among these nine places, Karuvoor is the most special. We can obtain the benefits received in the other eight Sthalams by merely having a darshan of Karuvoor. If we sincerely worship Karuvoor Pasupathi Peruman for nine days, all the sins we have committed in all our previous births will be eradicated. We will attain liberation (mukti). Shiva Sthalams have a name. The temple within those Shiva Sthalams has a unique name, as we have seen before. Similarly, the name of this place is Karuvoor or Karur. The Lord enshrined here is Pasupatheeswarar. The name of the temple where he is enshrined is Aanilay. The sands and stones within a five-krosha radius around this temple are all in the form of Shivlingams. All the water here is like the Akasha Ganga. All the trees are Kalpaka trees. All the people living in this place are sages excellent in penance. Even playful actions performed here are considered as Yagas (sacrifices). All food consumed in this Karuvoor is like an offering (Naivedyam) to Bhagavan. All words spoken here are the sounds of Vedas and Shivagamas. Sleeping in this place should be considered as attaining Samadhi. Those who say these are false will fall into hell. Those who visit this place will be freed from evil deeds. Those born in this Karuvoor will attain Shiva Sarupyam (form of Shiva). Those who die in this Karuvoor will attain the feet of Shiva. The benefits attained by those who perform Shiva Punya (meritorious deeds) such as puja and utsavam according to Shivagama rules here are immeasurable. Each act of mantra japa, receiving initiation (deeksha), Shraddha, charity, penance, purana reading, yaga, and tarpana will multiply a millionfold. If there is no rain, if 100 Aadi Saiva Brahmanas are invited, made to bathe in the Amravati river, made to chant Rudra Namaha from sunrise to 15 nazhigai (a unit of time) in the sanctum of the Thiru Aanilay temple, perform abhishekams and archanas to the Lord, and offer food to the Brahmanas, then rain will surely fall. Those who build mathams (monasteries), chathirams (rest houses), sacred ponds, and perform temple services here will attain liberation from rebirth. Those who apply fragrant substances in the morning and those who witness the festival held in the month of Panguni will attain both worldly pleasures and liberation. The Mahapurana states that this town is free from sin. Those who visit Karuvoor will attain all worldly and otherworldly benefits such as freedom from disease, victory over enemies, wealth, progeny, Shiva Gnanam (divine knowledge), education, and eloquence. In this place, Parashakti, Kamadhenu, Vishnu, Brahma, Vyasa Muni, Indra, Agni, Yama, Durjaya, Varuna, Vayu, Kubera, Ishana, the Vedas, Lord Subramanya, Thadagai, Chera, Chola, and Pandya kings, the Navagrahas, Musukundha Chakravarti, countless Lakshmi Devis, Saraswati Devis, celestial maidens, Rishis, a hunter named Vibandagan, Sivakami Aandaar, Pugazhchozha Nayanar, Eripaththa Nayanar, Karuvoor Devar, and many others worshipped and attained liberation. If the kings ruling Karuvoor rule unjustly, and as a result the world suffers, it is believed that termites will climb on the Shivlingam here. The special features of this place include the Ilanthai Vanam where Vyasa Muni lived, Gopithara Vanam, Arasa Vanam, Vanji Vanam, Vennai Malai, Brahmaya Kundam, the Agni Kundam where Pugazhchozhar attained liberation, Thirumukkoodal where three rivers converge, and the footprints of mobile and immobile objects in the southeast direction. Those who have seen this Karuvoor will not see another Karuvoor. Therefore, if we have darshan of the Lord in this Karuvoor, we will not experience the suffering of entering another womb; we will not get it. Its specialty is that there is no rebirth. Now, the glories of the Theerthams (sacred waters). Although all the waters in this town are Theerthams, there are six special Theerthams. They are Ambra Nadi, Dhenu Theertham, Muruga Theertham, Arasa Theertham, Thadagai Theertham, and Brahma Theertham. First, Ambra Nadi. This is currently known as Amravati. Once, when there was no rain here and living beings suffered, Lord Shiva asked Uma Devi to let her divine grace flow here in the form of a river. Therefore, the divine grace of Uma Ammaiyar flowed as a river from the Varaha Malai nearby. In this Varaha Malai, countless munis were performing penance in the form of mango trees. Since this river flowed through such mango trees, it was named Ambra Nadi. This river is the divine grace of Uma Ammaiyar. Therefore, it is also named Amba Nadi. It also has the name Suta Nadi. Amram and Sutam both mean mango tree. Next, Dhenu Theertham. This is located to the east of Vennai Malai. Kamadhenu, as per the words of Narada Muni, worshipped a Mahalingam that appeared from the netherworld under an anthill on the banks of the Ambra Nadi and received the grace and boon to create all mobile and immobile beings through that worship. The Theertham created by Kamadhenu at that time is called Dhenu Theertham. Those who bathe in this Dhenu Theertham on the full moon day of the Vaikasi month will attain liberation. Next, Muruga Theertham. This is located to the west of Ambra Nadi in Gopithara Vanam. Since it was created by Lord Muruga, it is named Muruga Theertham. If one bathes in this Muruga Theertham on a full moon day in the Chithirai month when a Sunday and Swathi star coincide, and gives charity as much as possible, Yama will not approach them. Next, Arasa Theertham. This is located on the banks of the Ambra Nadi, next to an Arasa tree in the Arasa Vanam. It was created by Vyasa Muni. Those who bathe in this Theertham while chanting the Gayatri Mantra will attain the abode of Vishnu and experience pleasures. Next, Thadagai Theertham. This is a well located in the eastern direction inside the temple where Pasupatheeswarar Peruman is enshrined. A demoness named Thadagai created this Theertham and worshipped, and as a result, she bore a son named Mareesan. Next, Brahma Theertham. This Theertham was created by Brahma Devar, who came here and performed penance seeking the power of creation. Those who bathe in this Brahma Theertham on the days of Chandra Grahanam (lunar eclipse), Athodayam, Mahodayam, Pournami (full moon), Shraddha Dinam, and Vyathipatham will be freed from all their sins and attain liberation. Besides these, there is a Theertham called Thirumukkoodal. This Mukkoodal is the place where three rivers, Amravati Nadi, Manimuttha Nadi, and Kaveri Nadi, converge. Those who bathe in this Kaveri Sangamam, where Amravati joins the Kaveri river, will receive the benefits of bathing in all the Theerthams in all the holy places.

The unique feature of this place is that it encompasses six types of auspiciousness. This is why it is called Shanmangala Kshetram. The first auspiciousness is mountains. This holy place has mountains called Varaha Malai, Vennai Malai, and Thaan Thondri Malai. Among these, Varaha Malai is where Varaha Muni, born in the Kashyapa Gotra, performed yoga from this mountain by the command of Lord Shiva. Therefore, it is named Varaha Malai. It is also called Yogagiri. This is also mentioned in the Palani Sthala Purana. Next is Vennai Malai. It is called Navaneethagiri. This is a special Subramanya Sthalam blessed by the Thiruppugazh songs of Arunagirinathar. Lord Subramanya is enshrined here as Dandapani. Since Kamadhenu created all mobile and immobile beings and gathered divine nectar for their sustenance, making it like butter, this mountain is named Vennai Malai. Next is Thaan Thondri Malai. This mountain appeared on its own. Therefore, it is named Thaan Thondri Malai. This is a Subramanya Sthalam located southeast of Pasupatheeswaram. Currently, it is a Vishnu Sthalam. The second auspiciousness is Aranyam (forest). This place has four forests: Vanjivanam, Gopitharavanam, Arasavanam, and Ilanthaivanam. Vanjivanam is also called Vanjularanyam, Arasavanam as Pippilaranyam, and Ilanthaivanam as Badarivanam or Badarivanam. The third auspiciousness is Nadi (river). The Amravati, also known as Ambra Nadi, is the river here. The fourth auspiciousness is Theertham (sacred water). We have already seen the glory of the Theerthams. The fifth auspiciousness is Nagaram (city). This city has been a great capital city with magnificent houses and mansions. The sixth auspiciousness is Koyil (temple). There are two temples here: Thiru Aanilay and Vanjuleswaram. Thiru Aanilay was built by Kamadhenu, the divine cow. Breaking down Thiru Aanilay into Thiru, Aan, Nilai, where Thiru means beautiful, Aan means cow, and Nilai means temple, it means a temple created by the beautiful cow, Kamadhenu. Therefore, it is named Thiru Aanilay. Vanjuleswaram was worshipped by Brahma Devar. In addition to these, there is a Harihara Putra temple established by Brahma. There are also Bhadrakali Ammaiyar temple and Vennai Malai Subramanyar temple. Now let us contemplate the special features of the idols. All the stones and sands in this place are in the form of Shivlingams. The Somesa Lingam, worshipped by Chandra to remove the curse of Guru's wife, is here. There are many Lingams here such as Anantha Ganesha Lingam, Agasthya Lingam (where Agasthya worshipped by piling up white sand), Konganesa Lingam, Tharakeswara Lingam, Kanjesa Lingam, Visala Lingam, and Thilakuleswara Lingam. However, only five Lingams are considered very glorious here. That is why Karur is also known as Panchalingesam. Those five Lingams are as follows: The Pasupathi Lingam, also known as Aadhi Nayagar, who emerged from an anthill. He is the Moolavar (presiding deity) of the Karur Thiru Aanilay temple. To its east is Koteeswara Lingam, which is inside the temple. To the east of Koteeswara Lingam is Kailayanatha Lingam. Then, Kariyamaaleesa Lingam. Following that, Vanjuleswara Lingam. Thus, Karur shines as a Panchalinga Kshetram. Now let us look at the explanation of the name Pasupatheeswarar, the Lord enshrined in this Kshetram. Although the Lord has many names such as Pasupatheesar, Pasupatheeswarar, Pasupathi Kadavul, Pasupatheeswara Linga Peruman, Aanma Nayagar, Thiru Aanilay Kadavul, and Thiru Aanilay Nadhar, the other names are derived from the name Pasupatheesar. Pasupatheesar is a name composed of three words: Pasu, Pathi, and Eesar. Pasu means bound. Since all souls are eternally bound by the Anava Mala (egoistic impurity), souls are called Pasus. Therefore, all living beings are Pasus. Next, Pathi.

Pathi means protector. We saw the meaning of the first word, Pasu. The second word, Pathi, means protector. Therefore, Pasupathi means the protector of living beings, who are Pasus. The Lord, who is the compassionate Pathi, is called Pasupathi because He drives the souls, who are Pasus, into this world to experience pleasures, destroys their Karma Mala, nourishes them with the water of His divine grace, removes their three kinds of suffering (Thapathrayangal), and places them in the resting place of Mukti (liberation). This is the meaning of the name Pasupathi. Generally, cowherds in the world do not raise and protect cows that are not beneficial to them. This principle is called Aabala Gopala Nyayam. But what does Lord Shiva, the Gopalan, do? Even though He gains no benefit from herding and protecting the souls, who are Pasus, He protects countless souls. That is why the Atharva Siras Shruti, which is excellent among the Vedas, praises the Lord thus: "O Lord, if cows give milk, it is customary for cowherds to protect such milk-giving cows. But if cowherds protect barren cows that do not give milk, the only reason for that is compassion. Similarly, O Lord, you protect us, who are barren cows that do not give milk and are of no benefit to You, out of Your compassion." This is how it praises. Thiruvundhiyar, a Saiva Siddhanta scripture, sings, "Kaayaththin meygnanak kallunna maattaathe maayak kal undaar endru undhi para varattu pasukkal endru undhi para" (Those who cannot eat the true knowledge stone of the body, but eat the illusory stone, fly up, saying 'barren cows,' fly up). "Pasunaam Pathi Pasupathi" is a grammatical rule. Therefore, the word Pathi refers only to Lord Shiva. "Pasunaam Pathaye Namo Nama" is the essence of the Sri Rudra Namaka mantra. The Rig Veda also refers to Lord Shiva as Pathi, saying "Kaathapathi Medhapathi." Thus, we have seen the glories of the names Pasu and Pathi within the name Pasupatheesar. The third word that follows is Eesan. Eesan means one who rules everything as his possession. The names Eesan, Eeswaran, and Eesanan all apply to Lord Shiva. He rules everything, and there is no one to rule Him. Thus, we can see that the three categories of Pathi, Pasu, and Pasam, taught by Vedanta Siddhantas, are contained in the sacred name Pasupatheesan. This entire world is comprised of eight categories of elements: Prithvi (earth), Appu (water), Theyu (fire), Vayu (air), Akash (space), Surya (sun), Chandra (moon), and Aanma (soul). In our Saiva tradition, Lord Shiva is called by eight names as the presiding deity for these, respectively: Bhavan, Sarvan, Rudran, Bheeman, Mahadevan, Ugran, Soman, and Pasupathi. Here, for the eighth element, Aanma (soul), we call Lord Shiva by the name Pasupathi as its presiding deity. Therefore, it is Siddhanta that Lord Shiva, as Pasupathi, is the ultimate God. There is no path beyond ten, and no deity beyond Pasupathi. Therefore, realizing that there is no place superior to this Karur (Karuvoor) and no God superior to Pasupathi, let us worship Karur Pasupatheeswarar. We will continue to contemplate the Karur Sthala Purana. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 04 — Eri-Pattha Nayanar

We are contemplating the purana of Eripaththa Nayanar in the third chapter of Periya Puranam, the Ilaimalindha Sarga. Karur is the birthplace of Eripaththa Nayanar. Therefore, we are contemplating the Mahatmyam of Karur. Kamadhenu, the divine cow of the celestial world, capable of granting all desired foods, reached the earthly realm by the grace of Lord Shiva to perform the act of creation. As per the words of Narada Muni, Kamadhenu reached this holy place of Vanji, Karur, and performed penance. At that time, an ethereal voice (Asareeri) was heard by Kamadhenu, instructing her to worship the Mahalingam that had emerged from the netherworld within an anthill. As Kamadhenu worshipped, blood flowed from the sacred head of the Shivlingam when her hoof accidentally struck it. Seeing this, Kamadhenu was greatly distressed. At that moment, the Lord appeared and said, "O Cow, do not grieve. This is our divine play. From today onwards, this temple and I will be known as Pasupatheeswaram Thiru Aanilay and Pasupatheesar, respectively. There are footprints of mobile and immobile beings in the southeast direction. Therefore, use that formula, observe them, and perform the act of creation." Having granted this grace, He disappeared. Kamadhenu did as instructed, went there, meditated on Shiva, studied the footprints of mobile and immobile beings, and created all mobile and immobile beings. Not only did she create mobile and immobile beings, but she also gathered divine nectar for their sustenance and created a mountain of butter (Vennai Malai). She also created a Theertham (sacred pond) named Dhenu Theertham after herself. After that, Kamadhenu worshipped Pasupatheeswarar Peruman and ascended to the celestial world. This Vennai Malai, even in the hottest season, remains cool, true to its name, even today. Next, the history of Uma Ammaiyar's worship here. Uma Pirattiyar, the Mother of all worlds, humbly approached Lord Shiva enshrined in Thirukailai and asked, "O Lord, what is the greatest of all penances?" To this, Lord Shiva replied, "Worshipping Me is the greatest penance." Therefore, Uma Devi, realizing that this place was the best for worshipping Lord Shiva, came to this Karur with her female companions like Anindithai and Kamalini, and celestial maidens like Saraswati and Lakshmi. Assuming the sacred name Alangaravathi, she worshipped here strictly following the Shivagama rules. Pasupatheeswarar Peruman appeared before Ambikai, Uma Devi, who is praised by the Vedas as "Thame bruhi mahavidyam moksha samrajya dayinim nidhagathvam kritharthosi sunuvidyam sanathanim," and granted her the boon of never being separated from Him. This Lord of the place has two Ambikais (consorts). This is because one Ambikai is the Abheda Shakti (non-dual power) who is always with the Lord, and the other is the Thathaanmiya Shakti who worshipped here under the name Alangaravathi. Therefore, the Abheda Shakti, Parashakti, is known by the sacred name Soundarya Nayaki, and the Ambikai who worshipped the Lord here, Alangaravathi, is known by the sacred name Alangaravalli as the Thathaanmiya Shakti. Therefore, it is a special feature that there are two Shakti shrines in the temple of this Karur Thiruthalam. Next, the history of Brahma's worship. Brahma Devar, unable to accomplish the act of creation, performed penance in Thirukailai. As per Lord Shiva's command, he came here, worshipped Pasupatheeswarar, received His grace, created a Theertham named after himself to the east of Vanjuleswaram, performed penance for some days, reached Vennai Malai, built a Yaga Kundam (sacrificial pit) to its north, completed the Yaga with many Devas and Munis, married Saraswati, built a temple for Pasupatheeswarar, performed chariot festivals and other celebrations on Panguni Uthiram day, and built Aiyanar temple and Bhadrakali Ammaiyar temple in the south direction, and then returned to his world. Next, the history of Vyasa's worship. Vyasa Muni, the son of Parashara Muni, came here during the time Brahma Devar was performing the Yaga. After the Yaga was completed and everyone had left, Vyasa Muni stayed here and worshipped Pasupathi. At that time, Pasupatheeswarar appeared before Vyasa and said, "There is an Ilanthai Vanam (jujube forest) in the Agni (southeast) direction of this place, to the north of Ambra Nadi. Worship Me from there." Accordingly, Vyasa Muni worshipped here with his disciples. Texts state that he also composed the Uttara Mimamsa Sutras from this town. Next, the worship by the Dikpalakas (guardians of directions). Indra, the son of Kashyapa, Agni, the son of Viswanara Muni, and Yama, the son of Surya, worshipped here and became the lords of the east, southeast, and south directions, respectively. Durjayan, the son of Agnibakkannan, who was born in the lineage of Murasuran, worshipped and became the lord of the southwest direction (Niruthi). Varuna by cleaning, Vayu by sweeping, Kubera by offering gem ornaments to Pasupathi Nadhar, and Ishana by worshipping, attained the blessing of becoming the lords of the west, northwest, north, and northeast directions, respectively. Now, the history of other deities' worship. Thirumal worshipped Thiru Aanilay Nadhar, married Lakshmi Devi, the daughter of Khyati, received the power of protection, and was enshrined in Karuvoor. The four Vedas, Rig and others, worshipped and became the authority for the three worlds. Many Munis performed Yagas and Shiva installations in the southeast direction and attained true life. Lord Subramanya created Theerthams named after himself to the east and west of Amravati and worshipped. The Navagrahas (nine planets) and Munis like Bheethagan, Savagan, and Vasishtan worshipped and attained higher realms. Thadagai worshipped and received the boon of progeny. She also gained the strength of 14,000 elephants. Chera kings, Chola kings, and Pandya kings worshipped and received boons. Shukracharya, the guru of the Asuras, performed a Yaga in the Ilanthai Vanam here, received the Mritha Sanjeevini mantra from Lord Shiva, which revives the dead, and revived his disciple Kacha, the son of Brihaspati, and his son Dadhichi Muni. When Uma Devi and Lord Shiva were seated under a Bilva tree on Thirukailaya Malai, a monkey (musu) from that tree playfully plucked and threw Bilva leaves. These leaves fell on the sacred body of Lord Shiva and Ambikai. Accepting this as worship, they granted that monkey the grace to become the emperor of the three worlds. Accordingly, that monkey, Musu, was born in the lineage of Harichandra. Its name was Musukundhan. Musukundhan worshipped Pasupathi, ruled, performed penance towards Lord Skanda, and received companions like Veerabagu and a hundred thousand warriors as his allies and army, and ruled. When Lord Muruga married Deivanai Ammaiyar in Thirupparankundram, Musukundha Chakravarti went from this Karuvoor with his army to witness it. Later, Musukundha Chakravarti ruled for a long time and attained the sacred feet of Pasupathi Nayagan. Kalavar Muni, an Aadi Saiva Munindra born in the Kashyapa Gotra, wished to avoid the suffering of being born again in another mother's womb and attain the supreme liberation of Advaitha Mukti. He approached his Acharya, Devathuthi Muni, and requested guidance. The Acharya explained the special features of the Karur Theerthams and instructed him to bathe in the six Theerthams according to the rules and worship Pasupathi. Accordingly, Kalavar Muni came here, worshipped Pasupathi Nadhar, and attained the supreme liberation of not being born again in another womb. The proverb "Karuvoor kandavar Karuvoor kaanaar" (Those who have seen Karuvoor will not see another womb) originated because of him. There was a hunter named Vibandagan, who was the embodiment of sin. The Margazhi Thiruvathirai Mahotsavam was taking place here in Karuvoor. At that time, this hunter Vibandagan planned to steal the Lord's ornaments. During the Margazhi Thiruvathirai Mahotsavam, he disguised himself as a Shiva devotee, wearing sacred ash, rudraksha, etc., along with other devotees, and waited for an opportunity to steal the Lord's ornaments. At that time, the Lord's procession came out and proceeded through the streets. Due to the large crowd, the hunter fell down and was trampled by the feet of the devotees and died. Yama's messengers came to take him to Yama Loka. Before them, Shiva's Ganas (attendants) arrived. The Shiva Ganas chased away Yama's messengers and took the hunter to Thirukailai Malai. Therefore, it is certain that even a sinner, if he has darshan of Karuvoor and dies here, will attain Shiva Sayujya Mukti (union with Shiva). Similarly, the leprosy of Gopitharathvajan was cured here. This holy place of Karuvoor is the land where Nayanmars like Eripaththa Nayanar and Pugazhchozha Nayanar lived. We are going to see their history in detail in the Periya Puranam. Next, the history of Karuvoor Devar. Karuvoor Devar was born in a Brahmana family in Karuvoor. Karuvoor Devar thoroughly understood all these four types of religions: Purappura Samayangal (heterodox systems) like Lokayatham, Boudham, Arugatham, Tharkikam, Meemamsam, Ekanmavatham, Pathanjalam, Pancharatram, Sankyam, Saivam, Pasupatham, Mahaveeratham, Kapalam, Vamam, and Agappura Samayangal (systems within Saivism but not pure Siddhanta) like Aikkyavatha Saivam, Pashanavatha Saivam, Sankiranthavatha Saivam, Nimitha Karana Parinamavatha Saivam, Avikaravatha Saivam, Suddha Saivam, and Bhedavatha Saivam. He clearly understood that Suddhadvaita Saiva Siddhanta is the ultimate religion and lived a virtuous life. Karuvoor Devar appeared as a Siddha (accomplished one), shone as a master of Shivagnana Nishtha (meditation on Shiva's knowledge), and attained all Siddhis (supernatural powers). Karuvoor Devar performed many divine plays. Many people, unaware of his true nature, scorned him as a follower of the Vama Marga. To reveal the truth to them, he caused rain to fall at an unusual time. He made the Amravati river overflow. He made the doors of the Thiru Aanilay temple open. He made Bhoothas (elemental beings) hold umbrellas for him. Thus, Karuvoor Devar performed many miracles. He traveled to countries like Kongunadu, Kudanadu, Kannada, Thozhuvam, Magatham, Kosikam, Kosalam, Unjai, Avanti, Saligramam, Ariyam, Malavam, Maratiyam, Ayodhya, Gaudam, Vallavam, and Matsam, and visited holy places like Chidambaram and Kanchi. When he came to Kurugoor, Vaishnavites, as per the command of Thirumal, came and worshipped him and honored him. Karuvoor Devar reached Thirunelveli and called out to Nellaiyappar. Nellaiyappar did not come even after he called. Therefore, he cursed, "Shiva is not here. Therefore, good people will diminish, and the city will become a ruin." Again, all the devotees pleaded, and he sang Thiruvichaippa and removed the curse. Then Karuvoor Devar reached the holy place called Thiruvidaimarudur. He came to Thiruvidaimarudur and called out to Lord Shiva there. At that time, Lord Shiva, like a cow hearing the call of its calf, appeared before him and granted him grace. Seeing that vision, he sang Thiruvichaippa. In Thanjavur, they tried to consecrate a Shivlingam in the temple built by the Chola king. During the consecration, they would use a medicinal paste called Ashtabandhanam.

But at that time, the Bandhana medicine did not solidify and bind. Therefore, they could not perform the consecration. Everyone was distressed, thinking that this task would only succeed if Karuvoorar, or Karuvoor Devar, came. At that moment, Bhoganatha Siddhar, who was there, tied a palm leaf to the leg of a crow and sent it to Karuvoor Devar. Karuvoor Devar came here. He came here, installed the Shivlingam with the Avudai (pedestal), spat the betel quid from his mouth there, and used it as the Bandhana medicine to consecrate the Shivlingam. Karuvoor Devar sang Thiruvichaippa. Thiruvichaippa is revered in the ninth Thirumurai among the twelve Thirumurais. This Karuvoor Devar is the great one who sang it. Karuvoor Devar worshipped Pasupathi Nadhar here, embraced him, and merged with him, attaining liberation. Among the Thiruvichaippas he sang, holy places like Koyil (Chidambaram), Thirukkalanthai, Aadhitheeswaram, Thirukkeezhkkoottur, Maniyambalam, Thirumugathalai, Thriloakya Sundaram, Gangaigonda Chozheeswaram, Thiruppoovanam, Thiruchattiyakkudi, Thanjai Rajarajeswaram, and Thiruvidaimarudur are prominently mentioned. These sacred verses are included with the Thiruvichaippas composed by eight other individuals. Nambiyandar Nambigal, the pinnacle of the Aadi Saiva lineage, who compiled the twelve Thirumurais by the divine grace and command of Lord Shiva, included these Thiruvichaippa verses in the ninth Thirumurai. The day Karuvoor Devar attained liberation was the full moon day with the Pushya star in the month of Thai. The glory of Karuvoor is mentioned in the Narasinga Purana, one of the eighteen Upapuranas. This Narasinga Purana describes the history of the appearance of Narasingam, Lord Vishnu, who killed the Asura Hiranyakashipu, the annihilation of Hiranyakashipu, and then the history of Lord Shiva appearing as Sarabeshwara Murthy and subduing Lord Narasingam. Brahma Devar reached Karuvoor and performed penance to obtain the power of creation. He began the festival when the sun entered the Revati star. All Devas, Munis, and other Ganas reached Karuvoor and witnessed the festival. Bhanukamban, the leader of the Rudra Ganas, came here by the command of Lord Virabhadra, explained all the glories of the Uttara day, and observed the Kalyana Sundara Viratham (vow for auspicious marriage). Thirumal, Brahma, and other Devas, Munis, and all other Ganas observed it and celebrated the Ratha Mahotsavam (chariot festival). By its glory, Thirumal and Brahma attained their respective duties, wives, vehicles, worlds, and enjoyments. Bhanukambar received a conch called Panibalam with a thousand heads and a thousand mouths to blow. Not only that, but he also received the honor of becoming the leader of the Ganas. Surya married Usha Devi. Chandra married the twenty-seven daughters of Daksha, including Ashwini. Indra received Indrani, the daughter of Puloman, as his consort. Amirthavalli and Sundaravalli, who emerged from the two eyes of Thirumal, appeared as Devasena and Valli respectively and attained Lord Muruga. Sukkesi, the daughter of Vedanga Muni and an ocean of beauty, married Thiru Nandi Devar, the Paramacharya of Saiva Siddhanta. Poornai and Pushkalai, who emerged from the eyes of Satyapoornar Muni, married Shasta, the son of Harihara Putra. Manmathan married Rati, Rama married Sita, Sambavati married Krishna, Agasthya married Lopamudra, and Vasishta married Arundhati. This is the Mahatmyam of the Kalyana Sundara Viratham. This Kalyana Sundara Viratham should be observed in Vanjularanyam. This Vanjularanyam in Karuvoor is therefore called Mangalapuri. All these matters are mentioned in the Narasinga Purana. Thus, the Karur (Karuvoor) Sthala Mahatmyam concludes. All true devotees who lovingly heard, read, wrote, and caused to be written this Karur Mahatmyam will be freed from disease, achieve victory over enemies, obtain good progeny, auspicious marriage, wealth, education, fame, knowledge, and all other blessings, and attain salvation. If so, the great blessings attained by those who reached Karuvoor and worshipped there are very difficult to describe. Suta Mahamuni concluded by telling the Munis in Naimisaranya that the greatness of the benefits attained by those who worshipped Karur cannot be expressed by us. All the Munis, having heard this history, lived by the grace of Lord Shiva. With this, we conclude the Karur Sthala Purana and will continue to contemplate the history of Eripaththa Nayanar, who was born in this holy place of Karur. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 05 — Eri-Pattha Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. We are contemplating the Puranam of Eri-Pattha Nayanar in the third chapter, Ilai Malinda Sarukkam, of Periya Puranam. The sacred place where Eri-Pattha Nayanar incarnated is Karuvur. Therefore, we have contemplated the greatness of Karuvur. Now, we will continue the Puranam of Eri-Pattha Nayanar through Sekkizhar's Periya Puranam. Sekkizhar Swamigal, who stated that Karuvur is the town where Eri-Pattha Nayanar incarnated, now proceeds to describe Karuvur. This can be considered the special feature of the sacred town for this Puranam. In this Karuvur, there are massive walls and mansions. Clouds envelop these massive walls, and the mansions soar to the sky. At the entrance of Karuvur's palace, a pure bell is tied, known as the bell of inquiry (Aaraaichi Mani). Sekkizhar Swamigal describes that the walls and mansions of Karuvur rise to the level of Devaloka in the sky. By singing thus, he implicitly conveys that the greatness of Karuvur surpasses that of Indraloka, the world of the Devas. It was with the help of Musukunda Chakravarthi, who ruled Karuvur, that Indra, who rules Indraloka, defeated his enemy. Therefore, this sacred place of Karuvur is a place that even helped Indraloka. Sekkizhar Swamigal mentioned that clouds envelop the walls of Karuvur, didn't he? In Indraloka, the world of the Devas, clouds are used only as vehicles for Indra. But here in Karuvur, those clouds gather and pour rain, which aids charity, distinction, and worship. Sekkizhar Swamigal mentions Indra by the name Satamagan here. Satam means one hundred. Magan here is a corrupted form of Magam. Magam means Yagam (sacrifice). So, Satamagan means one who has performed a hundred Yagams. One can attain the position of Indra only by performing a hundred Yagams. However, that position of Indra is perishable. Once the merits associated with Indra's position are exhausted, he descends from that position. Therefore, it is a perishable merit. But the Yagams performed in this sacred place of Karuvur bestow superior, imperishable Shiva Punya (merits) upon the people of this sacred place. Therefore, in that respect too, Sekkizhar Swamigal praises Karuvur as superior to Devaloka. Next, Sekkizhar Swamigal describes the prosperity of Karuvur. It is a very beautiful song: "Kadakari thuraiyil aadum kalimayil puravil aadum madarmani arangil aadum arivaiyar kuzhal vandaadum padar oli marugil aadum payil kodi kadhir meedhu aadum thadanedum puvi kondaadum thaninagar valamai eedhaal." In Karuvur, elephants play in the river banks. Elephants are generally very fond of playing in water. Especially rutting elephants enjoy playing in water. In Karuvur, rutting elephants play in the river banks of the river now called Amaravathi. We saw that this river, now called Amaravathi, was referred to as Amravathi in ancient times. Next, peacocks dance in the groves on the river banks. Peacocks dance when they are very happy. Also, peacocks dance upon seeing clouds. In Karuvur, dark clouds always gather. Moreover, there are countless groves on both banks of the river. Thus, since the clouds in the sky and the groves of the city always bring joy to the peacocks here, the peacocks dance in that joy. Next, Sekkizhar Swamigal says: Here in Karuvur, dancing girls dance in the dance halls. Due to this, their hair sways. Due to this, the bees that swarm the flowers in their hair also dance. The fact that dancing girls dance in the dance halls indicates that the people of that great city lead a very happy life, free from hunger and disease. Next, in Karuvur, flags wave on all the mansions. Moreover, the rays of the sun during the day, the rays of the moon at night, and the light emanating from the large lamps in the streets of this great city are always dancing here. Thus, Sekkizhar Swamigal sings of the prosperity of Karuvur. Elephants bathe in the river. Peacocks dance in the groves. Dancing girls dance in the dance halls. Flags wave on the mansions. Sekkizhar Swamigal sings separately that the world celebrates due to such greatness of Karuvur. We can also connect these and enjoy the meaning. How? When elephants bathe in the rivers, the peacocks, mistaking the dark elephants for clouds, dance. But the bees in the hair of the dancing girls dance, as if to say that the dance of these peacocks is not equal to the dance of our mistress. Thus, since the dance of the dancing girls has triumphed over the dance of the peacocks, as a sign of this, flags wave on the mansions like victory flags. Even if we connect them in this way, we can rejoice in the rich meaning that emerges. In such a Karuvur, the temple called Thiru Aanilai shines. In this Thiru Aanilai temple, Lord Shiva always resides. Moreover, Lord Shiva always resides in the minds of the devotees here, never leaving them. This Thiru Aanilai temple has very long, large walls. Those walls are covered with gold. It is our custom to adorn the walls of temples, their pinnacles, entrances, and other places with gold. In this great Thiru Aanilai temple, Aranaar (Lord Shiva) always resides. Devotees always praise and worship this Thiru Aanilai temple, never forgetting it in their thoughts. In the minds of such devotees, the Lord always resides. Sages say that the minds of devotees are a better abode for Lord Shiva than a temple. We can see this in the Puranam of Poosalar Nayanar as well. "Oruvarum ingu ariyaa vannam en ullathu ulle olithu vaitha siraiyaanai" (He who is imprisoned within my heart without anyone knowing) is from Thiruththandaagam, Thirunageswaram. "Maravaathe than thirame vaazhthum thondar manathagathe anavarathamum manninanai" (He who always resides in the hearts of devotees who praise his greatness without forgetting) is from Thirumuthukundram Thiruththandaagam. From these sacred sayings, we can understand that the Lord always dwells in the minds of devotees. Now, the next verse. "Porul thirumaraik kadantha punitharai inidhu akkoil marul thurai maatrum aatral vazhipadum thozhilar aagi irul kadu odungu kandathu iraiyavarkku urimai poondarkku thulperum thondu seivaar avar Eri-Patthar aavaar." In this beautiful verse, Sekkizhar Swamigal praises Eri-Pattha Nayanar. The explanation of this verse is: There is one who performs great service, standing in the path of His grace, to the devotees who have taken on the servitude belonging to Lord Shiva, whose throat holds the poison that is like darkness, and who worship Him in the Thiru Aanilai temple by the method that changes the delusion of birth, having become a worshipper of Lord Shiva, the pure one who transcends even the Vedas that bestow true knowledge. He is called Eri-Patthar. This is the literal meaning of this verse. Now, let us deeply contemplate this verse. First, Sekkizhar Swamigal refers to Lord Shiva, who is enshrined in the Karuvur Aanilai temple, as the pure one who transcends the Vedas. He describes the Vedas as "porulai tharum Vedangal" (Vedas that bestow meaning/truth). "Porulai tharum Vedangal" means Vedas that bestow true knowledge, the ultimate reality. Vedas bestow ultimate realities. What are ultimate realities? They are the things that give stability to human beings. They are Dharma, Artha, Kama, and Moksha. These are called Purusharthas in Sanskrit, and Dharma, Artha, Kama, Moksha. Great people call Purushartha as "urudhiporutkal" (ultimate realities) in Tamil. Dharma, Artha, Kama, Moksha are the things that give stability to human life. Therefore, they are ultimate realities. There is no Dharma without the Veda. All Dharma to be recited is in the Veda. "Tharkka vaathathai vittu mathignar valamutra Vedathai oothiye veedu petraargale" (Sages, abandoning logical arguments, attained liberation by reciting the prosperous Veda) is a verse from Thirumandiram, First Tantra, in the section "Veda Sirappu." Although it is generally said that there are four ultimate realities for human beings, Sekkizhar Swamigal here praises the Veda as "porul thiru marai" (sacred Veda that bestows true knowledge) because the Veda teaches that Moksha, which is eternal bliss, is the ultimate reality, and because Thirumoolar states in Thirumandiram that sages attained liberation by reciting the Veda. Lord Shiva shines as the pure one who transcends all these Vedas. The Vedas originate from the Pranava within the Nada Tattva. But the Lord shines as Nadanta at the end of Nada. Vedic knowledge is called Apara Gnana, which is worldly knowledge. The Lord is not perceived by Apara Gnana but by Para Gnana, which is true knowledge that is difficult to comprehend. That is why Sekkizhar Swamigal refers to Lord Shiva as the pure one who transcends all the Vedas. Nevertheless, the Vedas serve as the means to attain such liberation. That is why Sekkizhar Swamigal refers to the Veda as "porul thiru marai." The devotees worship Lord Shiva, the pure one who transcends such a "porul thiru marai." Why do they worship? Because worshipping Lord Shiva alone will cure the disease of birth. It will save us from this cycle of birth. Sekkizhar Swamigal here refers to the devotees as "marul thurai maatrum aatral vazhipadum thozhilar" (those who worship by the method that changes the delusion of birth). This means that the devotees of the Lord are those who worship by standing in the path of Shivagama, which is the path that changes the enemy of delusion, which is birth. Here, Sekkizhar Swamigal uses the word "marul," doesn't he? He uses the word "marul" and says that the way to change that "marul thurai" (path of delusion) is the Shivagama path. Let us contemplate this more elaborately through the Saiva Siddhanta scriptures. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 06 — Eri-Pattha Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. We are contemplating the Puranam of Eri-Pattha Nayanar in the third chapter, Ilai Malinda Sarukkam, of Periya Puranam. Lord Shiva, enshrined in the Karur Thiru Aanilai temple, is the one who revealed the Vedas. Those Vedas bestow Purusharthas upon people. Lord Shiva is the Lord who transcends even those Vedas. Devotees worship Him. Why? Because if one worships the Lord by following the path of Shivagama, which is the path that changes the enemy of delusion, which is birth, one can attain liberation. Therefore, the Lord's devotees worship and adore the Lord. The path of Shivagama revealed by the Lord is the path that changes the enemy of delusion, which is birth. In Saiva Siddhanta scriptures, "marul" is very important. Saiva Siddhanta scriptures beautifully describe Aanava Mala as darkness, its effect as "marul," the knowing power of souls as "therul," the grace of God as "arul," and Lord Shiva as the ultimate reality. Thus, Saiva religious elders explain all Saiva Siddhanta scriptures using these concepts of "irul" (darkness), "marul" (delusion), "therul" (clarity), "arul" (grace), and "porul" (ultimate reality). In this verse, Sekkizhar Swamigal uses the word "marul" and states that the way to change the "marul thurai" (path of delusion) is the Saivagama path. What is "marul"? First, let's see how the word "marul" is used in the ethical scripture Thirukkural, and then how it is used in Saiva Siddhanta scriptures. In Thirukkural, in the chapter "Meyyunarthal" (Realization of Truth) in the section "Thuravara Viyal" (Asceticism) of "Araththuppaal" (Book of Virtue), Valluvar speaks of "marul" in the first Kural. First, we must contemplate the title of this chapter, "Meyyunarthal." It is located in "Thuravara Viyal." Parimelazhagar, who wrote a commentary on Thirukkural, divides "Thuravara Viyal" into two parts: "Viratham" (Vows) and "Gnanam" (Wisdom). He classifies the chapters into these two major sections. "Viratham" means the practice performed in asceticism. "Gnanam" means the fruit of asceticism, as explained by Parimelazhagar, and he includes the chapter "Meyyunarthal" in the section of "Gnanam." What is "Meyyunarthal"? Parimelazhagar provides an introductory commentary for "Meyyunarthal." What is "Meyyunarthal"? That is, one must realize that there is suffering in birth. One must realize that there is a permanent state of bliss called "Veedu" (liberation) or "Mukthi," free from this suffering of birth. Not only realizing this, but also realizing what are the causes of birth. And realizing what are the causes of "Veedu" or "Mukthi." How should these be realized? One must realize them truly, without "vipareetham" (perversion) and "aiyam" (doubt), by removing these two. That is, one must realize birth. One must realize moksha. One must realize the causes of birth. One must realize the causes of moksha. Parimelazhagar explains that one must realize all these without doubt or error. "Aiyam" means doubt. "Vipareetham" means perversion. "Gnanam," or true knowledge, is knowledge without perversion and doubt. "Vipareetham" means mistaking one thing for another. "Aiyam" means doubting whether a thing is this or that. Parimelazhagar defines true knowledge, "Meyyunarthal," or philosophical knowledge as realizing birth and liberation, and their causes, as they are, by removing these two defects.

If we contemplate the first Kural of the chapter "Meyyunarthal" based on this introductory commentary, we will understand what "marul" means. The first Kural of the chapter "Meyyunarthal" is: "Porul allavatrai porul endru unarum marulaalaam maanaap pirappu." This is the Kural. Parimelazhagar comments on this: Birth is sorrowful. Any kind of birth is sorrow. Generally, births are classified into four types: animal, human, divine, and hellish. In all these four types of births, we experience only sorrow. That is why Valluvar refers to these births as "maanaap pirappu" (undesirable birth), according to Parimelazhagar's commentary. Now, by what is such a sorrowful birth caused? It is caused by the "marul" (delusion) of mistaking non-realities for realities. That is, the perverse understanding that mistakes non-realities for realities. Parimelazhagar comments that this perverse understanding is the cause of the suffering of birth. We saw earlier, didn't we? That "vipareetham" means mistaking one thing for another and believing it. For example, when we walk along the seashore in the evening, in that hazy time of mixed light and darkness, a shell on the sand might glitter like silver due to the dim sunlight. When we see it, we might mistake that shell for silver. Mistaking a shell for silver in this way, mistaking one thing for another, is "vipareetham." In Tamil, this is called "thiripu" (perversion). This perverse understanding is also the cause of our suffering of birth. This perverse understanding leads us to mistake non-realities for realities. That is, all the worlds we see here are non-realities. But we, deluded, mistake these false things for real, true things, engage in worldly life, and act. Doesn't this perverse understanding show us this unreal world as real? Because of that, we repeatedly experience the suffering of birth. This is the meaning of the first Kural. "Porul allavatrai porul endru unarum marulaalaam maanaap pirappu." Therefore, here Parimelazhagar interprets "marul" as perverse understanding and explains that this perverse understanding is the cause of births. Now, on this basis, let us contemplate "marul" in Saiva Siddhanta scriptures. Thirukkural is a book that came to speak of general dharma. Saiva Siddhanta is a book that came to specifically speak of Agamas and Siddhanta. Therefore, when learning any subject or any word, it is proper education to first see how it is used in general ethics, and then how it is specifically explained in Saiva Siddhanta. We are also following this method. Accordingly, let us now see how the word "marul" is explained in Saiva Siddhanta. Saiva Siddhanta explains that "marul," which is perverse understanding, occurs in souls in the Sakala Avastha (state of being bound by all three malas) due to Aanava Mala. According to Saiva Siddhanta scriptures, souls have been bound by Aanava Mala from eternity. To remove this Aanava Mala, God adds Karma Mala and Maya Mala, purifies the souls, weakens their Aanava, and then grants them liberation.

Therefore, according to Saiva Siddhanta Sastra, Aanavam is a substance. It has bound souls from time immemorial. Souls inherently possess three powers: Icchai (desire), Gnanam (knowledge), and Kriyai (action). That is why the soul is called a Chit-porul (conscious entity). But Saiva Siddhanta states that the soul has been bound by Aanava Mala from eternity. Aanavam is a substance. It is an inert substance. What does it do? It conceals the powers of Icchai, Gnanam, and Kriyai of the soul, i.e., the Atma. Because it conceals the Iccha, Gnana, Kriya powers of the soul, the soul at that time is like a stone. Saiva Siddhanta calls this the Pashana Nilai (stone state). The state in which the Atma is first bound by Aanava Mala and lies like a stone is called Kevala Nilai (isolated state) by Siddhanta. Kevalam means isolation. Aanava Mala is the inherent impurity of souls, like clay in copper or husk in paddy, from eternity. Paddy appears with the husk. Paddy does not appear alone. Therefore, the husk is an impurity to the paddy. It is not a quality. But when the husk is removed from the paddy, the paddy becomes pure. Similarly, from the moment a soul exists, it has the bond of Aanava Mala. Because it is always with the soul, Saiva Siddhanta calls Aanava Mala Sahaja Mala (inherent impurity). This Aanava Mala is one. It is not many. Although Aanava Mala is one, its powers are countless. That is why it conceals countless souls. Elders compare this Aanavam to darkness. Just as darkness, being a substance, conceals countless eyes with its countless powers, so too Aanava Mala, being a single substance, conceals the Icchai, Gnanam, and Kriyai powers of countless souls with its countless powers. This is the state in which Aanavam conceals souls in the primordial period. Siddhanta calls this the Kevala Nilai. Thus, because Aanavam conceals the soul's knowledge, desire, and action (Icchai, Gnanam, Kriyai), the soul is unable to do anything, unable to act, and remains like an inert substance, like a stone. It lies immersed in Aanavam. In this Kevala Nilai, the power of Aanava Mala that conceals the Icchai, Gnanam, and Kriyai powers of the Atma is called Aavaraka Shakti. Aavarakam means concealing. This Kevala Nilai or Kevala Avastha is the first state of the soul. Now, the second state is Sakala Avastha or Sakala Nilai. In this Sakala Avastha, souls are joined not only by Aanavam but also by Maya and Karma Malas. God's Tirodhana Shakti (concealing power) adds Maya and Karma Malas to the soul. Why? To remove the true Mala, Aanava Mala, God's Tirodhana Shakti adds these two Malas, Karma and Maya. To the soul that is alone with Aanava Mala from eternity, God adds two more Malas, Karma Mala and Maya Mala, subjects the souls to enjoyment and suffering, gives them experiences of enjoyment and suffering, makes them contemplate the way to come out of these experiences of enjoyment and suffering, and then, by teaching them Vedas, Shivagamas, etc., makes the souls follow them, and then, by bringing maturity to the souls through following the Vedas and Shivagamas, God unites those mature souls with liberation. It is for this purpose that God adds Karma Mala and Maya Mala to Aanava Mala. The state in which the soul is joined not only by Aanava Mala but also by Maya Mala and Karma Mala is called Sakala Avastha. Then, the state of attaining liberation by removing these three Malas is called Shuddha Avastha or Shuddha Nilai (pure state). Therefore, the soul has three states: Kevala Nilai, Sakala Nilai, and Shuddha Nilai, i.e., Kevala Avastha, Sakala Avastha, and Shuddha Avastha. In the second Avastha, Sakala Avastha, Aanava Mala causes perverse understanding in the Atma. In this Sakala Avastha, perverse understanding arises in souls as an effect of Aanava Mala. Due to this perverse understanding, one thing will appear as another to souls. That is, impermanent things will appear as permanent, and sorrowful things will appear as joyful, alternating. Because of this alternation, souls repeatedly experience birth. Because, without realizing the truth, they mistake joyful things for sorrowful and sorrowful things for joyful, and due to that perverse understanding, they perform countless Karmas, and to experience those Karmas, the soul takes countless births. Thus, in Sakala Avastha, Aanavam, which causes perverse understanding instead of true knowledge, is the cause of this perverse understanding, which Saiva Siddhanta Sastra calls "marul." Thus, by causing perverse knowledge, i.e., deluded understanding, in Sakala Avastha, the power of Aanavam at that time is called Adho Niyamika Shakti. Just as the power of Aanava Mala in Kevala Avastha was called Aavaraka Shakti, now in Sakala Avastha, when deluded understanding, which is the effect of Aanava Mala, arises, elders name that Aanava Mala as Adho Niyamika Shakti. Adho Niyamika means that which leads downwards. Adho means downwards. Niyamika means that which leads. Doesn't it truly make the great souls atomic? That is why that Aanavam got its name Aanavam. Because it makes souls atomic, it is called Aanava Mala. In Sakala Avastha, Aanavam thus performs "marul" as its effect, and because of that, it deludes the intellect of souls, which are conscious entities, and causes perverse understanding. This is the effect of Aanava Mala. This perverse understanding is called "marul." It is because of this "marul" that souls experience defects such as infatuation and darkness. Similarly, Saiva Siddhanta states that Aanavam performs countless actions as effects of Aanavam. The Saiva Siddhanta scripture "Irupa Irupattu" lists the following as effects of Aanava Mala: The nature of Aanavam is to consider oneself superior, to believe that everything happens because of oneself, to get angry at others when the fault lies with oneself, to be infatuated with things that are explained as evil by gurus and ethical texts, to perpetuate that infatuation without removing it, to kill other beings without compassion for oneself or for others, to grieve without the ability to bear suffering when it comes, to express oneself with excessive self-importance in speech and action, to be deluded by considering non-pleasurable things as pleasurable, to suffer from trivial joy that comes from ignorance by considering one's own wealth and prosperity as great. These are respectively called Vikarpam, Sankalpam, Krodham, Moham, Kolai, Vishadam, Madham, and Ariyamai. Thus, in the Sakala Avastha of beings, "marul," which is perverse understanding, appears as a manifestation of Aanava Mala, and because of that, souls experience the suffering of birth. This "marul thurai," that is, "marul," which is perverse understanding, and its "thurai," which is its consequence, the way to change these "marul thurai" is the Saivagama path. The Shivagama path is the conviction of Saiva Siddhanta Sastra. Saiva Siddhanta Sastra states that the only way to remove the effects of Mala is the practice of Shivagama. Worshipping Lord Shiva is the path of grace that changes this "marul thurai," which is the suffering of birth. Anyone who does not worship the path of grace, which is the path of truth, will only attain the path of delusion. Therefore, for souls to be freed from the suffering of "marul thurai," the path of grace, which is Shivagama revealed by Lord Shiva, is the way. Thus, the Lord, who removes the defects of souls, which are Malas, and who revealed the Vedas and Shivagamas for the removal of these Malas from souls, is called the Pure One. Because He removes the defects of souls and purifies them, the Lord also has the name Pure One. Thus, the devotees of Lord Shiva, realizing that Lord Shiva alone is the God who removes this "marul thurai," which is the suffering of birth, will worship such Lord Shiva according to the path of Shivagama revealed by Him. They are the devotees of the Lord, the devotees of Shiva, who always hold the Lord in their thoughts. There is one who performs great service, standing in the path of His grace, to such devotees of Shiva, to the devotees who have taken on the servitude belonging to Lord Shiva. He is Eri-Patthar, says Sekkizhar in this verse. The service of loving the devotees is born out of the connection of loving Lord Shiva. Therefore, those who love Lord Shiva will also love the devotees of Shiva. Eri-Pattha Nayanar is one who loves the devotees of Shiva and performs great service of grace to them. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 07 — Eri-Pattha Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. We are contemplating the Puranam of Eri-Pattha Nayanar in the third chapter, Ilai Malinda Sarukkam, of Periya Puranam. Eri-Pattha Nayanar is one who performs great service of grace to the devotees of the Lord in Karur. What service did he perform? If any suffering befell the devotees of the Lord, Eri-Pattha Nayanar would swiftly go to that place at that very moment, like an enraged lion, and alleviate that suffering. Lions hide in places like mountain caves. They only emerge from those caves when they have a necessity like hunger. When such a necessity arises, those lions emerge from the cave and bravely leap forward, fearless of any obstacle. Leaping onto an elephant is possible only for a lion. Other animals cannot do it. That is why Sekkizhar Swamigal here compares Eri-Pattha Nayanar to a lion. Just as a lion, when necessity arises, leaps forward bravely without fear of any obstacle, so too Eri-Pattha Nayanar would leap forward if any suffering befell the devotees of Shiva. In this story, suffering befell Sivakami Andar due to the king's royal elephant. Therefore, Eri-Pattha Nayanar went to that place and cut down the elephant. Sekkizhar Swamigal compares Eri-Pattha Nayanar to a lion because only a lion has the strength and power to fell an elephant. Now, the rage that Eri-Pattha Nayanar possessed was a rage that arose out of love to alleviate suffering when it befell the devotees of the Lord. It was a rage that came to protect Shiva Dharma. Therefore, this anger should not be considered as one of the mental defects, anger, which is forbidden by the Dharma Shastras. The anger that ordinary human beings experience arises from the three gunas. It arises from the mixture of Sattva, Rajas, and Tamas. But the anger that Nayanmars like Eri-Pattha Nayanar possessed was not born from this mixture of three gunas. It arose from the noble intention of protecting Shiva Dharma. Moreover, this anger is not the anger that others experience due to their individual consciousness (Jiva Bodham). Nayanmars, always being in the shadow of the Lord's grace, lose their individual consciousness (Jiva Bodham) and perform actions from Shiva Bodham (divine consciousness). Therefore, just as the Lord's anger is considered grace, and manifests as grace, so too the anger of the Lord's devotees is also grace. Just as the Lord's anger is called "Kopa Prasadam" (graceful anger), so too the Nayanmar's anger, which comes from grace, is "Kopa Prasadam." We must firmly remember that this is not the anger that ordinary worldly people experience. Eri-Pattha Nayanar always carried a battle-axe (Parasu) in his hand to remove the suffering that befell the devotees of Shiva. Parasu is the weapon in Lord Shiva's hand. "Parasu Hasthaya Namaha" is a Shiva Ashtottaram. Here, Eri-Pattha Nayanar also wielded a similar battle-axe, a Parasu. That is why Sundaramurthy Swamigal sings in Thiruththonda Thogai, "Ilai Malinda Vel Nambi Eri-Pattharkku Adiyen" (I am a servant to Eri-Pattha, the trusted one with the leaf-filled spear). This Parasu is a pure Parasu. This battle-axe, the axe, serves as an instrument for killing by throwing. Nevertheless, Sekkizhar Swamigal says here that the Nayanar carried a pure Parasu. This is because this axe aids in performing good Shiva Dharma, and it purifies those who cause suffering to the devotees of Shiva by punishing them. Therefore, this axe also becomes a pure Parasu. The service rendered to the devotees of God is of two types. One is to give what the devotees of God desire. This was done by Ilaiyankudi Maranayanar, Iyarpakai Nayanar, and others. The second type of service is the valor to come forward and alleviate any distress that befalls the devotees. This is the second type of service rendered to the devotees of God. Eri-Pattha Nayanar's service is of the second type. Similarly, that valor manifested in Viranminda Nayanar and Sakthi Nayanar, and their services in alleviating distress when it befell the devotees of God are also clearly understood by us. Thus, the Saiva religion, which was established by Eri-Pattha Nayanar's service to the devotees, flourished. And because of that, this world lived. What is Saivam? Saivam is being connected with Shiva. The world is filled with that Shiva. What kind of Saivam is this? It is "Maniya Saivam," meaning established Saivam. The special characteristic and nature of Saivam is to remain established and imperishable even in times of adversity. Although Saivam was established everywhere, it declined in some places. Just as during the time of Koon Pandiyan, Jainism flourished and the glory of Saivam diminished, so too in some places and at some times, Saivam declined. To ensure that Saivam remains established without such decline and to remove the obstacles that come to Saivam, Eri-Pattha Nayanar took up the weapon called Parasu in his hand. It is the daily prayer of Saivites that Saivam should flourish everywhere. When Saivam flourishes, rain will fall well. When rain falls well, the world will prosper. Thirugnanasambandar Peruman also says in Thiruppasuram, "Veezhga than punal vendhanum onguga" (Let cool water fall, and let the king prosper). Thus, when rain falls, the king will prosper. And because of that, the world will flourish. Thus, it is the Saiva tradition to pray that Saivam should be established, and because of that, rain should fall, and because of that, the king should rule well, and because of that, the world should be well. "Vaanmugil valaathu peyga malivalam surakka mannan konmurai arasu seyga kuraivillaathu uyirgal vaazhga naanmarai arangal onga natravam velvi malka menmai kol Saiva neethi vilanguga ulagamellam" (Let the clouds rain without fail, let abundant wealth flow, let the king rule righteously, let beings live without want, let the four Vedas and Dharmas flourish, let good austerities and sacrifices abound, let the noble Saiva justice shine throughout the world) is the daily prayer and supplication of Saiva devotees to God. Thus, Eri-Pattha Nayanar is a great devotee who lives serving for the flourishing of Saivam and the well-being of the world. In the same Karur, there was a devout devotee who performed the service of plucking flowers according to the rules, making garlands, and offering them to Lord Shiva, who is enshrined in the Thiru Aanilai temple. His name was Sivakami Andar. Sivakami Andar is a virtuous sage. Sivakami Andar is a name of the Lord. Its meaning is "the husband of Sivakami," the divine mother. The Lord is the husband of Sivakami, the divine mother, so He has the name Sivakami Andar. This virtuous sage, Sivakami Andar, was called by the very name of the Lord. Now, another meaning for the name Sivakami Andar is "one who has Lord Shiva as his master, with great love for Shiva." He is a sage who performs Shiva Punya (meritorious deeds for Shiva). Shiva Punyas are Chariya, Kriya, Yoga, and Gnana. Here, the Shiva Punya performed by the sage Sivakami Andar is Chariya, which is the first of the Shiva Punyas. Sivakami Andar had steadfast love for the Lord. Steadfast love is strong love that is not disturbed by anything. The love that ordinary people have in the worldly sense, expecting some benefit, is not like the love that our Sivakami Andar had. This was strong, pure love. The heart of the Veda is Lord Shiva. Only by worshipping Him can beings attain salvation. The love that our Sivakami Andar had for the Lord was the love that truly follows the conclusion of the Veda that the benefit of learning and the benefit of being born in this life is only to worship Lord Shiva. The Shiva Punya that Sivakami Andar performed out of that love was Chariya. One of the services of Chariya is to pluck flowers, string them into garlands, and offer them to the Lord. Plucking flowers, stringing them into garlands, and offering them are the nature and actions of the true service called Chariya. However, to pluck flowers, string them into garlands, and offer them to the Lord, love is very essential. Love is very essential. Only these true services called Chariya, performed with love, are true services. Any action performed without love is fruitless. That is why Sekkizhar says that Sivakami Andar performs service by plucking flowers with abundant love and offering garlands. He also says that he had steadfast love. Sekkizhar Swamigal explains in the next verse how Sivakami Andar undertook the service of plucking flowers and making garlands. Sekkizhar Swamigal states that Sivakami Andar performed this service, and through that, he teaches us, the people of the world, the method and rules of offering flower garlands. Now, let's see how Sekkizhar explains how Sivakami Andar performed this service. "Vaikarai pozhuthu" is dawn. The time of five nazhigai (approximately two hours) before sunrise is "vaikarai pozhuthu." It is the time when the previous day's "vaikuthal" (staying) is about to end. Therefore, it got the name "vaiku arai vaikaraip pozhuthu." It is the dawn that causes the night to pass. One should wake up at this "vaikarai pozhuthu." One should wake up realizing that "vaikarai pozhuthu" has arrived. Having risen thus, one should go out of the house to complete the morning rituals, proceed towards the Niruthi direction (south-west), and complete the morning rituals. This is Sekkizhar Peruman's statement, as interpreted by Sivakavimani. Now, Sivakavimani says that in these days, people defecate and urinate inside the house, collect the accumulated waste, and dispose of it outside the town, which causes various problems. He says that these are the harmful consequences and "upakarangal" (benefits) of modern civilization. This practice was not present in the village and city life of olden days. That is why people lived comfortably in those days. He says that this was the reason. Furthermore, Sivakavimani says that it was the custom of the people of those days to wake up early before sunrise, clean themselves as much as possible, worship God, go out to defecate and urinate, then brush their teeth, bathe, offer arghyam (water offering) to the rising sun, and then begin their daily life. We can find the details of this in books like Aachariya Noolkovai. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 08 — Eri-Pattha Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. We are contemplating the Puranam of Eri-Pattha Nayanar in the third chapter, Ilai Malinda Sarukkam, of Periya Puranam. Sekkizhar explains how Sivakami Andar performed his service. In the morning, we should bathe in cold water. Bathing in springs, ponds, and rivers with good flowing water is beneficial. Diving and bathing in water is pleasurable. We can find these rules in health scriptures. In contrast, bathing in hot water, drawing water from wells and filling small vessels to bathe in them, and other modern practices do not provide as much benefit. In any case, bathing in cold water, suitable for our country's nature, is beneficial to us. Now, bathing in water cleanses the body. If the body is clean, it contributes to the cleanliness of the mind. Without bodily cleanliness, mental cleanliness will not come. Rituals performed with an unclean body will be spoiled like milk kept in an unclean vessel, which is a truth affirmed by the wise. That is why Sekkizhar Peruman mentions "punal moozhgi" (bathing in water) here. Now, he mentions "vaayum katti" (tying the mouth). We must also remember this verse. That is the 559th verse. In the Puranam of Eri-Pattha Nayanar, the ninth verse is: "Vaikarai unarnthu ponthu punal moozhgi vaayum katti moy malar nerungu vaasa nantha vanaththu munni kaiyinil therinthu nalla kamazh mukai alarum velai theyva naayagarkku saaththum thiruppalli thaamam koythu." Here, Sekkizhar Swamigal says. We have seen the meaning of "vaikarai" (dawn). We have seen the meaning of "unarnthu" (having realized). We have seen the meaning of "ponthu" (having come). We have seen the meaning of "punal moozhgi" (having bathed in water). Now, Sekkizhar says "vaayum katti" (tying the mouth). When plucking flowers for the Lord, when going to the Nandavanam (flower garden), one must go with one's mouth tied with a cloth. This is the rule. This is because when making and offering garlands, impure air, vapor, sneezes, coughs, saliva, etc., should not come out of our mouth and affect the flowers, thereby defiling them. That is why we must tie our mouth and go to the Nandavanam. Moreover, when plucking flowers, the fragrance of the flowers should not enter our nose and cause us to smell the fragrance of the flowers. This is because these flowers are to be offered to the Lord. Therefore, we should not smell their fragrance. If we do, it is equivalent to the flower being defiled by saliva. Therefore, we must tie our mouth and go into the Nandavanam. Therefore, when making flower garlands and when plucking flowers for the garlands, one must keep one's mouth tied in this manner. Even today, in the sacred place of Kathirkamam, the priest performs his duties daily with his mouth tied. All the Shiva Agamas state this. But in these days, many Nandavanam workers, when plucking flowers and making garlands, and inside the Nandavanam and inside the flower mandapam, speak whatever comes to their mouth, spit saliva, sneeze, cough, chew betel leaves and tobacco, and perform their duties without bathing or cleaning their hands and feet, and commit such improprieties. This is very regrettable. Not only that, we see today the forbidden acts of defecating and urinating inside the sacred Nandavanam and near it, under and near the trees, plants, and creepers used for the Lord's worship, and on the ground covered with Arugampul (Bermuda grass) which is dear to the Lord, and committing other impurities. Sivakavimani writes that elders say that those who commit these acts will only attain hell. Not only that, providing facilities for residence inside the Lord's sacred Nandavanam, eating meals, having snacks and other enjoyments, sleeping, etc., are also Shiva Aparadham (offences against Shiva) and improprieties. Sivakavimani writes with sorrow that there are even wicked people who defecate and urinate fearlessly under the Vilva tree inside the temple. Therefore, the officials in charge of maintaining and managing the sacred Nandavanam and temple officials must pay attention to these and rectify them. That responsibility rests with them. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman sings, encompassing all these rules in the phrase "vaayum katti." Thus, when going inside the sacred Nandavanam with the mouth tied and plucking flowers, one must do so while contemplating the feet of the Lord. One must pluck flowers, leaves, roots, etc., that are suitable for the Lord, and their appropriate maturity for plucking, according to the rules. The time to go to pluck flowers is dawn. Therefore, it is not possible to pluck flowers, etc., by seeing them with the eyes. Therefore, through the special practice of plucking flowers, one must carefully select and pluck the suitable flowers with one's hands. For this, we must recall the story of Vyagrapadar, who received hands with eyes. When plucking flowers, one must distinguish between old flowers that bloomed the previous day, buds that are about to bloom tomorrow, and other such stages of maturity by practice, and pluck only the suitable flowers and discard the unsuitable ones. Only such known and plucked flowers should be strung into garlands for the Lord. These are called Thiruppalli Thaamam. Furthermore, Sivakavimani says that in the Shiva Agamas and books like Pushpa Vidhi, one can find rules such as which flowers are suitable for the Lord, which are not, which flowers are suitable for the Goddess, which are not, which flowers are suitable for which deities, and on which days which flowers should be plucked and on which days they should not be plucked. Now, Arumuga Navalar, in his commentary on the book "Saiva Samaya Neri" written by Maraignanasambandha Nayanar, explains the same concept in detail. We can also see that here. Arumuga Navalar, who wrote a commentary on the book "Saiva Samaya Neri" by Maraignanasambandha Nayanar, states thus: Before sunrise, one should bathe, wear clean, dry clothes, complete Sandhyavandanam (morning prayers) and other rituals, wash both hands, take a flower basket, attach it to the end of a stick, and reach the sacred Nandavanam either holding it above the head or above the waist. There, with thoughts always on Lord Shiva, one should collect leaves, flowers, etc. One should not let the hands hang below the waist, or keep shaking the hands, or keep touching the clothes. Speaking, laughing, and contemplating anything other than the sacred feet of Lord Shiva while collecting leaves and flowers are faults. Breaking the branches of plants, creepers, and trees in the sacred Nandavanam is a great fault. After plucking leaves and flowers, one should cover them with leaves, reach the sacred mutt, wash the feet there, enter, lift the sacred flower basket, wash the sacred garlands with water, place the leaves and flowers from the sacred flower basket in it, and silently and carefully examine the flowers and leaves, remove the damaged ones, and string the sacred garlands. People of low caste, those who have not received Shiva Diksha (initiation), those who are in a state of impurity (Aasoucham), those who have abandoned daily rituals (Nithya Karmam), those who have not bathed, and those who are wicked (Thoorthar) - flowers plucked by these people, and flowers grown in impure places, near cremation grounds, near Chandala lands, and flowers that have bloomed after being plucked and kept, flowers that have fallen by themselves, old flowers, withered flowers, buds, flowers plucked at night, flowers brought in cloth, Erukku leaves, castor leaves, flowers struck by wind, flowers with worms, excrement, cobwebs, threads, hair, smelled flowers, and flowers touched by crows, chickens, dogs, donkeys, pigs, monkeys, cats, etc., should not be offered. They are not suitable for the Lord. Flowers plucked at sunrise, or three nazhigai (about 1 hour 12 minutes) before dawn, or two nazhigai (about 48 minutes) before dawn, or one nazhigai (about 24 minutes) before dawn, are considered excellent. Flowers plucked at noon are not suitable for Lord Shiva, Devas, ancestors, or human beings. At dawn, Nandiyavarttapoo and Vellerukkampoo are excellent. At noon, Alarippoo, Thamaraippoo, and Thumbaippoo are excellent. In the evening, Mallikaippoo, Karumaththampoo, and Senbagappoo are excellent. At midnight, Kadappampoo, Ponmaththampoo, Siru Senbagappoo, and Pichchippoo are excellent. Thulasi is not suitable for Vinayagar. Thazhampoo is not suitable for Lord Shiva. Arugampul is not suitable for Uma Devi. Nelliyum (gooseberry) is also not suitable. Nandiyavarttam is not suitable for Bhairavar, Vilvam for Surya, Akshathai for Vishnu, and Thumbai for Brahmins. Vilvam should not be plucked on Mondays, Chaturthi, Ashtami, Navami, Ekadasi, Chaturdasi, Amavasya, Pournami, and the beginning of the month. Vilvam can be plucked and kept on other days. Those who circumambulate the Vilva tree daily with the sound "Harahara" will be freed from sin. If the soil from under the Vilva tree is smeared on the body at the time of death, they will attain Shivapuram. The Vilva leaf is the form of Lord Shiva's Iccha (desire), Gnana (knowledge), and Kriya (action). Thulasi should not be plucked on Sundays, Mondays, Tuesdays, Fridays, Thiruvonam star, Sapthami, Ashtami, Dwadasi, Chaturdasi, Amavasya, Pournami, Vyathipatha Yogam, the beginning of the month, morning, evening, and night. Vilvam and Kokkarkku Mandarai can be kept for six months. White Thulasi can be kept for one year, Thamaraippoo (lotus) for seven days, and Alarippoo (oleander) for three days. Thus, Sivakami Andar performs the service of plucking flowers according to the rules stated in the Saiva Agamas and in books like Pushpa Vidhi. He plucks flowers, fills them in the sacred flower basket, and then strings the sacred garlands needed for the Lord. Flowers should be plucked and then strung, and then offered for the Lord's worship. The flowers should be plucked and strung separately for the morning, evening, and night worships. Flowers prescribed for those specific times should be strung. Therefore, Sivakami Andar walks very quickly to provide flowers for the Lord's first worship in the morning, called Thiru Ananthal. Sekkizhar Peruman states here that the incident where Eri-Pattha Nayanar cut off the elephant's trunk, and all these divine events, happened on the day before Maha Navami. Therefore, even now, in this temple in Karur, this traditional festival is held annually on the day before Maha Navami. According to this tradition, the flowers required for the Lord's morning worship are provided as an endowment by the great mother of Sivakavimani Ayya, the commentator of Periya Puranam, Karur Meenambal, through a charitable trust, as noted by Sivakavimani. Thus, we will continue to contemplate what happened when the virtuous sage Sivakami Andar was walking through the streets with garlands of flowers for the Lord's early morning worship. Chapter 09 — Eri-Pattha Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. We are contemplating the Puranam of Eri-Pattha Nayanar in the third chapter, Ilai Malinda Sarukkam, of Periya Puranam. Sivakami Andar, a virtuous sage, was performing the service of offering sacred garlands, called Thiruppalli Thaamam, to Pasupathinathar, who is enshrined in Karur. As usual, one day, he woke up early in the morning, plucked flowers according to the rules, made them into garlands, and was walking through the streets of Karur to offer those flowers for the early morning worship of Thiru Aanilai Appar. That day was the day before Maha Navami. It was a day when the divine will intended to reveal the greatness of Sivakami Andar, the eminence of Eri-Pattha Nayanar, and the glory of Pugazhchola Nayanar to the world. That day was the day before Maha Navami. Maha Navami is a Saiva observance dedicated to Ambika, performed for nine days starting from the first day of the waxing moon in the month of Purattasi. What is an observance or vow? Arumuga Navalar of Jaffna writes in his book "Saiva Vinavidai" thus: What is a vow? A vow is the special worship of God with true devotion, according to rules, by mind, speech, and body, by abstaining from food or reducing food, so that the mind does not wander through the senses. Among these, Saivites are prescribed vows such as Shiva Vratam, Devi Vratam, Vinayagar Vratam, Subramaniya Kadavul Vratam, Bhairavar Vratam, and Veerapaththira Vratam. Arumuga Navalar mentions that the three vows for Ambika are Sukravara Vratam, Aippasi Uttara Vratam, and Navaratri Vratam. Among these three vows, one is Navaratri Vratam. On the first day of Maha Navami, which is Ashtami, this divine manifestation is about to occur in this Karur, in the Puranam of Eri-Pattha Nayanar. On such an Ashtami, the day before Maha Navami, Sivakami Andar, as usual, was carrying flowers for Shiva Puja in the morning when the royal elephant of Pugazhchola Nayanar, who was then ruling Karur, was walking through those streets. In this Puranam of Eri-Pattha Nayanar, the Puranam of Pugazhchola Nayanar is also intertwined. During the period when the Puranam of Eri-Pattha Nayanar took place, Pugazhchola Nayanar was ruling Karur. He is also one of the 63 Nayanmars. His story, history, and character are separately sung in the chapter "Poyyadimai Illatha Pulavar Sarukkam." "Pozhil Karuvur Thunjia Pugazhcholarukku Adiyen" (I am a servant to Pugazhcholar who passed away in Karuvur) is from Thiruththonda Thogai, sung by Sundaramurthy Swamigal. Although his history is sung separately there, Pugazhchola Nayanar also appears in the Puranam of Eri-Pattha Nayanar here. Through the royal elephant of King Pugazhchola, the king of Karur, Eri-Pattha Nayanar's heroic deed is revealed here, and the greatness of the service of both Eri-Pattha Nayanar and Pugazhchola Nayanar is demonstrated to the world. There was a royal elephant belonging to the glorious Pugazhchola. A royal elephant in those days was an elephant that held the leadership of the elephant army and was ridden by the king. It was also called Pattavarthanam. The methods of selecting a royal elephant are extensively described in Gajashastra (the science of elephants). For an elephant to be a king's royal elephant, it must be flawless. It must have an upright head, tusks of equal size, broad and forward-pointing, and milky white in color. Its eyes must sparkle with a honey-like hue. There must always be a calmness on the elephant's face. Excellent obedience, an attractive body structure, a tail with thick hair, beautiful nails, unbent ears, a back like a bed for a person to sit on, placing its feet correctly on the ground without changing its stride while walking, maintaining its surroundings cleanly, and a nature that is friendly with people who yearn for affection, especially children, and obeys commands - these are the Samudrika Lakshanas (auspicious marks) of a royal elephant. Such qualities are found in only one out of ten thousand elephants. It is said in various Gajashastras that the body of such a royal elephant emits the fragrance of a lotus flower. Pugazhchola Nayanar's royal elephant was bathing in the Amravathi river, which is now called Amaravathi, in Karur on this day, the day this divine manifestation was to occur. We contemplated the greatness of this Amravathi river in the Karur Sthala Puranam. This Amravathi river is now called Amaravathi river. It originates near Thirumoorthy Hills near Coimbatore and flows. It merges with the Kaveri river at Thirumukkoodal near Karur. The Thiru Aanilai temple of Karur is located on its northern bank. We saw in the Karur Sthala Puranam that the grace of Uma Devi flowed as the Amaravathi river. In the bathing ghats of such a glorious Amaravathi river, there is a ghat suitable for elephants to bathe. The royal elephant bathed in that ghat. After that, this royal elephant came running, exuding rut. Elephants exude a lot of rut fluid due to extreme joy. It is said that a rutting elephant runs uncontrollably and acts according to its own will. Thus, the king's royal elephant came running, exuding rut. Along with it, the spear-wielders (Kutthukkolkarargal) also came running. The spear-wielding mahouts (Kutthukkol Paagar) are those who carry spears and other piercing weapons to control the elephant, and they run in front of it and on both sides, clearing people and controlling the elephant, regulating its movement. Just like the mahouts who drive the elephant from above with an ankus (elephant goad), these are mahouts who drive the elephant by walking on the ground. They are also responsible for the elephant's orderly movement. Thus, the spear-wielders were running on both sides of the elephant. The royal elephant came running like a great black hill moving. Generally, there are separate streets for the royal elephant to pass. Such a street where the royal elephant passes would be filled with decorations and have a lot of human traffic on that day when Maha Navami was about to occur. If he went through that street with human traffic, it would delay carrying flowers to the Lord. For that reason, Sivakami Andar deviated from the main street and quickly walked through another street, carrying the Thiruppalli Thaamams. By the grace of God, the elephant, which was running, exuding rut, now came running along the street where Sivakami Andar was carrying the flower basket containing the Thiruppalli Thaamams. Generally, it is stated in the Pushpa Vidhi (rules for flowers) that flower garlands meant for the Lord should not be carried hanging below the waist, isn't it? Therefore, Sivakami Andar tied the flower basket to a stick and carried it above his head, walking very quickly. Even though he walked quickly, Sekkizhar Swamigal says that the flowers inside the basket were sleeping. That is, even though Sivakami Andar walked quickly, the flower basket did not sway due to his speed, and the flowers inside did not hit each other and get damaged. Sekkizhar Swamigal carefully sings that Sivakami Andar walked quickly while the flowers slept undisturbed in the flower basket. Due to the long-standing practice of carrying flowers for the Lord in this manner for many days and periods, he would have known the art of carrying them in such a way that no harm would come to the flowers inside, even if he walked quickly. This is an implicit meaning that this skill came to Sivakami Andar due to his long-standing service to the Lord. While Sivakami Andar was walking, the king's royal elephant came running down that street. The mahouts on the elephant were driving it. Nevertheless, it was running due to rut. It came running and snatched the basket containing the Thiruppalli Thaamams from Sivakami Andar and scattered the Thiruppalli Thaamams, the sacred garlands, on the street. Seeing this, the mahouts on the elephant quickly drove the elephant away, as if driving a hurricane. The elephant was naturally running fast due to its rut. After scattering the flower garlands, the mahouts on it drove the elephant even faster. Therefore, Sekkizhar Swamigal says that the mahouts drove the elephant as if driving a hurricane. The nature of a hurricane is that it is not under anyone's control and comes very fast, tossing everything it catches. Here, the elephant, being in rut and out of control, deviated from the royal street where the royal elephant usually walks and went to another street, snatched the flower basket from Sivakami Andar, scattered the sacred garlands inside, and thus, because the royal elephant came out of control, Sekkizhar Swamigal compares it to a hurricane. The mahouts drove such a hurricane even faster. The mahouts on the royal elephant were the servants of the great devotee of Shiva, King Pugazhchola, weren't they? A doubt might arise in our minds: even after seeing the elephant commit the Shiva Aparadham (offence against Shiva) of scattering the sacred garlands meant for Lord Shiva, did the mahouts on it ignore it, that is, ignore this Shiva Aparadham committed by the elephant, and drive the elephant even faster? If we interpret that these mahouts, who were the servants of Pugazhchola Nayanar, a great devotee of Shiva, ignored the elephant committing a Shiva Aparadham, it would not be correct. We saw earlier in the Puranam of Meypporul Nayanar that just as King Meypporul Nayanar was a devotee of Shiva, so too his palace servants were devotees of Shiva. That is why we know that Meypporul Nayanar's servants also possessed the Shiva devotion to open the palace gates to anyone who came to the palace in the guise of Shiva. Here, Pugazhchola Peruman is also a great devotee of Shiva. Therefore, his servants, the mahouts of the royal elephant, must also have been excellent in Shiva devotion. Nevertheless, if they ignored the elephant committing the Shiva Aparadham of scattering the sacred garlands meant for Lord Shiva and drove the elephant even faster, why did they do so? We must interpret that they did so out of fear that if they did not drive the elephant faster, further Shiva Aparadham might occur due to this royal elephant. Shiva Aparadham had already occurred. They drove the elephant faster out of a sense of caution that no further Shiva Aparadham should occur due to this elephant. Sivakami Andar, the virtuous sage with a sacred thread on his chest, who was carrying the Thiruppalli Thaamams to offer to the Lord, was distraught and agitated when he saw the elephant scatter them on the ground. After that, he looked at the elephant. Sivakami Andar ran to hit the elephant that had done this. Sivakami Andar went after the elephant to punish it for the Shiva Aparadham it had committed, trying to hit it with a stick. The virtuous sage Sivakami Andar could not quickly catch that huge elephant. Even if he caught it, he could not hit it. Even if he hit the elephant, if the rutting elephant attacked him in return, Sivakami Andar did not have the strength to protect himself. Even if he hit the elephant, that punishment would not be an adequate punishment for the great Shiva Aparadham committed by the elephant. Even though all these reasons existed, Sivakami Andar's attempt to hit the elephant, his agitation, and his overflowing emotions occurred only because of the immense love Sivakami Andar had for the Lord's service. Sivakami Andar quickly ran and tried to hit, but he did not hit. This is because the rutting elephant quickly went away. Due to his great old age, Sivakami Andar could not chase and catch the elephant to hit it. He fell down. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 10 — Eri-Pattha Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, the third canto, Ilai Malinda Sarukkam, the Purana of Eri-Pattha Nayanar. The elephant, which had scattered the garlands offered to the Lord and run away, could not be caught by Sivakami Andar, who, due to old age, fell down. Distressed by this, Sivakami Andar fell to the ground, beating it with his hands in great sorrow. Then, rising from the ground, he was greatly distressed and trembled with sweat, remembering the Lord, and cried out, "Sivada, Sivada," because the Lord's service that he regularly performed was interrupted today. "Sivada" is a cry of lamentation, remembering the Lord. Sekkizhar Swamigal has sung four verses describing Sivakami Andar's cries of "Sivada, Sivada." These verses describe the greatness of Lord Shiva and his cries of "Sivada, Sivada." The verses are as follows: "Kaḷiyāṉaiyiṉ īr uriyāy Sivada, Eḷiyār valiyām iṟaivā Sivada, Aḷiyār aṭiyār aṟivē Sivada, Teḷivār amutē Sivada Sivada. Āṟum matiyum maṇiyum caṭaimēl ēṟum malarai kari cintuvatē vēṟuṇ niṉaivār puram ventaviya cīṟum cilaiyāy Sivada Sivada. Tañcē caraṇam pukutum tamiyō neñcē tuyaram keṭa nēr toṭarum mañcē eṉa vīḻ maralikkum iṟai nīḷ ceñcēvaṭiyāy Sivada Sivada. Neṭiyōṉ aṟiyā neṟiyār aṟiyum paṭiyāl aṭimai paṇicey toḻukum aṭiyārkaḷil yāṉ ārā aṇaivāy muṭiyā mutalā eṉavē moḻiya," thus sings Sekkizhar Swamigal. Let us now look at its explanation. First, he sings, "Kaḷiyāṉaiyiṉ īr uriyāy Sivada." Its meaning is, "Oh Lord, who wears the skin of the intoxicated elephant as a covering!" Sivakami Andar calls out to Lord Shiva, who once flayed the skin of the demon Gajāsura and wore it as a covering to remove the troubles of the Devas. Therefore, He is known as Gajasamhara Murthy and has the form of Gajasamhara. This history is found in Puranas, including the Kanda Puranam. Next, Sivakami Andar cries out, "Eḷiyār valiyām iṟaivā Sivada." The Lord is the protector of the poor. Therefore, its implied meaning is, "Oh Lord Shiva, who bestows strength upon the weak and removes their suffering!" "If the weak are tormented by the strong, the strong will be tormented by the Lord in the two worlds," is a hymn to Bhairava in the Kanchi Puranam. Its meaning is that if the strong torment the weak, the Lord will torment the strong. It is said that Vamana, who defeated Mahabali, was punished by Sattinathar, who came as Bhairava. Here, the elephant tormented Sivakami Andar with its strength, its arrogance, and its pride in being associated with the king. Therefore, Sivakami Andar cries out, implying, "Oh Lord Shiva, who has the power to protect me by punishing that elephant!" Next, Sivakami Andar cries out, "Aḷiyār aṭiyār aṟivē Sivada." Sivakami Andar says to the Lord, "Oh Lord Shiva, the knowledge of the devotees who love You is the knowledge contained within Your pervasiveness. Therefore, You Yourself become the knowledge of the devotees. So, You know the suffering that arises within me without my even having to tell You. If I were to ask You to remove that suffering, it would be superfluous." Sivakami Andar cries out, "Oh Sivada, who possesses boundless compassion to alleviate the suffering of the entire world!" Now, the next line is, "Teḷivār amutē Sivada Sivada." Its meaning is, "Oh Lord, You are the nectar to those who clearly understand Your divine qualities. Here, where my mind is confused, You are the one who clarifies it and helps like medicine, Sivada, Sivada." Next, he speaks to the Lord, saying, "Āṟum matiyum maṇiyum caṭaimēl ēṟum malarai kari cintuvatē." Our Lord is the one who wears the Ganga and the moon in His matted locks. He wore the Ganga to subdue its arrogance. He wore the crescent moon to give it life. The story of the Lord wearing the Ganga in His matted locks, protecting Bhagiratha who bowed at His feet, and protecting this world from destruction as Gangadhara Murthy, is the story of His wearing the Ganga. The Lord's wearing of the moon signifies His great generosity in bestowing joys upon those who seek refuge in Him and protecting them. Alas, the elephant has scattered these flowers, which should have been placed on Your matted locks, where the Ganga and the moon reside! Is it not Your duty to protect me, who has made the service of offering flower garlands to You my very life? asks Sivakami Andar. Next, he says to Lord Shiva, "Vēṟu uḷ niṉaivār puṟam ventaviya cīṟum cilaiyāy Sivada Sivada." These lines refer to the Lord's act of destroying the three cities (Tripura Samhara). To burn the three cities, the Lord merely smiled a little. With that smile, the three cities burned to ashes. But in those three cities, three individuals continued to worship Shiva and perform meritorious deeds without fail. Everything else except them was burned to ashes. Sivakami Andar sings, "Oh such a Lord!" Next, Sivakami Andar sings, "If one takes refuge in the Lord, He will come directly and protect them." The Lord does not act through others to protect those who seek refuge. He Himself will come directly and protect them. When Yama, like a dark cloud on a buffalo, tried to take the life of Markandeya Muni, the Lord appeared and kicked Yama a little with His foot. Thus, Yama escaped with punishment. The history of Kala Samhara states that if those sacred feet had been a little longer, Yama would have been destroyed. This is the history of kicking Kala with His foot. This divine act occurred in Thirukadavur, one of the eight Veerattams. Sivakami Andar cries out, "Oh Lord, whose sacred red feet kicked Kala, granting auspiciousness, Sivada, Sivada!" Next, Sivakami Andar earnestly prays to the Lord. "Oh Lord Shiva, the path of Shiva is unknown even to Vishnu. Those who stand on that path of Shiva and serve in devotion are Your devotees. I am not worthy to be one of Your great and glorious devotees. I have no right to it. How then will You alleviate my suffering, who has neither worthiness nor right? I am a dog, good for nothing. That is why You stand there without embracing me." He melts, saying, "Oh Sivada, the endless beginning!" Thus, as Sivakami Andar cried out "Sivada, Sivada" remembering the Lord, Eri-Pattha Nayanar appeared there, who would appear immediately and remove any suffering that befell the devotees. The pervasiveness of Shiva, which is of the same nature, resided in the minds of both Sivakami Andar and Eri-Pattha Nayanar. Therefore, Sivakami Andar lamented to the Lord, "You will not come and protect me. I am not so devoted to You that You would come and protect me. I am not one of Your true great devotees." All those words were filled with the pervasiveness of Shiva. That word, that cry, filled with the pervasiveness of Shiva, struck the mind of Eri-Pattha Nayanar, who was also within that same pervasiveness of Shiva, and Eri-Pattha Nayanar resolved to remove that obstacle. When devotees faced injustice, Eri-Pattha Nayanar would appear like a lion and remove the obstacle. Therefore, Eri-Pattha Nayanar appears here in the same way. Eri-Pattha Nayanar, breathing heavily like three burning fires with great anger, says, "Is this elephant not a constant enemy to the devotees of Ambalavanar? I will kill and fell that elephant!" He came, wielding his deadly battle-axe. Sekkizhar describes Eri-Pattha Nayanar as saying that the elephant is a hereditary enemy to the devotees of the Lord. Gajāsura was an enemy of Lord Shiva. Thus, that elephant was an enemy of the Lord. Therefore, all elephants descended from that elephant were enemies of the Lord and enemies of the devotees who worshipped the Lord, which is why he calls it a "hereditary enemy." No one is an enemy to the Lord. However, why is it said here that the elephant was an enemy to the Lord? Because the elephant named Gajāsura tried to destroy the cosmic order by acting contrary to the rules laid down by the Lord, it became an enemy. That is why this elephant is sung about here as a "hereditary enemy." Furthermore, the battle-axe that Eri-Pattha Nayanar holds in his hand is a deadly axe. It is an axe used for killing as a punishment. Although killing is a sinful act, it is done out of love for the sacred feet of Lord Shiva. Therefore, it becomes a meritorious act. "The sin committed by a devotee out of love for the Lord's feet is also righteousness. The merit of those who have no love for the Lord's feet is sin," says Sivagnana Siddhiyar. Therefore, even the sinful acts committed by true devotees who love the Lord's feet, while worshipping and serving the Lord, become meritorious acts, acts of Shiva's merit. Even the meritorious acts of those who have no love for the Lord's feet become sins. Eri-Pattha, who appeared thus, saw Sivakami Andar, the devotee who had called out "Sivada" to Lord Shiva, and bowed to him, asking, "Where has the elephant gone that caused you such great distress?" To this, Sivakami Andar replied, "The elephant, which snatched the flowers meant for our Lord and scattered them on the ground, has gone down this very street." Although Eri-Pattha Nayanar came with great anger, upon seeing Sivakami Andar, he bowed to the devotee out of devotion. "To those who have joined the feet of Him who has neither desire nor aversion, there is no suffering anywhere," says Thirukkural. Its meaning is that the Lord is free from desire and aversion, these two. To those who have joined His feet, there is never any suffering. But here, Eri-Pattha Nayanar asked Sivakami Andar, "Where has the elephant gone that caused you suffering?" This implies that Sivakami Andar suffered. However, according to the Thirukkural we saw earlier, there is no suffering for those who have joined the feet of the Lord. So, what was the suffering that Sivakami Andar experienced here? This was not Sivakami Andar's personal suffering. He had no personal joy or sorrow. But the service of the Lord was currently interrupted, and this suffering that came to Sivakami Andar was a suffering that arose on account of the Lord. It was a suffering that arose due to his complete love for Lord Shiva, thinking, "The Lord's work has been interrupted!" Therefore, the underlying meaning is that this suffering occurred due to the interruption of the Lord's work. To this, Sivakami Andar says, "The elephant that scattered the flowers I had kept for the Lord's service and escaped went down this street." "Piḻaitta yāṉai" (escaped elephant) has two meanings: an elephant that committed a mistake, and an elephant that escaped with its life. The rule is that the atonement for a sin against Shiva is capital punishment. Sivakami Andar says here that even though this elephant committed a sin against Shiva, it escaped with its life. He says this to convey this inner meaning. We will continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 11 — Eri-Pattha Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. We are reflecting on the Purana of Eri-Pattha Nayanar from the third canto, Ilai Malinda Sarukkam, of the Periya Puranam. Eri-Pattha Nayanar, with anger, holding the battle-axe in his hand, went to the place where the elephant was, like a great storm, saying, "Now I will see how that elephant lives and thrives." The battle-axe he held in his hand glowed like fire. Just as an angry lion pounces on an elephant and tears its forehead with its sharp claws, Sekkizhar Swamigal compares Eri-Pattha Nayanar to a lion because he punished the elephant. Eri-Pattha Nayanar, approaching the elephant, saw it and thought, "This elephant is like the elephant that our Lord Shiva once flayed and destroyed." Therefore, Eri-Pattha Nayanar declared, "Even if the Devas and kings try to stop me, I will not care; I will punish and kill this elephant." The elephant's trunk caused the offense against Shiva. Therefore, Eri-Pattha Nayanar resolved to cut off only the trunk from the elephant's body and cast it down. Punishing the offending limb is one of the traditions of punishment. In the Purana of Chandeswara Nayanar, he cuts off the leg of his father who committed an offense. Seruthunai Nayanar cuts off the nose of the king's wife who smelled the flowers kept for Shiva Puja. Kalarshanga Nayanar cuts off the hand of that queen. We can see many such histories. As Eri-Pattha Nayanar pounced on the elephant, the red-eyed elephant leaped towards him. Eri-Pattha Nayanar intercepted the elephant, pounced on it, and severed its long, large trunk with his battle-axe, causing it to fall to the ground. Seeing this, Sekkizhar Swamigal sings, "Can fear stand before a mother's love? It cannot." If the intoxicated elephant had leaped and pounced on him, it would have been dangerous for Eri-Pattha Nayanar's life. A normal person, if an elephant leaped on them like this, would fear for their life. But Eri-Pattha Nayanar did not fear. This is because Eri-Pattha Nayanar's love for the devotee here was like a mother's intense love. A mother protecting her child, if any danger befalls the child, will try to protect it, and in that effort, she will not care about the danger to her own life, but out of love for the child, she will not fear and will strive to remove that danger. The love that Eri-Pattha Nayanar had was also like this mother's love. The conclusion of the scriptures is that love conquers fear. Therefore, Eri-Pattha Nayanar, due to his mother-like love for the devotees, did not fear even when the elephant leaped on him. As Eri-Pattha Nayanar severed the elephant's trunk and cast it down, the elephant cried out like the sea and fell, rolling on the ground like a black mountain. Eri-Pattha Nayanar killed all five people: the three mahouts who came near the elephant with their goads, and the two mahouts who drove the elephant from its back. Those nearby who witnessed this event quickly rushed to the king's palace and reported that the royal elephant, Pattavardhanam, and its mahouts had been killed. How did they convey the information to the kings? Those who witnessed the event told the palace guards, who then conveyed the news to the king. The king, upon hearing the news that his royal elephant and mahouts had been killed, became very angry and set out like a young lion, without even asking who had done this. Seeing the king set out, the ministers also proclaimed for their armies to assemble, and they too set out with the king. Mechanical chariots also set out with them. Mechanical chariots mean chariots equipped with machines. These chariots were equipped with machines that hurled weapons at enemy forces. Such chariots contained weapons like "Nootruvar Kolli" (killer of a hundred) and "Aayiravar Kolli" (killer of a thousand). It is noteworthy here that the technology to operate such machines that launched weapons existed in those days. Next, cavalry and elephant forces set out together. Armies carrying bows set out. Forces carrying a type of throwing weapon called "Pindipalam" also set out. Soldiers carrying weapons like "Musalam" (iron pestle), "Kazhukkadai" (trident), and "Mazhu" (battle-axe) accompanied them. Thus, the four types of forces—elephant, cavalry, chariot, and infantry—set out with the king. Conches blared, and drums boomed. War instruments like "Pambai," "Kandai," "Thimilai," "Viyanthudi," and "Thatti" (bear drum) blared. Thus, the armies advanced like the sea rising furiously during a deluge, or like the destructive wind during a cosmic dissolution. As all the armies followed, the king, riding on a magnificent horse and holding a cool, compassionate royal umbrella, proceeded along the royal street. The king quickly reached the spot where the elephant and mahouts had died. But there were no enemies there. Eri-Pattha Nayanar stood there, holding a shining battle-axe, like an elephant with two trunks. The king did not see any enemies on the field where his royal elephant lay suffering. But he saw an elephant there. It was a subdued elephant. It seemed to have two trunks. Sekkizhar Swamigal here describes Eri-Pattha Nayanar as a great elephant with two trunks. The elephant that committed the offense against Shiva had one trunk. But with that trunk, it committed an offense against Shiva. But here, Eri-Pattha Nayanar, standing like an elephant, has two great hands, like two trunks. These were good trunks, not like the elephant's trunk in form or quality. The elephant's trunk caused harm to the devotees. The two great hands of Eri-Pattha Nayanar, who was like an elephant, were hands that removed the suffering of the devotees. The king, who had come with great anger, thinking this was an act of enemies, found no enemies but saw a devotee of Shiva, a beloved servant of the Lord. But the thought did not occur to the king that Eri-Pattha Nayanar, a devotee of Shiva, would have done such a thing. The king, thinking that Eri-Pattha Nayanar, a devotee of Lord Shiva, who always performs the blissful dance in the sacred hall and possesses the silver mountain, would not have killed the elephant and mahouts, asked Eri-Pattha Nayanar himself, "Who did this?" As the king asked Eri-Pattha Nayanar, the other mahouts went to the king and said, "Oh King, who else in this world would have the power to oppose your elephant? It is this Eri-Pattha, who stands before you with the battle-axe, who caused this harm to your elephant." Hearing this, the king firmly believed in his mind that Eri-Pattha, a great devotee of Lord Shiva who wears earrings, would not commit murder. If he had committed murder, it meant that a mistake had occurred to such an extent that he had to kill. Having thus resolved, he stopped all the armies that had come with him and went alone to Eri-Pattha Nayanar. The king was convinced that if Eri-Pattha Nayanar had punished the elephant, then the elephant must have committed a mistake to such an extent that he had to punish it. Of the three proofs—Pratyaksha Pramana (direct perception), Anumana Pramana (inference), and Agama Pramana (scriptural testimony)—the king's decision here was based on Anumana Pramana. What is Anumana Pramana? As an example, elders speak of fire and smoke. There is no smoke without fire. If we only see smoke in a place, but not fire, we infer that fire, which is the cause, must also be present there, don't we? This is what elders give as an example of Anumana Pramana. It is based on this proof that the king here decides that the royal elephant committed a mistake, and Eri-Pattha Nayanar punished it. Punishing to rectify a mistake is the justice shown by the Lord through the Vedas. We can see the destruction of Daksha's sacrifice, the destruction of Sura, and other events as evidence of this. Elders call the Lord's punishment of those who commit mistakes "forgetful compassion." The king feared that a great mistake had occurred to make Eri-Pattha Nayanar, a devotee of Ambalavanar, so angry. Eri-Pattha Nayanar was a devotee who worshipped Thiruanilaiyudaiyar in Karur and served the devotees who were dedicated to the Lord. Therefore, the king worshipped him as a true devotee. The garb worn by Eri-Pattha Nayanar was a true garb, and therefore the king, out of love for Eri-Pattha Nayanar, regarded him as a true devotee. The king feared that such a great mistake had occurred to make Eri-Pattha Nayanar, a true devotee, so angry. This is because those who love Shiva also love all living beings. If such a devotee, who loves all living beings, inflicts punishment on a living being, it implies that a great offense against Shiva has occurred. We saw that the king stopped all his retinue at a distance and came alone to Eri-Pattha Nayanar, didn't we? The king's retinue merely reported the news without stating the truth that this was an act committed by a devotee of Shiva as punishment for the elephant's offense against Shiva. The king's retinue is like the senses to the soul. The senses, united with the soul, should convey the worldly matters they interact with directly to the soul. But generally, the senses, united with the soul, delude the soul and lead it to action. Similarly, here, the king's retinue, by not telling the truth, was about to put the king in a situation of offense against Shiva. Therefore, the king kept them at a distance, considering them to be removable, and approached Eri-Pattha Nayanar. The king, approaching Eri-Pattha Nayanar, said, "I did not know that such a great offense against Shiva had occurred. What I heard was something else. Is killing the elephant and killing the mahouts sufficient as a solution for the mistake caused by the dead elephant? Please enlighten me," the king said to Eri-Pattha Nayanar, standing with folded hands. Then Eri-Pattha Nayanar looked at the king and said, "Oh King, this elephant snatched and scattered the sacred flowers that Sivakami Andar had gathered to offer to the Lord. Therefore, I severed its trunk, causing it to fall to the ground. The elephant thus committed an offense against Shiva. And the mahouts who came with the elephant, and the mahouts who drove the elephant from its back, did not prevent the elephant when it committed this harm, and so they too died. This is what happened here," said Eri-Pattha Nayanar. Hearing this, the king was afraid and fell at the feet of Eri-Pattha Nayanar, worshipping him. Why was the king afraid here? Because an offense against Shiva and an offense against a devotee had occurred, hadn't they? Therefore, he was afraid of that. This is what the books of justice call "fear of evil deeds." "Not fearing what should be feared is foolishness; fearing what should be feared is the work of the wise," says Thirukkural, chapter on Wisdom, verse 428. Its meaning is that not fearing what should be feared is a quality of foolishness. Fearing what should be feared is the work of the wise. Furthermore, we should also consider what Manikkavasagar Peruman has graciously stated in Achchappattu in Thiruvachagam. Thus, the king, fearing, properly worshipped Eri-Pattha Nayanar by falling at his feet. It is the prescribed method in the scriptures to properly worship great people who purify oneself. Properly worshipping means worshipping with five limbs (Panchangam) and with eight limbs (Ashtangam). We will continue to reflect on Thirukkural.

Chapter 12 — Eri-Pattha Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. We are reflecting on the Purana of Eri-Pattha Nayanar from the third canto, Ilai Malinda Sarukkam, of the Periya Puranam. The king, who fell at the sacred feet of Eri-Pattha Nayanar and worshipped him, said to Eri-Pattha Nayanar, "The royal elephant scattered the sacred garlands meant for Lord Shiva, who wears serpents as ornaments. Therefore, you severed the elephant's trunk and killed the elephant and its mahouts. For such a great offense against Shiva, this punishment is a small one. The punishment you inflicted for such a great offense against Shiva is not enough. Therefore, as punishment for that offense against Shiva, you must kill me too. Because I kept that elephant and mahouts who caused the offense against Shiva, I am also responsible for this offense against Shiva. I must take responsibility for this offense against Shiva. Therefore, as a solution, you must kill me too," thus King Pugazhchozha requested Eri-Pattha Nayanar. King Pugazhchozha Nayanar was also a great devotee of Shiva. Therefore, from the perspective of a devotee, he now determines this crime. From the perspective of a king, he himself determines and states the appropriate punishment for that crime. Furthermore, Pugazhchozha Nayanar says, addressing Eri-Pattha Nayanar, "I am not pure enough to be killed by the auspicious battle-axe you hold in your hand. I am not worthy enough to be killed by your battle-axe. Because you are a very great devotee. I am a very small person. Moreover, because this offense against Shiva occurred through me, I have now become degraded. Therefore, I am not worthy enough to be killed by your battle-axe," thus King Pugazhchozha said to Eri-Pattha Nayanar, and drawing his sword, he gave his sword to Eri-Pattha Nayanar, saying, "You must kill and punish me with this sword." The inner intention of King Pugazhchozha was that this punishment would serve as an atonement for the offense against Shiva, preventing the miseries of hell and other torments due to the offense against Shiva. Eri-Pattha Nayanar was distressed to see King Pugazhchozha draw his sword and hand it to him, requesting him to kill him. Immediately, he looked at King Pugazhchozha and thought, "Oh, I have been defeated! I have now witnessed the boundless nature of the love of this king of endless glory." Thinking this, he did not take the sword offered by the king. But realizing that if he did not take the sword offered by the king, the king might even attempt to kill himself, Eri-Pattha Nayanar took the sword with his hand to prevent that harm from befalling the king. Sekkizhar Swamigal, who came to describe the greatness of Pugazhchozha Nayanar, calls him "the one of endless glory." The meaning of this is that there is no end or limit to the glory of Pugazhchozha Nayanar. Therefore, "Pugazhchozhar" (Glorious Chozha) became a descriptive name for this Nayanar. Just as his glory was immeasurable and boundless, so too was his love for Shiva and Shiva's devotees boundless. Eri-Pattha Nayanar delayed a little in taking the sword offered by the king. This was because he did not agree with the king's action. After a slight delay, Eri-Pattha Nayanar immediately took the sword from the king's hand. This was because Eri-Pattha Nayanar thought, "If I do not take that sword, the king, as he said earlier, will destroy this body that has erred against the devotee as a solution for the mistake." Fearing that the king would kill himself, Eri-Pattha Nayanar took the sword that the king held. Was King Pugazhchozha's act of attempting to kill himself just? Is suicide, which is "Atmahathi," not a forbidden act in our Dharma Shastras? Is it not an act condemned by our Dharma Shastras? Furthermore, human birth, that is, the human body, is a very rare birth to obtain. It is a very rare body, the human body. "Among countless births, human birth is rare," is the saying of the elders. If one obtains such a rare human birth, then to renounce such a great human birth, one must renounce it to attain an even greater blessing, say the wise. "If the body perishes, the life perishes; one cannot attain firm true knowledge. Knowing the means to nourish the body, I nourished the body and nourished the life," says Thirumoolar Peruman in Thirumandiram. "The Supreme One resides within the body, therefore I cherish and protect the body," is also from Thirumoolar's Thirumandiram. Appar Swamigal graciously said, "If one can see the Lord, then human birth is indeed desirable in this world." The benefit of this birth, having taken this body, is obtained only when one worships the sacred feet of the Lord, says the teacher. Our Pugazhchozha Nayanar was a great devotee and lover of the Lord and His devotees. Thus, our King Pugazhchozha had attained the definite benefit that should be achieved by taking this body. Is it appropriate for him to renounce this body, which obtained this rare human birth? What was the reason why Pugazhchozha Nayanar dared to renounce his body? It was because of the rule that this body, which had the sin of supporting the offense against Shiva, must be destroyed. In this age, in this country and in other countries, many people destroy their own bodies for trivial reasons. Similarly, they destroy the bodies of others. Sivakavimani writes that the world of this age, which wanders without knowing the true path, should gain strength to understand the efficacy of karma and be saved by the act of Pugazhchozha Nayanar, who sought to kill himself, and then Eri-Pattha Nayanar, who tried to take his own life. Thus, Eri-Pattha Nayanar takes the sword from King Pugazhchozha's hand. The king rejoices upon seeing Eri-Pattha Nayanar take the sword from his hand. King Pugazhchozha rejoices, thinking, "Eri-Pattha Nayanar is about to do me a great favor by killing me and rectifying my mistake. I have received that great blessing from Eri-Pattha Nayanar." Seeing this, Eri-Pattha Nayanar is afraid, thinking, "Even though his elephant and mahouts died, this great loving King Pugazhchozha offers his sword and says, 'Kill me too for my great mistake!' Was it not against him that I harbored ill will?" Eri-Pattha Nayanar takes the sword from King Pugazhchozha's hand only to prevent King Pugazhchozha from killing himself with that sword. But King Pugazhchozha, seeing Eri-Pattha Nayanar take the sword, rejoices, thinking that Eri-Pattha Nayanar has taken the sword from his hand to cut him. But if Eri-Pattha Nayanar did not do so even after taking the king's sword, Eri-Pattha Nayanar feared that the king would somehow accomplish that act. So, before the king could kill himself, Eri-Pattha Nayanar resolved to take his own life, placing the sword at his neck and attempting to carry out that act. Eri-Pattha Nayanar takes the sword from his hand and places it at his neck, attempting to cut it. This was something King Pugazhchozha did not expect. King Pugazhchozha, alarmed that such a great mistake was about to happen, rushed forward and firmly grasped Eri-Pattha Nayanar's hands and the sword he held. The king held the sword and Eri-Pattha's hands together so that Eri-Pattha Nayanar could not perform that act. Seeing the actions of both King Pugazhchozha and Eri-Pattha Nayanar, Lord Shiva, out of immense love for both of them, caused a divine voice to rise from the sky, audible to many, to remove that suffering that had arisen. That divine voice spoke as follows: "Oh great ones, worthy of worship by all, who are supreme in love! This incident, where the elephant angrily scattered the sacred flowers, occurred to demonstrate the nature of your sacred service in this earthly world." This was the divine voice that arose in the sky. As soon as this divine voice sounded, the dead mahouts came back to life. The dead elephant also came back to life immediately. Eri-Pattha Nayanar first killed the elephant, and then its mahouts. But now the mahouts rise first. The elephant then immediately came back to life. Whatever was dissolved last appears first in creation. Therefore, the mahouts who died last rose first. After that, the elephant came back to life. However, at the same time, without any perceptible difference, both the mahouts and the elephant rose. When did this happen? It happened at the very moment the divine voice sounded in the sky of knowledge. Eri-Pattha Nayanar, who had raised the sword to cut his neck, dropped the sword and fell at the sacred feet of King Pugazhchozha. He worshipped him. King Pugazhchozha Nayanar, the great king, also threw away the sword and praised Eri-Pattha Nayanar, falling to the ground and worshipping his sacred feet. Why did Eri-Pattha Nayanar fall at the sacred feet of Pugazhchozha Nayanar and worship him? What did the divine voice that appeared in the sky of knowledge say? It said that the elephant scattering the flowers happened by divine grace, didn't it? But what did Eri-Pattha Nayanar think before? He thought that the elephant scattering the flower garlands was an offense against Shiva. But the divine voice that arose in the sky said that it happened by divine grace. Therefore, Eri-Pattha Nayanar had mistakenly understood the act that happened by divine grace as an act of offense against Shiva. But now Eri-Pattha Nayanar understood. The elephant and the mahouts did not commit an offense against Shiva. This happened only due to divine grace. King Pugazhchozha was the instrument for this realization to come and for the divine grace to manifest as this divine voice. That is why Eri-Pattha Nayanar first fell at his sacred feet and worshipped him. Seeing this, Pugazhchozha, the king, fell at the sacred feet of Eri-Pattha Nayanar and worshipped him. This is because Eri-Pattha Nayanar was the brave and greatly loving devotee who was instrumental in the manifestation of divine grace. Therefore, King Pugazhchozha fell at the sacred feet of Eri-Pattha Nayanar and worshipped him. After that, both of them stood up and praised the sound of that great divine voice that arose in the sky of knowledge. That divine voice was a voice that arose in the sky of knowledge. Sekkizhar Swamigal here refers to that light, the light of that sound, which is the primary cause of pure Maya, as a great sound and a divine voice because it came from the Lord, who is the instrumental cause of everything. This was the sound of grace that arose in the sky of knowledge, called Chithakasa. Therefore, they praise the divine grace that caused it to arise. Then Lord Shiva, by His divine grace, caused the sacred flowers to fill the beautiful flower basket as they were before. Sivakami Andar, seeing that act of divine grace, rejoiced, having attained a great life. We will continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 13 — Eri-Pattha Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. We are reflecting on the Purana of Eri-Pattha Nayanar from the third canto, Ilai Malinda Sarukkam, of the Periya Puranam. By the grace of the Lord, the sacred flowers in the sacred flower basket were filled again. Seeing this, Sivakami Andar rejoiced. These flowers were the very flowers that the angry elephant had snatched and scattered on the ground. Generally, flowers scattered on the ground are not suitable for the worship of the Lord. It is stated in the Sivagamas as a rule of flowers (Pushpa Vidhi) that flowers scattered on the ground should not be used for the Lord's worship. That is why Sivakami Andar lamented and complained when the elephant scattered the flowers on the ground. But now, those flowers have again filled the sacred flower basket by the grace of the Lord. So, the question may arise in us: are these flowers suitable for worship? But what did the divine voice that sounded in the sky of knowledge say? Both the elephant scattering the sacred flowers and the same flowers now filling the sacred flower basket again happened due to divine grace. They happened by the grace of the Lord. Therefore, even though they were scattered on the ground, the rule we saw earlier does not apply now. These flowers are indeed suitable for worship. Because those flowers were scattered by the grace of the Lord and again filled the flower baskets by the grace of the Lord, they have been sanctified twice by the grace of the Lord, haven't they? Therefore, these flowers are suitable for the Lord's worship and service. That is why Sivakami Andar also rejoices greatly. The mahouts who had come back to life, driving the elephant that had also come back to life, stood beside the king with great joy. The dead elephant and mahouts coming back to life was like them waking up from sleep. Sekkizhar beautifully sings that they rose as if waking from sleep. "Death is like sleep, and birth is like waking from sleep," says Thirukkural. Furthermore, Sekkizhar Swamigal says that the elephant that had now come back to life was exuding rut fluid. It is customary for rut fluid to flow when the elephant's body experiences intense heat due to joy and other factors. Here, the elephant that rose was filled with great joy. Due to that joy, rut fluid flowed from the elephant. Not only that, but the elephant trumpeted and roared with great joy. Then Eri-Pattha Nayanar bowed to King Pugazhchozha and said, "Oh King, please ascend this great elephant and proceed to your palace with joy, so that I may behold and rejoice." The king, who had come to this place on horseback, heard Eri-Pattha Nayanar's words and ascended the elephant. This elephant was now an elephant that had come back to life by the grace of the Lord. Therefore, it was a divine प्रसाद (prasāda) of the Lord. It was fitting for King Pugazhchozha, who had great love for the Lord, to ride on this elephant, which was a divine प्रसाद of the Lord. Thirugnanasambandar Peruman rode on the pearl palanquin bestowed by Lord Shiva. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal rode on the white elephant sent by the Lord. We can recall these histories that occurred by the command of the Lord here. As King Pugazhchozha rode on the elephant, his re-energized armies roared with a sound equal to that of seven oceans joining together. All the people of the world rejoiced and blessed him. The celestials showered flowers from the Kalpaka tree. King Pugazhchozha Nayanar, who bore the sacred feet of the Lord who performs the eternal dance in the golden hall on his head, went to his palace. Sivakami Andar, who had diligently performed the sacred service of offering garlands to the Lord residing in Karur Thiruanilai with unwavering devotion, proceeded towards the temple of Thiruanilai to offer the flowers that he had received by the grace of the Lord. Eri-Pattha Nayanar, amazed by the greatness of King Pugazhchozha, thinking, "Who can comprehend the greatness of the devotees of Lord Shiva who dances in the hall?" also went to continue his sacred service. "The greatness of devotees is greater than words," is the saying of Avvaiyar, isn't it? Pugazhchozha Nayanar was a king. Within that royal form, he lived, embodying the greatness of a devotee more than the greatness of a king, which is difficult to comprehend, wasn't he? Eri-Pattha Nayanar said this, remembering that greatness again and again. Furthermore, we will see the greatness of Pugazhchozha Nayanar in his individual Purana in the Periya Puranam. "I am a devotee of Pugazhchozhar who slept in the groves of Karuvur," is the saying of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal in Thiruthondathogai. The idea that "whatever profession one does, whatever state one is in, the minds of the liberated remain in silence" is also noteworthy here. Eri-Pattha Nayanar, who came forward to remove the injustices that befell the devotees, remembered and melted at the greatness of love in King Pugazhchozha, who considered that he had caused harm to a devotee and was willing to give up his own life. He meditated. Thus, Eri-Pattha Nayanar goes to continue the sacred service he had undertaken. Sekkizhar Swamigal calls this service performed by Eri-Pattha Nayanar a "fierce great service." When injustices befell the devotees, he removed them with his battle-axe, overcoming all opposition. Therefore, that service is called a fierce service. This was a service rendered to the devotees who had great love for the Lord. Therefore, it was called a great service. Eri-Pattha Nayanar not only performed service himself but also enabled other devotees to perform their service without hindrance. Therefore, what he did is called a great service. Service is of two types: gentle service and fierce service. Gentle service means the service of performing the prescribed practices like Chariya, etc., as enjoined by the scriptures. What is fierce service? It is doing what is difficult for others in the world to do, going beyond the prescribed rules in the Dharma Shastras. Here, Sivakami Andar, Pugazhchozhar, Murugar, Thandiyadigal, Kungiliyakalayar, and other Nayanmars performed gentle service. Eri-Pattha Nayanar, Kottpuli Nayanar, Seruthunaiyar, Sakthi Nayanar, and other Nayanmars performed fierce service. "What is a gentle act, what is a fierce act difficult for the great people of the world, both, if spoken of, are Shiva Dharma," says Thirukalittruppadiyar. Sekkizhar Peruman further says, "Only the Lord, who bestows grace upon them day after day, fully knows the heroic act performed by Eri-Pattha Nayanar, the devotee of the Lord, and the greatness of the Chozha king who stood ready to give his own sword to be killed. Who has the strength to measure the greatness and qualities of their immense sacred service? No one can measure their greatness," thus Sekkizhar Peruman sings in wonder. Thus, Eri-Pattha Nayanar continued to perform this great service of removing the sufferings that befell the devotees of the Lord for a long time in this world, and then attained the leadership of the Ganas, becoming the chief among the chiefs of the divine hosts of Lord Shiva on Mount Kailash. There are many Ganas on Mount Kailash. The chiefs of those Ganas are separate. Here, Eri-Pattha Nayanar attained the leadership of being the chief among the chiefs of those Ganas. Sivakavimani states the following lessons we learn from this sacred Purana of Eri-Pattha Nayanar: When injustices befall the service of devotees, coming forward to remove them and enabling their sacred service to proceed without hindrance is a great sacred service. Plucking and stringing appropriate flowers with love and according to rules, and offering them to Shiva at the right time, is a rare Chariya sacred service. It is special for the country to celebrate festivals on Mahānavami. Even animals and other creatures of lesser intelligence, if they commit an offense against Shiva, it is evil. If they do so, they must be punished and reformed. It is the duty of those who drive and maintain those animals to prevent them from committing such offenses against Shiva. Deviating from that duty is also an offense against Shiva. These are grave crimes and sins. Even in current law, it is found that those who protect and own animals are responsible for the offenses caused by them. When an obstacle arises in their service to Shiva, appealing to Shiva Himself is the action of the elders. When harm befalls those one loves, going to protect them without fear of any great harm that may come to oneself in the attempt to prevent it, is the nature of those who have supreme love. By attaining the support of Shiva's sacred feet, Shiva's devotees become very brave. They become capable of doing anything with a fearless heart. They are swallowed by great anger. They lose the mental integrity to investigate thoroughly. "To examine a thing without bias or prejudice is the quality of the wise," says the book of justice. Just like excessive anger, preventing excessive joy from overwhelming our minds is the benefit of having wisdom. It is the duty of ministers to follow the king's instructions and protect the world. Devotees of Shiva are people of great character. They get angry only when a mistake occurs. They also punish other living beings. When they see the devotees they love, their love for them increases many times more than their love for any other worldly possessions they cherish. This is the nature of true devotees. It is the duty of everyone to prevent harm from befalling others due to their possessions, and if such harm occurs, to take responsibility for it. This duty is especially great for kings. Furthermore, if these mistakes are offenses against Shiva, it is the nature of great devotees to fear greatly for them and even attempt to offer their lives as atonement. The tools used in Shiva's service and in the service of devotees are auspicious. Those who are punished by them become purified. It is also noteworthy that when encountering devotees, along with other auspicious items, the beauty of the temple, etc., are also held as auspicious items. All obstacles that seem to arise in the true service performed by devotees are guided by the grace of the Lord. The Lord Himself will come forward and remove them. The love of devotees is immeasurable. No one other than Lord Shiva, who bestows grace upon them, can measure the greatness of devotees. Killing, stealing, etc., are evil, according to the books of justice, religious texts, and secular texts. But it is not right to consider them evil everywhere without considering their intent. Killing those who obstruct Shiva's sacred service is prescribed in great scriptures. Here, killing is not an evil; it is a meritorious act of Shiva. Whoever causes hindrance to sacred service will suffer greatly in the afterlife, experiencing the terrible pains of hell. Therefore, killing those who commit offenses against Shiva is to protect them from suffering the pains of hell. Even in secular law, prescribing capital punishment for murderers is based on this idea. "To the king, punishing the cruel is like weeding a field of green crops," says Thirukkural. It is the conclusion of true scriptures that those who kill or steal for reasons like greed for other living beings, and other cruel people, are greater sinners than those who harm the service of devotees. Thus, Sekkizhar Swamigal, placing the sacred feet of Eri-Pattha Nayanar, the devotee who had unceasing desire for the golden feet of Lord Shiva, who wears the Konrai flower in his red matted locks, on his head, concludes his Purana and begins to narrate the sacred service performed by Enadinatha Nayanar, who lived a life praising the sacred ash of Ambalavanar. We too will conclude this Purana of Eri-Pattha Nayanar and reflect on the Purana of Enadinatha Nayanar by the grace of the Lord. Thiruchitrambalam.
