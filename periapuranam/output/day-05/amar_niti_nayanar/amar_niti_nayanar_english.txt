Chapter 01 — Amar-Niti Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. In the Periya Puranam, in the second chapter, Thillai Vaazh Andhanar Sarukkam, we have so far contemplated the Puranas of Thillai Vaazh Andhanars, Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar, Iyarpagai Nayanar, Iḷayankudi Maara Nayanar, Meypporul Nayanar, and Viranminda Nayanar. Now, we are about to contemplate the Purana of Amar-Niti Nayanar. According to the sequence, the Purana of Amar-Niti Nayanar is the seventh Purana. First, we will look at a summary of the story, and then we will extensively contemplate the Purana of Amar-Niti Nayanar through the verses of Sekkizhar Peruman's Periya Puranam. Our Amar-Niti Nayanar was born in the Vaishya community in the town of Pazhaiyarai in the Chola country. Our Amar-Niti Nayanar was a great wealthy man. Amar-Niti Nayanar lived with great devotion to Shiva and devotion to Shiva's devotees, serving them sacred food, understanding their needs, and providing them with necessities such as kaupinam (loincloths). At that time, a grand festival (Mahotsavam) was taking place in the nearby Shiva temple of Thirunallur. Therefore, to serve sacred food to the Shiva devotees who came to witness the Mahotsavam in Thirunallur, our Amar-Niti Nayanar went to Thirunallur with his family and relatives and built a mutt (monastery) there for the Shiva devotees to partake of sacred food. In that mutt, he served sacred food to Shiva devotees and provided them with other necessities. One day, during these times, Lord Shiva appeared in the form of a Brahmin celibate (Brahmachari) at Amar-Niti Nayanar's mutt. Lord Shiva, who came in the form of a Brahmin celibate, was holding a staff in his hand. Tied to that staff were a vibhuti bag and two kaupinams. Seeing a Brahmin celibate Shiva devotee coming to his mutt, Amar-Niti Nayanar was overjoyed. He went forward to greet and worship the Brahmin celibate, saying, "Swami, what penance did I perform in my previous birth to have you grace this place?" and welcomed him with kind words. Then, the Brahmachari addressed Amar-Niti Nayanar, saying, "I have heard that you serve sacred food to devotees and provide them with clothes and kaupinams. Desiring to see you, I have come." Hearing this, Amar-Niti Nayanar prayed, "Swami, in this mutt, Brahmins also cook for Brahmins to dine. Therefore, Your Holiness should also partake of sacred food here." The Brahmachari who had come agreed to this, saying, "I will go and bathe in the Kaveri. If it rains then, please keep this dry kaupinam for me to wear." Saying this, he untied one of the two kaupinams tied to the staff in his hand and gave it to Amar-Niti Nayanar. He asked him to keep the kaupinam safely and return it to him after he had bathed and returned. Saying this, the Brahmachari went to bathe in the Kaveri. Amar-Niti Nayanar carefully placed the kaupinam given by the Brahmachari in a safe place. Lord Shiva, the Brahmachari who had gone to bathe, made the kaupinam he had given to Amar-Niti Nayanar disappear. Then, Lord Shiva, the Brahmachari, while returning after bathing in the Kaveri, caused rain to fall and, drenched in the rain, reached Amar-Niti Nayanar's mutt. Amar-Niti Nayanar, seeing the Brahmin celibate approaching, went forward to greet him and worshipped him, saying, "The cooking is done, and the food is ready." Now, Lord Shiva, in the form of the Brahmachari, addressed Amar-Niti Nayanar, saying, "It rained on my way here, and I got drenched. Therefore, the kaupinam tied to the staff in my hand has become wet. So, please bring the kaupinam I gave you." Immediately, Amar-Niti Nayanar went inside and looked for the place where he had kept the kaupinam. The kaupinam was not there. He searched in other places as well. The kaupinam was not found in other places either. Therefore, greatly distressed, he took another kaupinam and went before the Brahmachari, saying, "Swami, the kaupinam you gave me is missing. I had kept it safely. However, it is now missing. Even after searching in other places, I could not find it. I do not know how it disappeared. I have brought another good kaupinam now. This is not a torn kaupinam. It is a specially woven kaupinam. Therefore, Your Holiness should remove your wet kaupinam and wear this one, and forgive the fault I have committed." Amar-Niti Nayanar stood praying. Hearing this, Lord Shiva, in the form of the Brahmachari, became very angry and said, "What you say is very good. It has not been many days since I gave you the kaupinam. I gave it just now. In such a situation, you have taken my kaupinam and are asking me to accept another kaupinam. What a wonder is this? You have spread news in the town that you will give kaupinams to all Shiva devotees, and now you have taken my kaupinam. Your business is doing very well," said Lord Shiva, who came in the form of the Brahmachari. Hearing this, Amar-Niti Nayanar was frightened, his face fell, and he trembled, saying, "Swami, I did not commit this fault knowingly. Somehow, the kaupinam you gave me has disappeared. Therefore, you must forgive this fault. I will perform all the services you need. Besides this alternative loincloth, I will also give you much gold, silk garments, jewels, and so on. Please accept them," he said. To this, the Brahmin celibate, appearing as if his anger had subsided, said, "Why do I need gold, silk garments, and jewels? It is enough if you give me another kaupinam of equal weight to the useful kaupinam I wear." Amar-Niti Nayanar was delighted and asked, "To the weight of which kaupinam should I give an equal weight?" Then the Brahmachari said, "This is the kaupinam that is equal in weight to the kaupinam you lost," and untied the kaupinam tied to the end of the staff in his hand, saying, "Give me a kaupinam of equal weight to this." Amar-Niti Nayanar said, "Very well," and brought a balance and placed it there. The Brahmin celibate placed his kaupinam on one pan. Amar-Niti Nayanar placed the newly woven kaupinam in his hand on the other pan. At that time, since the balance was not of equal weight, the pan containing the loincloth placed by Amar-Niti Nayanar rose up. Seeing this, Amar-Niti Nayanar, thinking that he must provide a kaupinam of equal weight to the Brahmachari's kaupinam, brought all the kaupinams he had kept to give to Shiva devotees and placed them on the other pan. Even after placing them, Amar-Niti Nayanar's pan still rose higher. The pan containing the Brahmachari's kaupinam remained below. Seeing this, Amar-Niti Nayanar was astonished and brought various kinds of clothes and silks and kept adding them. As he kept adding them, his pan continued to rise.

Astonished by this, Amar-Niti Nayanar worshipped the Brahmachari and said, "Even after placing countless clothes and silks, it is not equal to your kaupinam. Therefore, please permit me to place my other possessions as well." To this, the Brahmin celibate said, "What more is there for me to say? Try placing your other possessions as well. Anyway, it must stand at an equal weight to my loincloth." Immediately, Amar-Niti Nayanar brought loads of nine gems, gold, silver, and other metals and placed them. Still, the pan remained elevated. Amar-Niti Nayanar, seeing this, worshipped the Brahmachari and said, "None of my possessions are left now. I have placed everything on this pan. Now, only I, my wife, and my son remain. If it pleases Your Holiness, please permit us to also ascend this pan." Lord Shiva, in the form of the Brahmachari, agreed to this. Seeing that, Amar-Niti Nayanar was delighted. He worshipped the Brahmin celibate, circumambulated the balance clockwise with his wife and son, and said, "If we have not faltered in our service to Shiva devotees, then as soon as we ascend, let this pan become equal to the pan containing the Brahmachari's kaupinam." Saying this, he worshipped Parameswaran residing in Thirunallur, recited the Sri Panchaksharam, and joyfully ascended the pan. As soon as they ascended, the kaupinam adorned on the sacred waist of Parameswaran and the unwavering devotion of Amar-Niti Nayanar, who was a servant of Shiva, were equal in greatness. Thus, the two pans of the balance stood equally. All who witnessed this miracle worshipped Amar-Niti Nayanar and praised him. The Devas showered flowers from the Kalpaka trees in the sky like rain. Lord Shiva, the Lord of Thirukailasam, concealed his form as a Brahmin celibate and appeared in the sky, mounted on the Rishabam (bull), with Goddess Parvati. Amar-Niti Nayanar, his wife, and his son worshipped them. Lord Shiva, casting his gracious gaze upon the three of them, blessed them, saying, "You three shall receive Our grace and worship Us in Our presence," and then disappeared. Amar-Niti Nayanar, his wife, and his son, by the grace of that God, ascended to the Shiva Loka (abode of Shiva) as the balance itself transformed into a vimana (celestial vehicle). They forever immersed themselves in the great flood of Shiva's bliss and experienced the joy of liberation. This is the concise history of Amar-Niti Nayanar. Arumuga Navalar, who provided the summary for this Purana, writes that building mutts in Shiva temples and serving sacred food to worthy Shiva devotees with sincerity and devotion during auspicious times and festival periods, and providing them with clothes, kaupinams, and other necessities, is a great act of merit. As evidence for this, he quotes from texts such as Shiva Dharmotharam and Shiva Punniya Thelivu. Now, let us extensively contemplate the Purana of Amar-Niti Nayanar through the verses of Sekkizhar's Periya Puranam. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 02 — Amar-Niti Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. We are contemplating the Purana of Amar-Niti Nayanar in the second chapter of Periya Puranam, the Thillai Vaazh Andhanar Sarukkam. We have seen the concise history of Amar-Niti Nayanar's Purana. Now, let us extensively contemplate the Nayanar's Purana. The phrase about Amar-Niti Nayanar in Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's Thiruthondathogai is "Allimen Mullaiyandhar Amar-Niti-kku Adiyen" (I am a servant to Amar-Niti, who wears a garland of soft jasmine). Contemplating this through the verses of Thiruthondathogai, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal sings, "I am a servant to the devotees of Thillai Vaazh Andhanars, I am a servant to Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar, I am a servant to Iyarpagai, who never said 'no', I am a servant to Ilaiyan Kudi Maran's devotees, I am a servant to Meypporul, who was very skilled in winning, I am a servant to Viranmindar of Kundrai surrounded by vast groves," and then adds the phrase about Amar-Niti Nayanar: "Allimen Mullaiyandhar Amar-Niti-kku Adiyen." First, let us contemplate the meaning of this line from the anthology Thiruthondathogai by Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, and then we will proceed to contemplate the type and detailed texts in order. The Purana of Amar-Niti Nayanar is the seventh Purana in sequence. "Allimen Mullaiyandhar Amar-Niti-kku Adiyen." Its meaning is, "I am a servant to Amar-Niti Nayanar, a merchant who wears a soft jasmine garland with inner petals." Sundaramoorthy Swamigal sings "Allimen Mullaiyandhar Amar-Niti," meaning Amar-Niti Nayanar who wears a garland of jasmine flowers. What kind of jasmine flower is it? It is "Allimen Mullaiyandhar," meaning a soft jasmine garland with inner petals. "Alli" means inner petal. "Pulli" means outer petal. Generally, flowers have two types of petals: inner and outer. It is our custom to call the inner petal "alli" and the outer petal "pulli." For all flowers, the inner petal called "alli" and the outer petal called "pulli" are different. They have different shapes. But what is the specialty of the jasmine flower? Here, the inner petal, "alli," and the outer petal, "pulli," are exactly alike. They do not appear different; the outer petal is just like the inner petal. This is the special characteristic of the jasmine flower. Similarly, the inner meaning here is that Amar-Niti Nayanar's inner and outer self, that is, his inside and outside, were one. Furthermore, jasmine (mullai) is the symbolic garland for merchants. And what kind of jasmine flower is this? Sundaramoorthy Swamigal adds the epithet "very soft jasmine flower." Jasmine flowers are very soft, fragrant, and yield flowers for a long time. Not only that, but they also yield many flowers. Thus, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal sings here, "I am a servant to Amar-Niti Nayanar, who wore a garland made of soft jasmine flowers, whose inner and outer petals are one." Now we have seen the anthology. Next, let us look at the verse from the type text, Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi. "Mindum Pozhil Pazhaiyarai Amar-Niti Venpodiyin Mundan Dharitta Prarkku Nallurin Mun Kovana Ner Kondinggarulendru Than Perunchelvamum Thanmaiyum Thandrunda Madhi Nudhalaalaiyum Eendha Thozhilinane" is from Nambiyandar Nambi's Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi. Now, let us contemplate the Purana of Amar-Niti Nayanar through Sekkizhar's Periya Puranam, the detailed text. The town where Amar-Niti Nayanar was born is Pazhaiyarai. This is a town in the good Chola country. The Cholas were a lineage of long-standing glory, spreading the greatness of Lord Shiva's sacred white ash everywhere.

They were descendants of Emperor Sibi. Their country was the Chola country. That Chola country also had the name Ponni Nannadu. Ponni is another name for Kaveri. Because the Kaveri river flows through it, this good Chola country is a land of great prosperity. Another name for Kaveri is Ponni. Ponni means a river that brings gold particles. In the Periya Puranam, in the Thirunattu Sirappu, we saw that the Kaveri brings gold particles in its waters. This Kaveri river was compared to Shiva devotees there. Shiva devotees would offer water, flowers, and gold and other precious gems in fragrant water, perform Arghya, Achamana, and Padya, and worship the Lord with abhishekam. Similarly, the Kaveri river, with its abundant waters, encircles and circumambulates countless Shiva temples on both its banks, offering the flowers it carries to the Lord in those temples and worshipping Him. Therefore, we must remember here that Sekkizhar, in the Thirunattu Sirappu, described this Kaveri river as a parallel to the actions of Shiva devotees. "Punniyam Seyvorkku Poo Undu Neer Undu" (For those who do good deeds, there are flowers and water) is a Thirumandhiram. Arghya water and Thirumanjana water should contain gold and other precious gems and flowers; this is the characteristic of Arghya water and Thirumanjana water. Why does the Kaveri perform Shiva Puja when it flows through the Chola country? Because, due to its connection with Agastya, the great sage Agastya, it receives the blessing of performing Shiva Puja. The previous state of the Kaveri was the Ganga. When it was the Akasha Ganga, it did not receive the blessing of performing Shiva Puja. But when that Akasha Ganga flowed from Agastya's kamandalu (water pot) and then, after the kamandalu was overturned by Lord Ganesha, flowed into the Chola country, it performed Shiva Puja. This is because, in its previous state as Akasha Ganga, it did not receive the blessing of performing Shiva Puja. But it was in the kamandalu of Agastya, the great Shiva sage, wasn't it? Due to that connection, when it later flowed as the Kaveri, it received the merit of performing Shiva Puja. Indra, fearing Asuras like Surapadman, took refuge in Sirkazhi and worshipped the Lord by maintaining a garden there. When those gardens withered without water, Indra worshipped Lord Ganesha and prayed. At that time, Agastya was bringing the Akasha Ganga in his kamandalu to the South. In Kongu Nadu, Ganesha overturned that kamandalu, and it flowed as the Kaveri, entering the Chola country and making the garden in Sirkazhi flourish. This is a history known to all of us. Its details are extensively and gloriously described in the Kandapuranam. Thus, in the great Chola country, where the Kaveri flows, there is a town of enduring glory, surrounded by flower gardens, called Pazhaiyarai. This Pazhaiyarai is a place worshipped by the moon. It has two temples. The one in the north is known as Pazhaiyarai Vadathali, a separate temple, and the one in the west is known as Aararai Melthali, a separate temple. In the Pazhaiyarai Vadathali temple, the Jains once concealed the Swami's shrine. Appar Swamigal, knowing this, undertook a hunger strike, vowing not to leave the place until he could directly see the Lord. By the command of the Lord, the king identified and removed the concealments made by the Jains, revealed the sacred form of Lord Shiva, and allowed Appar Swamigal to have darshan, thus eradicating the Jain community. This is its previous history. In Pazhaiyarai Vadathali, Vimali, the daughter of Kamadhenu, performed puja. Therefore, the name of the Goddess here is Vimala Nayaki. In Pazhaiyarai Melthali, Sabali, another daughter of Kamadhenu, performed puja. Therefore, the name of the Goddess there is Sabala Nayaki. Nandhini, another daughter of Kamadhenu, performed puja at Muzhaiyur, a Maadakkoil, which is a secondary temple. This is also located nearby. It is considered very special to worship the five temples surrounding Pazhaiyarai – Nallur, Valanjuzhi, Sakthimuttram, Pattiswaram, and Avoor – on the same Dakshinayana Punniya day. In such a glorious town of Pazhaiyarai, our Amar-Niti Nayanar was born into the merchant community. Amar-Niti Nayanar was abundantly blessed with gold, pearls, other precious gems, and various fine silks (such as silk garments) and other riches from all lands. Therefore, he excelled greatly in trade. Sekkizhar Peruman here specifically mentions that Amar-Niti Nayanar possessed gold, pearls, and good gems. Gold is different from pearls and other gems. Although pearls are also gems, they are very special. Therefore, he mentions pearls separately and other gems separately. Why are pearls different from other gems? Because pearls are born as complete gems. Furthermore, other navaratnas (nine gems) only shine and reveal their natural brilliance when cut and polished. But pearls naturally possess the quality of shining. They shine from birth. That is why Sekkizhar Peruman mentions pearls separately and other good gems separately here. Amar-Niti Nayanar thus excelled greatly due to the wealth of gold, gems, silks, and other riches from all lands. Each item is found in a different land. Each is produced in a different place. But for the purpose of trade, all these items were found together at Amar-Niti Nayanar's place. What kind of person was Amar-Niti Nayanar? Sekkizhar says: "He thought of nothing but Shiva's feet, served sacred food to the devotees of the Lord of twilight hue, and knowing their needs, provided them with torn clothes and loincloths, thus reaping the benefits of his abundant wealth." Sekkizhar Peruman sings about Amar-Niti Nayanar. Lord Shiva is of twilight hue. "Andhi" means evening. In the evening, the sky appears reddish. Therefore, our Lord Shiva appears reddish like the twilight sky. This is the meaning of the phrase "Andhi Vannar." Furthermore, if "Andhi" is split as "Am Thee" (beautiful fire), it would mean Lord Shiva is of the color of fire. Amar-Niti Nayanar acted according to the wishes of the devotees of Lord Shiva, who is of twilight hue. He always contemplated only the sacred feet of Lord Shiva. He was one who understood the intentions of Shiva's devotees and helped them. Devotees are those who ask for nothing. Therefore, Amar-Niti Nayanar was one who understood their intentions himself and helped them. He lived by serving sacred food to devotees. Furthermore, he provided torn clothes (kandai) to devotees. "Kandai" is a garment like a blanket, made by layering four or five pieces of cloth one over another and tying them with threads. He provided this garment to Shiva devotees. Not only that, but he also provided "keezhkovanam," which is a kaupinam, to devotees. "Keezh" is a string that ascetics tie instead of a waist thread.

Therefore, "keezhkovanam" is a kaupinam with a string like a waist thread. "Kovanam" is a garment. It is tied with a "keezh" (string) to cover the private parts. This kaupinam, or "kovanam," is woven to be four or five fingers wide. The story of Amar-Niti Nayanar originated from the kaupinam, so Sekkizhar Peruman specially mentions the kaupinam in his verses here. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 03 — Amar-Niti Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. We are contemplating the Purana of Amar-Niti Nayanar in the Thillai Vaazh Andhanar Sarukkam of Periya Puranam. We saw that Amar-Niti Nayanar was born in Pazhaiyarai and lived by providing necessities to Shiva devotees. Thirunallur is a Shiva temple very close to Pazhaiyarai. A festival was going on in the Shiva temple at Thirunallur. At that time, to serve sacred food to Shiva devotees who came to witness the festival in that temple and to attend to their other needs, Amar-Niti Nayanar went to Thirunallur, built a mutt (monastery) there, and was providing for the Shiva devotees. Let us briefly contemplate the special features of this Thirunallur temple. The Thirunallur temple is blessed with the special features of Moorthy (deity), Thalam (place), and Theertham (sacred water), and it is also glorified by Thevaram hymns and celebrated with festivals. The Thirunallur temple is a Shiva temple glorified by Thevaram hymns, located on the southern bank of the Kaveri in the Chola country. It is currently near Papanasam in Thanjavur district. The temple in Thirunallur is one of the 70 Maada temples built by Kochengat Cholan. Maada temples are also called Perungkoils (large temples). Their structure is designed with many steps so that an elephant cannot enter the temple. Even the main entrance of the temple is not a direct entrance but is arranged to lead through another side entrance. In this structure, the temple has 25 steps on the south side, with the shrines of the Swami and Ambikai (Goddess) located at a height. The Vinayagar (Ganesha) of this place is Kasi Pillaiyar. In this Thirunallur temple, the Swami's name is Kalyana Sundarar. He is known by many sacred names such as Moolalingamurthi Andar, Kalyana Sundarar, Soundara Nayagar, Sundaranathar, Panchavarneswarar, Amrithalingar, and Thirunallur Udaiya Nayanar. The Bana (lingam part) of the Moolalingam (main lingam) here is a self-manifested, dark-colored lingam that cannot be described as being made of any particular material, appearing like a dark sugarcane. Even today, this lingam miraculously changes color six times a day during daylight hours. In the first six nazhigai (a unit of time), it is copper-colored, in the next six to twelve nazhigai, it is light red, in the next twelve to eighteen nazhigai, it is like molten gold, in the eighteen to twenty-four nazhigai, it is emerald green, and in the twenty-four to thirty nazhigai, the Moolalingam appears in a color that cannot be described. Since it appears in five colors, the Lord here is named Panchavarneswarar. He is called Andar because he blessed Amar-Niti Nayanar and Appar Peruman. He is called Kalyana Sundarar because he showed the wedding posture to Agastya. He is called Sundaranathan and Soundarya Nayagar because he is exceedingly beautiful. The sacred wedding posture shown to Agastya is still behind the Shivlingam in stucco form. Not only this, but there is another remarkable sight in this lingam. In all temples, there is only one Bana in the middle of the Avudaiyar (base of the lingam). But here, to the right of the main Bana, there is another small Bana worshipped by Agastya. This is a unique miracle. The Ambikai here is known by various names such as Sri Giri Sundari, Kalyana Sundari, Malai Azhagi, and Thirumalai Sokki. The Ambikai here is of a massive form and exceedingly beautiful, gracing from a separate temple to the northeast of the Swami, facing south. The Sthala Purana praises this Ambikai thus: "We shall ever praise Thirumalai Sokki, who resides in Nallur, who is the primeval five-headed one, who is grace itself, who is the ocean of bliss, from whom all the celebrated Bindu Mohini and Mahakali arise, and who bestows all powers, forms, and instruments upon the pure three-eyed Lord." The form of Nataraja Peruman here is huge and exceedingly beautiful. Not only that, but the form of Chathura Thandava Murthy, who dances with eight arms outstretched, is also depicted here with great subtlety and beauty. In the outer circumambulation of the temple, on the south side, facing north towards Ambikai Giri Sundari, sits Ashtabhuja Mahakali, who is very benevolent and grants all wishes to those who pray to her. In this temple, at the top of the western gopuram entrance, the deity in the form of a Balipeetam (sacrificial altar) is Ganapathi Murthy. This form of his is worshipped in this temple and in Kasi. The special Ganapathi Puja, which takes place once a year at night, is a very rare ritual. On the day of that puja, people from this village and neighboring villages offer the milk their cows yield directly to glorify the puja. The following Theerthams (sacred waters) are found in this temple. First, the Sapthasagara Theertham. This Theertham is located in front of the temple with 12 steps on all four sides. This sacred tank was originally created by Brahma. Therefore, it was known as Brahma Theertham. At a later time, on a Maasi Magam day, Kunti Devi, the mother of the Pandavas, desired to bathe in the ocean. She prayed to the Lord in this temple, and the Lord made all seven oceans appear in this tank. From that day onwards, it was named Sapthasagara Theertham. It is believed that even those suffering from leprosy will be cured if they bathe in all 12 ghats of this Sapthasagara Theertham and circumambulate the temple 12 times for one mandalam (48 days). The Sthala Purana of this village states that Brahma consecrated this sacred tank by placing the Rig Veda in the southeast, the Yajur Veda in the south, the Sama Veda in the west, the Atharva Veda in the north, and the Sapthakoti Mahamantras and 18 Puranas in the center. This sacred tank, with all its glories, is revered as equal to the Mahamaham tank in Kumbakonam. People gather here on Maasi Magam day, saying, "Magam was born in Nallur, Mahamaham was born in Kumbakonam," and bathe in it. The Lord of this temple, Kalyana Sundara Peruman, grants Theertham on Vaikasi Visakam, Karthigai Sundays, Margazhi Thiruvathirai, Maasi Magam, and other days. Other Theerthams include Agni Theertham, Nagakanni Theertham, Dharma Theertham, Deva Theertham, Brahma Kundam, Airavatha Theertham, Chandra Theertham, Surya Theertham, and Kaveri Theertham. The Sthala Viruksham (sacred tree) of this temple is the Vilva tree. Since this place was originally filled with Vilva trees, it was also known as Vilvaranya Kshetram. The Purana of this temple is very special. The Purana of this temple is described in Sanskrit Puranas such as Brahmanda Purana, Garuda Purana, Bhavishyottara Purana, Shiva Rahasya, and Upamanyu Bhakta Vilasam. From these, many parts were taken and compiled into a Sanskrit Purana called Sundaragiri Mahatmyam by the Sanskrit scholar Paingaanadu Ganapathi Sastrigal. This Sthala Purana narrates the history of how Sundaragiri came here. Once, there was a contest of strength between Vayu Deva and Adisesha. According to this, Vayu had to uproot the peak of Mount Meru with his great speed. Adisesha had to prevent it from being uprooted. As Vayu showed great speed, Adisesha spread his hood to block it, and Vayu's action did not succeed. Everyone suffered due to the speed of the wind. So, all the Devas asked Adisesha to retract his hood. Due to the force of the great wind that had been held back, two peaks of Meru broke off. Among them, the peak named Sundaragiri fell in Thirunallur, and the peak named Manikooda Giri fell in Avoor, which is in the southeast. At the top of Sundaragiri, which fell here, there was a Vilva tree.

Under this Vilva tree, the Lord manifested as a self-born lingam and has been blessing everyone. Once, during a competition between Brahma Devan and Thirumal, Brahma Devan lied that he had seen Lord Shiva's crown. To atone for that sin, Brahma Devan established a pond in front of this Sundaragiri in his own name, bathed in it, worshipped the Lord, and received blessings. Therefore, that Theertham was named Brahma Theertham and later Sapthasagara Theertham. Thirunallur is the place where Lord Shiva showed his wedding posture to Agastya. Once, all living beings gathered to witness Lord Shiva's marriage. Due to the immense weight, the northern part of the world sank, and the southern part rose. Lord Shiva commanded Agastya, the Tamil sage, to go to the south and balance it. Agastya then submitted that if he went to the south, he would miss the opportunity to witness the Lord's marriage. To this, Lord Shiva promised to show him his wedding scene. Accordingly, in this Thirunallur temple, Lord Shiva showed his wedding posture to Agastya. Delighted by this, Agastya placed another Bana to the right of the Moolalingam, Sundaralingam, and worshipped it, receiving blessings. The sculpture depicting the wedding posture he saw can still be seen behind the Moolalingam in the sanctum. Now, the greatness of the Agni Theertham here. A Brahmin boy who lived in Madurai during the time of Malayathvaja Pandiyan once starved his guru's cow. Because of this, the owner of the cow was reborn as a dog in his next life and wandered around. One day, he came to this Thirunallur temple. It was the auspicious night of Shivaratri. At that time, a Brahmin boy bathed in the Agni Theertham in this temple and shook the water from his hair, and those drops fell on the dog. Because of this, the dog's form disappeared. The Brahmin, who was in the form of a dog due to a curse and sin, regained his true form and received blessings by worshipping Sundaranatha Peruman here. Sumathi, the daughter of Adisesha, the king of serpents, desired a suitable husband. She would come to Thirunallur daily through a hole, bathe in a Theertham, worship the Lord, and return to her Naga Loka. Once, when the prince of the Chola king came here to have darshan of the Lord, he saw Sumathi and desired her. Later, by the grace of the Lord, they were married. Thirumal (Vishnu) performed penance in this temple, seeking the form of Narasimha to kill Hiranyakashipu. Lord Shiva, the Lord of this temple, granted Thirumal that form. After Hiranyakashipu was slain, the Lord commanded Thirumal to reside in the Narasimha form, facing west, at the top of his sanctum vimana. We can still see that form in the vimana today. Once, Kunti Devi, the mother of the Pandavas, visited this temple and met Narada Muni. It was the auspicious day of Maasi Magam. When Narada said that bathing in the ocean on Maasi Magam day was a great merit, Kunti Devi prayed to the Lord, asking how she could bathe in the ocean while being in Thirunallur. Just as the Lord had made the seven oceans appear in the Potramarai Kulam (Golden Lotus Pond) for Malayathvaja Pandiyan's queen Kanjanamaalai in Madurai, here, for Kunti Devi, he made the seven oceans of salt, sugarcane, toddy, ghee, curd, milk, and water appear in the Brahma Theertham. Kunti Devi bathed and received blessings. Thus, since the seven oceans came into this tank, it was named Sapthasagara Theertham. Once, Airavatham, Indra's white elephant, which was cursed by Indra, came to this temple, worshipped, and was freed from the curse. This is also part of the history of this temple. When Musukunda Chakravarthi brought Thyagaraja Peruman from Indra in Thiruvarur, he kept Thyagaraja Peruman in this Thirunallur temple for three days, worshipped him according to the Lord's command, and only then took him to the Thiruvarur temple. This is another specialty of the Thirunallur temple. Not only that, but the hunter king Sangukannan worshipped here and was blessed with a child. Many other Devas, Siddhas, and Munis also attained Siddhi (spiritual perfection) in this place. In our country, it is customary to place the Sadari (sacred feet) on the head of devotees who visit Perumal (Vishnu) temples. In Shiva temples, it is not customary to place Sadari in this manner. However, in Thirunallur, there is still a custom of placing a crown with Lord Shiva's sacred feet imprinted on it on the heads of those who come for darshan. The reason for this is that Thirunavukkarasu Swamigal, after leaving Thiruvathigai and visiting temples like Pennagadam, Thillai, Sirkazhi, and Thiruvidaimarudur, worshipped Pazhaiyarai and came to Thirushakthi Muttram. He prayed to the Lord to remove the fear of Yama (death) and place His sacred feet on his head. To this, Lord Shiva commanded, "Come to Nallur." Navukkarasu Peruman came to Thirunallur, prostrated before the Lord, and when he rose, Lord Shiva's sacred feet were placed on his head. That is why, for the devotees who come here for darshan, there is a custom in this temple to place the Sadari, which has the Lord's sacred feet imprinted on it, on their heads. This is a unique custom not found in any other Shiva temple. Gnanasambandha Peruman has also sung about the Lord of this temple. This is the place of liberation for Amar-Niti Nayanar. Sundarar also worshipped in this temple. In this temple, daily pujas are performed in the morning, noon, evening, and at midnight. Maasi Magam holds a special place in Thirunallur. The Maasi Magam Brahmotsavam used to be celebrated with a grand chariot procession. Similarly, the Vaikasi Visakam 10-day festival and, at its conclusion, the Ezhur (Sapthasthanam) festival, like in Thiruvaiyaru, used to be celebrated. Now, the Vaikasi Visakam festival is a one-day festival, the Aani Poora Amar-Niti Nayanar Mukthi festival, the Margazhi 10-day Thiruvempavai festival, and the Maasi Magam one-day festival are celebrated, during which Sri Somasakanda Murthy, the sole Lord of this place, graces the occasion. These are the special features of the Thirunallur temple. Amar-Niti Nayanar reaches this Thirunallur temple. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 04 — Amar-Niti Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. We are contemplating the Purana of Amar-Niti Nayanar in the second chapter of Periya Puranam, the Thillai Vaazh Andhanar Sarukkam. Thirunallur, a sacred place in the Chola country, is very close to Amar-Niti Nayanar's sacred place, Pazhaiyarai. Here, Sekkizhar Swamigal refers to Lord Shiva of Thirunallur as "Mukkan Nakkar Mudhalvan" (the primordial Lord, the three-eyed Naked One). "Mukkan" means that the Lord has three sacred eyes. The three sacred eyes of the Lord are the Moon, the Sun, and Agni (fire). Now, Sekkizhar Swamigal here calls Lord Shiva "Nakkar." "Nakkar" refers to the sacred form of being naked, without even a loincloth. Lord Shiva having three eyes and the form of Nakkar signifies the primary nature of the Lord. How so? The three eyes represent the Lord's complete luminous form. The form of Nakkar signifies that the Lord takes everything else as his garment, and during the Mahasamhara (great dissolution), everything else, except the Lord, merges into Him. Therefore, the sacred form of the Lord without clothes is the true sacred form where everything else merges into Him, and only the Lord remains. This name "Nakkar" signifies that. The Vedas themselves are called the loincloth or kaupinam of the Lord. But in this Nakkar form, there is not even a loincloth. That is, since the loincloth is symbolized by the Vedas here, this Nakkar sacred form signifies the Nadhantha Swarupam, which shines within the Vedas and transcends them. Thus, Lord Shiva, the primordial one who shines as the three-eyed Nakkar, is the Lord who lovingly resides in Thirunallur. The Thiruviruttham states, "Kailasam in the north and Nallur in the south are His abodes," illustrating the greatness of Nallur. That is, its meaning is that the Lord resides in Kailasam in the north and in Nallur, which is equal to it, in the south. A festival is taking place here in Thirunallur. Amar-Niti Nayanar considered it his greatest benefit to build a mutt and serve sacred food to the devotees of Shiva who came to this festival. For this very purpose, Amar-Niti Nayanar eagerly participates in the festival. The festival held in the Shiva temples of the Lord is a festival that leads to the liberation (mukthi) of living beings and assures people of their well-being. Amar-Niti Nayanar built a mutt to serve sacred food to the devotees who came to this festival and has been serving them. Just as the Lord's temple is called Thirukkoil, a mutt should also be called Thirumadam. This is because the place where the Lord resides is Thirukkoil, and the place where His devotees reside is Thirumadam. Amar-Niti Nayanar, who lived in Pazhaiyarai, left Pazhaiyarai and came to Thirunallur with his relatives to serve food to the devotees who came to the festival in nearby Thirunallur. "Sutrattar" means those who are around. It refers to one's wife, children, and others who are around them. Amar-Niti Nayanar was a very wealthy person. He excelled greatly in trade. Therefore, he had the responsibility to support all his relatives. Amar-Niti Nayanar not only supported his relatives but also intended to involve them in the service of the Lord and His devotees. His relatives also had the same intention.

That is why Amar-Niti Nayanar came to Thirunallur with his relatives. Amar-Niti Nayanar was living a good family life. Therefore, it is clear from this that he supported his relatives, which is one of the five duties performed by those who lead a householder's life, known as Grihasthashrama. Thiruvalluvar, in his Thirukkural, mentions the five duties: ancestors, deities, guests, relatives, and oneself. Pazhaiyarai was the Nayanar's birthplace and the place where he conducted his business. However, leaving all that, the Nayanar settled in Thirunallur for the service of the Lord and the service of feeding devotees. This shows us the intensity of his love for Shiva and Shiva's devotees. He established a mutt in Thirunallur and has been serving food to devotees. He does not entrust these tasks to others but personally oversees them. According to the rule that Shiva Dharmas should be performed directly and not left to others, Amar-Niti Nayanar has been serving food in the mutt he built. Just as the grace of the Lord is needed to worship Him, similarly, the grace of the Lord is needed to worship His devotees. Therefore, Amar-Niti Nayanar, realizing the grace of Lord Shiva who granted him the blessing of building such a mutt and serving food to the Lord's devotees, melted in devotion and joyfully performed this unique sacred service. While this was happening, the Lord residing in Thirunallur, to bestow grace upon Amar-Niti Nayanar, who was filled with abundant love, by first demonstrating the greatness of His loincloth, assumed the form of a Brahmachari from the Brahmin community and arrived. The Lord residing in Thirunallur is a great benefactor. What does "Perunthagai" mean? It means possessing the greatest of all great qualities. What is the Lord's great beneficence? No matter how many mistakes we, as living beings, make, He does not reject us but tolerates all our mistakes and continues to show compassion, doesn't He? This quality is called Perunthagaimai (great beneficence). The Lord's quality of great beneficence. Thiruvachagam says, "O You who tolerate my smallness, which does hateful things, by Your greatness, it is the duty of the great to tolerate all mistakes." Thirugnanasambandar's Thevaram says, "The great benefactor was with the ignorant woman." Such a great benefactor, the Lord, resides in Thirunallur. The Lord residing in Thirunallur decides to demonstrate the greatness of His loincloth and to bless Amar-Niti Nayanar's abundant love. To demonstrate the greatness of His loincloth, Lord Shiva, the Lord, descends from His state as a householder (Grihastha) and comes here as a Brahmachari. Not only to demonstrate the greatness of His loincloth, but also to bestow grace upon the abundant love of Amar-Niti Nayanar, the devoted servant, He comes. The love Amar-Niti Nayanar had for the Lord was abundant love. Abundant love means love that is so complete that there is no room for more. Thus, Amar-Niti Nayanar possessed abundant love. The Lord bestows upon that abundant love an enduring grace. Enduring grace means grace that is boundless in measure, time, experience, and joy, an unending, blissful grace.

This is the meaning. Thus, to show the greatness of his loincloth and to bless Amar-Niti Nayanar, the Lord comes in the form of a Brahmachari of the Brahmin community. Among the four varnas (social classes) – Brahmin, Kshatriya, Vaishya, and Sudra – the first three varnas have stages of life (ashramas) such as Brahmacharya, which are prescribed by the scriptures. The Lord decides to appear here as a Brahmachari belonging to the first varna, the Brahmin varna. We will continue to contemplate how he appears. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 05 — Amar-Niti Nayanar

We are contemplating the Purana of Amar-Niti Nayanar in the Thillai Vaazh Andhanar Sarukkam of Periya Puranam. To bestow grace upon Amar-Niti Nayanar, the Lord comes in the form of a Brahmin Brahmachari. Sekkizhar Swamigal describes this as "assuming the form of a Brahmachari from the Vedic lineage." The meaning of Brahmachari is one who lives in the state of Brahma upasana (contemplation of Brahman). That is, one who wanders and remains steadfast in the state of Brahma upasana is the meaning of Brahmachari. One who recites the Vedas is a Brahmachari. What are the actions of a Brahmachari? In the three varnas – Brahmin, Kshatriya, and Vaishya – a boy, after his Upanayanam (sacred thread ceremony), without chewing betel leaves, applying sandalwood paste, or wearing flowers, wraps only one garment around his waist, goes to a Brahmin agraharam (settlement) in another village (not his own), spreads darbha grass on the ground at night and sleeps on it, eats food obtained through alms, and studies the Vedas under a suitable guru until the time of his marriage. This is the characteristic and conduct of a Brahmachari. Elders say that this Brahmacharya ashrama can extend up to 48 years of age. Lord Shiva appears in such an unparalleled form of a Brahmin Brahmachari. How does he appear? Lord Shiva always has matted locks (Jadamudi). But now, appearing as a Brahmachari, Lord Shiva conceals his matted locks and instead has the topknot (sigai) appropriate for a Brahmachari. He has a sacred topknot. "Sigai" means a topknot, which is appropriate for the Brahmachari state. He shines with the three-line sacred ash mark (Thiruneetru Kaappu) on his forehead, which is a special identifying mark for Saivism. The Vedas prescribe this three-line sacred ash mark, known as Tripundram, as appropriate for all ashramas. Furthermore, Lord Shiva, who came in the form of a Brahmachari, wears a deer skin with the sacred thread (Poonool). Wearing a deer skin with the sacred thread is one of the identifying marks appropriate for the Brahmachari state. It is customary to wear a deer skin about one inch long with the sacred thread. Moreover, he wears a Pavithram (sacred ring) on the Anamika (ring finger) of his right hand. The Pavithram in his hand is newly tied and made of fresh, undried darbha grass. Therefore, it shines like an emerald due to the green color of the fresh darbha grass. The Pavithram has a knot. Therefore, Sekkizhar describes here that the knot of the Pavithram shines like an emerald gem, and the long part of the Pavithram shines like a ray of light, like the brilliance of that emerald gem. Lord Shiva, who came in the form of a Brahmachari, has tied a waist thread made by twisting Munji (darbha grass) around his waist. A waist thread made of Munji darbha grass is appropriate for Brahmacharya. In Vedic tradition, there are seven types of darbha grass. Munji darbha grass is one of them. It is prescribed that this should be worn as a waist thread, which is appropriate for the Brahmachari state. Brahmacharis are forbidden to wear waist threads made of thread, silk, etc. The loincloth worn by this Brahmachari is the Vedas themselves. For Lord Shiva, everything is the Vedas. The Vedas are his sandals, the Vedas are his abode, the Vedas are his loincloth. Therefore, Lord Shiva, in the guise of a Brahmachari, also wears the Vedas as his loincloth here. Thus, Lord Shiva, concealing his matted locks and having a topknot, adorned with Tripundrams, wearing a deer skin with a white sacred thread, with a Pavithram in his hand, and with the Vedas as his loincloth around his waist, which is tied with a twisted cord of Munji grass, walks with his sacred feet touching the ground. What is the greatness of these sacred feet of the Lord? The sacred feet of the Lord are the feet that never leave the hearts of His devotees. What kind of devotees are those who hold those sacred feet in their hearts? Deception results from the actions of the five senses. The strong karma (evil deed) that results from deception is the karma mala. Those who have cut off this strong karma mala that results from deception are the devotees of the Lord. Not only that, but they are devotees who have cut off the ego of "I" and "mine" that arises from the anava mala (ego impurity) and whose minds are devoted to worshipping only the sacred feet of the Lord. The sacred feet of the Lord are the lotus feet that never leave the hearts of such devotees. Lord Shiva, with those sacred feet touching the ground, comes here in the form of a Brahmin Brahmachari. This sacred form taken by the Lord is a form that melts the hearts of all who behold it. The Brahmachari who came with this sacred form carries a Palasa staff in his hand, which is appropriate for his Brahmachari state. It is also appropriate for Brahmacharis to carry a staff in their hand. The Brahmachari has tied two loincloths to that staff. It is customary to carry two loincloths on the staff to change into after bathing. Not only that, but two loincloths would be needed for this historical event – one to give to Amar-Niti Nayanar and one to place on the pan of the balance. This is the inner meaning. Not only that, but this Brahmachari also carries a sacred ash bag. Carrying a sacred ash bag, like deer skin, is also an identifying mark appropriate for the Shiva form of a Brahmachari. It is a rule that Brahmacharis and those in the other three ashramas should always wear sacred ash. Elders say that wearing vibhuti (sacred ash) and reciting mantras yields the full benefit of mantra chanting. A Brahmachari is one who always recites the Vedas. Therefore, it is a rule that sacred ash should always shine on his forehead. That is why Sekkizhar Swamigal states in the previous verse that he came wearing Tripundram in this Brahmachari form. And in this verse, he states that this Brahmachari carries a sacred ash bag. Not only that, but the Brahmachari also carries darbha grass. It is a rule that Brahmacharis should sleep on darbha grass mats. For that purpose, and for rituals like Pavithram, Brahmacharis should always keep darbha grass. Thus, Sekkizhar Swamigal describes the form of the Lord who came in the guise of a Brahmachari from head to foot (Kesadhipadam). Amar-Niti Nayanar sees Lord Shiva, who came in the guise of a Brahmin Brahmachari, in front of his mutt. The Lord is one who cannot be seen by our outer eyes, our physical eyes. However, Amar-Niti Nayanar sees the Lord here. The Lord is one who has no form of His own. But here, He has come with a form. Therefore, Amar-Niti Nayanar sees the Lord.

Upon seeing this Brahmin Brahmachari, a Shiva devotee, Amar-Niti Nayanar's heart blossoms. His face blossoms even more than his heart. It was the eyes on his face that saw this Brahmachari form, this Brahmin Brahmachari Shiva devotee form. But do these eyes see on their own? They cannot see on their own. The eyes inform the inner instruments (antahkaranas) – mind, intellect, consciousness, and ego. These antahkaranas inform the soul's consciousness. That soul's consciousness informs the soul. The Lord, being present, shows this form to the soul. Because of this, the soul knows. Because the soul knew this, the mind rejoices. Thus, the mind first rejoices and blossoms, and then, because of this, the face also blossoms. The face shines even more brightly than the mind that caused it to blossom. "The beauty of the inner self is seen on the face" is a proverb. Thus, Amar-Niti Nayanar, seeing the Brahmin Brahmachari Shiva devotee, rejoiced in his heart, his face blossomed, and he came forward to worship him. He worshipped Lord Shiva, who was in the form of the Brahmachari, with his three instruments: mind, body, and speech. Amar-Niti Nayanar says to this Brahmachari, "That you, whom I have never seen in this mutt before, have come here today for me, a humble servant, to see openly, is it not the result of my penance?" He says this with melting devotion. The inner meaning of this is that the Lord Himself comes to this mutt as a Brahmachari. Therefore, the first meaning is that no one has ever seen such a person come to this mutt before. The second meaning is that this mutt is not worthy of Your unparalleled greatness. Until now, the Lord would come mingled with devotees, but He would not come alone to be seen openly like this. That He has come alone like this is the next meaning. Sivakavimani explains several such inner meanings. It is a rule that only due to good penance performed previously does one receive the blessing of such great persons visiting. The Brahmachari says to Amar-Niti Nayanar, "I have heard of your service of feeding the devotees of the Lord and providing them with torn clothes, lower garments, and new, high-quality loincloths. Hearing of this, I have come to see you." Lord Shiva, in the form of the Brahmachari, says this, concealing his third eye. Lord Shiva Himself praises Amar-Niti Nayanar with His own mouth. How so? Amar-Niti Nayanar provides all necessities to devotees, whether one devotee comes, many devotees come, or countless devotees come. He feeds devotees sacred food. Just as a mother feeds her child, he understands the time, quantity, quality, and desire.

He provides torn clothes, lower garments, and loincloths, says Lord Shiva with His sacred mouth, praising Amar-Niti Nayanar here. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 06 — Amar-Niti Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. We are contemplating the Purana of Amar-Niti Nayanar in the Thillai Vaazh Andhanar Sarukkam of Periya Puranam. A festival is taking place in the Shiva temple in Thirunallur. There, our Amar-Niti Nayanar, with his relatives and family, has established a mutt and is providing for the needs of Shiva devotees. Lord Shiva comes there in the guise of a Brahmachari Brahmin. That Brahmachari Brahmin tells Amar-Niti Nayanar, "I have heard of your sacred service of providing for the needs of devotees here, and so I have come." Seeing the Brahmachari Brahmin in his divine form, which was an unprecedented sight, Amar-Niti Nayanar greeted him respectfully and said, "In this mutt, food is prepared purely by Brahmins for the revered Brahmins and ascetics to partake. Therefore, you too should partake of sacred food here." Generally, it is customary to serve food to devotees in a mutt. However, the custom of preparing food purely by Brahmin cooks for Brahmin devotees and ascetics has existed from that day until now in many mutts. This is because food must be prepared purely for those who recite the Vedas and are great in their penance, in a manner befitting their penance and conduct. Therefore, in this mutt too, there is a custom of preparing food separately by Brahmins for such great Vedic scholars and separately for other devotees. Thus, Amar-Niti Nayanar requests and appeals to the Brahmin Brahmachari to partake of food. He also states that it is customary for sages who perform great penance like him to partake of food here. Thus, Amar-Niti Nayanar requests the Brahmin Brahmachari to partake of sacred food. Sivakavimani subtly points out here that while the Devas kept the nectar for themselves and offered poison to Lord Shiva, Amar-Niti Nayanar, unlike them, prepares to offer nectar to Lord Shiva. The Brahmin Brahmachari agrees to his request. If he is to partake of food as Amar-Niti Nayanar requested, he must bathe before eating. Therefore, the Brahmin Brahmachari states that he will go and bathe in the Ponni river, which is the Kaveri river. Here, Sekkizhar Peruman refers to the Kaveri as "Anangu Neer Ponni" (the divine water Ponni). "Anangu" means a divine woman. Sekkizhar states here that the Kaveri river is not just a woman, but a divine woman, a river. Just as a mother nourishes a child with milk, so too, rivers are called women in our Bharatadesha (India) because they nourish this world with their abundant waters, sustaining the living beings of this world. That is why our ancestors gave feminine names like Bhavani, Godavari, Kanni, Kumari, etc., to rivers. This Kaveri river is also a woman. And she is not an ordinary woman, but an "Anangu," a divine woman. This is because, as we saw earlier, the Kaveri river, with its abundant waters, worships Lord Shiva by circumambulating countless Shiva temples on both its banks, the northern and southern. Therefore, this Kaveri woman is called "Anangu Neer Ponni" by Sekkizhar. Thus, the Brahmin Brahmachari says that he will bathe in the Kaveri and return. And if it rains while he is returning, he asks Amar-Niti Nayanar to keep this dry loincloth with him and return it to him later for use. Saying this, he unties one of the two loincloths tied to the staff in his hand and gives it to Amar-Niti Nayanar. Amar-Niti Nayanar attained liberation in the month of Aani, under the Poora star. Aani month is the peak summer season (Mudhuvenil Kalam). Therefore, there is no chance of rain during this peak summer season. The period that Tamils designated as Mudhuvenil Kalam means summer. So, there is no chance of rain in the month of Aani. If it were to rain, a dry loincloth would be needed to wear. Therefore, he unties one of the two loincloths on the staff in his hand and gives it to Amar-Niti Nayanar. Whatever the Lord gives is divine grace. Therefore, the loincloth given by the Lord here is also grace. This loincloth given by the Lord is grace. So, what does grace do? It removes the ego (Jivabodha) associated with impurities (mala) and bestows pure love (nirmala anbu). That is why Sekkizhar Swamigal refers to this loincloth as "Ven Gunangol Kovanam" (the loincloth that embodies white qualities). Furthermore, the Lord's loincloth is the Vedas themselves. The Vedas originated from the Lord's supreme knowledge (Vaalarivu), which is true wisdom. Therefore, the Vedas, which originated from the Lord's true wisdom, are the Lord's loincloth. Therefore, Sekkizhar Swamigal also refers to it as "Ven Gunangol Kovanam." What does the Lord, that Brahmin Brahmachari, say to Amar-Niti Nayanar after giving him such a loincloth? He tells Amar-Niti Nayanar, "You yourself know the greatness of this loincloth. I don't need to explain it. Therefore, keep it safely and protect it until I return after bathing, and then return it to me." This loincloth's greatness is immeasurable. The Brahmin Brahmachari tells Amar-Niti Nayanar, "You yourself will realize its immense greatness." What is the immense greatness of this loincloth? This loincloth is one that surpasses everything else, stands supreme, and is greatly renowned. Furthermore, as we saw, this loincloth is the Vedas. The Vedas, in the form of sound (Nada), stand supreme, and all other principles originate from them. Therefore, this loincloth is a supreme loincloth. Manikkavachagar Peruman also describes the Vedas as the Lord's loincloth in his Thiruvachagam. One section in Thiruvachagam is Thiruchazhal. In this, Manikkavachagar Peruman sings in such a way that the first two lines are a question, and the next two lines are the answer. That is, Manikkavachagar arranges it such that two women sing this song. One woman asks a question in the first two lines, and the other woman answers in the next two lines; this is how the Thiruchazhal hymn is structured. In it, in one song, one friend first asks a question. How so? She asks, "Ennappan Embirān Ellārkkum Thān Īsan Thunnambai Kōvanamā Koḷḷumathu Ennēḍi?" (Why does my Father, my Lord, who is the master of all, wear a torn cloth as a loincloth, O friend?). This is the second verse in the Thiruchazhal hymn. The meaning of this question is that she asks her friend, "O friend, why does that Lord, who is my father and my master, and the master of all, wear a torn cloth as a loincloth? Why does he wear a loincloth?"

In response, the second woman replies, "Mannu Kalai Thunnu Porul Marai Nāṅkē Vān Saraḍā Thannaiyē Kōvaṇamā Cāttiṇan Kāṇ Cāḷalō" (He wears the four Vedas, which are enduring arts and profound truths, as a celestial thread, and He Himself as a loincloth, O friend!). That is, its meaning is that the Lord wears a loincloth. How so? The Lord wears the Vedas themselves as a loincloth. The friend replies that He wears the Vedas as a loincloth only to bestow grace upon us, living beings. Thus, in the second verse of this Thiruchazhal, Manikkavachagar Peruman describes the greatness of the Lord's loincloth, which is the Vedas, as sung by two friends. Therefore, I do not need to tell you the greatness of this loincloth, which is so revered. This is because you already know the greatness of this loincloth and have been providing it to devotees. Therefore, keep it safely, says the Brahmin Brahmachari to Amar-Niti Nayanar. "You keep this loincloth; I will take it back later," says the Brahmin Brahmachari. In reality, giving and taking are the Lord's actions. Thiruvachagam says, "You keep and take everything." Thus, giving and taking are the Lord's actions. Here, taking is dissolution. Keeping is creation. The Brahmin Brahmachari says, "Take and keep it," doesn't he? Sivakavimani says this refers to the disappearance of the loincloth and then the Nayanar's merging into the Lord. In truth, you are buying and keeping, meaning the Brahmin Brahmachari implicitly tells Amar-Niti Nayanar, "Amar-Niti Nayanar, you are performing an action with the soul's consciousness that 'I am buying, I am keeping.' But later, the act of returning that loincloth is not an action that you will perform. You will not perform it. You will lose your Jiva Bodha (individual consciousness) and offer yourself to be weighed against the loincloth." This is the inner meaning of the Brahmin Brahmachari's words. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 07 — Amar-Niti Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. We are contemplating the Puranam of Amar-Niti Nayanar in the Thillai Vazh Andanar Sarga of Periya Puranam. Lord Shiva, who came as a Brahmachari Brahmin, gave one of the two loincloths he had on his staff to Amar-Niti Nayanar, saying that he would bathe in the Kaveri and that Amar-Niti Nayanar should keep it safe until then. He added that if it rained and the loincloth in his hand got wet, this one might be needed, so he should keep it carefully. Amar-Niti Nayanar accepted the loincloth and bid him farewell, saying, "Please return soon after your bath." Lord Shiva, in the form of the Brahmin Brahmachari, departed from there to bathe in the Kaveri. The Lord is omnipresent. Therefore, "departed from there" should be understood as the omnipresent Lord, who appeared as a Brahmin Brahmachari, made that appearance disappear. Amar-Niti Nayanar, a great devotee who received the loincloth from the Lord, kept it very carefully in a separate, special place, not with the other old loincloths he kept for other devotees, bearing in mind the words of Lord Shiva, the foremost among Brahmins. In this verse, Sekkizhar Swamigal refers to Lord Shiva as "Mundai Andanar." "Mundai Andanar" means the one who existed before all Brahmins, the first one. If we keep tracing back, father, father's father, his father, and so on, the one who stands first at the end of this lineage is our Lord Shiva. Furthermore, sages like Kashyapa originated from Brahma, the Brahmin, and through them, creation came into being in the world. Therefore, Brahma, the Brahmin, is called Pitamahan (grandfather). His father is Vishnu. And His father is Lord Shiva. Thus, it is also indicated that Lord Shiva is prior to all Brahmins. Moreover, the Upanishads state that our Lord Shiva is the Brahmin who created Brahma, the primordial Brahmin, and taught him the Vedas. Amar-Niti Nayanar kept the loincloth given by the Brahmin Brahmachari very safely. But what did this Vedic scholar, who went to bathe, do? He made the loincloth that Amar-Niti Nayanar had kept safe disappear from there. He made it vanish. The Lord made this loincloth disappear. What is the inner meaning, the philosophical explanation, of making it disappear? Saiva Siddhanta explains the concept of Maya through the doctrine of Satkaryavada. What is Satkaryavada? It means that what exists does not perish, and what does not exist does not come into being. This is the summary of Satkaryavada. If so, here the Lord made an existing object, the loincloth, disappear. It is said that He made it cease to exist. But according to Saiva Siddhanta scriptures and Satkaryavada, an existing object does not perish. So how did this loincloth disappear? The philosophy behind this is that during creation, the Lord takes a part from Maya, the primordial substance, and creates the universes. Then, during dissolution (samhara), He absorbs these universes. Where does He absorb them? Into Maya. He creates this universe from Maya and then, during dissolution, absorbs it back into Maya. Therefore, Maya is the cause for this world. This world, the universe, is the effect. The truth is that the effect merges into its cause. Effects are always formless. Effects are always with form. Therefore, this world, which is visible and perceivable in form,

when it merges into its cause, Maya, during the period of dissolution (samhara), it transforms into a formless entity. That is, it disappears in its causal form, which is a truth of Saiva Siddhanta scriptures. Therefore, the disappearance or vanishing of the loincloth here does not mean that the existing loincloth was destroyed. We should understand it to mean that the Lord made the loincloth disappear into its cause, in such a way that no one could see it. Sivakavimani blesses us with this explanation. Similarly, in every line, every word, and every phrase of Periya Puranam, the truths of Saiva Siddhanta scriptures are embedded. Now, let's continue the story. The Vedic scholar who went to bathe in the Kaveri thus made the loincloth disappear. It is not known whether this Vedic scholar, who went to bathe, returned after bathing in the Kaveri or after bathing in the Ganga. But Sekkizhar Swamigal states that he returned drenched as rain, water from the sky, poured down. The Vedic scholar who went to bathe in the Kaveri returns. As he returns, he causes rain to fall. The Vedic scholar comes, drenched in that rain. Therefore, those in the monastery and near the monastery saw the Vedic scholar coming, drenched in the rain. But Sekkizhar Swamigal sets the scene here, singing that they would not know whether this Vedic scholar bathed in the Kaveri or the Ganga. However, the Vedic scholar who came was a man of truth. Therefore, he would do as he said. So, having said he was going to bathe in the Kaveri and would return, he must have definitely bathed in the Kaveri. But Sekkizhar writes here that everyone thought they did not know whether he bathed in the Kaveri or the Ganga. Lord Shiva, who came as the Vedic scholar, concealed his matted locks and came with his hair tied up. Therefore, he also concealed the Ganga that was in his matted locks. So, it is unlikely that he bathed in the Ganga. But the Vedic scholar is coming, drenched in rain from the sky, making one doubt whether he bathed in the Kaveri. Whether it is the Kaveri or the Ganga, the clouds that formed as water in the sky poured down as rain, and that rainwater from the sky becomes both the Ganga water and the Kaveri water. Therefore, it is also a hint that by being drenched in this rain, he is considered to have bathed in these rivers. As the Vedic scholar came drenched in the rain, our devotee Amar-Niti Nayanar, filled with mature love, before the Vedic scholar arrived, had already meticulously prepared the six-taste divine ambrosia according to culinary rules and waited. When the Vedic scholar arrived, he went to receive him and stood bowing. The Brahmin Brahmachari, who had bathed in the sacred Kaveri or Ganga, and then was drenched in the sky-water, the rain, thought that all this water was impure and its purity was insufficient. Desiring to bathe in the superior purity of the devotee's love, he now proceeds to do the following, says Sekkizhar Swamigal here. The Lord desires to bathe in the pure water of love. That is, it is pleasing to the Lord to see love manifest in devotees, to be delighted by it, to accept that love, and to bestow grace. The Lord is one who has no desires or dislikes. Even so, the only thing He desires from His devotees is love. Thus, the Lord greatly desires to bathe in the pure water of the devotees' love. When does water become pure water? When does ordinary water become sacred water? It becomes sacred and holy water only by touching the body of the Lord,

that is, by touching the divine form of the Lord. All other waters that do not touch the divine form of the Lord do not bestow purity. All other waters are merely physical water. But the water that touches and flows from the divine form of the Lord is sacred water. Bearing this in mind, if we consider it, the Brahmin Brahmachari was drenched in rainwater that poured from the sky. Therefore, it was impure water. Since he was drenched in such impure water, to atone for it, the Lord comes towards the monastery to bathe in the water of Amar-Niti Nayanar's pure love. During temple festivals, when the Lord's processional deities are taken out in processions through the streets, if they happen to get wet in the rain, according to the Agamas, it is considered impure. So, a Punyaham (purification ritual) is performed, followed by an Abhishekam (ablution) to purify them. This is an Agamic rule. Therefore, just as when the Lord is paraded in the streets during a festival and gets wet in the rain, a Punyaham is performed and an Abhishekam with sacred water is done, here too, Lord Shiva, who came as a Brahmin Brahmachari, got drenched in the rain. To perform a Punyaham for this, He needs to receive an Abhishekam with sacred water. Therefore, the Lord came towards Amar-Niti Nayanar's monastery to receive an Abhishekam, considering Amar-Niti Nayanar's pure love as the sacred water. Sekkizhar describes who this Lord was who came. Sekkizhar Swamigal refers to that Lord as "Kovanam Kalvar" (the thief of the loincloth). In the Vedas, Sri Rudram addresses the Lord as "O thief, salutations to you. O chief of thieves, salutations to you." "My heart-stealing thief" is the divine utterance of Thirugnanasambandar Peruman. Why does Sekkizhar refer to the Lord as "Kovanam Kalvar" here? Here, the loincloth belongs to the Lord Himself. That is, it belongs to the Brahmin Brahmachari. But he entrusts it to Amar-Niti Nayanar. He gives it to him. Thus, the Lord makes the item given to Amar-Niti Nayanar disappear from there without his knowledge, thereby causing Amar-Niti Nayanar to be at fault. Therefore, Sekkizhar Swamigal refers to the Lord as "Kovanam Kalvar." That is, Sekkizhar Swamigal says that He is the one who committed a deceptive act through the loincloth. This "Kovanam Kalvar" tells Amar-Niti Nayanar, "I have come completely drenched in the rain. Therefore, the loincloth on the staff in my hand has become wet. So, please bring the loincloth I already gave you." The Brahmin Brahmachari asks Amar-Niti Nayanar for that loincloth. Amar-Niti Nayanar, unaware that the loincloth had disappeared due to the deceit of the visiting Ayya, goes inside and searches for it. The loincloth is not found where he had placed it. Seeing that the loincloth he had carefully kept had vanished, he is bewildered and searches for it. In this verse, Sekkizhar Swamigal refers to Lord Shiva, who came as a Brahmin Brahmachari and made the loincloth disappear, as "Kaithavam Udaiya Ayya" (the deceitful Ayya). "Kaithavam" means falsehood, deceit. Though an Ayya is a person of greatness, worthy of respect, this visiting Ayya, with falsehood and deceit, made the loincloth disappear. "Kaithavam," meaning falsehood and deceit, is not a characteristic of a respectable Ayya.

"I will not speak deceit" is from Thirugnanasambandar Puranam. But this respectable Ayya makes the loincloth disappear from where it was kept. He makes it disappear without Amar-Niti Nayanar knowing. Then he makes Amar-Niti Nayanar search for the loincloth and makes Amar-Niti Nayanar say that the loincloth is not there, thus performing many actions. All these are qualities called "kaithavam," meaning falsehood or deceit. That is why Sekkizhar Swamigal here uses the figure of speech "muranaṇi" (oxymoron) to refer to Lord Shiva, who came as the Brahmin Brahmachari, as "Kaithavam Udaiya Ayya." What is its inner meaning? The Lord's deceit, this "kaithavam" of falsehood, here signifies His power of concealment. Souls know only if the Lord makes them know. If they do not know by His grace, souls cannot know. Appar Swamigal said this: "Who can see if You do not show? Who can see, O Lord with the eye on Your forehead, if You do not show?" Therefore, here, "the deceitful Ayya" is used by Sekkizhar Swamigal to refer to the Lord's power of concealment, His Tirodhana Shakti, as the inner meaning. Let us continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 08 — Amar-Niti Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. We are contemplating the Puranam of Amar-Niti Nayanar in the Thillai Vazh Andanar Sarga of Periya Puranam. Amar-Niti Nayanar is bewildered upon finding that the loincloth is missing from the place where he had kept it. Amar-Niti Nayanar's mind is pure, devoid of impurities like envy, desire, and anger. Therefore, Amar-Niti Nayanar is certain about the place where he kept the loincloth. It was only a short while ago that he placed it there. He is bewildered to find it missing from that spot. Usually, when a lost item is searched for, we search with doubt, wondering if we placed it here or there. This doubt and forgetfulness arise in us due to the impurities in our minds, such as envy, desire, and anger. But Amar-Niti Nayanar had no such doubt. He was certain that this was the place where he kept the loincloth. Yet, the loincloth was not there. That is why he is bewildered. Why did Amar-Niti Nayanar not doubt, as we would, whether he placed it here or there? Because there was not even a trace of impurity in his mind. Therefore, Amar-Niti Nayanar doubts, thinking, "The loincloth has disappeared from the place where I kept it; did it disappear by itself?" So, he searches among all the other clothes he had. The loincloth is not found anywhere. Amar-Niti Nayanar fell into great distress, caught in the trick played by the visiting Vedic scholar. Amar-Niti Nayanar feels great sorrow, thinking that such an incident has befallen him, his wife, and his relatives. He thinks, "Such a misfortune has come upon us," and grieves. The item he received from the Vedic scholar has gone missing. Therefore, he must give a proper answer to the Vedic scholar. Moreover, Amar-Niti Nayanar is distressed in two ways: that the item is lost, and that trouble has befallen the Vedic scholar because of him. Sekkizhar notes here that this distress was not something Amar-Niti Nayanar brought upon himself. It came to him beyond his attention and care, surpassing his situation. When our distress becomes excessive, our actions momentarily freeze. We stop. But Amar-Niti Nayanar, even when such great distress came upon him, did not freeze. He immediately takes action. He grieves, thinking that the visiting devotee would suffer from the cold, drenched in rain, with no way to change his wet loincloth. Without the slightest delay, he quickly sets off, taking another loincloth for the Brahmin to change into, to replace the wet one. This swift action shows his unending love and affection for the devotees of Shiva. Thus, taking a new loincloth from inside his house, he comes out of the house and reaches the Vedic scholar. Sivakavimani Ayya explains that his coming out from inside his dwelling, that is, from his home, implicitly signifies Amar-Niti Nayanar transcending all worldly attachments like his house and possessions, which he considered his own, and moving away from them, thereby aligning himself and all his possessions and worldly attachments with Shiva. Thus, coming out of the house, he approaches the Brahmin Brahmachari, the Lord, and says,

"I had kept the loincloth you gave me safely. But it is not found there now. It has disappeared. No one else has taken it or hidden it. I do not know if it disappeared by itself or what happened. I don't know. I have never seen such a wonder anywhere else," he says. "Therefore, I have brought another good loincloth. It is not a loincloth torn from another cloth. It is a separate loincloth woven specifically as a loincloth. I have brought it with the desire that you should change your wet one and wear this. O devotee with a forehead adorned with sacred ash, please remove your wet loincloth and wear this dry, new loincloth in its place, and forgive my fault," Amar-Niti Nayanar pleads with the Brahmin Brahmachari. The loincloth Amar-Niti Nayanar offered was not as good as the one given by the Brahmin Brahmachari. However, this loincloth was better than all the other loincloths Amar-Niti Nayanar possessed. Therefore, Amar-Niti Nayanar requests him to change his wet loincloth and wear this dry one. Furthermore, Amar-Niti Nayanar requests him to forgive his fault. Here, Amar-Niti Nayanar has not committed any fault. Yet, he admits to having committed a fault and asks for forgiveness. Here, Amar-Niti Nayanar has not committed any fault. But why does he admit to having committed a fault and seek forgiveness? Faults are of two types: intentional (buddhipurvam) and unintentional (abuddhipurvam). An intentional fault is one done knowingly, deliberately, and with planning. An unintentional fault is one committed unknowingly. However, these categories of intentional and unintentional faults depend on the person who committed the fault. These two divisions exist from the perspective of the one who committed the fault. But whether a fault is committed intentionally or unintentionally, the suffering that comes to those affected by the fault is the same, whether the fault was intentional or unintentional; it causes suffering. There is no difference in the suffering that comes to the affected. That is why Amar-Niti Nayanar says that this action of his, though it happened unknowingly, is still a fault. Furthermore, due to that fault committed unknowingly, a devotee of Shiva, an ascetic, is affected. Amar-Niti Nayanar considers why this fault, committed against an ascetic, a devotee of Shiva, becomes a great fault. Therefore, he says, "my small great fault," meaning he admits that his small action is a great fault, and tells the Brahmachari, but also states that the fault was not committed knowingly by him. Saying this, he bows. Bowing here shows Amar-Niti Nayanar bending his body, stooping, pleading, prostrating, humbling himself, and praising the ascetic. The greatness and superiority of the loincloth given by the devotee of Shiva were diminished by the smallness of its disappearance. That is why Amar-Niti Nayanar bows before the Brahmin Brahmachari. Now, the Brahmin Brahmachari speaks to Amar-Niti Nayanar: "Amar-Niti, what you say is good. It hasn't been long since I gave you the loincloth. I gave it to you just now, today. But you keep that loincloth yourself and say you are giving another loincloth in its place. You have been spreading news in the world for many days that you give loincloths to all Shiva devotees. Was the act you did only to usurp my loincloth? Your business is doing well," saying this, the Brahmin Brahmachari, the Lord who had come, became angry, with sparks flying from his eyes. Thus, Lord Shiva, who came as a Brahmin Brahmachari, becomes angry. Lord Shiva, who had concealed the deer in his hand and instead carried a staff, became angry in this manner. Amar-Niti Nayanar's senses were all bewildered, his face withered, and he fell to the ground, exhausted, at the feet of the Brahmin Brahmachari, bowing and saying, "Please forgive this great fault of mine, a small person. This is not a fault that happened knowingly to this servant." Here, Sekkizhar Swamigal, describing Lord Shiva, says that Lord Shiva concealed the deer he held in his hand and came as a Brahmachari carrying a staff. Lord Shiva holds a deer in his divine hands. But here, when he comes in the guise of a Brahmachari, he carries a staff in his hands, befitting the Brahmachari's state. Why does the Lord hold a deer in his divine hands? A deer arose from the yagna performed by the Rishis of Darukavana, intending to destroy this world. The Lord held that deer, which was sent by the Rishis of Darukavana, on the tips of his two fingers. This story is recounted in various Puranas. Here, two states of the Lord are mentioned. One is the state of holding the deer in his divine hands, and the other is the state of coming as a Brahmachari carrying a staff. The first form, holding the deer, is a principle called "showing a deer to catch a deer." When the Lord comes to take souls into His grace, He comes as a Guru in human form. Saiva Siddhanta explains this as "showing a deer to catch a deer." Now, the Lord has come as a Brahmachari carrying a staff. In a classroom, a teacher wakes up sleeping students with the staff or stick in his hand. "Like the act of waking a sleeping student before the teacher with a powerful staff," says Thirumandiram. Sivakavimani Ayya explains these subtleties. Now, the anger (vegutchi) that the Lord assumed is the form of His primordial power, Tirodhana Shakti. This later becomes Arul Shakti (power of grace). Furthermore, the fact that the Lord, who came as a Brahmachari, became angry should be understood as "He appeared to be angry." In reality, the Lord was not angry here. He only appeared to be angry. If the Lord were truly angry, this world would be destroyed. The destruction of Manmatha by the Lord's angry gaze, and the destruction of Brahma and the Tripuras, are well-known stories. Seeing the Brahmachari who pretended to be angry, Amar-Niti Nayanar's senses were all bewildered, his face withered, and he bowed, saying, "This is a fault that happened unknowingly to me. Please forgive this. Instead of this loincloth, I will give you good silk garments and jewels. Please accept them, O Lord." With a disturbed mind and great fear, Amar-Niti Nayanar bowed many times at the feet of the Brahmin Brahmachari. Let us continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 09 — Amar-Niti Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. We are contemplating the Puranam of Amar-Niti Nayanar in the Thillai Vazh Andanar Sarga of Periya Puranam. Amar-Niti Nayanar pleads with the Brahmin Brahmachari, who had come, offering gold, jewels, silk garments, and gems in place of the lost loincloth, and prostrates many times. Hearing this, the Brahmin Brahmachari, feigning a slightly calmed mind, says, "What use are the gold garments, gems, and other things you offer to me? What am I going to do with them? It is enough if you give me a loincloth that is equal in value to the loincloth I wear." For someone in the Brahmachari state, these jewels, gold garments, and gems are of no use. That is why the Brahmin Brahmachari says, "All that you offer will be of no use to me. It is enough if you give me a loincloth equal to mine." Hearing this divine instruction, Amar-Niti Nayanar's mind brightens. Until now, his senses were all bewildered and confused, filled with great fear, and his body trembled as he stood before the Brahmin Brahmachari, Amar-Niti Nayanar, who was like a lion among merchants. Why did Amar-Niti Nayanar, who was like a lion in business, stand trembling and shaking before the Brahmin Brahmachari? It was solely due to the thought that he should not commit any offense against Shiva's devotees, solely due to that fear. This shows Amar-Niti Nayanar's unending love and devotion towards the Lord's devotees. When the Brahmin Brahmachari said that a loincloth equal to his own would suffice, Amar-Niti Nayanar, hearing this, asked the Brahmin Brahmachari, "Yes, I will give you a loincloth equal to yours. But I need your loincloth to place on the scale." Then, the Brahmin Brahmachari gave Amar-Niti Nayanar the wet loincloth, drenched by rain, tied to the tip of the staff he held in his hand, and said, "I gave you one earlier, didn't I? That loincloth and this loincloth are equal. So, give me a loincloth equal to this one." The Brahmin Brahmachari had three loincloths. He was wearing one of them around his waist. So, that loincloth could not be placed on the scale. Apart from that, he had two other loincloths. He had them tied to his staff. Before setting off to bathe in the Kaveri, he gave one of those two loincloths to Amar-Niti Nayanar. The other one is now wet from the rain. The loincloth given to Amar-Niti Nayanar has disappeared. So, the Brahmin Brahmachari asks Amar-Niti Nayanar to give him a loincloth equal to the one remaining loincloth. When this Brahmin Brahmachari first came, the two loincloths tied to the staff he held in his hand were of equal weight. If two loincloths are woven separately, they might not be equal to each other; there could be differences in weight. There could also be differences in size. But if two loincloths are torn equally from the same cloth, they will be equal. The loincloths tied to the staff carried by the Brahmin Brahmachari were such loincloths, torn into two from the same cloth and woven. That is why he says that this loincloth, wet from the rain, is equal to the lost loincloth, and asks for a loincloth equal to it. Furthermore, this Brahmin Brahmachari, without desiring any other object, says that he only wants a loincloth equal to his own.

He takes it. Hearing what the Brahmin Brahmachari said, Amar-Niti Nayanar brought a balance and set it up there. Amar-Niti Nayanar was a merchant, so he always kept a balance for buying and selling goods. Buying and selling was his profession. "For those who thought 'we will see in this trick,' He removed even that trick," says Thiruvachagam. Its meaning is that no matter what tricks or strategies anyone employs to escape, it is the nature of the Lord to enter into those very tricks, transcend their cunning, and bestow grace. This is the meaning of this line from Thiruvachagam. Here, Amar-Niti Nayanar was a merchant. So, in accordance with the practice of trade, by giving one item and asking for an equal item through the balance, the Lord blessed Amar-Niti Nayanar through his trade and took him into His grace. We can relate this to the above lines from Thiruvachagam and rejoice. In one pan of the balance brought by Amar-Niti Nayanar, the Brahmin Brahmachari places his loincloth. Amar-Niti Nayanar places the new loincloth he brought in the other pan. But the two pans are not equal. The pan with the loincloth brought by the Brahmin Brahmachari was lower. The pan with the loincloth placed by Amar-Niti Nayanar rose up. The two pans were not equal. That is, the loincloth placed by Amar-Niti Nayanar was not equal to the weight of the loincloth given by the Brahmin Brahmachari. So now, Amar-Niti Nayanar brings all the loincloths he had kept to give to the Lord's devotees and places them one by one in his pan. Even after placing all the loincloths, the pan on Amar-Niti Nayanar's side remained higher. The pan with the Brahmin Brahmachari's loincloth remained lower. All the loincloths placed by Amar-Niti Nayanar were not equal to that weight. Amar-Niti Nayanar witnesses this miracle. A miracle is a feeling of awe in a state of not knowing what it is. This is an illusion not found in the world. Even though countless loincloths are placed for this one loincloth, they are not equal, he marvels. Amar-Niti Nayanar continued to place fine cloths, silks, and other items, but his pan remained elevated. Earlier, Amar-Niti Nayanar experienced a feeling of wonder. Now he stands amazed. Wonder is a feeling of awe in a state of not knowing what it is. But amazement is a feeling of awe that arises from understanding the reason for something. Earlier, Amar-Niti Nayanar was amazed to see that all the loincloths were not equal to the Brahmachari's loincloth. Now, realizing and determining that the reason for this is Maya, he stands amazed. Therefore, now, in addition to loincloths, he places more silks, cloths, and gold garments in that pan. The Brahmachari asked only for a loincloth. Only a loincloth. He did not ask for jewels, gems, gold, or garments. That is why Amar-Niti Nayanar first placed only loincloths. But even after placing all the loincloths in that pan, the loincloths ran out. Still, the pan did not balance. Therefore, after the loincloths, he places various silk garments in that pan. He places them in the pan on Amar-Niti Nayanar's side. He places silk cloths one by one. Still, they do not balance. So, he places bundles and bundles of silk cloths in Amar-Niti Nayanar's pans. Still, the two pans do not balance. What happens next?

Chapter 10 — Amar-Niti Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. We are contemplating the Puranam of Amar-Niti Nayanar in the Thillai Vazh Andanar Sarga of Periya Puranam. Amar-Niti Nayanar first places the loincloth he held in his hand to match the loincloth of Lord Shiva, who came as a Brahmin Brahmachari. The two do not balance. Then he places all the loincloths he had kept to distribute to all devotees. Even then, the balance does not stand even. Then he places all the fabric items he possessed there. Even then, it does not balance. At this point, Sekkizhar Swamigal has composed a wonderful verse to express the greatness of Amar-Niti Nayanar's love and the greatness of Lord Shiva's grace. In this instance, silk clothes and all precious garments are placed in Amar-Niti Nayanar's pan. But that pan remains elevated. In the other pan, that is, the Brahmin Brahmachari's pan, there is only his single loincloth. But that pan remains lowered. To convey this, Sekkizhar Swamigal illustrates the principle that "when the pan containing the boundless love of devotees is considered, the pan containing only the Lord will descend with grace." Chapter 11 — Amar-Niti Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. We are contemplating the Puranam of Amar-Niti Nayanar in the Thillai Vazh Andanar Sarga of Periya Puranam. While the pan containing the Brahmin Brahmachari's loincloth remains low, Amar-Niti Nayanar's pan, with various cloths and silk garments, rises high. Amazed by this, Amar-Niti Nayanar approaches the Brahmin Brahmachari and pleads, "I have placed all my clothes, all my fabric items, here. Yet, this pan does not balance. Therefore, please grant me permission to place my other possessions." Hearing this, Lord Shiva, the consort of the Goddess, assents and says, "What else is there for me to say? Place your other possessions, your other items. Any item will do. I just need an item equal to my loincloth. That is all I ask." In this verse, Sekkizhar Swamigal refers to the Lord as "Mangai Pakar" (the one who has the Goddess as a part). In all the verses until now, Sekkizhar Swamigal referred to the Lord as a Brahmin Brahmachari, a Vedic Brahmachari. Here, for the first time, Sekkizhar Swamigal refers to the Lord as "Mangai Pakar." Its meaning is "the one who has Uma Devi as one part." This is because the Lord, who came, is about to bestow grace upon Amar-Niti Nayanar very soon. It is the tradition we have seen that when the Lord bestows grace upon beings, He should come with Uma Devi. Therefore, Sekkizhar Swamigal subtly sings here, for the first time, referring to the Lord as "Mangai Pakar," in accordance with that tradition. Immediately, Amar-Niti Nayanar placed all his gold, silver, other metals, and all his precious gems, jewels, and other valuables in his pan. Even now, the two pans did not balance. Amar-Niti Nayanar's pan remained elevated. Seeing this, all the people of the world were astonished. At this point, Sekkizhar Peruman says: The Lord's loincloth is the Vedas. This loincloth is made of the essence of the four Vedas, filled with penance, and composed of scriptures. Lord Shiva Himself lovingly places that loincloth on the scale. Will not only Amar-Niti Nayanar's wealth on this small earth, but even all the worlds, be equal to this loincloth? Even saying that all the worlds are not equal to this loincloth would not be a praise for that loincloth. The glory of this loincloth stands many crores of times greater than that, says the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. The threads in Lord Shiva's loincloth are the scriptures of the Vedas. They are also the other artistic texts that explain the Vedas. What is in the loincloth is thread. The Vedas revealed by the Lord are also texts. What is a text? "Just as a thread straightens crookedness, so too a text of merit straightens the crookedness of human minds," says the Nannool sutra. What does this mean? A carpenter, who cuts trees from the forest and brings them to make doors, windows, frames, etc., in houses, always carries a thread with him. Wooden items need to be made from the trees cut and brought from the forest. The trees cut and brought from the forest will be crooked. Therefore, to remove their crookedness and make them into beautiful wooden articles, the carpenter uses the thread he has to straighten the crookedness of the wood. This is the use of a thread or string. Does "nool" (thread) not also mean "book"? Books are those that remove the crookedness in human minds and straighten them.

That is why books are also called "nool" in Tamil. The thread-like string held by a carpenter removes the crookedness of wood. The Vedas and other texts revealed by Lord Shiva, the Vishwakarma of this universe, remove the crookedness in human minds, that is, remove faults, and regulate human minds. That is why Sekkizhar Peruman here uses a pun (siledai) to sing, implying both meanings: that the threads in the Lord's loincloth are the Vedas and the texts that explain the Vedas. Will the small wealth of Amar-Niti Nayanar on this small earth be equal to such a glorious loincloth? No, it will not. Not only that, this earth is a tiny particle of this universe. Is it just the Prithvi (earth) sphere that encompasses this small earth? Even if all the spheres in all the other worlds, such as water and air, are combined, they would be a mere speck when compared to the glory of the Lord's loincloth. Here, Sekkizhar Swamigal mentions various worlds, doesn't he? Worlds are countless abodes provided by the Lord for countless souls of various types to experience their enjoyments. These are called worlds. Each world contains numerous universes. Each universe contains numerous worlds. Saiva Siddhanta states that there are a total of 224 worlds. It is said in Kandapuranam that Surapadman, who tormented all the Devas, ruled 1008 universes. When Lord Muruga, who had a gracious gaze upon him, showed his Vishwaroopam (cosmic form) to Surapadman, saying, "Behold my Vishwaroopam," the 1008 great universes ruled by Surapadman were seen hanging from the tip of a single hair on Lord Muruga's divine foot. Therefore, considering this, we can understand the import of why Sekkizhar Peruman says that all the worlds are not equal to even a tiny speck of the glory of the Lord's loincloth. If we delve deeper into this, according to Saiva Siddhanta scriptures, Maya is of two types: Suddha Maya (pure Maya) and Asuddha Maya (impure Maya). From this Maya, the Lord creates 36 Tattvas (principles). Within these 36 Tattvas, 224 worlds are situated. However, the Lord's loincloth, the Vedas, is the first Tattva among these 36 Tattvas, known as Nada Tattva. All the worlds are contained below this. That is why Sekkizhar Swamigal explains that all the worlds, which are below the Nada Tattva (the Vedas, which are the Lord's loincloth), cannot be equal to it. Even after Amar-Niti Nayanar placed all his wealth in his pan, the two pans did not balance. Seeing this, Amar-Niti Nayanar went before the Brahmin Brahmachari and pleaded, "My Lord, I have placed all our wealth, without leaving anything, in our pan. Now, only I, my wife, and my young son remain. If you agree, please grant us permission to also climb into this pan." The wealth placed by Amar-Niti Nayanar was wealth he had earned himself. It was faultless wealth, sinless wealth. Until now, Amar-Niti Nayanar had thought that this wealth would make the loincloth balance. But even after placing all that wealth, the two pans did not balance. According to Saiva Siddhanta

scriptures, many things are described as manifestations or effects of the Anava Malam (ego impurity). Among them, the most important are the ego ("I") and possessiveness ("mine"). Thinking "I" is ego (ahankaram). Thinking "mine" is possessiveness (mamakaram). Here, Sekkizhar Swamigal implicitly indicates that after Amar-Niti Nayanar placed all the wealth he had earned in his pan, his possessiveness, his sense of "mine," that all those were his wealth, his possessions, was eradicated. Following this, Amar-Niti Nayanar pleads with the Brahmin Brahmachari, saying, "Now only I, my wife, and my child remain. If you agree, we too wish to climb into this pan," doesn't he? Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman implicitly indicates here that now even the ego of "I" is removed. In this sequence, Amar-Niti Nayanar first mentions his wife, and then his child. A child comes through the wife. Therefore, Sekkizhar Swamigal places the wife first and the child second in the sequence. Now, the wife is a life partner. The wife is a support for life, a life-support. That is why Amar-Niti Nayanar says "my wife." But when referring to his child, he only says "young son." This is because a child is a support for both. Moreover, a child, that is, a child of young age, depends on the mother. That is why he places the child after the wife. Thus, Amar-Niti Nayanar tells the Brahmachari, "I, my wife, and child can climb into this pan for this loincloth, for the pan with this loincloth to stand straight, to balance. If you think we have the qualification for it, if you consent, we three will climb in." Here, Amar-Niti Nayanar says this, realizing the greatness of the loincloth and his own insignificance. The Brahmin Brahmachari, who had until now feigned anger, now, hearing Amar-Niti Nayanar say this, casts his beautiful glance of grace upon Amar-Niti Nayanar. He looks with a glance of grace. This means that the actions of the Lord, which had until now manifested as the power of concealment (Tirodhana Shakti), now manifest as acts of grace. Thus, Lord Shiva, casting a glance of grace upon Amar-Niti Nayanar, resolved to free Amar-Niti Nayanar from this "salakku" (false claim). What is "salakku"? "Salakku" means a false case. The opposite of "valakku" (case) is "salakku." If one applies to claim ownership of one's own property, it is called a "valakku." If one creates a false case, files a false case, claiming ownership of property that does not belong to them, that they have no right to, it is called a "salakku." What is the "salakku" that Amar-Niti Nayanar had? All this world, all these universes, all these worlds, are the Lord's property, the Lord's possession. Yet, people in this world think that these worldly things, which belong only to the Lord, are theirs, things they have no right to. This possessiveness, thinking "my property, my wealth" about Shiva's property that does not belong to them, and the ego of "I," is the true "salakku," the true suffering, that we, as souls, have. Here, in this story, the state of being involved in unreal things is referred to as "salakku." Therefore, Lord Shiva bestows grace here to free Amar-Niti Nayanar from this "salakku." Just as someone stuck in mud and unable to get out is helped by someone on the bank reaching out a hand and pulling them out of that suffering, here Lord Shiva resolved, with great compassion, to free Amar-Niti Nayanar from that "salakku" of being involved in his possessions. Chapter 12 — Amar-Niti Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. We are contemplating the story of Amar-Niti Nayanar in the Thiruvazh Andhanar Sarukkam of Periya Puranam. Amar-Niti Nayanar places all his possessions on his scale to match the loincloth of the Brahmin Brahmachari. Yet, the two scales do not balance. Therefore, Amar-Niti Nayanar tells the Brahmin Brahmachari that he has placed all his possessions on the scale, and now only he, his wife, and his child remain. He asks for permission for them to also ascend the scale. The Brahmin Brahmachari agrees and gives his consent. Amar-Niti Nayanar is greatly delighted by the Brahmin Brahmachari's consent. This is because he found a way to atone for his mistake by giving the Brahmin Brahmachari something equivalent to the loincloth, even if it meant giving himself. This was the reason for Amar-Niti Nayanar's joy. Therefore, in his happiness, Amar-Niti Nayanar bows his head at the feet of the Brahmin Brahmachari. After this, Amar-Niti Nayanar resolves to place himself, his wife, and his son on the scale. When Sekkizhar Swamigal speaks of Amar-Niti Nayanar's wife in this verse, he refers to her as "punai malar kuzhal manaiviyar" (wife with hair adorned with flowers). This means that Amar-Niti Nayanar's wife had hair adorned with flowers. It is a sign of great auspiciousness for married women (sumangalis) to always keep their hair tied and adorned with flowers. Keeping one's hair untied or unadorned is a sign of inauspiciousness. Similarly, wearing real flowers in one's hair is also an auspicious sign for married women. Amar-Niti Nayanar's wife always maintained such an auspicious appearance. Therefore, the implicit meaning here is that she attained the eternal auspiciousness of liberation (mukti) with her husband. Now, Amar-Niti Nayanar prepares to place himself, his wife, and his son on the scale. Earlier, when Amar-Niti Nayanar placed his own possessions on the scale, Sekkizhar states that he placed each item one by one. First, he placed a loincloth. Then, he placed all the loincloths he had, one by one. Then, he placed all the garments he possessed, one by one. Then, he placed all his jewels, ornaments, gold, and other items. Sekkizhar, who said he placed all these one by one, now states that Amar-Niti Nayanar ascended the scale with his wife and child together. Why did he not place them one by one, but rather ascended the scale with his wife and child? Sivakavimani Ayya offers several justifications for this. Why not place them one by one? Why should all three ascend together? Let's assume he places his son first. The child is small. Therefore, the child cannot be placed on the scale first, as the child is small. It is unlikely that the child would remain without his parents, Amar-Niti Nayanar and his wife. Therefore, the son cannot be placed first. Now, could he not have placed his wife on the scale first? Why did he not do so? Because during their marriage, Amar-Niti Nayanar promised his wife that he would never leave her. If he were to place his wife on the scale first now, that promise would be broken. That is why he did not place his wife first. Now, could Amar-Niti Nayanar not have ascended the scale alone first? Instead, he ascends with his wife and child. Why did he not ascend the scale alone? Because the Brahmin Brahmachari had previously said, "Whatever your wealth, whatever your possessions, you can place them on this scale." The Brahmin Brahmachari said that first, didn't he? Therefore, Amar-Niti Nayanar ascends this scale with his wife and children, who are his assets, his possessions. For a person, their wife and children are the very essence of their life, their possessions. That is why Amar-Niti Nayanar attempts to ascend the scale with these possessions, his wife and children. Amar-Niti Nayanar told the Brahmin Brahmachari, "I will place all my possessions on this scale." Therefore, it would not be just to not place his wife and children, who are his life's essence, on this scale. That is why he takes his wife and children with him. If he were to ascend the scale alone, without his wife and children, two possibilities could arise. One, when Amar-Niti Nayanar ascends, the two scales might balance. Or, the second possibility, even after Amar-Niti Nayanar ascends, the scale might not balance. Both these possibilities, if Amar-Niti Nayanar were to ascend alone, would turn into errors for Amar-Niti Nayanar. Let's consider the first possibility. If Amar-Niti Nayanar were to ascend the scale alone and the two scales were to balance, Amar-Niti Nayanar would become the property of the Brahmin Brahmachari. Amar-Niti Nayanar's wife and child, who did not ascend the scale, would be left alone in this world. This would be a great error and sin for Amar-Niti Nayanar. Because when Amar-Niti Nayanar got married, he swore to his wife, "I will not leave you as long as I live," didn't he? That oath would be broken here. Therefore, this would result in an error. This is the first possibility. Now, what could happen as the second possibility if Amar-Niti Nayanar were to ascend the scale alone? The two scales might not balance. If Amar-Niti Nayanar were to stand on one side of the scale and the Brahmin Brahmachari's loincloth on the other, and still the two scales were not balanced, then Amar-Niti Nayanar would lose the right to place his wife and child on the scale. Because even if Amar-Niti Nayanar ascended alone and the two scales did not balance, Amar-Niti Nayanar would become the property of the Brahmin Brahmachari. He would have become the property of the Brahmin Brahmachari. Therefore, it would mean that he no longer owned anything. So, Amar-Niti Nayanar would become someone who has no right to place his wife and child on the scale. That is why, considering all these justifications, Amar-Niti Nayanar decides to ascend the scale together with his wife and son. Now, an important doubt will arise in our minds. What did the Brahmin Brahmachari say? He said, "Whatever possessions you own, you can place them on this scale," didn't he? In that case, Amar-Niti Nayanar, his wife, and his son ascend the scale. Amar-Niti Nayanar does not ascend alone but places his wife and son on the scale. Does this mean that a person's wife and child are considered their property? Are a wife and child a person's possession? Can a wife and children be a man's property, just as a house and land are considered property? Such progressive questions may arise. To find an answer to this, we can turn our thoughts to the chapters "Vazhkai Thunai Nalam" (The excellence of a life partner) and "Puthalvarai Peruthal" (The acquisition of children) in the Thirukkural, authored by Thiruvalluva Deva Nayanar. In the chapter "Vazhkai Thunai Nalam," Thiruvalluvar speaks of the greatness of a wife. In the tenth Kural, "Mangalam enba manaichchi matru adhan nankalam nanmakkad perru" (Auspiciousness is the excellence of a wife; its good ornament is the obtaining of good children). Here, the phrase "Mangalam enba manaichchi" appears, doesn't it? Parimelazhagar explains this. What is good for a person? The good that a person receives is the good qualities and good deeds of his wife. Whether good or bad comes to a person, they are both his own possessions, aren't they? Therefore, the good qualities and good deeds of his wife, which are among the greatest benefits that come to a person, become the possessions of a husband. They become the property of the husband. That is why the Brahmin Brahmachari commanded Amar-Niti Nayanar to place all his possessions on the scale, didn't he? Amar-Niti Nayanar's wife, through her good qualities and good deeds, had always brought good to Amar-Niti Nayanar until now. And she would continue to do so. Thus, the good that Amar-Niti Nayanar's wife brought, and the good that would come through her, were Amar-Niti Nayanar's possessions. That is why Amar-Niti Nayanar places his wife on the scale with him. If, by placing his wife on the scale, the scale were to balance, then the good he had received through his wife's good qualities and deeds, and the good he would receive in the future, would all be given to the Brahmin Brahmachari. It is for this reason that Amar-Niti Nayanar decides to place his wife on the scale. According to this principle, elders refer to a wife and children as a person's "uyir saarbu" (life's support/dependence). "Saarbu" means to depend on. To depend on someone can be a thing or a life. Therefore, wife and children become life's support. Now, if the question arises whether a person's child is his property, we can find the answer in the chapter "Puthalvarai Peruthal" in Thirukkural by Thiruvalluva Deva Nayanar. In the third Kural of the chapter "Puthalvarai Peruthal," "Tham porul enbadham makkal avar porul tham tham vinaiyaan varum" (Their wealth, say the wise, are their children; their children's wealth comes from their own deeds). Parimelazhagar explains this as follows: What do the wise say? The wise say that their children are their wealth. This is because the wealth acquired by a child comes to the father through the good deeds the child performs towards his father. Therefore, a good child becomes a father's wealth, his possession, his property. If a person has a good child, that child will use the wealth he has accumulated to care for his father throughout his life. Even after his father's passing, a good child will perform the necessary ancestral rites (pithur kariyas) for his father without fail. Through these ancestral rites, his father, no matter which world or form he is in, will benefit from his son's actions. Furthermore, a good child, after his father's passing, will not only perform the necessary ancestral rites and bring good to his father, but also perform numerous good deeds and charities in his father's name with his own wealth. Through these charities, a good child will fulfill his duty by ensuring that the merits reach his father. Therefore, if a father has a good son, all the wealth accumulated by that son goes to the father. Even if the father is in this birth or in any other world in any other form, he experiences joy and receives good merits through his child's good deeds. Therefore, since the wealth accumulated by their children goes to their father, the wise consider a father's good child to be the greatest wealth among all the wealth a father possesses. Here, Amar-Niti Nayanar also promised the Brahmin Brahmachari that he would give all his possessions, didn't he? That is why Amar-Niti Nayanar ascends the scale, including his son, who is the greatest possession among all the possessions he has acquired. Now, after ascending the scale with his son, if the two scales were to balance, then he and his son would become the property of the Brahmin Brahmachari. Therefore, all the possessions his son accumulates and all the good his son does would become the property of the Brahmin Brahmachari. Here, Amar-Niti Nayanar's promise would also become true. Therefore, based on these justifications, Amar-Niti Nayanar decides to ascend the scale not alone, but with his wife and son. We will continue to contemplate this. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 13 — Amar-Niti Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. We are contemplating the story of Amar-Niti Nayanar in the Thiruvazh Andhanar Sarukkam of Periya Puranam. Amar-Niti Nayanar resolves to ascend the scale with his wife and child to match the loincloth of the Brahmin Brahmachari. Before ascending the scale, Amar-Niti Nayanar worships Lord Shiva, who resides in the holy place of Thiruvalur. He chants the sacred five-letter mantra (Thiru Aindezhuthu) of the Lord. Amar-Niti Nayanar chants the Sri Panchaksharam, the sacred five-letter mantra, which is a means and benefit for the love that souls have for the Lord to flourish. He chants the Sri Panchaksharam according to the prescribed method and rules. To contemplate according to the rules requires the action of the mind. To chant according to the rules requires the action of speech. Thus, by contemplating and chanting the Sri Panchaksharam according to the rules, he worships the Lord. Worship is the action of the body. Therefore, the inner meaning here is that Amar-Niti Nayanar embraces the Lord with his three instruments: mind, speech, and body. Having thus worshipped the Lord residing in Thiruvalur, thought of Him with his mind, and chanted the Sri Panchaksharam with his speech, Amar-Niti Nayanar makes a vow before ascending the scale. He pledges: "If it is true that we have stood firm in our genuine devotion to the Lord, with great love for Him and His sacred ash, without fault or error, then upon our ascent, this scale must balance. The two pans must stand equally." Thus vows Amar-Niti Nayanar. Having made this vow, Amar-Niti Nayanar, his wife, and his child ascend the scale. At that moment, the pan containing the loincloth placed by the Brahmin Brahmachari and the pan on which Amar-Niti Nayanar, his wife, and child stood, balanced. The loincloth worn by the Lord around His waist and the service of Shiva's devotees, who have unwavering love for the Lord, are equal. That is why the scales now balance. The two pans of a scale will only balance when the items placed in them are of equal weight. The needle of the scale will stand straight. Here, the Lord's loincloth and the service of His devotees are of equal weight. That is why the scale now stands straight. There were three people on Nayanar's scale: Nayanar, his wife, and his son. However, it is the greatness of Amar-Niti Nayanar's devotion, his self-sacrifice to the devotees, that stands equal to the loincloth. That is why Sekkizhar Peruman does not say that Amar-Niti Nayanar is equal to the loincloth, but rather that the service Amar-Niti Nayanar rendered to the devotees of the Lord is equal to the loincloth. Amar-Niti Nayanar, before ascending the scale, vows that their true devotion to the Lord is faultless. Initially, Nayanar placed all his possessions, his belongings, on the scale. But he did not place his body and soul, considering them his own. At that time, the two pans did not stand straight, they did not balance. Now, Nayanar has truly surrendered his body, soul, and himself to the Lord. Now the scales stand straight. According to Manikkavasagar Peruman's Thiruvachakam, "Andrey endhan aaviyum udaludaimai ellamum kundre anaiyayi ennai aatkonda pothe kondilaiyo indru or idaiyuru enakku undo" (Did you not take my soul, body, and all my possessions, O mountain-like Lord, when you enslaved me? Is there any obstacle for me today?). After surrendering his body, wealth, and soul to the Lord, the Lord manifests and supports the Nayanar, preventing him from further suffering. According to Appar Swamigal's words, "Than kadan adiyenaiyum thanguthal en kadan pani seithu kidapathe" (His duty is to support me, my duty is to serve and remain). Here, Amar-Niti Nayanar, having nothing else, considers serving the Lord's devotees as his duty. The Lord, in His grace, supports Amar-Niti Nayanar, fulfilling His duty to support His servant. This state of the Lord's devotees serving was praised by Sekkizhar as "anbil kuraipada adiyar adimai thondu" (the unwavering loving service of the devotees). The Lord's loincloth is equal to this service, hence the scale balanced. The Lord, filling all deficiencies, faced Amar-Niti Nayanar. The fourth verse in the "Sampradayam" section of the seventh Tantra of Thirumandiram states: "Pettadithu engum pithatrit thirivenai ottadithu ulla maasellam vaangip pin thattokka maarinan thannaiyum ennaiyum vattamathu otthu vaanipam vaayththe." The meaning of this is: What did I do? I wandered everywhere, babbling and blabbering whatever came to my mouth. Such a one as me, the Sivaguru came before me and removed all my faults. Just as one cleanses the dust-laden cobwebs of a house, the Sivaguru came before me and removed all my faults. Not only that, after removing all those faults from me, and taking all those faults upon Himself, the Sivaguru made Him and me equal, taking me to Himself and giving Himself to me. Thus, this trade of giving and taking was accomplished without hindrance, says Thirumoolar Peruman. Lord Shiva, as Sivaguru, first comes before souls and removes their faults. Removing the faults of souls means removing the three faults of anavam (ego), kanmam (karma), and mayai (illusion). Even after removing them, this soul and that Shiva do not become one at any time. Yet, what does this Shiva do? He makes this unequal jiva (soul) equal to Himself, selling Himself to that jiva and buying that jiva, thus engaging in an unequal trade. Why is this an unequal trade? Because in trade, what is bought and sold are equal goods. It is the basic nature of trade to give goods of equal value and receive goods of equal value in return. But here, the jiva is never equal to Shiva. Such a jiva, out of His compassion, the Lord elevates its worth, gives Himself, and buys the jiva, thus engaging in this unequal trade. Lord Shiva sells Himself here. In return, He buys jivas, or souls. What does it mean to sell Himself to the jiva? It means to imprint His Lordship (pathitthanmai) upon the soul. Now, He buys the jiva, doesn't He? He buys the souls, doesn't He? What is that? He merges the jiva-consciousness within the souls into His Shiva-consciousness. This is the meaning of the Lord buying the souls. Therefore, Lord Shiva performs this unequal trade of imprinting His Lordship upon the soul and selling Himself, and merging the jiva-consciousness of the souls into His Shiva-consciousness and buying the souls. Manikkavasagar Peruman also sings of this in the Thiruvachakam, Koyil Thirupathikam: "Thanthathu unthannai kondathu entannai Sankara aarkalo sathurar antham ondru illaa aanantham petren yaathu nee petrathu ondru enpaal sinthaiye koyil konda emperumaan thirupperunthurai urai sivane enthaiye eesa udal idam kondaay yaan itharku ilan or kaimmaare" (You gave Yourself, and took me, Sankara! Who is the clever one? I received endless bliss. What did You gain from me? O Lord who made my mind Your temple, O Shiva residing in Thirupperunthurai, my Father, O Lord, You took my body as Your abode. I have no recompense for this). Here too, Manikkavasagar sings of Shiva's unequal trade. Manikkavasagar asks the Lord, "Sankara, You gave Yourself, but in return, You took me. Whose trade was successful in this? Who benefited from this? You gave Yourself to me. Because of that, I received endless bliss. In return for giving Yourself to me, You took me. But what did You gain by taking me, a flawed being? What was Your benefit? Nothing at all. Therefore, Sankara, in this trade, You are the one who lost. O Lord who made the minds of devotees His temple, O Shiva residing in Thirupperunthurai, my Father, O Lord, what recompense can I offer for this?" Thus sings Manikkavasagar, melting with devotion. The story of Amar-Niti Nayanar, which serves as an explanation for Thirumandiram and Thiruvachakam, is something we should contemplate and rejoice in. Seeing the pan on which Amar-Niti Nayanar, his wife, and his child stood, and the pan with the Lord's loincloth, standing equally, all the people of the world were filled with wonder and praised and worshipped Amar-Niti Nayanar. The Devas residing in the celestial world showered Karpaga flowers like rain. "Atthan aandu than adiyil koottiya athisayam kandame" (We saw the wonder of the Lord gathering His devotees) is from Thiruvachakam, Athisayappathu. The Devas showered Karpaga flowers. Using this shower of flowers as a screen, the Brahmin Vedic scholar, adorned with the Tripundra, disappeared and appeared in the sky in the same form as Lord Shiva with Uma Devi in the holy place of Thiruvalur, granting darshan to Amar-Niti Nayanar. We have already seen in the Thiruvalur Sthala Puranam that the Lord resides in the temple of Thiruvalur as a Linga form and as Uma Maheswarar, haven't we? The Lord now grants Amar-Niti Nayanar a vision of Himself in that form with Uma Devi. Seeing that divine vision, Amar-Niti Nayanar, his wife, and his son, who were standing on one pan of the scale, praised and worshipped Him. Thus, Uma Maheswarar, who granted them this vision, bestowed upon these three the imperishable and rare Shiva Pada, where they would forever worship Him, granting grace to Amar-Niti Nayanar, his wife, and his son. The Shiva Pada granted by the Lord is the liberation (mukti) that ensures they will never be reborn and will always remain in Shiva Sayujyam. Thus, as Parvati and Parameswara showered their grace, by their divine grace, the scale on which Amar-Niti Nayanar, his wife, and his son stood transformed into a vimana (celestial vehicle) and ascended, carrying all three of them to Shivapuram. The scale itself took the form of a vimana and carried them upwards. Even when Amar-Niti Nayanar placed all his possessions on the scale, the loincloth pan remained down, which was also due to divine grace. Later, when these three people stood on one pan, and the two pans balanced, it was also due to divine grace. Now, the scale itself transforming into a vimana and ascending was also due to divine grace. It was the act of divine grace. Thus, by the divine grace of the Lord, Amar-Niti Nayanar, his wife, and his son reached Shivapuri, the pure realm. Lord Shiva bestowed upon them the liberation (moksha), the path to the world from which there is no return, where they would never be reborn. Thus, Sekkizhar Swamigal concludes the story of Amar-Niti Nayanar. Through this story, Sivakavimani Ayya provides the following lessons we learn: Offering food to devotees and understanding their needs, providing them with rags, undergarments, loincloths, etc., are great benefits derived from the wealth we possess. Serving in the Lord Shiva's festivals and offering food to devotees are excellent Shiva Punya (meritorious deeds) that yield great benefits. Earning wealth in a righteous way is one thing. Enjoying the benefits of that earned wealth is another. Amar-Niti Nayanar, in Pazhaiyarai, performed unwavering service to Shiva's devotees and earned great wealth in a righteous way. Later, he left that town and settled in Thiruvalur, where he served in festivals and offered to devotees, thus enjoying the benefits of his earned wealth. However, most people in this world today, without considering what is right or wrong, spend their time thinking only of earning wealth by any means. They make no effort to enjoy the benefits of the wealth they earn. Such people, who acquire wealth but fail to experience its true benefits, are ignorant. Devotees who have an unwavering mind fixed on the Lord's lotus feet will cut off deceitful and powerful karmas. Lord Shiva is more pleased with bathing in the pure water of the love of devotees who have an unwavering mind fixed on Him, than bathing in the Ganges or Cauvery. "Anbardham anbuku mudalvar tham arul thazhvurum" (The Lord's grace will be humbled by the love of His devotees). Even if all the worlds are placed against the loincloth, which is the essence of the four Vedas, they will not balance. But only the unwavering loving service of the devotees will be equal to it. In whatever fields individuals perform genuine and truthful service to the Lord, those very fields will become the paths for them to enter the Lord's world. Here, we must observe that Nayanar's trade instrument, the scale, served to measure his devotion and ultimately became the vimana that carried Nayanar to Shivapuri. Even if all one's possessions are surrendered to the Lord, if one does not surrender oneself, the Lord's grace will not manifest. Here, after Nayanar surrendered all his material possessions, the scale did not balance. But after he surrendered his wife and son, who were his life's support, and himself to the Lord, the pans balanced, and divine grace manifested. As seen in Nayanar's story, the Lord places all souls on a scale and only after they are balanced by overflowing love does He allow those souls to enter His world. This is the inner meaning of the Lord's trade. It is our duty to protect and return any small item entrusted to us. To steal, damage, or lose anything entrusted to us is a highly improper act and a great error. If such an error occurs, it is the act of the noble to make amends and rectify the error, no matter what it takes. Here, we must observe that for a single loincloth, Nayanar surrendered not only his immeasurable wealth but also himself. Thus, Sivakavimani Ayya explains the lessons we learn from the story of Amar-Niti Nayanar. Thus, Sekkizhar Swamigal concludes the story of Amar-Niti Nayanar. Praise to the feet of Amar-Niti Nayanar! Praise to the feet of the Lord of Thiruvalur! Praise to the feet of Sundaramurthy Swamigal! Praise to the feet of Sekkizhar Swamigal! Praise to the feet of Sivakavimani Ayya, who graciously expounded on Periya Puranam! We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.
