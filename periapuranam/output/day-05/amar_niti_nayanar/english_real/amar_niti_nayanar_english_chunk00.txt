Chapter 01 — Amar-Niti Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. In the Periya Puranam, in the second chapter, Thillai Vaazh Andhanar Sarukkam, we have so far contemplated the Puranas of Thillai Vaazh Andhanars, Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar, Iyarpagai Nayanar, Iḷayankudi Maara Nayanar, Meypporul Nayanar, and Viranminda Nayanar. Now, we are about to contemplate the Purana of Amar-Niti Nayanar. According to the sequence, the Purana of Amar-Niti Nayanar is the seventh Purana. First, we will look at a summary of the story, and then we will extensively contemplate the Purana of Amar-Niti Nayanar through the verses of Sekkizhar Peruman's Periya Puranam. Our Amar-Niti Nayanar was born in the Vaishya community in the town of Pazhaiyarai in the Chola country. Our Amar-Niti Nayanar was a great wealthy man. Amar-Niti Nayanar lived with great devotion to Shiva and devotion to Shiva's devotees, serving them sacred food, understanding their needs, and providing them with necessities such as kaupinam (loincloths). At that time, a grand festival (Mahotsavam) was taking place in the nearby Shiva temple of Thirunallur. Therefore, to serve sacred food to the Shiva devotees who came to witness the Mahotsavam in Thirunallur, our Amar-Niti Nayanar went to Thirunallur with his family and relatives and built a mutt (monastery) there for the Shiva devotees to partake of sacred food. In that mutt, he served sacred food to Shiva devotees and provided them with other necessities. One day, during these times, Lord Shiva appeared in the form of a Brahmin celibate (Brahmachari) at Amar-Niti Nayanar's mutt. Lord Shiva, who came in the form of a Brahmin celibate, was holding a staff in his hand. Tied to that staff were a vibhuti bag and two kaupinams. Seeing a Brahmin celibate Shiva devotee coming to his mutt, Amar-Niti Nayanar was overjoyed. He went forward to greet and worship the Brahmin celibate, saying, "Swami, what penance did I perform in my previous birth to have you grace this place?" and welcomed him with kind words. Then, the Brahmachari addressed Amar-Niti Nayanar, saying, "I have heard that you serve sacred food to devotees and provide them with clothes and kaupinams. Desiring to see you, I have come." Hearing this, Amar-Niti Nayanar prayed, "Swami, in this mutt, Brahmins also cook for Brahmins to dine. Therefore, Your Holiness should also partake of sacred food here." The Brahmachari who had come agreed to this, saying, "I will go and bathe in the Kaveri. If it rains then, please keep this dry kaupinam for me to wear." Saying this, he untied one of the two kaupinams tied to the staff in his hand and gave it to Amar-Niti Nayanar. He asked him to keep the kaupinam safely and return it to him after he had bathed and returned. Saying this, the Brahmachari went to bathe in the Kaveri. Amar-Niti Nayanar carefully placed the kaupinam given by the Brahmachari in a safe place. Lord Shiva, the Brahmachari who had gone to bathe, made the kaupinam he had given to Amar-Niti Nayanar disappear. Then, Lord Shiva, the Brahmachari, while returning after bathing in the Kaveri, caused rain to fall and, drenched in the rain, reached Amar-Niti Nayanar's mutt. Amar-Niti Nayanar, seeing the Brahmin celibate approaching, went forward to greet him and worshipped him, saying, "The cooking is done, and the food is ready." Now, Lord Shiva, in the form of the Brahmachari, addressed Amar-Niti Nayanar, saying, "It rained on my way here, and I got drenched. Therefore, the kaupinam tied to the staff in my hand has become wet. So, please bring the kaupinam I gave you." Immediately, Amar-Niti Nayanar went inside and looked for the place where he had kept the kaupinam. The kaupinam was not there. He searched in other places as well. The kaupinam was not found in other places either. Therefore, greatly distressed, he took another kaupinam and went before the Brahmachari, saying, "Swami, the kaupinam you gave me is missing. I had kept it safely. However, it is now missing. Even after searching in other places, I could not find it. I do not know how it disappeared. I have brought another good kaupinam now. This is not a torn kaupinam. It is a specially woven kaupinam. Therefore, Your Holiness should remove your wet kaupinam and wear this one, and forgive the fault I have committed." Amar-Niti Nayanar stood praying. Hearing this, Lord Shiva, in the form of the Brahmachari, became very angry and said, "What you say is very good. It has not been many days since I gave you the kaupinam. I gave it just now. In such a situation, you have taken my kaupinam and are asking me to accept another kaupinam. What a wonder is this? You have spread news in the town that you will give kaupinams to all Shiva devotees, and now you have taken my kaupinam. Your business is doing very well," said Lord Shiva, who came in the form of the Brahmachari. Hearing this, Amar-Niti Nayanar was frightened, his face fell, and he trembled, saying, "Swami, I did not commit this fault knowingly. Somehow, the kaupinam you gave me has disappeared. Therefore, you must forgive this fault. I will perform all the services you need. Besides this alternative loincloth, I will also give you much gold, silk garments, jewels, and so on. Please accept them," he said. To this, the Brahmin celibate, appearing as if his anger had subsided, said, "Why do I need gold, silk garments, and jewels? It is enough if you give me another kaupinam of equal weight to the useful kaupinam I wear." Amar-Niti Nayanar was delighted and asked, "To the weight of which kaupinam should I give an equal weight?" Then the Brahmachari said, "This is the kaupinam that is equal in weight to the kaupinam you lost," and untied the kaupinam tied to the end of the staff in his hand, saying, "Give me a kaupinam of equal weight to this." Amar-Niti Nayanar said, "Very well," and brought a balance and placed it there. The Brahmin celibate placed his kaupinam on one pan. Amar-Niti Nayanar placed the newly woven kaupinam in his hand on the other pan. At that time, since the balance was not of equal weight, the pan containing the loincloth placed by Amar-Niti Nayanar rose up. Seeing this, Amar-Niti Nayanar, thinking that he must provide a kaupinam of equal weight to the Brahmachari's kaupinam, brought all the kaupinams he had kept to give to Shiva devotees and placed them on the other pan. Even after placing them, Amar-Niti Nayanar's pan still rose higher. The pan containing the Brahmachari's kaupinam remained below. Seeing this, Amar-Niti Nayanar was astonished and brought various kinds of clothes and silks and kept adding them. As he kept adding them, his pan continued to rise.

Astonished by this, Amar-Niti Nayanar worshipped the Brahmachari and said, "Even after placing countless clothes and silks, it is not equal to your kaupinam. Therefore, please permit me to place my other possessions as well." To this, the Brahmin celibate said, "What more is there for me to say? Try placing your other possessions as well. Anyway, it must stand at an equal weight to my loincloth." Immediately, Amar-Niti Nayanar brought loads of nine gems, gold, silver, and other metals and placed them. Still, the pan remained elevated. Amar-Niti Nayanar, seeing this, worshipped the Brahmachari and said, "None of my possessions are left now. I have placed everything on this pan. Now, only I, my wife, and my son remain. If it pleases Your Holiness, please permit us to also ascend this pan." Lord Shiva, in the form of the Brahmachari, agreed to this. Seeing that, Amar-Niti Nayanar was delighted. He worshipped the Brahmin celibate, circumambulated the balance clockwise with his wife and son, and said, "If we have not faltered in our service to Shiva devotees, then as soon as we ascend, let this pan become equal to the pan containing the Brahmachari's kaupinam." Saying this, he worshipped Parameswaran residing in Thirunallur, recited the Sri Panchaksharam, and joyfully ascended the pan. As soon as they ascended, the kaupinam adorned on the sacred waist of Parameswaran and the unwavering devotion of Amar-Niti Nayanar, who was a servant of Shiva, were equal in greatness. Thus, the two pans of the balance stood equally. All who witnessed this miracle worshipped Amar-Niti Nayanar and praised him. The Devas showered flowers from the Kalpaka trees in the sky like rain. Lord Shiva, the Lord of Thirukailasam, concealed his form as a Brahmin celibate and appeared in the sky, mounted on the Rishabam (bull), with Goddess Parvati. Amar-Niti Nayanar, his wife, and his son worshipped them. Lord Shiva, casting his gracious gaze upon the three of them, blessed them, saying, "You three shall receive Our grace and worship Us in Our presence," and then disappeared. Amar-Niti Nayanar, his wife, and his son, by the grace of that God, ascended to the Shiva Loka (abode of Shiva) as the balance itself transformed into a vimana (celestial vehicle). They forever immersed themselves in the great flood of Shiva's bliss and experienced the joy of liberation. This is the concise history of Amar-Niti Nayanar. Arumuga Navalar, who provided the summary for this Purana, writes that building mutts in Shiva temples and serving sacred food to worthy Shiva devotees with sincerity and devotion during auspicious times and festival periods, and providing them with clothes, kaupinams, and other necessities, is a great act of merit. As evidence for this, he quotes from texts such as Shiva Dharmotharam and Shiva Punniya Thelivu. Now, let us extensively contemplate the Purana of Amar-Niti Nayanar through the verses of Sekkizhar's Periya Puranam. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 02 — Amar-Niti Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. We are contemplating the Purana of Amar-Niti Nayanar in the second chapter of Periya Puranam, the Thillai Vaazh Andhanar Sarukkam. We have seen the concise history of Amar-Niti Nayanar's Purana. Now, let us extensively contemplate the Nayanar's Purana. The phrase about Amar-Niti Nayanar in Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's Thiruthondathogai is "Allimen Mullaiyandhar Amar-Niti-kku Adiyen" (I am a servant to Amar-Niti, who wears a garland of soft jasmine). Contemplating this through the verses of Thiruthondathogai, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal sings, "I am a servant to the devotees of Thillai Vaazh Andhanars, I am a servant to Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar, I am a servant to Iyarpagai, who never said 'no', I am a servant to Ilaiyan Kudi Maran's devotees, I am a servant to Meypporul, who was very skilled in winning, I am a servant to Viranmindar of Kundrai surrounded by vast groves," and then adds the phrase about Amar-Niti Nayanar: "Allimen Mullaiyandhar Amar-Niti-kku Adiyen." First, let us contemplate the meaning of this line from the anthology Thiruthondathogai by Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, and then we will proceed to contemplate the type and detailed texts in order. The Purana of Amar-Niti Nayanar is the seventh Purana in sequence. "Allimen Mullaiyandhar Amar-Niti-kku Adiyen." Its meaning is, "I am a servant to Amar-Niti Nayanar, a merchant who wears a soft jasmine garland with inner petals." Sundaramoorthy Swamigal sings "Allimen Mullaiyandhar Amar-Niti," meaning Amar-Niti Nayanar who wears a garland of jasmine flowers. What kind of jasmine flower is it? It is "Allimen Mullaiyandhar," meaning a soft jasmine garland with inner petals. "Alli" means inner petal. "Pulli" means outer petal. Generally, flowers have two types of petals: inner and outer. It is our custom to call the inner petal "alli" and the outer petal "pulli." For all flowers, the inner petal called "alli" and the outer petal called "pulli" are different. They have different shapes. But what is the specialty of the jasmine flower? Here, the inner petal, "alli," and the outer petal, "pulli," are exactly alike. They do not appear different; the outer petal is just like the inner petal. This is the special characteristic of the jasmine flower. Similarly, the inner meaning here is that Amar-Niti Nayanar's inner and outer self, that is, his inside and outside, were one. Furthermore, jasmine (mullai) is the symbolic garland for merchants. And what kind of jasmine flower is this? Sundaramoorthy Swamigal adds the epithet "very soft jasmine flower." Jasmine flowers are very soft, fragrant, and yield flowers for a long time. Not only that, but they also yield many flowers. Thus, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal sings here, "I am a servant to Amar-Niti Nayanar, who wore a garland made of soft jasmine flowers, whose inner and outer petals are one." Now we have seen the anthology. Next, let us look at the verse from the type text, Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi. "Mindum Pozhil Pazhaiyarai Amar-Niti Venpodiyin Mundan Dharitta Prarkku Nallurin Mun Kovana Ner Kondinggarulendru Than Perunchelvamum Thanmaiyum Thandrunda Madhi Nudhalaalaiyum Eendha Thozhilinane" is from Nambiyandar Nambi's Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi. Now, let us contemplate the Purana of Amar-Niti Nayanar through Sekkizhar's Periya Puranam, the detailed text. The town where Amar-Niti Nayanar was born is Pazhaiyarai. This is a town in the good Chola country. The Cholas were a lineage of long-standing glory, spreading the greatness of Lord Shiva's sacred white ash everywhere.

They were descendants of Emperor Sibi. Their country was the Chola country. That Chola country also had the name Ponni Nannadu. Ponni is another name for Kaveri. Because the Kaveri river flows through it, this good Chola country is a land of great prosperity. Another name for Kaveri is Ponni. Ponni means a river that brings gold particles. In the Periya Puranam, in the Thirunattu Sirappu, we saw that the Kaveri brings gold particles in its waters. This Kaveri river was compared to Shiva devotees there. Shiva devotees would offer water, flowers, and gold and other precious gems in fragrant water, perform Arghya, Achamana, and Padya, and worship the Lord with abhishekam. Similarly, the Kaveri river, with its abundant waters, encircles and circumambulates countless Shiva temples on both its banks, offering the flowers it carries to the Lord in those temples and worshipping Him. Therefore, we must remember here that Sekkizhar, in the Thirunattu Sirappu, described this Kaveri river as a parallel to the actions of Shiva devotees. "Punniyam Seyvorkku Poo Undu Neer Undu" (For those who do good deeds, there are flowers and water) is a Thirumandhiram. Arghya water and Thirumanjana water should contain gold and other precious gems and flowers; this is the characteristic of Arghya water and Thirumanjana water. Why does the Kaveri perform Shiva Puja when it flows through the Chola country? Because, due to its connection with Agastya, the great sage Agastya, it receives the blessing of performing Shiva Puja. The previous state of the Kaveri was the Ganga. When it was the Akasha Ganga, it did not receive the blessing of performing Shiva Puja. But when that Akasha Ganga flowed from Agastya's kamandalu (water pot) and then, after the kamandalu was overturned by Lord Ganesha, flowed into the Chola country, it performed Shiva Puja. This is because, in its previous state as Akasha Ganga, it did not receive the blessing of performing Shiva Puja. But it was in the kamandalu of Agastya, the great Shiva sage, wasn't it? Due to that connection, when it later flowed as the Kaveri, it received the merit of performing Shiva Puja. Indra, fearing Asuras like Surapadman, took refuge in Sirkazhi and worshipped the Lord by maintaining a garden there. When those gardens withered without water, Indra worshipped Lord Ganesha and prayed. At that time, Agastya was bringing the Akasha Ganga in his kamandalu to the South. In Kongu Nadu, Ganesha overturned that kamandalu, and it flowed as the Kaveri, entering the Chola country and making the garden in Sirkazhi flourish. This is a history known to all of us. Its details are extensively and gloriously described in the Kandapuranam. Thus, in the great Chola country, where the Kaveri flows, there is a town of enduring glory, surrounded by flower gardens, called Pazhaiyarai. This Pazhaiyarai is a place worshipped by the moon. It has two temples. The one in the north is known as Pazhaiyarai Vadathali, a separate temple, and the one in the west is known as Aararai Melthali, a separate temple. In the Pazhaiyarai Vadathali temple, the Jains once concealed the Swami's shrine. Appar Swamigal, knowing this, undertook a hunger strike, vowing not to leave the place until he could directly see the Lord. By the command of the Lord, the king identified and removed the concealments made by the Jains, revealed the sacred form of Lord Shiva, and allowed Appar Swamigal to have darshan, thus eradicating the Jain community. This is its previous history. In Pazhaiyarai Vadathali, Vimali, the daughter of Kamadhenu, performed puja. Therefore, the name of the Goddess here is Vimala Nayaki. In Pazhaiyarai Melthali, Sabali, another daughter of Kamadhenu, performed puja. Therefore, the name of the Goddess there is Sabala Nayaki. Nandhini, another daughter of Kamadhenu, performed puja at Muzhaiyur, a Maadakkoil, which is a secondary temple. This is also located nearby. It is considered very special to worship the five temples surrounding Pazhaiyarai – Nallur, Valanjuzhi, Sakthimuttram, Pattiswaram, and Avoor – on the same Dakshinayana Punniya day. In such a glorious town of Pazhaiyarai, our Amar-Niti Nayanar was born into the merchant community. Amar-Niti Nayanar was abundantly blessed with gold, pearls, other precious gems, and various fine silks (such as silk garments) and other riches from all lands. Therefore, he excelled greatly in trade. Sekkizhar Peruman here specifically mentions that Amar-Niti Nayanar possessed gold, pearls, and good gems. Gold is different from pearls and other gems. Although pearls are also gems, they are very special. Therefore, he mentions pearls separately and other gems separately. Why are pearls different from other gems? Because pearls are born as complete gems. Furthermore, other navaratnas (nine gems) only shine and reveal their natural brilliance when cut and polished. But pearls naturally possess the quality of shining. They shine from birth. That is why Sekkizhar Peruman mentions pearls separately and other good gems separately here. Amar-Niti Nayanar thus excelled greatly due to the wealth of gold, gems, silks, and other riches from all lands. Each item is found in a different land. Each is produced in a different place. But for the purpose of trade, all these items were found together at Amar-Niti Nayanar's place. What kind of person was Amar-Niti Nayanar? Sekkizhar says: "He thought of nothing but Shiva's feet, served sacred food to the devotees of the Lord of twilight hue, and knowing their needs, provided them with torn clothes and loincloths, thus reaping the benefits of his abundant wealth." Sekkizhar Peruman sings about Amar-Niti Nayanar. Lord Shiva is of twilight hue. "Andhi" means evening. In the evening, the sky appears reddish. Therefore, our Lord Shiva appears reddish like the twilight sky. This is the meaning of the phrase "Andhi Vannar." Furthermore, if "Andhi" is split as "Am Thee" (beautiful fire), it would mean Lord Shiva is of the color of fire. Amar-Niti Nayanar acted according to the wishes of the devotees of Lord Shiva, who is of twilight hue. He always contemplated only the sacred feet of Lord Shiva. He was one who understood the intentions of Shiva's devotees and helped them. Devotees are those who ask for nothing. Therefore, Amar-Niti Nayanar was one who understood their intentions himself and helped them. He lived by serving sacred food to devotees. Furthermore, he provided torn clothes (kandai) to devotees. "Kandai" is a garment like a blanket, made by layering four or five pieces of cloth one over another and tying them with threads. He provided this garment to Shiva devotees. Not only that, but he also provided "keezhkovanam," which is a kaupinam, to devotees. "Keezh" is a string that ascetics tie instead of a waist thread.

Therefore, "keezhkovanam" is a kaupinam with a string like a waist thread. "Kovanam" is a garment. It is tied with a "keezh" (string) to cover the private parts. This kaupinam, or "kovanam," is woven to be four or five fingers wide. The story of Amar-Niti Nayanar originated from the kaupinam, so Sekkizhar Peruman specially mentions the kaupinam in his verses here. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 03 — Amar-Niti Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. We are contemplating the Purana of Amar-Niti Nayanar in the Thillai Vaazh Andhanar Sarukkam of Periya Puranam. We saw that Amar-Niti Nayanar was born in Pazhaiyarai and lived by providing necessities to Shiva devotees. Thirunallur is a Shiva temple very close to Pazhaiyarai. A festival was going on in the Shiva temple at Thirunallur. At that time, to serve sacred food to Shiva devotees who came to witness the festival in that temple and to attend to their other needs, Amar-Niti Nayanar went to Thirunallur, built a mutt (monastery) there, and was providing for the Shiva devotees. Let us briefly contemplate the special features of this Thirunallur temple. The Thirunallur temple is blessed with the special features of Moorthy (deity), Thalam (place), and Theertham (sacred water), and it is also glorified by Thevaram hymns and celebrated with festivals. The Thirunallur temple is a Shiva temple glorified by Thevaram hymns, located on the southern bank of the Kaveri in the Chola country. It is currently near Papanasam in Thanjavur district. The temple in Thirunallur is one of the 70 Maada temples built by Kochengat Cholan. Maada temples are also called Perungkoils (large temples). Their structure is designed with many steps so that an elephant cannot enter the temple. Even the main entrance of the temple is not a direct entrance but is arranged to lead through another side entrance. In this structure, the temple has 25 steps on the south side, with the shrines of the Swami and Ambikai (Goddess) located at a height. The Vinayagar (Ganesha) of this place is Kasi Pillaiyar. In this Thirunallur temple, the Swami's name is Kalyana Sundarar. He is known by many sacred names such as Moolalingamurthi Andar, Kalyana Sundarar, Soundara Nayagar, Sundaranathar, Panchavarneswarar, Amrithalingar, and Thirunallur Udaiya Nayanar. The Bana (lingam part) of the Moolalingam (main lingam) here is a self-manifested, dark-colored lingam that cannot be described as being made of any particular material, appearing like a dark sugarcane. Even today, this lingam miraculously changes color six times a day during daylight hours. In the first six nazhigai (a unit of time), it is copper-colored, in the next six to twelve nazhigai, it is light red, in the next twelve to eighteen nazhigai, it is like molten gold, in the eighteen to twenty-four nazhigai, it is emerald green, and in the twenty-four to thirty nazhigai, the Moolalingam appears in a color that cannot be described. Since it appears in five colors, the Lord here is named Panchavarneswarar. He is called Andar because he blessed Amar-Niti Nayanar and Appar Peruman. He is called Kalyana Sundarar because he showed the wedding posture to Agastya. He is called Sundaranathan and Soundarya Nayagar because he is exceedingly beautiful. The sacred wedding posture shown to Agastya is still behind the Shivlingam in stucco form. Not only this, but there is another remarkable sight in this lingam. In all temples, there is only one Bana in the middle of the Avudaiyar (base of the lingam). But here, to the right of the main Bana, there is another small Bana worshipped by Agastya. This is a unique miracle. The Ambikai here is known by various names such as Sri Giri Sundari, Kalyana Sundari, Malai Azhagi, and Thirumalai Sokki. The Ambikai here is of a massive form and exceedingly beautiful, gracing from a separate temple to the northeast of the Swami, facing south. The Sthala Purana praises this Ambikai thus: "We shall ever praise Thirumalai Sokki, who resides in Nallur, who is the primeval five-headed one, who is grace itself, who is the ocean of bliss, from whom all the celebrated Bindu Mohini and Mahakali arise, and who bestows all powers, forms, and instruments upon the pure three-eyed Lord." The form of Nataraja Peruman here is huge and exceedingly beautiful. Not only that, but the form of Chathura Thandava Murthy, who dances with eight arms outstretched, is also depicted here with great subtlety and beauty. In the outer circumambulation of the temple, on the south side, facing north towards Ambikai Giri Sundari, sits Ashtabhuja Mahakali, who is very benevolent and grants all wishes to those who pray to her. In this temple, at the top of the western gopuram entrance, the deity in the form of a Balipeetam (sacrificial altar) is Ganapathi Murthy. This form of his is worshipped in this temple and in Kasi. The special Ganapathi Puja, which takes place once a year at night, is a very rare ritual. On the day of that puja, people from this village and neighboring villages offer the milk their cows yield directly to glorify the puja. The following Theerthams (sacred waters) are found in this temple. First, the Sapthasagara Theertham. This Theertham is located in front of the temple with 12 steps on all four sides. This sacred tank was originally created by Brahma. Therefore, it was known as Brahma Theertham. At a later time, on a Maasi Magam day, Kunti Devi, the mother of the Pandavas, desired to bathe in the ocean. She prayed to the Lord in this temple, and the Lord made all seven oceans appear in this tank. From that day onwards, it was named Sapthasagara Theertham. It is believed that even those suffering from leprosy will be cured if they bathe in all 12 ghats of this Sapthasagara Theertham and circumambulate the temple 12 times for one mandalam (48 days). The Sthala Purana of this village states that Brahma consecrated this sacred tank by placing the Rig Veda in the southeast, the Yajur Veda in the south, the Sama Veda in the west, the Atharva Veda in the north, and the Sapthakoti Mahamantras and 18 Puranas in the center. This sacred tank, with all its glories, is revered as equal to the Mahamaham tank in Kumbakonam. People gather here on Maasi Magam day, saying, "Magam was born in Nallur, Mahamaham was born in Kumbakonam," and bathe in it. The Lord of this temple, Kalyana Sundara Peruman, grants Theertham on Vaikasi Visakam, Karthigai Sundays, Margazhi Thiruvathirai, Maasi Magam, and other days. Other Theerthams include Agni Theertham, Nagakanni Theertham, Dharma Theertham, Deva Theertham, Brahma Kundam, Airavatha Theertham, Chandra Theertham, Surya Theertham, and Kaveri Theertham. The Sthala Viruksham (sacred tree) of this temple is the Vilva tree. Since this place was originally filled with Vilva trees, it was also known as Vilvaranya Kshetram. The Purana of this temple is very special. The Purana of this temple is described in Sanskrit Puranas such as Brahmanda Purana, Garuda Purana, Bhavishyottara Purana, Shiva Rahasya, and Upamanyu Bhakta Vilasam. From these, many parts were taken and compiled into a Sanskrit Purana called Sundaragiri Mahatmyam by the Sanskrit scholar Paingaanadu Ganapathi Sastrigal. This Sthala Purana narrates the history of how Sundaragiri came here. Once, there was a contest of strength between Vayu Deva and Adisesha. According to this, Vayu had to uproot the peak of Mount Meru with his great speed. Adisesha had to prevent it from being uprooted. As Vayu showed great speed, Adisesha spread his hood to block it, and Vayu's action did not succeed. Everyone suffered due to the speed of the wind. So, all the Devas asked Adisesha to retract his hood. Due to the force of the great wind that had been held back, two peaks of Meru broke off. Among them, the peak named Sundaragiri fell in Thirunallur, and the peak named Manikooda Giri fell in Avoor, which is in the southeast. At the top of Sundaragiri, which fell here, there was a Vilva tree.

Under this Vilva tree, the Lord manifested as a self-born lingam and has been blessing everyone. Once, during a competition between Brahma Devan and Thirumal, Brahma Devan lied that he had seen Lord Shiva's crown. To atone for that sin, Brahma Devan established a pond in front of this Sundaragiri in his own name, bathed in it, worshipped the Lord, and received blessings. Therefore, that Theertham was named Brahma Theertham and later Sapthasagara Theertham. Thirunallur is the place where Lord Shiva showed his wedding posture to Agastya. Once, all living beings gathered to witness Lord Shiva's marriage. Due to the immense weight, the northern part of the world sank, and the southern part rose. Lord Shiva commanded Agastya, the Tamil sage, to go to the south and balance it. Agastya then submitted that if he went to the south, he would miss the opportunity to witness the Lord's marriage. To this, Lord Shiva promised to show him his wedding scene. Accordingly, in this Thirunallur temple, Lord Shiva showed his wedding posture to Agastya. Delighted by this, Agastya placed another Bana to the right of the Moolalingam, Sundaralingam, and worshipped it, receiving blessings. The sculpture depicting the wedding posture he saw can still be seen behind the Moolalingam in the sanctum. Now, the greatness of the Agni Theertham here. A Brahmin boy who lived in Madurai during the time of Malayathvaja Pandiyan once starved his guru's cow. Because of this, the owner of the cow was reborn as a dog in his next life and wandered around. One day, he came to this Thirunallur temple. It was the auspicious night of Shivaratri. At that time, a Brahmin boy bathed in the Agni Theertham in this temple and shook the water from his hair, and those drops fell on the dog. Because of this, the dog's form disappeared. The Brahmin, who was in the form of a dog due to a curse and sin, regained his true form and received blessings by worshipping Sundaranatha Peruman here. Sumathi, the daughter of Adisesha, the king of serpents, desired a suitable husband. She would come to Thirunallur daily through a hole, bathe in a Theertham, worship the Lord, and return to her Naga Loka. Once, when the prince of the Chola king came here to have darshan of the Lord, he saw Sumathi and desired her. Later, by the grace of the Lord, they were married. Thirumal (Vishnu) performed penance in this temple, seeking the form of Narasimha to kill Hiranyakashipu. Lord Shiva, the Lord of this temple, granted Thirumal that form. After Hiranyakashipu was slain, the Lord commanded Thirumal to reside in the Narasimha form, facing west, at the top of his sanctum vimana. We can still see that form in the vimana today. Once, Kunti Devi, the mother of the Pandavas, visited this temple and met Narada Muni. It was the auspicious day of Maasi Magam. When Narada said that bathing in the ocean on Maasi Magam day was a great merit, Kunti Devi prayed to the Lord, asking how she could bathe in the ocean while being in Thirunallur. Just as the Lord had made the seven oceans appear in the Potramarai Kulam (Golden Lotus Pond) for Malayathvaja Pandiyan's queen Kanjanamaalai in Madurai, here, for Kunti Devi, he made the seven oceans of salt, sugarcane, toddy, ghee, curd, milk, and water appear in the Brahma Theertham. Kunti Devi bathed and received blessings. Thus, since the seven oceans came into this tank, it was named Sapthasagara Theertham. Once, Airavatham, Indra's white elephant, which was cursed by Indra, came to this temple, worshipped, and was freed from the curse. This is also part of the history of this temple. When Musukunda Chakravarthi brought Thyagaraja Peruman from Indra in Thiruvarur, he kept Thyagaraja Peruman in this Thirunallur temple for three days, worshipped him according to the Lord's command, and only then took him to the Thiruvarur temple. This is another specialty of the Thirunallur temple. Not only that, but the hunter king Sangukannan worshipped here and was blessed with a child. Many other Devas, Siddhas, and Munis also attained Siddhi (spiritual perfection) in this place. In our country, it is customary to place the Sadari (sacred feet) on the head of devotees who visit Perumal (Vishnu) temples. In Shiva temples, it is not customary to place Sadari in this manner. However, in Thirunallur, there is still a custom of placing a crown with Lord Shiva's sacred feet imprinted on it on the heads of those who come for darshan. The reason for this is that Thirunavukkarasu Swamigal, after leaving Thiruvathigai and visiting temples like Pennagadam, Thillai, Sirkazhi, and Thiruvidaimarudur, worshipped Pazhaiyarai and came to Thirushakthi Muttram. He prayed to the Lord to remove the fear of Yama (death) and place His sacred feet on his head. To this, Lord Shiva commanded, "Come to Nallur." Navukkarasu Peruman came to Thirunallur, prostrated before the Lord, and when he rose, Lord Shiva's sacred feet were placed on his head. That is why, for the devotees who come here for darshan, there is a custom in this temple to place the Sadari, which has the Lord's sacred feet imprinted on it, on their heads. This is a unique custom not found in any other Shiva temple. Gnanasambandha Peruman has also sung about the Lord of this temple. This is the place of liberation for Amar-Niti Nayanar. Sundarar also worshipped in this temple. In this temple, daily pujas are performed in the morning, noon, evening, and at midnight. Maasi Magam holds a special place in Thirunallur. The Maasi Magam Brahmotsavam used to be celebrated with a grand chariot procession. Similarly, the Vaikasi Visakam 10-day festival and, at its conclusion, the Ezhur (Sapthasthanam) festival, like in Thiruvaiyaru, used to be celebrated. Now, the Vaikasi Visakam festival is a one-day festival, the Aani Poora Amar-Niti Nayanar Mukthi festival, the Margazhi 10-day Thiruvempavai festival, and the Maasi Magam one-day festival are celebrated, during which Sri Somasakanda Murthy, the sole Lord of this place, graces the occasion. These are the special features of the Thirunallur temple. Amar-Niti Nayanar reaches this Thirunallur temple. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 04 — Amar-Niti Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. We are contemplating the Purana of Amar-Niti Nayanar in the second chapter of Periya Puranam, the Thillai Vaazh Andhanar Sarukkam. Thirunallur, a sacred place in the Chola country, is very close to Amar-Niti Nayanar's sacred place, Pazhaiyarai. Here, Sekkizhar Swamigal refers to Lord Shiva of Thirunallur as "Mukkan Nakkar Mudhalvan" (the primordial Lord, the three-eyed Naked One). "Mukkan" means that the Lord has three sacred eyes. The three sacred eyes of the Lord are the Moon, the Sun, and Agni (fire). Now, Sekkizhar Swamigal here calls Lord Shiva "Nakkar." "Nakkar" refers to the sacred form of being naked, without even a loincloth. Lord Shiva having three eyes and the form of Nakkar signifies the primary nature of the Lord. How so? The three eyes represent the Lord's complete luminous form. The form of Nakkar signifies that the Lord takes everything else as his garment, and during the Mahasamhara (great dissolution), everything else, except the Lord, merges into Him. Therefore, the sacred form of the Lord without clothes is the true sacred form where everything else merges into Him, and only the Lord remains. This name "Nakkar" signifies that. The Vedas themselves are called the loincloth or kaupinam of the Lord. But in this Nakkar form, there is not even a loincloth. That is, since the loincloth is symbolized by the Vedas here, this Nakkar sacred form signifies the Nadhantha Swarupam, which shines within the Vedas and transcends them. Thus, Lord Shiva, the primordial one who shines as the three-eyed Nakkar, is the Lord who lovingly resides in Thirunallur. The Thiruviruttham states, "Kailasam in the north and Nallur in the south are His abodes," illustrating the greatness of Nallur. That is, its meaning is that the Lord resides in Kailasam in the north and in Nallur, which is equal to it, in the south. A festival is taking place here in Thirunallur. Amar-Niti Nayanar considered it his greatest benefit to build a mutt and serve sacred food to the devotees of Shiva who came to this festival. For this very purpose, Amar-Niti Nayanar eagerly participates in the festival. The festival held in the Shiva temples of the Lord is a festival that leads to the liberation (mukthi) of living beings and assures people of their well-being. Amar-Niti Nayanar built a mutt to serve sacred food to the devotees who came to this festival and has been serving them. Just as the Lord's temple is called Thirukkoil, a mutt should also be called Thirumadam. This is because the place where the Lord resides is Thirukkoil, and the place where His devotees reside is Thirumadam. Amar-Niti Nayanar, who lived in Pazhaiyarai, left Pazhaiyarai and came to Thirunallur with his relatives to serve food to the devotees who came to the festival in nearby Thirunallur. "Sutrattar" means those who are around. It refers to one's wife, children, and others who are around them. Amar-Niti Nayanar was a very wealthy person. He excelled greatly in trade. Therefore, he had the responsibility to support all his relatives. Amar-Niti Nayanar not only supported his relatives but also intended to involve them in the service of the Lord and His devotees. His relatives also had the same intention.

That is why Amar-Niti Nayanar came to Thirunallur with his relatives. Amar-Niti Nayanar was living a good family life. Therefore, it is clear from this that he supported his relatives, which is one of the five duties performed by those who lead a householder's life, known as Grihasthashrama. Thiruvalluvar, in his Thirukkural, mentions the five duties: ancestors, deities, guests, relatives, and oneself. Pazhaiyarai was the Nayanar's birthplace and the place where he conducted his business. However, leaving all that, the Nayanar settled in Thirunallur for the service of the Lord and the service of feeding devotees. This shows us the intensity of his love for Shiva and Shiva's devotees. He established a mutt in Thirunallur and has been serving food to devotees. He does not entrust these tasks to others but personally oversees them. According to the rule that Shiva Dharmas should be performed directly and not left to others, Amar-Niti Nayanar has been serving food in the mutt he built. Just as the grace of the Lord is needed to worship Him, similarly, the grace of the Lord is needed to worship His devotees. Therefore, Amar-Niti Nayanar, realizing the grace of Lord Shiva who granted him the blessing of building such a mutt and serving food to the Lord's devotees, melted in devotion and joyfully performed this unique sacred service. While this was happening, the Lord residing in Thirunallur, to bestow grace upon Amar-Niti Nayanar, who was filled with abundant love, by first demonstrating the greatness of His loincloth, assumed the form of a Brahmachari from the Brahmin community and arrived. The Lord residing in Thirunallur is a great benefactor. What does "Perunthagai" mean? It means possessing the greatest of all great qualities. What is the Lord's great beneficence? No matter how many mistakes we, as living beings, make, He does not reject us but tolerates all our mistakes and continues to show compassion, doesn't He? This quality is called Perunthagaimai (great beneficence). The Lord's quality of great beneficence. Thiruvachagam says, "O You who tolerate my smallness, which does hateful things, by Your greatness, it is the duty of the great to tolerate all mistakes." Thirugnanasambandar's Thevaram says, "The great benefactor was with the ignorant woman." Such a great benefactor, the Lord, resides in Thirunallur. The Lord residing in Thirunallur decides to demonstrate the greatness of His loincloth and to bless Amar-Niti Nayanar's abundant love. To demonstrate the greatness of His loincloth, Lord Shiva, the Lord, descends from His state as a householder (Grihastha) and comes here as a Brahmachari. Not only to demonstrate the greatness of His loincloth, but also to bestow grace upon the abundant love of Amar-Niti Nayanar, the devoted servant, He comes. The love Amar-Niti Nayanar had for the Lord was abundant love. Abundant love means love that is so complete that there is no room for more. Thus, Amar-Niti Nayanar possessed abundant love. The Lord bestows upon that abundant love an enduring grace. Enduring grace means grace that is boundless in measure, time, experience, and joy, an unending, blissful grace.

This is the meaning. Thus, to show the greatness of his loincloth and to bless Amar-Niti Nayanar, the Lord comes in the form of a Brahmachari of the Brahmin community. Among the four varnas (social classes) – Brahmin, Kshatriya, Vaishya, and Sudra – the first three varnas have stages of life (ashramas) such as Brahmacharya, which are prescribed by the scriptures. The Lord decides to appear here as a Brahmachari belonging to the first varna, the Brahmin varna. We will continue to contemplate how he appears. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 05 — Amar-Niti Nayanar

We are contemplating the Purana of Amar-Niti Nayanar in the Thillai Vaazh Andhanar Sarukkam of Periya Puranam. To bestow grace upon Amar-Niti Nayanar, the Lord comes in the form of a Brahmin Brahmachari. Sekkizhar Swamigal describes this as "assuming the form of a Brahmachari from the Vedic lineage." The meaning of Brahmachari is one who lives in the state of Brahma upasana (contemplation of Brahman). That is, one who wanders and remains steadfast in the state of Brahma upasana is the meaning of Brahmachari. One who recites the Vedas is a Brahmachari. What are the actions of a Brahmachari? In the three varnas – Brahmin, Kshatriya, and Vaishya – a boy, after his Upanayanam (sacred thread ceremony), without chewing betel leaves, applying sandalwood paste, or wearing flowers, wraps only one garment around his waist, goes to a Brahmin agraharam (settlement) in another village (not his own), spreads darbha grass on the ground at night and sleeps on it, eats food obtained through alms, and studies the Vedas under a suitable guru until the time of his marriage. This is the characteristic and conduct of a Brahmachari. Elders say that this Brahmacharya ashrama can extend up to 48 years of age. Lord Shiva appears in such an unparalleled form of a Brahmin Brahmachari. How does he appear? Lord Shiva always has matted locks (Jadamudi). But now, appearing as a Brahmachari, Lord Shiva conceals his matted locks and instead has the topknot (sigai) appropriate for a Brahmachari. He has a sacred topknot. "Sigai" means a topknot, which is appropriate for the Brahmachari state. He shines with the three-line sacred ash mark (Thiruneetru Kaappu) on his forehead, which is a special identifying mark for Saivism. The Vedas prescribe this three-line sacred ash mark, known as Tripundram, as appropriate for all ashramas. Furthermore, Lord Shiva, who came in the form of a Brahmachari, wears a deer skin with the sacred thread (Poonool). Wearing a deer skin with the sacred thread is one of the identifying marks appropriate for the Brahmachari state. It is customary to wear a deer skin about one inch long with the sacred thread. Moreover, he wears a Pavithram (sacred ring) on the Anamika (ring finger) of his right hand. The Pavithram in his hand is newly tied and made of fresh, undried darbha grass. Therefore, it shines like an emerald due to the green color of the fresh darbha grass. The Pavithram has a knot. Therefore, Sekkizhar describes here that the knot of the Pavithram shines like an emerald gem, and the long part of the Pavithram shines like a ray of light, like the brilliance of that emerald gem. Lord Shiva, who came in the form of a Brahmachari, has tied a waist thread made by twisting Munji (darbha grass) around his waist. A waist thread made of Munji darbha grass is appropriate for Brahmacharya. In Vedic tradition, there are seven types of darbha grass. Munji darbha grass is one of them. It is prescribed that this should be worn as a waist thread, which is appropriate for the Brahmachari state. Brahmacharis are forbidden to wear waist threads made of thread, silk, etc. The loincloth worn by this Brahmachari is the Vedas themselves. For Lord Shiva, everything is the Vedas. The Vedas are his sandals, the Vedas are his abode, the Vedas are his loincloth. Therefore, Lord Shiva, in the guise of a Brahmachari, also wears the Vedas as his loincloth here. Thus, Lord Shiva, concealing his matted locks and having a topknot, adorned with Tripundrams, wearing a deer skin with a white sacred thread, with a Pavithram in his hand, and with the Vedas as his loincloth around his waist, which is tied with a twisted cord of Munji grass, walks with his sacred feet touching the ground. What is the greatness of these sacred feet of the Lord? The sacred feet of the Lord are the feet that never leave the hearts of His devotees. What kind of devotees are those who hold those sacred feet in their hearts? Deception results from the actions of the five senses. The strong karma (evil deed) that results from deception is the karma mala. Those who have cut off this strong karma mala that results from deception are the devotees of the Lord. Not only that, but they are devotees who have cut off the ego of "I" and "mine" that arises from the anava mala (ego impurity) and whose minds are devoted to worshipping only the sacred feet of the Lord. The sacred feet of the Lord are the lotus feet that never leave the hearts of such devotees. Lord Shiva, with those sacred feet touching the ground, comes here in the form of a Brahmin Brahmachari. This sacred form taken by the Lord is a form that melts the hearts of all who behold it. The Brahmachari who came with this sacred form carries a Palasa staff in his hand, which is appropriate for his Brahmachari state. It is also appropriate for Brahmacharis to carry a staff in their hand. The Brahmachari has tied two loincloths to that staff. It is customary to carry two loincloths on the staff to change into after bathing. Not only that, but two loincloths would be needed for this historical event – one to give to Amar-Niti Nayanar and one to place on the pan of the balance. This is the inner meaning. Not only that, but this Brahmachari also carries a sacred ash bag. Carrying a sacred ash bag, like deer skin, is also an identifying mark appropriate for the Shiva form of a Brahmachari. It is a rule that Brahmacharis and those in the other three ashramas should always wear sacred ash. Elders say that wearing vibhuti (sacred ash) and reciting mantras yields the full benefit of mantra chanting. A Brahmachari is one who always recites the Vedas. Therefore, it is a rule that sacred ash should always shine on his forehead. That is why Sekkizhar Swamigal states in the previous verse that he came wearing Tripundram in this Brahmachari form. And in this verse, he states that this Brahmachari carries a sacred ash bag. Not only that, but the Brahmachari also carries darbha grass. It is a rule that Brahmacharis should sleep on darbha grass mats. For that purpose, and for rituals like Pavithram, Brahmacharis should always keep darbha grass. Thus, Sekkizhar Swamigal describes the form of the Lord who came in the guise of a Brahmachari from head to foot (Kesadhipadam). Amar-Niti Nayanar sees Lord Shiva, who came in the guise of a Brahmin Brahmachari, in front of his mutt. The Lord is one who cannot be seen by our outer eyes, our physical eyes. However, Amar-Niti Nayanar sees the Lord here. The Lord is one who has no form of His own. But here, He has come with a form. Therefore, Amar-Niti Nayanar sees the Lord.

Upon seeing this Brahmin Brahmachari, a Shiva devotee, Amar-Niti Nayanar's heart blossoms. His face blossoms even more than his heart. It was the eyes on his face that saw this Brahmachari form, this Brahmin Brahmachari Shiva devotee form. But do these eyes see on their own? They cannot see on their own. The eyes inform the inner instruments (antahkaranas) – mind, intellect, consciousness, and ego. These antahkaranas inform the soul's consciousness. That soul's consciousness informs the soul. The Lord, being present, shows this form to the soul. Because of this, the soul knows. Because the soul knew this, the mind rejoices. Thus, the mind first rejoices and blossoms, and then, because of this, the face also blossoms. The face shines even more brightly than the mind that caused it to blossom. "The beauty of the inner self is seen on the face" is a proverb. Thus, Amar-Niti Nayanar, seeing the Brahmin Brahmachari Shiva devotee, rejoiced in his heart, his face blossomed, and he came forward to worship him. He worshipped Lord Shiva, who was in the form of the Brahmachari, with his three instruments: mind, body, and speech. Amar-Niti Nayanar says to this Brahmachari, "That you, whom I have never seen in this mutt before, have come here today for me, a humble servant, to see openly, is it not the result of my penance?" He says this with melting devotion. The inner meaning of this is that the Lord Himself comes to this mutt as a Brahmachari. Therefore, the first meaning is that no one has ever seen such a person come to this mutt before. The second meaning is that this mutt is not worthy of Your unparalleled greatness. Until now, the Lord would come mingled with devotees, but He would not come alone to be seen openly like this. That He has come alone like this is the next meaning. Sivakavimani explains several such inner meanings. It is a rule that only due to good penance performed previously does one receive the blessing of such great persons visiting. The Brahmachari says to Amar-Niti Nayanar, "I have heard of your service of feeding the devotees of the Lord and providing them with torn clothes, lower garments, and new, high-quality loincloths. Hearing of this, I have come to see you." Lord Shiva, in the form of the Brahmachari, says this, concealing his third eye. Lord Shiva Himself praises Amar-Niti Nayanar with His own mouth. How so? Amar-Niti Nayanar provides all necessities to devotees, whether one devotee comes, many devotees come, or countless devotees come. He feeds devotees sacred food. Just as a mother feeds her child, he understands the time, quantity, quality, and desire.

He provides torn clothes, lower garments, and loincloths, says Lord Shiva with His sacred mouth, praising Amar-Niti Nayanar here. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 06 — Amar-Niti Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. We are contemplating the Purana of Amar-Niti Nayanar in the Thillai Vaazh Andhanar Sarukkam of Periya Puranam. A festival is taking place in the Shiva temple in Thirunallur. There, our Amar-Niti Nayanar, with his relatives and family, has established a mutt and is providing for the needs of Shiva devotees. Lord Shiva comes there in the guise of a Brahmachari Brahmin. That Brahmachari Brahmin tells Amar-Niti Nayanar, "I have heard of your sacred service of providing for the needs of devotees here, and so I have come." Seeing the Brahmachari Brahmin in his divine form, which was an unprecedented sight, Amar-Niti Nayanar greeted him respectfully and said, "In this mutt, food is prepared purely by Brahmins for the revered Brahmins and ascetics to partake. Therefore, you too should partake of sacred food here." Generally, it is customary to serve food to devotees in a mutt. However, the custom of preparing food purely by Brahmin cooks for Brahmin devotees and ascetics has existed from that day until now in many mutts. This is because food must be prepared purely for those who recite the Vedas and are great in their penance, in a manner befitting their penance and conduct. Therefore, in this mutt too, there is a custom of preparing food separately by Brahmins for such great Vedic scholars and separately for other devotees. Thus, Amar-Niti Nayanar requests and appeals to the Brahmin Brahmachari to partake of food. He also states that it is customary for sages who perform great penance like him to partake of food here. Thus, Amar-Niti Nayanar requests the Brahmin Brahmachari to partake of sacred food. Sivakavimani subtly points out here that while the Devas kept the nectar for themselves and offered poison to Lord Shiva, Amar-Niti Nayanar, unlike them, prepares to offer nectar to Lord Shiva. The Brahmin Brahmachari agrees to his request. If he is to partake of food as Amar-Niti Nayanar requested, he must bathe before eating. Therefore, the Brahmin Brahmachari states that he will go and bathe in the Ponni river, which is the Kaveri river. Here, Sekkizhar Peruman refers to the Kaveri as "Anangu Neer Ponni" (the divine water Ponni). "Anangu" means a divine woman. Sekkizhar states here that the Kaveri river is not just a woman, but a divine woman, a river. Just as a mother nourishes a child with milk, so too, rivers are called women in our Bharatadesha (India) because they nourish this world with their abundant waters, sustaining the living beings of this world. That is why our ancestors gave feminine names like Bhavani, Godavari, Kanni, Kumari, etc., to rivers. This Kaveri river is also a woman. And she is not an ordinary woman, but an "Anangu," a divine woman. This is because, as we saw earlier, the Kaveri river, with its abundant waters, worships Lord Shiva by circumambulating countless Shiva temples on both its banks, the northern and southern. Therefore, this Kaveri woman is called "Anangu Neer Ponni" by Sekkizhar. Thus, the Brahmin Brahmachari says that he will bathe in the Kaveri and return. And if it rains while he is returning, he asks Amar-Niti Nayanar to keep this dry loincloth with him and return it to him later for use. Saying this, he unties one of the two loincloths tied to the staff in his hand and gives it to Amar-Niti Nayanar. Amar-Niti Nayanar attained liberation in the month of Aani, under the Poora star. Aani month is the peak summer season (Mudhuvenil Kalam). Therefore, there is no chance of rain during this peak summer season. The period that Tamils designated as Mudhuvenil Kalam means summer. So, there is no chance of rain in the month of Aani. If it were to rain, a dry loincloth would be needed to wear. Therefore, he unties one of the two loincloths on the staff in his hand and gives it to Amar-Niti Nayanar. Whatever the Lord gives is divine grace. Therefore, the loincloth given by the Lord here is also grace. This loincloth given by the Lord is grace. So, what does grace do? It removes the ego (Jivabodha) associated with impurities (mala) and bestows pure love (nirmala anbu). That is why Sekkizhar Swamigal refers to this loincloth as "Ven Gunangol Kovanam" (the loincloth that embodies white qualities). Furthermore, the Lord's loincloth is the Vedas themselves. The Vedas originated from the Lord's supreme knowledge (Vaalarivu), which is true wisdom. Therefore, the Vedas, which originated from the Lord's true wisdom, are the Lord's loincloth. Therefore, Sekkizhar Swamigal also refers to it as "Ven Gunangol Kovanam." What does the Lord, that Brahmin Brahmachari, say to Amar-Niti Nayanar after giving him such a loincloth? He tells Amar-Niti Nayanar, "You yourself know the greatness of this loincloth. I don't need to explain it. Therefore, keep it safely and protect it until I return after bathing, and then return it to me." This loincloth's greatness is immeasurable. The Brahmin Brahmachari tells Amar-Niti Nayanar, "You yourself will realize its immense greatness." What is the immense greatness of this loincloth? This loincloth is one that surpasses everything else, stands supreme, and is greatly renowned. Furthermore, as we saw, this loincloth is the Vedas. The Vedas, in the form of sound (Nada), stand supreme, and all other principles originate from them. Therefore, this loincloth is a supreme loincloth. Manikkavachagar Peruman also describes the Vedas as the Lord's loincloth in his Thiruvachagam. One section in Thiruvachagam is Thiruchazhal. In this, Manikkavachagar Peruman sings in such a way that the first two lines are a question, and the next two lines are the answer. That is, Manikkavachagar arranges it such that two women sing this song. One woman asks a question in the first two lines, and the other woman answers in the next two lines; this is how the Thiruchazhal hymn is structured. In it, in one song, one friend first asks a question. How so? She asks, "Ennappan Embirān Ellārkkum Thān Īsan Thunnambai Kōvanamā Koḷḷumathu Ennēḍi?" (Why does my Father, my Lord, who is the master of all, wear a torn cloth as a loincloth, O friend?). This is the second verse in the Thiruchazhal hymn. The meaning of this question is that she asks her friend, "O friend, why does that Lord, who is my father and my master, and the master of all, wear a torn cloth as a loincloth? Why does he wear a loincloth?"

In response, the second woman replies, "Mannu Kalai Thunnu Porul Marai Nāṅkē Vān Saraḍā Thannaiyē Kōvaṇamā Cāttiṇan Kāṇ Cāḷalō" (He wears the four Vedas, which are enduring arts and profound truths, as a celestial thread, and He Himself as a loincloth, O friend!). That is, its meaning is that the Lord wears a loincloth. How so? The Lord wears the Vedas themselves as a loincloth. The friend replies that He wears the Vedas as a loincloth only to bestow grace upon us, living beings. Thus, in the second verse of this Thiruchazhal, Manikkavachagar Peruman describes the greatness of the Lord's loincloth, which is the Vedas, as sung by two friends. Therefore, I do not need to tell you the greatness of this loincloth, which is so revered. This is because you already know the greatness of this loincloth and have been providing it to devotees. Therefore, keep it safely, says the Brahmin Brahmachari to Amar-Niti Nayanar. "You keep this loincloth; I will take it back later," says the Brahmin Brahmachari. In reality, giving and taking are the Lord's actions. Thiruvachagam says, "You keep and take everything." Thus, giving and taking are the Lord's actions. Here, taking is dissolution. Keeping is creation. The Brahmin Brahmachari says, "Take and keep it," doesn't he? Sivakavimani says this refers to the disappearance of the loincloth and then the Nayanar's merging into the Lord. In truth, you are buying and keeping, meaning the Brahmin Brahmachari implicitly tells Amar-Niti Nayanar, "Amar-Niti Nayanar, you are performing an action with the soul's consciousness that 'I am buying, I am keeping.' But later, the act of returning that loincloth is not an action that you will perform. You will not perform it. You will lose your Jiva Bodha (individual consciousness) and offer yourself to be weighed against the loincloth." This is the inner meaning of the Brahmin Brahmachari's words. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.
