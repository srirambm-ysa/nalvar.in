Chapter 01 — Tiruneelanakkar Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam by Sekkizhar Swamigal, Part 516, Thirunindra Sarukkam, Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar Puranam. In Periyapuranam, the Puranam of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar follows that of Appudhi Adigal Nayanar. First, let us briefly look at the history of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar and then contemplate it in detail. The Chozha Nadu is a fertile land watered by the Kaveri. In that land, the holy place called Thiruchaththamangai was a town where Brahmins flourished. In that holy place, Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar shone as a devotee of Lord Shiva, endowed with excellent character and knowledge of the inner meaning of the Vedas. Realizing that the inner meaning of the Vedas is to worship and revere Lord Shiva and His devotees, Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar lived his life performing both with unceasing desire. In that holy place of Thiruchaththamangai, there is a temple called Ayavanthi. Thiruchaththamangai is the name of the holy place. Ayavanthi is the name of the temple. The deity enshrined there is Ayavanthi Nadhar. The Mother is Malarkkanni Ammai. Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar worshipped this deity daily according to the Sivagama rules. Additionally, he performed all Shiva services, such as feeding the devotees. One day, during this period, he went to the temple with his wife, carrying all the items required for worshipping Lord Ayavanthi Shiva. His wife held all the items needed for the puja, and he performed the elaborate Shiva puja according to the prescribed rituals. After the puja was completed, his devotion, still overflowing with love, led him to circumambulate the deity, prostrate, praise, and then chant the holy five-lettered mantra, Sri Panchaksharam, according to the rules. At this time, a spider fell onto the sacred form of the deity in the temple. Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar's wife saw this and was greatly distressed. Seeing the spider fall on the deity's sacred form, she became very anxious, just as a mother would protect her child if a spider fell on it by removing the spider. Similarly, she immediately blew on the spider with her mouth to remove it from the deity's sacred form. Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar, the Brahmin, witnessed this. He did not consider that his wife was acting out of love but became angry, thinking, "How dare she blow on the deity's sacred form, allowing our saliva to touch it?" He asked, "Oh foolish one, what have you done?" His wife replied, "A spider fell on the deity's sacred form, and I blew it away." Hearing this, the Nayanar said, "This is improper in Shiva puja. This is an unpardonable mistake. Instead of removing the spider by other means, you blew on it, letting your saliva touch it. This is a great offense to Shiva. Therefore, from today, I renounce you. From now on, there is no connection between you and me." By then, evening had arrived. So, the Nayanar returned to his home. The Nayanar had cast off his wife. Therefore, his wife, not knowing what to do, stayed in the temple. Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar went to his house and slept. At that time, Ayavanthi Nadhar appeared in his dream and showed him His sacred form. In that sacred form, the place where Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar's wife had blown was perfectly fine, but all the other places where Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar's wife had not blown had blisters caused by the spider. Lord Shiva showed this to Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar in his dream. Upon seeing this, Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar, the devotee, perceived it as a reality, and with fear, he clasped his hands, woke up, worshipped the Lord, danced, sang, and praised Him. He stood and wept, contemplating the Lord's compassion. At dawn, he went to the temple, worshipped the Lord, and brought his wife back home with him. Even after this, he continued to perform the Lord's pujas according to the rules with great joy. He lived his life, gladly giving whatever the devotees desired. During that time, hearing the world praise the greatness of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, he desired to worship Thirugnanasambandha Peruman. At that time, Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, having worshipped many holy places, arrived at Thiruchaththamangai. Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, accompanied by Thiruneelakanda Yazhpanar, who always traveled with him, and his wife Mathanga Chulamaniyar, and many other devotees, arrived at Thiruchaththamangai. Hearing this, Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar immediately rejoiced greatly and arranged garlands, flags, and ornamental arches to welcome Thirugnanasambandha Peruman and the other devotees. He went with his relatives to meet Thirugnanasambandha Peruman and his retinue, invited them all to his home, and served them sacred food. By then, evening had arrived. Therefore, Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar made arrangements for Thirugnanasambandha Peruman to stay at his house that night. After partaking of the sacred food, Thirugnanasambandha Peruman called Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar and requested him to provide a place for Thiruneelakanda Yazhpanar and his wife Mathanga Chulamani Ammaiyar to stay that night. Hearing this, Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar rejoiced and provided them a place to stay on the platform in the middle of his house where he daily nurtured the sacrificial fire. As soon as a place was given, the sacred sacrificial fire of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar, which always burned without extinguishing, grew, swirled clockwise, and shone brightly. Thiruneelakanda Yazhpanar and his wife rested there. Then, Thirugnanasambandha Peruman praised Lord Ayavanthi and sang a patikam. In that patikam, he glorified Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar. Having sung thus, Thirugnanasambandha Peruman then departed to visit many other holy places. Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar, out of love, followed him without separating. Later, he returned to Thiruchaththamangai. He continued to live, always remembering Thirugnanasambandha Peruman. After some days, he would go to the holy places where Thirugnanasambandha Peruman was present, stay with him, and then return to his own Thiruchaththamangai. Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar continued this practice. After many days passed in this manner, due to the penance performed earlier, Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar attended the sacred marriage of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman. At that time, in the great fire of the Lord that arose, in that light, Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar attained the sacred feet of the Lord. This is a brief summary of the history of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar. Now, let us systematically contemplate the holy Puranam of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar in detail, following the order of Thogai Nool, Vagai Nool, and Viri Nool. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 02 — Tiruneelanakkar Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam by Sekkizhar Swamigal, Part 517, Thirunindra Sarukkam, Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar Puranam. In Periyapuranam, the Puranam of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar follows the Puranam of Appudhi Adigal Nayanar. Therefore, in the concluding verse of Appudhi Adigal Nayanar's Puranam, Sekkizhar Peruman introduces the Puranam of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar. He says, "I have begun to narrate the sacred service performed by Neelanakkar, the master of the four Vedas, who was born in Saaththamangai, surrounded by fields with lotus-filled ponds like a forest." Thus, in the concluding verse of Appudhi Adigal Nayanar's Puranam, he praises the sacred feet of Appudhi Adigal Nayanar and, with its support, states, "I am about to narrate the sacred service performed by Thiruneelanakkar, the master of the four Vedas, who was born in Thiruchaththamangai, surrounded by fields with ponds filled with lotus flowers like a forest." Thus, he provides an introduction. The town where Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar was born and lived was Thiruchaththamangai. This is located in the Chozha Nadu. Therefore, when referring to it, he sings that Saaththamangai is a town surrounded by ponds filled with lotus flowers like a forest. Therefore, continuing this, Sekkizhar Peruman, who begins the Puranam of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar, starts it by saying, "In the Kaveri country, surrounded by fields of ripened red paddy like a forest, where carp leap over blossoming lotus buds." Similarly, Sekkizhar Peruman, who introduces the Puranam by saying, "I have begun to narrate the sacred service of Neelanakkar, the master of the four Vedas," sings. By saying "Naanmarai" (four Vedas), he indicates the lineage of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar here. He states here that Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar performed the sacred service of the four Vedas. The greatness of the sacred service performed by Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar is that he was blessed with the great fortune of performing all the Vedic sacrificial rituals at the marriage of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman. Furthermore, the historical account of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar is that he attained the sacred feet of Shiva along with Thirugnanasambandha Peruman there. Therefore, to encompass all these, Sekkizhar Peruman introduces the Puranam by saying, "I have begun to narrate the sacred service of Neelanakkar, the master of the four Vedas." Now, let us systematically begin to contemplate the Puranam of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar. In Periyapuranam, the Puranam of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar is the 26th Puranam. This section describes the historical account and character of the Nayanar named Thiruneelanakkar. In chronological order, the Puranam of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar is the sixth Puranam in the Thirunindra Sarukkam. Sekkizhar Peruman sings the Puranam of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar in 38 verses. Now, let us contemplate the Nayanar's Puranam as Thogai Nool, Vagai Nool, and Viri Nool. First, in the Thogai Nool, Thiruthondath Thogai, Sundaramurthi Swamigal sings about Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar as follows: "I am a devotee of Neelanakkar of Saaththamangai, surrounded by bright waters." Thus, Sundaramurthi Swamigal sings about Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar in the fourth verse of Thiruthondath Thogai. "I am a devotee of Neelanakkar of Saaththamangai, surrounded by bright waters." The meaning of this is, "I am a devotee of Thiruneelanakkar, who was born in the holy place called Thiruchaththamangai, surrounded by resounding waters." Thus sings Sundaramurthi Swamigal. In this phrase, he indicates that the holy place of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar is rich in water resources. Therefore, this gives a clue that it is the Chozha Nadu where the Nayanar was born. He sings about that place as "Saaththamangai, surrounded by bright waters." "Punal" means flood. "Oli punal" means that the Kaveri, flowing through canals, spreads and flows gently through the fields, making a soft sound and enriching the land. Therefore, he sings "Saaththamangai, surrounded by bright waters." Additionally, "Oli punal" refers to the nature of water. This also refers to the nature of love. Sundaramurthi Swamigal sings "Oli punal suzh" to indicate that this history is about the nature of love, where Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar, out of immense love, sat and chanted the five letters before the Lord Ayavanthi, and at that time, his wife, out of love, blew and spat to remove the spider that fell on the Lord's sacred form. This is the first reference to the nature of love. Furthermore, the phrase "Oli punal suzh" also refers to the fact that Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar, in the central part of his house, where he always nurtured the fire, allowed Thiruneelakanda Yazhpanar and his wife to rest, and as a result, the ever-burning sacred sacrificial fire rose swirling clockwise, imbued with such a nature of love. Moreover, the special act of feeding sacred food to Thirugnanasambandha Peruman and the thousands of devotees who accompanied him is also mentioned here. This is because water resources are essential for feeding devotees in this manner. Therefore, the phrase "Oli punal suzh Saaththamangai" is used here to also indicate this. Furthermore, Sundaramurthi Swamigal sings "Oli" (light) instead of "Osai" (sound). We should note this here. "Osai" is a mere sound. "Oli" means a sound with meaning. "Osai" is sound. "Oli" is nada (primordial sound). "Osai" is mere sound, empty sound. "Oli" is sound combined with meaning. Thus, that which conveys a meaning is called "Oli" or "Ezhuthu" (letter). That which serves as its cause is called "Osai." Therefore, Sundaramurthi Swamigal has sung "Oli punal suzh" meaningfully, instead of merely saying "Osai" for the sound of the flowing Kaveri water. He sings "Oli punal suzh Saaththamangai." Through this, he refers to the place called Thiruchaththamangai where the Nayanar was born. Sundaramurthi Swamigal sings "Neelanakkarukku adiyen" (I am a devotee of Neelanakkar). Neelanakkar is the sacred name of the Nayanar. Thus, in the Thogai Nool, Thiruthondath Thogai, Sundaramurthi Swamigal sings about Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar. Now, in the Vagai Nool, Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi, Nambiyandar Nambi composes the following verse. The 30th verse in Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi is about Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar. "When a spider fell on the sacred body of Shiva, whose shoulders are adorned with sacred ash, his wife, fearing, blew and spat to remove it. When he renounced her, the Lord, the Pinjñakan, showed him in a dream that all other parts were affected, but the part she blew was fine. He was Neelanakkan, the Brahmin of Saaththai, who shone with righteousness." This is the sacred verse. Its meaning is as follows: When a spider fell on the Linga form of Lord Shiva, whose arms are adorned with sacred ash, Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar's wife, seeing it and fearing, blew and spat to remove the spider with her saliva. Seeing this, Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar, considering it improper, renounced his wife. In Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar's dream, the Lord showed him that the part where Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar's wife had blown was perfectly fine, but all other parts had blisters caused by the spider's bite. Thus, Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar was blessed to be shown by the Lord Himself. He was Thiruneelanakkar, the Brahmin, who was born in Thiruchaththamangai, which shone with Vedic righteousness. This is the meaning of the verse sung by Nambiyandar Nambi in the Vagai Nool, Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi. The village, name, and historical details of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar were all conveyed by Sundaramurthi Swamigal in the Thogai Nool. In addition to that, Nambiyandar Nambi has systematically elaborated on the historical part of the Puranam in the Vagai Nool. Thus, the historical account of the Nayanar, compiled in the Thogai Nool and elaborated in the Vagai Nool, has now been extensively sung by Sekkizhar Peruman in the Puranam of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar in Periyapuranam. Now, let us extensively contemplate the Puranam of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar through Periyapuranam. As we have already seen, Sekkizhar Peruman sings the Puranam of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar very extensively in 38 verses. When singing the Puranam of a Nayanar, Sekkizhar Peruman would describe the holy place where the Nayanar was born, the country, the prosperity of that country, the prosperity of the town, the Nayanar's lineage, his clan, the sacred service performed by the Nayanar, and the explanation of the Nayanar's sacred name, and thus compose the Puranam. Similarly, Sekkizhar Peruman has sung the Puranam of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar. Let us continue to contemplate it. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 03 — Tiruneelanakkar Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam by Sekkizhar Swamigal, Part 518, Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar Puranam. Sekkizhar Swamigal begins the Puranam of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar by describing the country, town, water resources, etc., where the Nayanar was born. Sekkizhar Peruman sets the first verse as: "In the Kaveri country, surrounded by fields of ripened red paddy like a forest, where carp leap over blossoming lotus buds, the town of Saaththamangai, praised by the whole world for its auspiciousness, is the foremost place of auspicious Brahmins." Sekkizhar Peruman begins the Puranam of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar auspiciously with the auspicious word "pootha pangayam" (blossoming lotus). Similarly, he concludes this first verse by placing "vaaytha mangalam" (auspiciousness that has come to pass) in the fourth line of this verse. The history of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar concludes with the sacred marriage of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, which is a complete auspiciousness, a Shiva auspiciousness. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman begins this first verse with the auspiciousness of "pootha pangayam" and concludes it with "vaaytha mangalam." "Pootha pangayam" means a blossomed lotus. Chozha Nadu is a land rich in water, where the Kaveri river flows. Those who travel in Chozha Nadu know that the entire Chozha Nadu is filled with water bodies, rivers, and canals. In these, lotus flowers bloom very densely. It looks like a forest of lotuses. In such lotuses, carp fish leap and play on their buds. Those carp fish play on the lotus buds as if they are fighting with each other. There, red paddy crops are well-grown and ripened, appearing like a forest of red paddy. Usually, it is not customary to describe red paddy fields as a forest. "Kaadu" (forest) means an area where things grow and flourish on their own without human effort. But here, it is used for paddy fields. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman, singing about the prosperity of this town, Thiruchaththamangai, sings "Kaaytha sennellin kaadu suzh Kaveri Naadu" to indicate that in the fields of Thiruchaththamangai, paddy crops grow and ripen naturally, without artificial human effort, and lie in abundance. The reason for the growth and cultivation of these paddy crops is the Kaveri. Therefore, because the Kaveri river flows and enriches the land, Chozha Nadu is named Kaveri Nadu. In such a prosperous Kaveri Nadu, there is a holy place called Saaththamangai. In this holy place called Thiruchaththamangai, many auspicious Brahmins live. Therefore, it shines as a prominent holy place. It is a suitable place for all auspiciousness to reside, and thus, it is filled with auspiciousness. The auspiciousness mentioned here is the nature of Shiva. Similarly, in this place, many Brahmins who are blessed with auspiciousness live. Therefore, this Thiruchaththamangai shines as a highly renowned place in this world. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman begins to sing. Sekkizhar Peruman has composed the verses in the Puranam of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar in the poetic form of Kalinilaiththurai with five feet. The essence of the Vedas is the holy five-lettered mantra (Thiruvaindhezhuthu). Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar is the Nayanar who attained the sacred feet by loving and listening to that holy five-lettered mantra. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman has composed his entire Puranam, which is glorified by the holy five-lettered mantra, in Kalinilaiththurai with five feet. In this holy place of Thiruchaththamangai, a fragrance emanates from the foreheads of the women. There are two reasons for this fragrance on the women's foreheads. Firstly, their foreheads are naturally fragrant. Secondly, the fragrance emanates from the tilak applied to their foreheads. Such women go to bathe in the water bodies. Along with them, swans also bathe there. Women would bathe by going a little deeper into the water. But swans cannot go into such deep areas. Therefore, these swans bathe in the shallow part of the steps at the water's edge in those ponds. It was customary in those days for women to go out of the town to water bodies, rivers, ponds, and tanks to bathe. In Thiruvachagam, in the Thiruvempavai section, we know that it is extensively sung about how women would wake up early in the morning and go to the ponds outside the village to take holy baths. We saw that swans also bathe with such women. This is because these swans are very intimately accustomed to the women of that village. Similarly, these women are, in a way, of the same species as the swans. Therefore, these swans also bathe with those women who are of their own kind. Moreover, the swans also bathe with the women there, due to their certainty that these women would not harm them. "Annam mun thurai aaduva" (swans play in the front steps) implies that they bathe, and it can also be interpreted as they dance. Sekkizhar Peruman sings that the parrot-like birds in that village recite the Sama Veda. Although he specifically mentions reciting the Sama Veda, it can be taken generally as all Vedas, meaning those parrot-like birds sing all the Vedas. It can also be taken as they sing the Sama Veda. This is because the Sama Veda is special for its practice with all seven notes. Therefore, the parrots, which are parrot-like birds, sing the Sama Veda and other Vedas. This is because many Brahmins live in that village. They are those who daily and always study the Vedas. Therefore, because the elderly Brahmins living in that village always study the Vedas, even the birds in that village, having heard it repeatedly, sing and recite the Vedas. There are places arranged for teaching the Vedas to Brahmin boys. In the gardens in the urban areas of that city, there are places for teaching the Vedas to Brahmin boys. There, the boys recite the Vedas. What do these birds do? These birds, having heard the Vedas recited by the Brahmins for a long time, have become proficient in them and recite them again. Therefore, if the boys who are studying the Vedas make a mistake while reciting, these birds correct that mistake. That is, these birds, due to their long practice, recite the Vedas very accurately. Hearing them recite, the boys correct any mistakes they make while reciting. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman metaphorically sings that these birds correct the Vedic boys. We are reminded here of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman's patikam in the Meharaga Kurinji pan, in the Thiruveezhimizhalai holy place, where he sings, "Hearing the sound of learned scholars who study daily, the parrots in the fragrant groves explain the meaning of the Vedas in Mizhalai." Also, we are reminded here of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman's Thevaram verses, "Since the thoughtful parrots correct the mistakes of the great Brahmins who recite the Vedas, it is Kaazhi by the sea." These are all events that take place outside the town of Thiruchaththamangai, in the outer part of the town. The boys should learn arts. They should learn their ancestral art. And they should learn the art of wisdom. All these references are present here. In this second verse, Sekkizhar Peruman has arranged it so that swans dance and parrot-like birds sing. Sekkizhar Peruman, who began the first verse auspiciously with an auspicious path, continues to place auspicious actions like dancing and singing in the second verse, and this beauty amazes us. Furthermore, we can also see here the ethical references of women bathing and Brahmins and boys of Brahmin lineage studying the Vedas, which are their respective disciplines. The swan, excellent in its gait, performs a dance here. The parrots, excellent in speech, sing even more. Therefore, it becomes clear to us here that the things present here are very special and excellent compared to other ordinary towns. This illustrates the glory of that city to us. It becomes clear that even among other creatures that are not fully intelligent, the creatures living in Thiruchaththamangai are very special. Therefore, by saying this, Sekkizhar Peruman implies that if even less intelligent creatures are so special, how much more special would the intelligent people there be, and he explains this in the subsequent verses. Sekkizhar Peruman composes the next verse as: "The twice-born, who nurture the sacred ash, the well-researched true substance, within that protection, nurture the three fires. Their chaste wives nurture that fire, which thrives in good righteousness, as the fourth." "Aayndha meipporul neeru" is the first phrase. The true substance, thoroughly researched and understood, is the sacred ash (Vibhuti). The righteousness prescribed in the Vedas is to wear sacred ash. The Bhasma Jabala Upanishad in the Vedas describes the greatness of sacred ash. The Siva Agamas also emphasize the importance of sacred ash with great pride. Saivism states that sacred ash and Rudraksha are indispensable for true devotees of Shiva. Furthermore, we have seen the greatness of sacred ash in the Puranam of Meipporul Nayanar and Enadhinatha Nayanar. Thirugnanasambandha Peruman has sung, "The Vedas contain sacred ash." Therefore, believing that sacred ash is the true substance, the unwavering devotion to Shiva, which holds sacred ash as its protection, shines in Thiruchaththamangai. Within that protection, the Brahmins of Thiruchaththamangai nurture the sacrificial fire. They nurture three fires, three Agnis. Nurturing fire, performing Agni Homam, refers to Sivagni here. It refers to the worship of Shiva. In the rules of Shiva puja, it is stated that to protect the place of worship from obstacles, a magical wall and moat should be created as protection. This is part of the Shiva puja rules. These are all Agamic rules. Therefore, with the protection of sacred ash, the Brahmins nurture the three Agnis. Sacred ash also has the name Rakshai in Sanskrit. There is a custom in Tamil to call it Thiruneetrukkappu (protection of sacred ash). Therefore, among all protections, the protection of sacred ash is the best. That is why, among all mantras, the protection of sacred ash is the best.

The Tamil sacred saying "Aavathu Neeru" (Sacred ash is what is) explains the nature of sacred ash as a protection. They do not desire many other protections. "He applied sacred ash on his forehead and fulfilled it" is from the 43rd verse of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman Puranam. Thus, those who do not consider sacred ash as the true substance and do not nurture sacrifices with it as a protection, their sacrifices will not help them attain the primordial Lord Shiva. It is also implied here that it will be in vain. Thus, the Brahmins of Thiruchaththamangai nurture three fires. These are Dakshinagni, Garhapatyam, and Ahavaniyam. Among these, Ahavaniyam is nurtured for the Devas in a square pit in the northeast of the sacrificial hall. Dakshinagni is nurtured in a crescent-shaped pit in the south. They say this is for the ancestors. The next, Garhapatyam, is nurtured in a circular pit next to Ahavaniyam and is maintained by householders, hence its name Garhapatyam. Thus, these Brahmins nurture these three fires. These three fires can also be interpreted as Vaidika Agni, Saiva Agni, and Vaindhava Agni. Those who nurture these three Agnis are the twice-born, the Brahmins. This twice-born status for Brahmins comes from the Upanayana ceremony, which bestows Brahma Upadesam, and thus they attain a second birth, which is why they are called twice-born, or Dvijas. The righteousness performed by the Brahmins here is good righteousness. It is good Shiva Dharma that helps one cross the ocean of birth. Therefore, they hold onto these three Agnis without fail and protect them without deviation. It is the duty of the Brahmins' wives to protect these fires from extinguishing. It is a rule prescribed for Brahmins that at the time of their marriage, the fire nurtured for that purpose in the sacrificial hall should be continuously maintained in their household daily until their last day, and they should be cremated by that fire. Here, in the history of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar, we are going to see that the fire nurtured by Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar's wife was taken to Thirunallur Perumanam and merged with the great fire of our Lord Shiva there. Therefore, the wives of the Vedic scholars in Thiruchaththamangai protect the three Agnis nurtured by the Vedic scholars without fail. Along with these three fires, the housewives in the homes of the Brahmins in that village also nurture the fire of chastity as the fourth. The historical reference to Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar's wife, which ensured the completeness of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar's Shiva puja, is also present here. Why is chastity, which these women possess, considered the fourth fire along with the three Agnis? This is because chastity is said to be a fire. In the story of Nala, there is an account of a hunter being burned by Damayanti's chastity. Similarly, there are other accounts. Therefore, generally speaking, the women in Thiruchaththamangai are excellent in chastity. It is natural for them to possess it, which is the general meaning. There is another special meaning to this. It also refers to the high religious chastity of those who stand in the path taught by Lord Shiva. In this Puranam, we are reminded here of the histories of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar's wife, Karaikkal Ammaiyar, and Mangaiyarkkarasi Ammaiyar, and how they excelled in both worldly and religious chastity.

Sekkizhar Peruman here composes this idea in such a way that it applies both to the domestic state, where it is one of the duties of housewives to protect and increase the wealth earned by their husbands without diminishing it, and to the transcendent Shiva state. Here, he refers to women with the word "madavar." Sekkizhar Peruman uses the word "madavar" (ignorant women) to indicate that even though these women do all these things, they appear as if they know nothing, as if they are ignorant. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman gives such a special meaning to the quality of ignorance that women should possess. Let us continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 04 — Tiruneelanakkar Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam by Sekkizhar Swamigal, Part 519, Thirunindra Sarukkam, Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar Puranam. Sekkizhar Peruman, having sung the glories of the holy place Thiruchaththamangai, continues to sing the glories of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar. With such singular love, Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar takes all the necessary items for puja, such as sandalwood, flowers, etc., without any deficiency, and comes to Lord Shiva's temple with his wife from his house. When the items prescribed for puja are not available, to prevent the puja from being abandoned due to the lack of items, one can compensate for the unavailable items with love, using what is available. Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam by Sekkizhar Swamigal, Part 522. Thirunindra Sarukkam, Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar Puranam. Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar's wife removes the spider that fell on the Shiva Linga form by blowing on it with her mouth. Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar, seeing this, is greatly distressed. Sekkizhar Peruman composes the verse at this point: "Seeing his wife's agitated action, the great ascetic Brahmin, who wore the sacred thread, covered his eyes and asked, 'What is this foolish thing you have done?' She replied, 'A venomous spider fell, and I blew and spat to remove it.'" The action of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar's wife was prompted by her distress at the thought that blisters might form on the Lord's sacred form. As she did this, Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar, unable to bear it, covered his eyes. He asked, "What is this foolish thing?" That is, he asked his wife, "Oh foolish one, why did you do this improper act? What was the reason?" For our saliva to fall on the Lord is improper; it is an impropriety not even to be seen with the eyes; it is a great offense to Shiva. Therefore, if one is forced to witness an offense to Shiva, one should cover one's eyes with one's hands. Therefore, Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar, unable to see his wife's action, unable to bear witnessing that offense to Shiva, covered his eyes with his hands. He says this is an improper act. Knowledge is knowing what is proper and what is improper. Therefore, having done such an improper act, he asks, "Oh ignorant one, what was the reason for doing this improper act?" To that, Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar's wife replies that a venomous spider fell on the Lord's sacred form, and she blew on it to remove it. Sekkizhar Peruman composes the next verse: "Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar did not consider the love in his wife's action. He considered it an impropriety in the purity of his worship, and with that thought, he renounced her and cast her away." Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar did not consider the love in his wife's action. He did not think it was an act done out of love but considered it an impropriety. Without considering the love that was the cause of this action, he only considered the action his wife performed. Therefore, he thought it was an evil act that would corrupt the purity of the puja he had performed. He thought that an impropriety had occurred. "Anuchitham" means "na uchitham" (not proper), meaning an improper act. It implies an act that brings harm. It is used here in the same sense as "adharma." The prefix "a" here gives a contrary meaning, implying evil, contrary to what is proper. In Sanskrit grammar, when the letter "na" precedes a word, it can have six different meanings. One of these meanings is a contrary meaning. Therefore, "anuchitham" here, when separated as "na uchitham," gives a contrary meaning. Thus, it means not proper, and also that which causes harm. Because this impropriety occurred, Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar renounces his wife. He severs his connection with his wife. He removes his wife from his life. Sekkizhar Peruman composes the next verse: "You tried to remove the spider that fell on the pure Lord with the long, lightning-like matted hair by blowing on it, letting your saliva touch it, instead of removing it by another means. Therefore, I renounce you from now on," he said. Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar tells his wife, "You could have removed the spider that fell on the Lord's sacred form in another way. Instead, you did it by blowing on it, letting your saliva touch it. Therefore, I renounce you now." As we have already seen, the Nayanar, without considering his wife's love, scolds and renounces her based only on the external event. Here, when referring to the Lord, the verse says, "the spider that fell on the pure Lord with the long, lightning-like matted hair." Sekkizhar Peruman has referred to the Lord as "Vimalar" here. "Vimalar" means one who is free from impurities. It means one who is naturally free from all bonds. Therefore, the Lord is Vimalar. Therefore, Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar says that the spider could have been removed from the Lord's sacred form without agitation, by another means. Furthermore, the Lord is Vimalar. Therefore, the implied meaning here is that by blowing and spitting with love, allowing saliva to touch, He would not be affected by that action as an impropriety. Sekkizhar Peruman's subtle composition, where Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar himself forgot this and spoke thus, is worthy of our reverence. Now comes the next verse. Sekkizhar Peruman then describes what happens next: "At that time, the sun set behind the mountain. His wife, by his command, moved aside. The devotee of the Lord with the matted hair, having completed the flawless puja as fully as needed, entered his protected home." As these events unfolded, the sun set behind the mountain at that time. That is, evening arrived. "The sun set behind the mountain" means the sun went to the setting mountain. The world revolves, and as it revolves, the western sea moves towards the sun. It appears to us as if the sun sets in the western sea. Therefore, saying that the sun sets in the western sea is a figure of speech. This happens naturally here. Thirugnanasambandha Peruman is a Jivanmukta (liberated soul) who transcends the waking state. Therefore, he is not subject to the states of Jagrat (waking), Svapna (dream), and Sushupti (deep sleep) like other beings. That is why Sekkizhar Peruman sings here, "He stayed in the house," and "the house remained." He does not explicitly state that Thirugnanasambandha Peruman rested but refers to it indirectly. That is, Sekkizhar Peruman first sings that Thirugnanasambandha Peruman stayed in Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar's house. In this verse, he composes the verse describing the actions performed by Thirugnanasambandha Peruman in the early morning. Therefore, by omitting to describe Thirugnanasambandha Peruman's resting in between, Sekkizhar Peruman illustrates his Jivanmukta state to us. Thus, Thirugnanasambandha Peruman arrives. Then, he praises the Lord with the moon-adorned hair, who is enshrined in the Ayavanthi temple in Thiruchaththamangai, and sings a Thiruppathikam. In that Thiruppathikam, he glorifies Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar and sings that pure Tamil Thiruppathikam. This Thiruppathikam is for the holy place of Thiruchaththamangai. It is the 58th Thiruppathikam in the third Thirumurai of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman's Thevaram. In the second verse of this Thiruppathikam, Thirugnanasambandha Peruman has glorified Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar. Thirugnanasambandha Peruman sings, "The Lord who resides in Ayavanthi, whom Adigal Nakkan praises in Saaththamangai, a town of fragrant groves, with sacred ash on his chest and a sacred thread on one side, is united with the graceful woman." In this verse, Thirugnanasambandha Peruman glorifies Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar, stating that Lord Shiva resides in the Ayavanthi temple, where Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar worships Him in Thiruchaththamangai. This patikam is a Thiruppathikam sung by Thirugnanasambandha Peruman as he took leave from the Lord in Thiruchaththamangai. The Thiruppathikam sung by Thirugnanasambandha Peruman when he first arrived at Thiruchaththamangai is not available to us due to our lack of merit. Thus, Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, who praised the sacred feet of his Lord with a new garland of flowers in the form of this Thiruppathikam, bestowed his great friendship upon Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar. When it is said that Thirugnanasambandha Peruman sings a Thiruppathikam like a new garland of flowers, it means that he expresses the ancient Vedas in ever-new pure Tamil. Furthermore, only freshly bloomed flowers are specially suitable for puja. Therefore, since Thirugnanasambandha Peruman praises the Lord with different patikams in each holy place he visits, these patikams become a new garland of flowers. Thus, by singing, he bestows his exalted friendship upon Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar. This friendship is a bond of love and intimacy that resides in both their hearts as one. After this, it is also explained here the divine indication of the special privilege of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar to perform the sacrificial rituals at Thirugnanasambandha Peruman's marriage and to accompany Thirugnanasambandha Peruman. Thus, Thirugnanasambandha Peruman now departs to visit other holy places. Let us continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 11 — Tiruneelanakkar Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam by Sekkizhar Swamigal, Part 526, Thirunindra Sarukkam, Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar Puranam. Thirugnanasambandha Peruman departs from Thiruchaththamangai to visit holy places and compose patikams. Sekkizhar Peruman composes the verse: "Having praised the Lord's feet with the four new flower-like patikams, and having bestowed abundant friendship upon Neelanakkar, the Brahmin who spoke the ancient Vedas in ever-new pure Tamil, he departed to the holy places that welcomed him." Thirugnanasambandha Peruman is praised here as "the Brahmin who spoke the ancient Vedas in ever-new pure Tamil." "Poosuran" means a celestial being living on this earth. "Poo" means earth, and "surar" means celestials. "Poosuran" means a celestial being living on this earthly world. This is a special epithet generally given to Brahmins. However, here, even though Thirugnanasambandha Peruman was born into a Brahmin lineage, the sacred name "Poosuran" is not merely a family name but also a name indicating his divine nature, his Shiva nature. All that he composed were ever-new pure Tamil ancient Vedic patikams. Here, Sekkizhar Peruman refers to Tamil as "ever-new pure Tamil." Sekkizhar Peruman's enthusiasm for the divine Tamil language is evident in many places, and this is one of the best examples. All the explanations we saw in the 970th verse, where he refers to divine Tamil as "Gnanam alandha menmai" (greatness that measured knowledge), are now worth recalling here. Tamil is eternal. Tamil is always new. Tamil bestows purity. These are all special characteristics of Tamil. Tamil is a language that has been in use throughout all ages. It is a language that is always new, without any deficiencies such as destruction, decay, increase, or decrease. Furthermore, it is a novel language because it always offers newness without the characteristic of being discarded as ancient. It is also interpreted as a divine language with unchanging youth. The Tamil language also has the glory of being "Kanni Tamil" (virgin Tamil). Thayumanavar Swamigal praises Goddess Parvathavarthini, enshrined in Rameswaram, saying, "O Mother who bore countless universes, yet the Vedas call you a virgin." This praise also applies to the Tamil language. Furthermore, Tamil is a language that bestows the imperishable bliss of liberation, which is called "semmai" (purity). The sacred sayings of the Samayachariyars and the philosophical texts of the Santhanachariyars were composed in Tamil, which is why the Tamil language possesses such glory. Generally, these characteristics belong to the Tamil language, but these epithets are especially applicable to the sacred sayings of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman. Therefore, Thirugnanasambandha Peruman expresses the meanings of the ancient Vedas in new pure Tamil patikams. The Vedas are the sacred words spoken by Lord Shiva Himself. Here, the patikams composed by Pillaiyar are the divine words spoken by Lord Shiva through Thirugnanasambandha Peruman. Therefore, even though the Vedas and Thirugnanasambandha Peruman's Thevaram hymns are different in language, they are one in meaning and in the benefit they bestow. This truth is clearly evident to us here. Thirugnanasambandha Peruman mentions in the Thevaram of Ilambaiyangottur, "My word is His word." Therefore, the patikams composed by Thirugnanasambandha Peruman are the patikams spoken by Lord Shiva through Thirugnanasambandha Peruman. This is derived from this. Thus, as Thirugnanasambandha Peruman departed from Thiruchaththamangai to visit other holy places, Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar's love urged him to follow Thirugnanasambandha Peruman. Although that love and friendship were irresistible, Thirugnanasambandha Peruman commanded Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar to stay in Thiruchaththamangai. Therefore, Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar, unable to disobey the divine command of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, found some peace of mind and remained in Thiruchaththamangai. Thus, the devotion and immense love that Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar had for Thirugnanasambandha Peruman's sacred words are clearly evident to us here. Thus, Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar, the Vedic scholar who remained in Thiruchaththamangai, continued to perform the flawless worship of the Lord, praised by the Vedas, and the services to the devotees, even more excellently than before. Moreover, he continued to cultivate a deep sense of devotion to the beautiful sacred feet of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, the divine child of Lord Shiva. As many days passed in this manner, Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar's love to be with Thirugnanasambandha Peruman grew intense. Therefore, Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar would visit the holy places where Thirugnanasambandha Peruman was present and have his darshan from time to time. Thus, he would spend some days with Thirugnanasambandha Peruman in the holy places where he was present, and then return to Thiruchaththamangai to continue his sacred service. Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar lived in this manner. Sekkizhar Peruman composes the 37th verse as follows: "The great ascetic Brahmin, who had performed great penance, with increasing love, after many days, attended the sacred marriage of the unique Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, who appeared in Sirkazhi, and along with him, attained the feet of the Lord." Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar, the great ascetic Brahmin, participated in and rejoiced at the sacred marriage of the incomparable Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, who appeared in Sirkazhi. He performed the sacrificial rituals at Thirugnanasambandha Peruman's marriage. Thus, by the good fortune of worshipping Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, he, along with him, attained the sacred feet of Lord Shiva. Now, the 38th verse is the concluding verse in the Puranam of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar. The verse is: "Having worshipped the feet of Thiruneelanakkar, the great and foremost Brahmin who performed sacred duties in Saaththamangai, we shall now narrate the sacred service of Naminandhi Adigal, the twice-born Brahmin who, with a unified mind, understood the Lord who raised the bull banner." Sekkizhar Peruman, according to the rule he established, concludes the history of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar, which he has narrated so far, in this concluding verse, and now introduces the next history in this verse. Having worshipped the sacred feet of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar, the great and foremost Brahmin who appeared in Thiruchaththamangai and excelled in devotion, and with that support, Sekkizhar Peruman now begins to narrate the sacred service performed by Naminandhi Adigal, the twice-born Brahmin who lived with a unified mind, understanding Lord Shiva, who raised the bull banner. We too shall conclude the Puranam of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar and, with the support of his sacred feet, begin to contemplate the Puranam of Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar. Praise to the sacred feet of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman! Praise to the sacred feet of Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar! Praise to the sacred feet of Thiruneelakanda Yazhpanar! Praise to the sacred feet of Sundaramurthi Swamigal! Praise to the sacred feet of Nambiyandar Nambi! Praise to the sacred feet of Sekkizhar Peruman! Praise to the sacred feet of Sivakavimani Ayya, who wrote the commentary on Periyapuranam! Let us continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.
