Chapter 01 — Siruthonda Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Part 924, by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, Varkonda Vanamulaiyal Sarukkam, Siruthonda Nayanar Puranam. Following the Sirappuli Nayanar Puranam in the divine Sekkizhar Peruman’s Periyapuranam, the 36th Puranam is the Siruthonda Nayanar Puranam. We have been contemplating the Thirupuranams, following the path shown by the commentator, as Thogai Nool, Vagai Nool, and then Viri Nool. In that regard, Sundaramurthy Swamigal praises Siruthonda Nayanar in the sixth verse of Thiruthondathogai. He praises him saying, "Sengattangudi Meya Siruthondarkku Adiyen" (I am a servant to Siruthondar, who resides in Sengattangudi). The meaning of this is, "I am a servant to Siruthonda Nayanar, who is associated with the sacred place of Thiruchengattangudi." Sengattangudi is the sacred place of Siruthonda Nayanar. Siruthondar is a descriptive name given to the Nayanar. Siruthonda Nayanar's birth name is Paranjothiyar. Siruthondar is a descriptive name that came about because our Nayanar always considered himself very small in the presence of Shiva's devotees. Sundaramurthy Swamigal praises our Nayanar, saying, "I am a servant to such Siruthondar, who resides in Sengattangudi." Now, in the Vagai Nool, Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi, Nambiyandar Nambigal Peruman praises Siruthonda Nayanar in the 43rd verse. That verse is as follows: "Puliyin adathudai Punniyarkku in amudha thanadhu or Oliyin sadhangai kuthalai puthalvan udal thunithu Kaliyin vali keduthu ongum pugazh Siruthondan kandeer Malaiyum pozhil on Sengattangudi avar mannavane." The meaning of this verse is: "He is the king of Thiruchengattangudi, surrounded by mountains and groves, that Siruthondan, whose fame grows by cutting the body of his only son, who wore jingling anklets and spoke in lisping words, to offer as sweet ambrosia to the Pious One (Shiva) who wears a tiger skin, and who destroyed the power of Kali (the age of sin)." In this sacred story, Lord Shiva, the Pious One who wears a tiger skin, comes in the form of Uthirapathi (Bhairava). Our Nayanar cut the body of his only son, who wore jingling anklets and spoke in lisping words, to offer as sweet ambrosia to Lord Shiva who came in that form. He is Siruthonda Nayanar, whose fame grows by destroying the power of Kali, which is the birth disease caused by the strength of mala (impurity). He is the leader of the people of Thiruchengattangudi, which is surrounded by many groves. Now, the Puranam composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, the Viri Nool, is the Siruthonda Nayanar Puranam. This section describes the historical account and character of Siruthonda Nayanar. In the order of composition, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman begins to narrate the Siruthonda Nayanar Puranam as the third one in the Varkonda Vanamulaiyal Sarukkam, which is the seventh in the series. The first verse is: "Oru nattum seyal Kama ozhiya vizhi pozhi senthee varum natta thirunuthalar magizhndhu arulum pathi vayalil Karunatta kadaisiyar tham kali kattum Kaveri thirunattu valam kattum Sengattangudi agum." Thiruchengattangudi is a sacred place where Lord Shiva joyfully resides. Who is that Lord? He is Lord Shiva, "Oru nattum seyal Kama ozhiya vizhi pozhi senthee varum natta thirunuthalar." That is, Lord Shiva has a sacred forehead from which red fire emanates, causing Kama (Manmatha) to lose his form and his actions, which he performs with a form and directs towards living beings. This is the meaning. Manmatha, in ancient times, had a form and exercised his command. Later, he was burned by the fire from the Lord's third eye. After that, he continued to exercise his command without a form. When he had a form, at the same time that our Lord was explaining the essence of Gnana Pada to Sanakathi Munis on Mount Kailash, Goddess Uma, as the daughter of Himavan and the daughter of wealth, was performing penance towards the Lord. At that time, Manmatha acted to make Uma Devi fall in love with the Lord and marry her, by focusing his thoughts on her beautiful form. We see this in the Kandapuranam and other Puranams. Lord Shiva has a sacred forehead from which red fire emanates, causing Manmatha's actions and Manmatha himself to be destroyed. The reason he lost his form after having one is this divine act of our Lord. Even now, his formlessness is only to the sight of others. But to his wife Rati, he appears to have a form. We see all these histories in Kandapuranam, Kamadahanam Padalam, and other Puranams. Such a sacred place where Lord Shiva joyfully resides is Thiruchengattangudi. While there are countless divine plays and miraculous acts of grace performed by the Lord, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman has placed the burning of Manmatha here and sung that it is a place where such a Shiva resides. There is a connection, a beautiful connection, between this and the sacred story of Siruthonda Nayanar. The idea that all living beings, including humans, should enjoy their pleasures and experiences is created by the distinction between male and female forms. The worldly attachments, such as desire and attachment to children and other possessions, obtained through these pleasures, are also a continuation of these pleasures. In this sacred story, our Siruthonda Nayanar Peruman and his divine wife lived a householder's life. Living such a life, they were blessed with a very rare and excellent child. However, the devotion of these two great individuals to the divine form of the Lord was far greater than their attachment to these physical forms. Therefore, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, in his divine poetic skill, first sings of this miraculous act of grace, placing it here because of the historical connection that Lord Shiva is the one who, by his grace, ensures that Manmatha's command, which instills love in the bodies of living beings, does not affect our Siruthonda Nayanar Peruman, his wife, and their son, who lived in this sacred place as divine incarnations, or even their household maid. The fire that burned Manmatha was the fire from the Lord's third eye. Fire is also present in the Lord's smile and in his divine hand. But here, the fire that burned Manmatha came from the Lord's third eye. Therefore, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman says, "Vizhi pozhi senthee" (red fire emanating from the eye). That same third eye first burned. Later, the Lord showed grace to Manmatha, and he came back to life. That compassionate gaze of the divine eye also performed that act of grace. Thirunavukkarasu Peruman praised this third eye, saying, "Netriyil kankanda kan kondu matru ini kanbadhenne" (What else can be seen with the eye seen on the forehead?), indicating that the divine eye on the Lord's forehead is not only for destruction but also for showing grace to devotees. Accordingly, the sacred place where our Lord resides is Thiruchengattangudi, located in the Chola country, which is called Kaveri Thirunadu. Now, the second verse: "Nilaviya aththiruppathiyil nedunjadaiyar neetru adaival Ulagil valar uyirkku ellam Uyar kaval thozhil poondu malar pugazh mamaththirar tham kulam peruga vandhullar Palar pugazhum thirunamam Paranjothiyar enbar." In that established sacred place, our Nayanar, whose name is praised by many as Paranjothiyar, resides. The name Paranjothiyar is praised by people of many religions. However, this name, Paranjothi, generally and specifically belongs to the Shiva Jyothi, which is the supreme light beyond all lights, as stated in the divine saying, "Sothiyay ezhum sothiyil sothi" (Light of lights that rises as light). Our Nayanar bears such a divine name. He is associated with the sacred ash of Lord Shiva, who has a long matted hair. Because of this, our Nayanar undertakes and performs the noble duty of protecting all living beings that are born and grow in this world. The duty of protecting all living beings mentioned here refers to the medical profession. The lineage in which such a Nayanar was born is called the Mamaththirar Kulam, as mentioned by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. This Mamaththirar Kulam is a tradition of hereditary medical practitioners. The lineage of Amaththiyar is different; it is a type of Brahmin lineage. The Mamaththirar Kulam mentioned here is different. When the author Umapathi Sivachariyar categorized the true Nayanmars as 13 Brahmins and two Shiva Vediyars, he mentioned Siruthonda Nayanar Peruman as "Siruthondar, one of the Mamaththirar lineage." Therefore, this lineage, the Mamaththirar lineage, is a tradition placed between Brahmins and kings. It is their tradition to wear the sacred thread. We see this in many places. He came from a tradition excelling in the noble protective profession of medicine and learned it. In addition, our Nayanar was also skilled in the art of warfare, the profession of a commander, which comes from training in weaponry. We will see this next. Therefore, the "noble protective profession" here refers to both types of professions undertaken by the Nayanar: the medical profession and the commander's profession. Our Nayanar bears such a divine name, Paranjothiyar. Let us continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 02 — Siruthonda Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Part 925, by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, Varkonda Vanamulaiyal Sarukkam, Siruthonda Nayanar Puranam. The third verse: "Ayul vedakkalaiyum alagil vadanoorkalaiyum Thooiya padaikkalath thozhilum thurai nirambap payindravatral Payumadak kunjiramum pariyumugaikkum panbu Meya thozhil vinjaiyinum mediniyin melanaar." This is the verse that the divine Sekkizhar Peruman composed about our Paranjothiyar Siruthonda Nayanar. Our Nayanar has mastered the medical science called Ayul Vedakkalai, countless other arts and sciences found in northern texts, and the art of weaponry, which is the art of war, to their fullest extent. Ayul Vedakkalai is the science of medicine. Alagil Vadanoorkalai refers to the various other arts and sciences spoken of in Sanskrit, the northern language. This implies that Ayul Kalai in Tamil refers to the medical science found in Tamil texts. Paranjothiyar studied all these. The Ayul Vedakkalai in Tamil refers to Siddha medical texts, composed by great medical masters like Bogar. There was even a Sangam poet named Maruthuvan Damodaranar. Therefore, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman refers to this medical art as Ayurveda. Ayurveda is one of the six angas (limbs) of the Vedas. Vadanoorkalai refers to other arts and sciences related to the Nayanar's lineage. The texts for these are all in Sanskrit. Therefore, he mastered these as well and excelled. In addition, there is the pure art of weaponry. This is the training in warfare undertaken to protect the people of this world. In these days, this profession leads to great destruction of people, unacceptable harm, and sin and murder. When war comes, there is a practice of attacking innocent people and civilians in these days. But in ancient times, this art of weaponry, the art of war, was undertaken to protect the world. That is why the divine Sekkizhar Peruman refers to it as "Thooiya padaikkalath thozhil" (pure art of weaponry). Pure profession means performing that art of war in a pure and righteous manner. There were pure rules established in those days, such as not attacking those who turn their backs and flee, innocents, women, cows, Brahmins, the elderly, and the sick. Wars in those days followed these rules. That is why the divine Sekkizhar Peruman refers to that profession as the pure art of weaponry. In addition, Paranjothiyar not only learned elephant riding and horse riding as an art, but he also became a commander who could lead an army to victory against enemies. The fourth verse: "Ulla nirai kalaithuraigal ozhivinri payindru avatral Thelli vadithu arindha porul Sivan kazhalil serivu endre Kollum unarvinil munne kootru uthaitha kazharkku anbu Pallamadaiyaay endrum payindru varum panbudaiyar." This is the verse. Paranjothiyar continuously studied and mastered all the arts and sciences that filled his mind. These are arts related to the body and the world. In addition, he found fulfillment in his heart by realizing the soul and the Lord. Therefore, he continuously studied and mastered these arts and sciences, and through his understanding, the essence he distilled from all of them was that devotion to Lord Shiva's feet is the true discipline. Therefore, the ultimate truth, the essence that comes from examining and clarifying all the arts and sciences, is to have intense devotion to Lord Shiva's feet.

He understood this as the ultimate truth. With such clear understanding, his devotion to the feet that once kicked Yama (the god of death) flowed continuously and swiftly, like water flowing in a channel, a characteristic he always possessed. "Pallamadaiyaay" refers to the traditional practice of agriculture. The opening through which water flows from the sluice gates to the fields is called "madai." A field where water flows from a "pallamadai" is a fertile land that yields abundant crops, never lacking water. Similarly, Paranjothi Nayanar's devotion flowed continuously in his heart, words, and deeds, like water flowing from above to below. The Thiruvachakam states, "Pallam thazhuru punalil keezh melaga pathaithu urugum avar" (He melts and trembles like water flowing down a slope). Now, the fifth verse: "Eesan adiyarkku endrum iyalbana pani seidhe Aasil pugazh mannavan pal anukkaray avarkku aga Poosal munaikkaliru ukaithu por vendru porum arasar Thesangal pala kondu ther vendhan pal sirandhar." Paranjothi Nayanar always performs natural services to Shiva's devotees. This is due to the love that naturally arises in his heart, and he does whatever is necessary for Shiva's devotees. Similarly, he is a close companion to the king of flawless fame, out of love. He is an intimate companion. A king having flawless fame means ruling justly, protecting his subjects, and not harming other kings and people through unjust wars. The fame that arises from these actions is flawless fame for a king. Such a king rules excellently. Paranjothiyar is an intimate companion to such a king. This king is identified by historians as Narasimhavarman, the son of the Pallava king Mahendravarman. In the next verse, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman will describe the Vathapi war in the northern region. Therefore, the Vathapi war, also known as the Badami war, is known from inscriptions to have occurred between 640 and 643 CE. This period is also the time of this king, Narasimhavarman Pallava. Through this, the period of Paranjothiyar, Siruthonda Nayanar, is also determined. Since the history of Thirugnanasambandar Peruman's visit to Thiruchengattangudi is associated with this period, historians and researchers conclude that the period of Thirugnanasambandar Peruman is also this period. Therefore, this information is the primary and most significant aid in researching the period of the Nayanmars. They also state that the period of Mahendravarman Pallava is between 625 and 650 CE. This research was first conducted by Professor P. Sundaram Pillai. Paranjothiyar was so close to such a king, having an intimate relationship, that the king consulted him. Now, the sixth verse: "Mannavarkku thandu poy vadapulathu vathapi Thol nagaram thoolaga thulai nedungai varai ukaithu Panmaniyum nithikkuvaiyum pagattinnum parithogaiyum Innana ennilla kavarndhe ikal arasan mun konarndhar." Paranjothiyar, as an intimate companion and commander to the king, led an army and went to the northern region, where he conquered the ancient city of Vathapi, also known as Badami, by deploying an elephant army, reducing it to dust. He led the army against the enemies for the king. That city is referred to by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman as "vadapulathu vathavi thol nagaram" (the ancient city of Vathavi in the northern region). This is a city known as Badami. Before independence, Badami was located in the Belgaum district of the Bombay Presidency. Currently, this city is located in the state of Karnataka. It was the capital of the Chalukya king Pulikesi. We learn from inscriptions that Paranjothiyar, as the commander, destroyed this city of Badami in the war between the Chalukya king and the Pallava king Narasimhavarman. The remnants of the strong, ruined fort still exist there today. Not only did he conquer that king and Badami, but Paranjothiyar also brought many jewels, piles of wealth, herds of elephants, groups of horses, and countless other things and presented them before his king. These were customary practices during wars in those days. It was a tradition for victors in war to take all the possessions of their enemies. If the enemy king submitted during a war, they would receive many treasures as tribute and return. If the enemy king did not submit and fought, then conquering them and seizing their symbols was an act performed by victorious commanders. Here, since the enemy king, the Chalukya king, fought without submitting, Paranjothiyar destroyed his capital fort and brought such goods as symbols of war. There is another note here. It is said that Paranjothiyar also brought a statue of Lord Ganesha from that city as a memorial and installed that Lord Ganesha in the Ganapatheeswaram temple in his city of Thiruchengattangudi, where he is widely known and worshipped as Vathapi Ganapathi. The seventh verse: "Kathir mudi mannanum ivartham kalitru urimai aanmaiyinai Adhisayithu pugazhndhu uraippa arindha amaichargal uraippar Mathi anindhar thiruthondu vaiththa vali udaimaiyinal Ethir ivarkku ivvulagil illai ena eduthu uraithar." The king with the radiant crown marvels at and praises Paranjothiyar's prowess in leading the elephant army. The king is amazed to see that the impregnable fort of Badami was reduced to dust and that many symbols, including elephants, were captured. Therefore, he praises Paranjothiyar's valor and skill. It was customary for kings of that era to praise and honor their army commanders. The king, knowing the true reason for this victory, praised the army commander. The wise ministers who were with the king then told him the reason. They told the king the reason for Paranjothiyar's victory. They explained that because he possessed the strength of performing service to Lord Shiva, there was no one in this world who could fight against him. That was the reason. Those who possess the firm strength of service to Shiva are capable of conquering anyone in this world. It is difficult for anyone to defeat them. We have seen how the king who was allied with the wicked Jains was defeated by the strength of divine service. We have seen in Thirukoottachirappu that those who serve the feet of the universal cause are worthy of ruling the entire world. Therefore, they explained that such valor came from the firm love for Lord Shiva. Let us continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 03 — Siruthonda Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Part 926, by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. That Varkonda Vanamulaiyal Sarukkam, Siruthonda Nayanar Puranam. The eighth verse: "Thamberuman thiruthondar enakketta thar vendhan Umbar piran adiyarai unarade kettolindhen Vembu kodum por munaiyil vittirundhen ena veruvutru Emperuman idhu porukka vendum ena irainjanan." When the king heard that Paranjothiyar was a devotee of his Lord, a devotee who served Lord Shiva and his devotees, he trembled, saying, "I, without realizing that he was a devotee of the Lord, sent him to the fierce battlefield to fight!" The ministers had only told the king that the reason for Paranjothiyar's victory was his devotion to Shiva. But upon hearing this, the king, who had great love for Lord Shiva, abandoned the thought of victory and trembled, realizing his transgression, "I sent him to the battlefield without knowing for so long that he was a great devotee!" This shows us the depth of the king's great love for Lord Shiva and his devotees. Thus, the king, fearing, said to Paranjothiyar, "My Lord, please forgive this mistake," and bowed down. Forgetting his position as a commander serving under him, the king, upon learning that Paranjothiyar possessed the strength of divine service, addressed him as "Emperuman" (My Lord) and bowed, revealing the depth of his mature devotion to Shiva. His plea to Paranjothiyar to forgive his mistake was also for this reason. The ninth verse: "Irainjathalum mun irainji en urimaithozhilkku adutha Thiram puriven adarkku enno theengu enna aangavarkku Niraindha nithikkuvaigaludan needu viruthigal aliththe Aram puri sengol arasan anjali seidhu uraikkindran." This is the ninth verse, and following it, the tenth verse: "Ummudaiya nilaimaiyinai ariyamaal kondu uyndheer Emmodaiya manakkaruthukku inithay isaindhum umadhu Meimmai puri seyal vilanga vendiyavare sarithu Semmai neri thiruthondu seyyum ena vidai koduthan." These two verses follow. We saw that the king bowed down to Paranjothiyar. When he bowed down, Paranjothiyar replied to the king, "I only performed my rightful duty of warfare, taking on the leadership of the army and serving as an intimate companion to the king. I was not commanded by you to do any inappropriate or wrong act. Therefore, no harm will come to you," Paranjothiyar told the king. At that very moment, the king gave Paranjothiyar countless piles of wealth and long-lasting sarvamanyams (tax-free grants of land), and the righteous king, who ruled according to dharma, stood with folded hands and bowed to Paranjothiyar. This age is a young age for Paranjothiyar, not yet mature. He possessed excellent training in weaponry and unparalleled leadership skills as a commander. He had won many battles, conquered many enemy kingdoms, and enriched the government. He had reduced the difficult-to-conquer ancient city of Badami to dust, defeated the enemy, and brought fame and glory to the king. If he had continued to serve the king for many more days, the kingdom would have flourished even more. Even though there were many more years for him to serve the king and then retire from that service, the king, knowing all this, bowed to Paranjothiyar, realizing that his service to Shiva was far greater than his service as a commander, and that it was greater than any worldly profession. The king requested him to continue his service to Shiva, granting him countless piles of wealth and grants. From now on, Paranjothiyar should not engage in any other profession for his livelihood or serve anyone. The king gave him all the sarvamanyams, lands, and piles of wealth so that he could live without worry, serving only Lord Shiva. He gave him piles of wealth. He also gave him lands so that it would not be spent away easily. He gave them as tax-free lands. This is because he should help the Shiva devotee to perform his Shiva devotion, not keep him in service and command him. The king understood that it was a great sin to do so. Therefore, he acted accordingly. The Shiva dharma rules, as stated in the Vedas and Shivagamas, are superior to worldly dharma. Thus, the king gave all these and bowed with folded hands. He bowed to Paranjothiyar. He further said, "I led you without knowing your true state of devotion. Now, you must agree to my heartfelt desire. May your true acts of devotion shine brightly in the world as you wish, and perform your service to Shiva, the path of righteousness." With these words, he released him from his duty in the army, which was under him, and freed Paranjothiyar from that obligation. He requested him to perform only Shiva devotion. This was the king's decision and judgment. Paranjothiyar had said that he had only performed his rightful duty and that there was no harm in it, but the king did not accept it. Worldly duties are different. The nature of Shiva's servitude is different. The latter is far superior and greater than the former. Politics is concerned with the welfare of the people's bodies and ends there. The nature of Shiva, which is superior, benefits the soul. Thus, the king granted him release from royal service. Now, the 11th, 12th, and 13th verses: "Mannavanai vidai kondu thampathiyil vandhadaindhu Pannu pugazh Paranjothiyar thamum panimathi vazh Senniyarai Ganapathichcharaththu irainji thiruthondu Munna nilaiyin vazhuva murai anbil seykkindrar. Vedakkarunar adiyar vendiya meippani seyya Theethil kudi pirandha Thiruvenkattu Nangai enum Kadhal manaikkizhaththiyar karuththu ondra varum perumai Neethi manai aram puriyum nermaiyinai nilai nirpar. Narai ithazhi thirumudiyar adiyarai nal thorum Muraimaiyinil thiru amudhu mun ootti pin unnum Niraiyudaiya peruviruppal niyathiyaga kollum Thurai vazhuva vagai ozhugum thooya thozhil thalai nindrar." These three verses describe Paranjothiyar taking leave of the king and returning to his native place, Thiruchengattangudi. Historians say that the king's city was Kanchipuram. From there, Paranjothiyar set out and reached his sacred place, Thiruchengattangudi, which is near Thiruvarur in the Chola country. Paranjothiyar worships the Lord, who wears the moon on his head, at Ganapatheechcharam. One can worship the Lord anywhere and everywhere, in all things. He is brilliantly and clearly enshrined in the Ganapatheechcharam temple. Paranjothiyar worships him in the Ganapatheechcharam temple. Thiruchengattangudi is the name of the sacred place. Ganapatheechcharam is the name of the temple. When Vinayakamurthy killed Gajamukasuran, the place was named Sengattangudi because of the overflowing blood. After that, Ganapathi installed and worshipped a Shiva lingam. He performed Shiva puja. Therefore, the temple came to be known as Ganapatheechcharam.

We can see this in detail in the Gayamukan Uthpatti Padalam of the Kandapuranam. Due to his great penance in the past, Paranjothiyar performs divine service in Thiruchengattangudi without fail, according to the rules prescribed by the Shiva Agamas, with love. While performing all the true services required by the devotees of Lord Shiva, who is the cause of the Vedas, he and his wife, named Thiruvenkattu Nangai, together perform Shiva dharmas with a unified mind. Paranjothiyar marries the lady named Thiruvenkattu Nangai. Our Nayanar, who performs Shiva devotion with proper love, undertook this householder's dharma solely to serve the devotees. The Thirukkural states, "Thenpulathar deivam virundhu okkal than" (The ancestors, God, guests, and relatives). It also states, "Thurandharkkum thuvvathavarkkum endrum" (For ascetics and the destitute). The Thirukkural also states, "Ilvalvan enban iyalbudaiya moovarkkum endrum" (The householder is for the three natural ones). Thus, the book of ethics outlines the duties of those who engage in householder's life. But all those are worldly dharmas. The divine service to Shiva's devotees mentioned here is of a different nature altogether. All worldly dharmas turn into corresponding merits, give corresponding enjoyments, and lead to rebirth. But the divine service to devotees, being different and superior to worldly dharmas, becomes the very service to Shiva and ultimately leads to liberation. Here, our Nayanar did not marry for worldly pleasures or for progeny. He married solely to perform true service to devotees. We can confirm this in the events that follow in this sacred story. We can also recall the sacred stories of great individuals like Ilaiyankudi Mara Nayanar, Iyarpagai Nayanar, Kungiliyakkalaiya Nayanar, who served devotees while remaining in the householder's state. We see in their respective stories that they too lived a householder's life with the sole aim of serving devotees. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman mentions the name of Paranjothiyar's divine wife as Thiruvenkattu Nangai. Her birth name is not known to us. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman mentions the lady's name as Thiruvenkattu Nangai, referring to the town of Thiruvenkadu. Just as Karaikkal Ammaiyar is known, this lady's name is widely known as Thiruvenkattu Nangai. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman mentions that the lineage she was born into is a "theethil kudi" (flawless lineage). It is a good lineage where both families are pure. We can recall the Thirumurugatrupadai, which speaks of "iru marabum thoolku thol kudi" (ancient lineage of two pure traditions). The householder's life of Paranjothiyar and Thiruvenkattu Nangai Ammaiyar was also filled with love, as described in Akapporul texts. As Avvaiyar said, "Kadhal iruvar karuththu orumiththu adharavu pattathe inbam" (Love is the joy when two minds unite and support each other), their minds were united. In what were they united? Their minds were united in performing divine service to devotees and for Shiva's puja. We will see this greatness throughout this sacred story. Thus, engaging in the householder's dharma, Paranjothiyar daily followed the rule of first offering sacred food to the devotees of Lord Shiva, who wears a garland of honey-laden Konrai flowers on his head, according to scriptural injunctions, and only then partaking of it himself. He excelled in such pure service and devotion. Therefore, in these three verses, we see the appropriateness of first mentioning Paranjothiyar's service to Shiva, then stating that he undertook the householder's dharma for the sake of serving devotees, and then describing his continued service to devotees while remaining in that householder's dharma. Now, the divine Sekkizhar Pillaiyar Peruman will explain how he offered sacred food to the devotees. Let us continue to contemplate that. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 04 — Siruthonda Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Part 927, by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, Varkonda Vanamulaiyal Sarukkam, Siruthonda Nayanar Puranam. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman sings about how Siruthonda Nayanar offered sacred food to the devotees. The 14th verse: "Thooya thiru amudhu kani kannal arusuvai kari ney Paaya thayir pal inniya panniyam unnir amudham Meya padiyal amudhu seivikka isaindhu adiyar Mayiru nyalam potra varum ivarpal manam magizhndhar." This verse describes how sacred food should be prepared for devotees. Paranjothiyar was an excellent commander and a great devotee of the king. Therefore, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman elaborates that he prepared all these without any deficiency and offered them to the devotees. Previously, in the Ilaiyankudi Nayanar Puranam, he generally mentioned "undi nalu vidathil aru suvai thiraththinil" (four kinds of food with six tastes). Here, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman elaborates on this point. He also explains this to us in a way that emphasizes and teaches us. He offered pure sacred food, fruits, jaggery, six kinds of curries, ghee, thick curd, milk, sweet delicacies, and nectar-like drinking water to the devotees in an appropriate manner. "Thiru amudhu" here refers to cooked rice made from red paddy. "Pal" refers to milk that has been thickened by boiling. "Unnir amudham" here refers to purified water for drinking. Drinking water for thirst is different. Purified water, which is an important part of the meal when eating, is different. "Paaya thayir" refers to curd that has been set and is thick. All these were done regularly every day. Thus, when sacred food was served, all the devotees were greatly pleased and rejoiced. The 15th verse: "Seetha mathi aravinudan senjadai mel seriviththa Nathan adiyar thammai nayappattu vazhipattal Methakaiyar avar munbu migach chiriyarai adaindhar Adhalinal Siruthondar ena nigazhndhar avaniyin mel." We saw that the Nayanar's birth name was Paranjothiyar. The name Siruthondar is prevalent in the world for him. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman sings in this verse the reason for that name. He worships the devotees of Lord Shiva, who wears the cool moon and snakes on his red matted hair, with devotion. He considered himself very small in the presence of the great Shiva devotees and served at their feet. Therefore, he became known by the name Siruthondar (the small devotee) in the world. Although Paranjothiyar was the Nayanar's birth name and was praised by many, the name Siruthondar became very famous and prevalent in the world. It will be praised by Sundaramurthy Swamigal in the Thiruthondathogai Thiruppathigam. This name also seems to be in accordance with the Nayanar's wish. That is why the name Siruthondar became more widely praised and used than his birth name Paranjothiyar. He saw himself as very small in the presence of the greatness of the devotees and served them. Whenever he saw devotees, he thought, "Where is their greatness? Where is my smallness? By their grace, they have come to take me in, without considering their greatness." And he acted accordingly. That is the nature of Paranjothi Nayanar. Just as Thirunavukkarasar Peruman's Thevaram says, "Ethanaiyum ariya nee eliyai aanay eliyenai vandhu erangi endru kondai Ethanaiyum emperamo aiya ayyo emperuman thirukkarunai irundhavare" (You, who are so rare, became simple; you came and embraced me, the lowly one. Is all this mine, oh Lord? Alas, alas, how great is the divine grace of our Lord!), Siruthonda Nayanar's nature is similar. Now, the 16th and 17th verses: "Kannuthalar Ganapathichcharaththinkan karuththu amara Ullanirai anbinil pani seidhu ozhuguvar vazhuvinri Ennil perum seer adiyar idaividadhu amudhu seyya Nanniya per uvakaiyudan nayandhu uraiyum nalin kan Neerarum sadaimudiyar arulinal nirai thavaththu Peralar avarthamakku perugu thirumanai araththin Veragi vilangum Thiruvenkattu Nangai pal Seeralathevar enum thirumaindhar avatharithar." Siruthonda Nayanar serves with unwavering devotion and overflowing love in his heart, focusing his thoughts on Lord Shiva, who has an eye on his forehead, at Ganapatheechcharam. While countless great devotees continuously partook of the sacred food, Siruthonda Nayanar, filled with great joy, resided there with pleasure. By the grace of Lord Shiva, who has water in his matted hair, a divine son named Seeraladevar was born from the womb of Thiruvenkattu Nangai, who was the root of the flourishing householder's dharma for Siruthonda Nayanar, the great ascetic whose penance was complete. The birth of the divine son was due to Shiva's grace, which flowed from Siruthonda Nayanar's service to Shiva and his feeding of the devotees. Siruthonda Nayanar's penance was complete, and his penance was to be fulfilled by this progeny. Such a great ascetic, whose penance was complete, had a divine son born to him through his wife, Thiruvenkattu Nangai Ammaiyar. The wife, the householder, is the root of the householder's dharma, which is a form of penance. We will see the greatness of their householder's life in the events that follow in this sacred story. To such a Siruthonda Nayanar, a divine son named Seeraladevar was born from the womb of his divine wife. How many great qualities does Seeraladevar possess! Seeraladevar is the source of the events in the Siruthonda Nayanar Puranam. Seeraladevar is the one who received the great blessing of being called the Lord's desired offering. Seeralan Siruthondan is the one who received the blessing of being mentioned in the divine words of Thirugnanasambandar Peruman. He is the one who received the unique blessing of being taken by the Lord, along with Lord Muruga and the Goddess. He is destined to receive great blessings. He possesses so many great qualities, and therefore he was praised as "Needu Maganar" (the long-lived son) and "Seeraladevar enum Thirumaindhar" (the divine son named Seeraladevar) by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. The 18th and 19th verses: "Arumaiyinil thani puthalvar pirandha pozhudhu alangariththa Perumaiyinil kilai kalippa pirarkkariya mani pettru varum Magizhchi thathaiyar manaththu adangaa vagai valara Thiru mali neyyadal vizha Sengattangudi eduppa Mangala nalliyam muzhakka marai muzhakkam van alappa Anganar tham seer adiyarkku alavirandha nithi aliththu Thangal marabinil urimai sadangu dasa thinaththinilum Pongu perum magizhchiyudan purindhu kaappu ani punaindhar." These two verses describe the joyful events and auspicious ceremonies that took place during the divine birth of Seeraladevar. When the rare and unique son was born, decorations were made, and the relatives rejoiced in that greatness. They performed decorations, including household decorations. The father rejoiced at having received a son who was difficult to obtain. It was customary in those days to understand and know the countless great qualities of the divine son through astrology, the child's physical structure, and other grammatical texts at the time of birth. Therefore, the father was very happy that he had received a son who was rare to others. The joyful ceremony that all outsiders perform for a good childbirth is the Neyyadal Vizha (ghee bathing ceremony). We see this in the divine birth of Aludaiya Pillaiyar Thirugnanasambandar Peruman. Some also call it Ennai Adal Vizha (oil bathing ceremony). Now, auspicious musical instruments are played. Vedic mantras are chanted to bless. Siruthonda Nayanar gives immeasurable wealth to the Lord's great devotees. He performs the customary rituals of his lineage for 10 days. There is also a tradition of performing rituals for 10 days after birth. There are also lineages that perform rituals for 16 days. These vary from lineage to lineage. They adorn the child with the sacred ash as a protective charm. The 20th verse: "Arvam nirai perum sutram akamalar aliththu avartham Par perugum magizhchiyudan paruvamurai parattu Seer peruga seyya valar thiru maganar seeradaiyil Thar valar kinkini asaiya thalar nadaiyin patham sarnthan." After the child's birth, giving special gifts to relatives with great affection and rejoicing is also one of the rituals. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman first mentions the greatness of giving wealth to Shiva's devotees and then states that Siruthonda Nayanar also gives great wealth to his relatives with a joyful heart. In those days, Siruthonda Nayanar performed all the seasonal rituals with excellence. The growing divine son, Seeraladevanayanar, reached the stage of toddling, with the jingling anklets, strung like a garland, swaying on his small divine feet, as described in the Pillaitamil stages, starting from the Sengirrai Paruvam. We have contemplated these stages and their special rituals in detail in the Thirugnanasambandar Peruman Puranam. Thus, as these seasonal rituals were performed, Siruthonda Nayanar's divine son, Seeraladevanayanar, grew and reached the stage of toddling, with the jingling anklets, strung like a garland, swaying on his small divine feet. The 21st verse: "Surulum mayir nuthal sutti thunaikkathin manik kudambai Maruvu thirukkanda nan marbinil aimpadai kaiyil Poruvil vairach sarigal pon araignan punai sadhangai Theruvil oli vilanga valar thiru vilaiyattinil amardhar." In this verse, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman shows us the divine form of Seeraladevanayanar, adorned in his childhood, from head to toe. He wears a forehead ornament on his curly hair-filled forehead. Curly hair falling on the forehead is a sign of physical beauty. He wears diamond earrings in both ears. Kudambai is an earring worn for the growth of the ears. It is made of precious stones. He wears a golden chain called "nan" around his sacred neck. He wears an "aimpadai thali" on his chest. The "aimpadai thali" is an ornament made of gold, depicting the five weapons of Vishnu – conch, discus, sword, mace, and bow – which are believed to protect the child as it grows. It is customary for this to protect the child. He wears bracelets on his divine hands and armlets on his divine shoulders. He wears a golden waist-cord around his waist. He wears anklets on his divine feet. Thus, adorned with all these ornaments, from head to toe, from the forehead ornament to the anklets, Seeraladevar, shining brightly in the street, delights in his divine play. The 22nd verse: "Vandhu valar moovandil mayir vinai mangalam seidhu Thanthaiyarum payandha thayarum thanich chiruvar Sinthai malar sol thelivil sezhungalaigal payila tham Banthamara vandhavarai palliyinil iruththinar." Seeraladeva Nayanar is growing well. He reaches his third year. In the third year, they perform the auspicious ritual of hair removal, which is called Saula Kalyanam. The father and the mother, who gave birth to him, placed their unique little son in school so that he could learn the rich arts with clarity of words, which would make his mind blossom, and so that their birth bond would be severed. Seeraladevar did not come there for himself. He did not take birth for himself. He was born to sever the birth bond of his parents, which was due to their attachment. We will see in this historical event how he became the means to sever the birth of his parents by severing the worldly attachment of a son. Therefore, they placed him in school, the one who came to sever their bond. The 23rd verse: "Annallil Sanpai nagar andagaiyar ezhundharula Munnaga ethirkondu kodu pugundhu munnool ser Pon marbil Siruthondar pugali kavalartham Nannama sevadihal potri isaiththu nalam sirandhar." In those days, when Aludaiya Pillaiyar Thirugnanasambandar Peruman, the Lord of Sirkazhi, came to Thiruchengattangudi, Siruthondar, whose chest was adorned with the sacred thread, went forward to receive him, brought him into the city, and made him reside in his house. He praised the good and glorious feet of Thirugnanasambandar Peruman, the protector of Pugali, and attained great welfare. He welcomed Thirugnanasambandar Peruman into his divine house and honored him. The 24th verse: "Sanpaiyartham perumanum thangiya perungathal Panbudaiya Siruthondarudan payindru matru avai Man paravum thiruppathigam thanil vaiththu sirappiththu Nanbaruli ezhundharula tham inithu nayapputrar." We also saw in the Puranam of Thirugnanasambandar Peruman that Thirugnanasambandar Peruman came to Thiruchengattangudi and resided in Siruthonda Nayanar's divine house. Aludaiya Pillaiyar, the Lord of Sirkazhi, resided happily with Siruthondar, who possessed the great and ever-growing love that was difficult to bear. In accordance with "Anbarodum maree" (mingling with devotees), Thirugnanasambandar Peruman praised Siruthonda Nayanar's greatness in a Thiruppathigam that would be praised by the world and honored him. Only those who are born in this world with the blessing of merit and follow the path of Shiva can spread and attain salvation through the Thiruppathigams of Thirugnanasambandar Peruman. Even other celestial beings and others are unable to understand and spread them. In such a Thiruppathigam of Thirugnanasambandar Peruman, he praised Siruthonda Nayanar. We recall here the great blessings that came from Pillaiyar's divine words, where even inanimate palm trees attained liberation and a bone became a woman. We see that Thirugnanasambandar Peruman praised Siruthonda Nayanar in the Thiruppathigam for Thiruchengattangudi, saying, "Podhi nugarum Siruthondarkku arul seyyum poruttaga Kadinagaray veetrirundhan Ganapathichcharane" (For the sake of bestowing grace upon Siruthondar who partakes of the sacred ash, Ganapathichcharan resided in the strong city) and "Seeralan Siruthondan Sengattangudi meya Peralan" (Seeralan, Siruthondan, the great one who resides in Sengattangudi). In addition to praising him, Thirugnanasambandar Peruman bestowed upon Siruthonda Nayanar the great blessing of being his friend. Nambiyandar Nambigal Swamigal also sang about this in the Aludaiya Pillaiyar Thiru Andhadhi. The Thirukkural states, "Unarchithan natpam kizhamai tharum" (Understanding brings friendship and kinship). It was the great compassion of our Paramacharyar, Thirugnanasambandar Peruman, that he bestowed the blessing of friendship upon Siruthondar, who served at his divine feet. Thus, bestowing the blessing of friendship, Thirugnanasambandar Peruman departed from Thiruchengattangudi to other sacred places. Siruthonda Nayanar and his wife always contemplated Pillaiyar's greatness and rejoiced. In this sacred story of Siruthonda Nayanar, the Lord will now come as Bhairava and take Siruthonda Nayanar and Seeraladevar into his grace. We rejoice to see how the greatness of Seeraladevar, which will be revealed in the historical event of the great grace that the Lord will now bestow upon them, was previously praised by Thirugnanasambandar Peruman in these Thiruppathigams as a premonition by divine grace. Now, we will continue to contemplate the very important section where the Lord comes as Bhairava. Thiruchitrambalam.
