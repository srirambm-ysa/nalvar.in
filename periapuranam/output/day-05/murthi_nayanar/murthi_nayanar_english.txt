Chapter 01 — Murthi Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 180, Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam. The Periya Puranam, also known as Thiruthondar Puranam, is an elaborate work based on the Thiruthondathogai, a sacred decad composed by Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Sekkizhar Peruman extensively narrated the histories of the devotees praised in the Thiruthondathogai, integrating them with the life story of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, in this divine text, Periya Puranam. So far, we have contemplated three sarukkams in Periya Puranam: Thirumalai Sarukkam, Thillaivaazh Andhanar Sarukkam, and Ilaimalindha Sarukkam. The Periya Puranam is structured into a total of 13 sarukkams. The Thiruthondathogai decad contains 11 verses. For these 11 verses, 11 sarukkams are arranged in Periya Puranam. Additionally, the work begins with Thirumalai Sarukkam as the first, and concludes with Vellaanai Sarukkam as the 13th. In accordance with the first verse, which begins with "Thillaivaazh Andhanar," we contemplated the histories of those Nayanmars in the Thillaivaazh Andhanar Puranam. Following that, in the Ilaimalindha Sarukkam, we saw the histories of the Nayanmars praised in the second verse of Thiruthondathogai, which begins with "Ilaimalindha Vel Nambi Eripattarkku Adiyen." In that sequence, the third verse in the Thiruthondathogai decad now begins with "Mummaiyaal Ulaganda." Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman has named this sarukkam "Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam" and composed it. The third verse of Thiruthondathogai is: "Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Murthikkum Adiyen, Muruganukkum Uruththira Pasupathikkum Adiyen, Semmaiye Thirunaalaip Povaarkkum Adiyen, Thirukkuripth Thondar Tham Adiyaarkkum Adiyen, Meymmaiye Thirumeni Vazhipada Nirkka Vegundezhundha Thathai Thaal Mazhuvinaal Erindha Ammaiyaan Adichchandip Perumaanukku Adiyen, Arooran Arooril Ammaanukku Aale." This is the third verse of the Thiruthondathogai decad. The section that narrates the histories of the devotees praised in this verse is the fourth sarukkam, named Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam. The devotees glorified in this third verse are Murthi Nayanar, Muruga Nayanar, Uruththira Pasupathi Nayanar, Thirunaalaip Povaar Nayanar, Thirukkuripth Thondar Nayanar, and Chandeesa Nayanar – a total of six Nayanmars. Therefore, in this sarukkam, we are going to contemplate the sacred puranas of these six Nayanmars. In order, the first among them is Murthi Nayanar. "Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Murthikkum Adiyen" is the line composed by Sundaramoorthy Swamigal about Murthi Nayanar. First, we will contemplate a brief summary of the Nayanar's life, and then we will delve into a detailed contemplation of his purana. The Pandya country is renowned. It is a land of great fame, praised by its loving and good people. The Thamirabarani River enriches it. The Podhigai Hills bestow cool breezes upon the Pandya country. Moreover, it bestows the divine Tamil language of the deity who measured the world. The capital city of this Pandya country, distinguished by wealth and education, is Madhurapuri, also known as Madurai. In Madurai, in the merchant community, as a result of the penance performed by that lineage, Murthiyar was born. He held the sacred feet of Lord Shiva as his sole true devotion. He lived considering those sacred feet as his sole support and his ultimate attainment. He unfailingly performed the sacred service of grinding sandalwood paste daily for the anointing of Lord Sokkalinga's idol at Thiru Aalavaai. In those days, a Vaduga Karnataka king, driven by his desire to conquer lands through his military might, waged war against the Pandya king with a vast army, emerged victorious, seized control of the Pandya country, and crowned himself king in Madurai. He was not aligned with the righteous path of Saivism, which embraces the sacred ash, but was deeply immersed in the unrighteous path of Jainism, persecuting devotees of Shiva and their devotion. He made many attempts to convert Murthi Nayanar to Jainism. However, none of his efforts succeeded. Murthi Nayanar continued his regular sacred service. Unable to tolerate this, the Jain king attempted to obstruct Murthi Nayanar's service of grinding sandalwood paste. He blocked all routes through which sandalwood logs arrived. He ensured that Murthi Nayanar could not obtain sandalwood logs. Due to this, Murthi Nayanar was deeply distressed and thought, "When will this cruel sinner perish, and when will this land receive a Saivite king who upholds the righteous path of sacred ash?" He was greatly saddened. One day, he searched for sandalwood logs throughout the day, but could not find any. Therefore, Murthi Nayanar, deeply grieved and disheartened, came to the Lord's temple. "Today, sandalwood is not available for the Lord's anointing. But there is no obstacle to this hand that grinds it." Thus, with great resolve, he placed his plump elbow on the sandalwood grinding stone and rubbed it. Blood flowed. His skin, flesh, nerves, and bones wore away. Thinking, "Today, there is an obstruction to the Lord's sandalwood anointing," he thought, "What if sandalwood logs are unavailable? I have a hand to grind sandalwood," and began to grind his own hand. He placed his own hand on the sandalwood grinding stone, as if it were a sandalwood log, and rubbed it. As a result, skin, bones, blood, flesh, nerves, and everything else emerged. That very night, the Lord spoke an ethereal voice to the Nayanar, "O noble one, you have performed such acts out of your love for me. Do not perform such acts anymore. That Jain king who tormented you and other Saivites will fall, and you yourself will rule this land, remove all past troubles, protect this world, perform your sacred service, and attain our world." Murthi Nayanar heard this. The wound on his hand healed, and his hand became beautiful and vibrant as before. Murthi Nayanar shone with a radiant body imbued with the fragrance of Shiva. That very night, the cruel king who had tormented and persecuted all devotees of Shiva died. The chief ministers gathered and performed the funeral rites for him according to custom the next morning. The king had no sons. Therefore, according to the rule that a country should not be without a king, they decided to blindfold an elephant, and whoever was chosen and carried by this blindfolded elephant would become king. Hearing all this, Murthi Nayanar recalled the divine words the Lord had spoken to him the previous night. "If this is the Lord's grace, I will undertake this task of ruling the world," he resolved, his despondency gone, and he emerged from the Thiru Aalavaai temple. The elephant went there, bowed low to Murthi Nayanar, lifted him, and placed him on its back. Seeing this, the people of Madurai hailed the Nayanar and chanted auspicious slogans. They dismounted Murthi Nayanar from the elephant, took him to the coronation hall, and began the rituals for his coronation. At that time, Murthi Nayanar declared, "If Jainism declines and Saivism flourishes, then I will accept the kingship and rule this earth. For the coronation ritual, sacred ash must be the anointing substance. Rudraksha must be the ornament. Matted hair must be the crown." The ministers and scholars well-versed in sacred texts, hearing this, bowed and agreed, saying, "Very well," and performed all the rituals accordingly. Murthi Nayanar was crowned king of Madurai, king of the Pandya country, amidst auspicious sounds, Vedic chants, and blessings. From the coronation hall, Murthiyar went to the Thiru Aalavaai temple in Madurai, bowed there, and then mounted the elephant to proceed in a royal procession through the city streets. He arrived at the palace gates and sat on the royal throne in the royal court. The ministers acted according to his indications. The darkness of Jainism dispersed, and the light of sacred ash shone everywhere. Murthi Nayanar ruled, ensuring that Saivism, which pervaded the world, flourished, that the tenets of Shiva Agamas were understood, and that sins were dispelled, by wearing sacred ash, Rudraksha, and matted hair. Thus, Murthi Nayanar ruled the world with these three – sacred ash, Rudraksha, and matted hair. That is why Sundaramoorthy Swamigal sang of him as "Murthiyar, who ruled the world by the three" (Mummaiyinaal). He sang, "Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Murthikkum Adiyen." Murthi Nayanar was a Nayanar who embraced complete asceticism, having no desire for women. He eliminated both internal enemies, such as the five senses, and external enemies, such as foreign kings including the Jains. He ruled this world for a long time, removing suffering from all beings and protecting them. Moreover, he continued his sacred service to Lord Shiva without fail throughout his life and finally attained the shade of the Lord's feet. Thus, by adorning these three – sacred ash, Rudraksha, and matted hair – and ruling the world, he was worshipped and praised by devotees as Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Murthi Nayanar. Arumuga Navalar Peruman provides the following commentary on Murthi Nayanar Puranam: Offering sandalwood paste for anointing Lord Shiva is a supreme act of Shiva punya (merit). Anointing with a mixture of sandalwood paste, rosewater, saffron, gorochana, musk, and green camphor is the most supreme of all. Shiva Agamas state that among all services to Shiva, offering gandham (sandalwood) is the best. The books "Somavaara Viratha Kalpam" and "Shiva Punya Thelivu" also speak about this sacred service of offering sandalwood for the Lord's anointing. Murthi Nayanar attained great distinction through this Shiva punya. Even when the king caused him immense suffering in his sacred service of grinding sandalwood for the Lord's anointing, he remained undeterred and continued his service without fail. When sandalwood logs were obstructed, he ground his own elbow on the sandalwood stone. Later, even when he attained the kingship of the world, he did not become arrogant but continued this service himself. Without any attachment to his body, he firmly believed that Shiva alone was dear to him, clung to Him incessantly, and lived as a true devotee. Texts like "Shiva Dharmottaram" state that those who harm devotees of Shiva will suffer in hell. Therefore, the Karnataka king who caused suffering to Shiva's devotees fell into hell. Hence, we should never even inadvertently think of harming Shiva's devotees. A king should rule according to the righteous path of Saivism, as revealed by the Vedas and Agamas. Consequently, the subjects living under him will also live according to the path followed by that king. Murthi Nayanar was greatly saddened by the cruel actions of the king from Karnataka, who invaded, conquered, became king, embraced Jainism, and thereby caused suffering to Saivites. He lamented, "When will the Pandya country get a Saivite king?" To alleviate Murthi Nayanar's sorrow, the Lord caused that king to die that very night and cast him into hell. Murthi Nayanar was someone who did not desire royal life even a little. But after the Lord Himself, through an ethereal voice, commanded him to rule, out of great compassion for living beings, and with the intention that the Pandya country should have a Saivite king, and above all, due to the Lord's ethereal command, Murthi Nayanar became king. He ruled with sacred ash, Rudraksha, and matted hair. Furthermore, before accepting the royal position, he laid down a condition to the ministers, saying, "If Jainism departs and Saivism flourishes, then I will rule." He ruled with sacred ash as the anointing substance, Rudraksha as ornaments, and matted hair as the crown. Thus writes Arumuga Navalar Peruman in his commentary. Let us continue to contemplate the Purana of Murthi Nayanar in detail. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 02 — Murthi Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 181, Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam, Murthi Nayanar Puranam. In the Periya Puranam, also known as Thiruthondar Puranam, the Purana of Murthi Nayanar is presented by Sekkizhar Peruman in 49 verses within the Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam. This Purana is the section that narrates the life history and character of the Nayanar named Murthiyar. First, in the compendium, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's phrase about Murthi Nayanar is "Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Murthikkum Adiyen." Its meaning is, "I am a devotee of Murthiyar, who ruled the world by the three Shiva symbols: sacred ash, Rudraksha, and matted hair." Next, in the category of works, Nambiyandar Nambi's verse about Murthi Nayanar in Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi is: "Avandhiri kondu amanavathin maalvan endra andru Aalavaai Sivan Thirumenikku senchandhanamaaga sezhumuzhangai uvandhu olir paaraiyil theyththu ulagandda on murthithannoor nivandha ponmaada Madhurapuriyanum neelpathiye." This is the verse about Murthi Nayanar found in Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi. Now, let us contemplate the Purana of Murthi Nayanar in detail in the extensive work, Thiruthondar Puranam, also known as Periya Puranam. The town where Murthi Nayanar was born is Madhurapuri, also known as Madurai. Therefore, the country where he was born is the Pandya country. In all the Puranas of the Nayanmars, it is Sekkizhar Peruman's tradition to systematically narrate the country, city, lineage, name of the Nayanar, and their sacred service. In that tradition, Murthi Nayanar was born in the Pandya country. Murthi Nayanar was born and lived in Madurai. Here, in the Thiruthondar Puranam, also known as Periya Puranam, the first mention of the Pandya country and Madurai occurs in this Murthi Nayanar Puranam. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman, following his chosen tradition, has beautifully and extensively described the prosperity of the Pandya country and the great city of Madurai. The Pandya country is a land ruled by kings of the Pandya dynasty. The Pandya country is specially known as Tamil Nadu. Generally, in Tamil literary tradition, Tamil Nadu refers to the Pandya country. The history of the Pandya country is intertwined with the history of the Tamil language. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman, when he began to sing the praises of the Pandya country, started with the Kalithurai meter and composed the entire Purana in that single type of poetic meter. We all know the history of how Periya Puranam came into being. Anapaya Chozhan was deeply engrossed in the Jain religious text "Seevaga Chinthamani" and was wasting his time studying it. Although "Seevaga Chinthamani" was rich in Tamil and material wealth, he spent his time on it without realizing that it did not provide ultimate truth. Sekkizhar Peruman guided his mind to the right path by narrating the histories of Shiva's devotees, the Nayanmars. That is why this divine work, Thiruthondar Puranam, also known as Periya Puranam, came into existence. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman began this history related to Tamil and the Pandya country with the Kalithurai meter of Chinthamani, "Kaaymaanda Thengin Pazham Veezha Kamuginettri," and composed the entire Purana in that single type of poetic meter. The poetic structure of this history, which allows readers to simultaneously enjoy the taste of Tamil and the benefits of this world and the next, showcases Sekkizhar Peruman's compassion and poetic genius. The suitability of Murthi Nayanar's Purana, the king of the Pandya country and Tamil Nadu, for the richness of Tamil can also be applied here. It can be understood from the verses in this Purana that the author entered into it with the richness of Tamil in his mind. The Pandya country is a land where righteousness flourished. It is a land filled with citizens of enduring wealth. It is a land filled with lofty mansions. This ancient Pandya country, praised by people of noble hearts, scholars, and sages renowned in various fields, has stood firm in the world. This Pandya country is a land that bestows the four ultimate pursuits: dharma (righteousness), artha (wealth), kama (pleasure), and moksha (liberation). The river that flows through this Pandya country, making it fertile, is the Thamirabarani River. Because this Thamirabarani River merges with the sea, the Pandya country is rich in pearl production. The Thamirabarani River is a cool river. It is only because this Thamirabarani River flows into the sea that the sea becomes capable of producing pearls. The fact that pearls are dived for near the city of Korkai on the coast where the Thamirabarani flows is well-known to all of us. Furthermore, the pearls carried down by the Thamirabarani from mountains including the Podhigai Hills shine on both banks of the river. They also shine at the point where it merges with the sea. Due to the coolness of the Thamirabarani River, the pearls produced by it are also abundant in water flow and cool. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman sings of the river and the power of the Pandya country. Next, Sekkizhar Peruman sings about the Podhigai Hills. The Podhigai Hills are a mountain filled with groves where sandalwood trees grow densely. The wind that originates from there transforms into a fragrant breeze and blows throughout the Pandya country. In the sandalwood groves of the Podhigai Hills, bees swarm densely and create a buzzing sound. The wind coming from the sandalwood forests of the Podhigai Hills possesses the gentle movement, coolness, and fragrance characteristic of wind, and it imparts coolness upon contact with the body. Does the Podhigai Hills only offer this fragrant breeze? It also offers the exalted divine Tamil that measured the entire world. Tamil is divine Tamil. It is the Tamil that measured the world. Gnālam means world. Gnālam alandha theiva Tamil means Tamil that has the greatness of having determined and measured the nature of the world. It means Tamil that revealed the true philosophy of the world. It also means Tamil that spread throughout the world and Tamil that is respected by the exalted. Such is the greatness of divine Tamil. Because the Tamil in which the grammar of meaning revealed by Lord Shiva, and the sacred divine texts like Thevaram, Thiruvasagam, and philosophical treatises composed by true Nayanmars who became Shiva through knowledge, arose, is divine Tamil. The divinity of Tamil is known through the actions of these divine utterances and other powers. Praising this very idea, Kambar also says, "He measured the world with a long Tamil like a discus," praising Agastya. Furthermore, there is another meaning for "Gnālam alandha theiva Tamil." In Tholkappiyam, Tholkappiyar divided this world into four land types, measured them, and extensively described their meanings and the grammars contained within them. Thus, by encompassing the entire world and all objects in it and creating grammar, this divine Tamil is the exalted divine Tamil that measured the world. Perundevanar, who composed Bharatham, in the invocation of Ainkurunuru, sings of Lord Shiva as follows: "Neelameni vaalizhai paagaththu oruvan iru thaal nizhal keezh moovar ivvulagum igazhththana muraiye." This also signifies the quality of having measured the world. Moreover, this divine Tamil bestows "sevvi manam sei eeram," meaning it creates the moisture of love. This love spoken of by divine Tamil unites the soul, the beloved, with Lord Shiva, our soul's beloved. The Tamil that holds this love within itself is divine Tamil. It possesses Thirukkovaiyar, the great divine Tamil work that produces this love. Iraiyanar Agapporul, the great divine Tamil work given by Iraiyanar, is similarly praised as "Anbin Ainthinai" (the five divisions of love). Furthermore, Ramanaatha Chettiyar, in his commentary, says that divine Tamil bestows love by giving Shiva-consciousness according to the maturity of souls. Also, by combining "theiva Tamil" with "theiva manam sei sevvi tharum," it can be interpreted as divine Tamil that can transform the heat of this birth and bestow beautiful grace capable of creating divinity. Above all this, why is Tamil called divine Tamil? Because Lord Shiva, the unique supreme deity, was the first to give this divine Tamil. He was the first to research this divine Tamil. This Tamil language is what He desired and what explains Lord Shiva Himself as its inner meaning. Therefore, it becomes divine Tamil. Sekkizhar explains this fact, that Lord Shiva Himself was in the Sangam and nurtured Tamil, in the Purana of Thirunavukkarasu Peruman. In the Purana of Thirunavukkarasar, Sekkizhar Peruman later sings, "Maduraiyil thirundhiya nool sangaththil andru irundhu Tamil aaraaindhu aruliya Anganar." "Kannudhal perum kadavulum kazhagamodu amarndhu pannura thirindhu aaindha ip pasundhamizh" is from Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam. "Nizhal poli kanichchimani nettri umizh chengan thalar poli sudarkadavul thandha Tamil" is Kambar's verse. Just as the breeze from Podhigai Hills removes our heat and gives coolness to the body, this Tamil, given by Agastya, the Tamil sage living in the sacred Podhigai Hills, removes the heat of birth from souls and provides the shade of grace. Therefore, it is divine Tamil. Thus, Sivakavimani elaborately explains the term "divine Tamil" as sung by Sekkizhar. Next, Sekkizhar Peruman describes Madurai, the capital of the Pandya country. The city of Madurai is shaped like a lotus flower. Like Lakshmi residing on that lotus flower, the women of Madurai live here. Sweet music emanates from the lutes they play. Exquisite melodies arise from their voices. Sweet music also comes from the bees in the women's hair. Such is the city of Madhurapuri. This Madurai is a city that thrives with unique qualities, always with greatness and without any deficiency. On a lotus flower, one Lakshmi resides. But here, all the women in the lotus flower that is Madurai city shine like Lakshmi. Sweet music emanates from the lutes they play. This reminds us of the music and histories of Viraliyar, the wife of Paanabhatrar, and others, in the Thiruvilaiyadal where music triumphed, concerning the dwelling of music in the language of the lute. These women wear freshly bloomed flowers in their hair. Bees swarm on them. Those bees also make sounds. This reminds us of the history of the fragrance of the queen of Shanbhaga Maran's hair, and other such stories, in the Thiruvilaiyadal where the golden purse was given to Dharumi, concerning the dwelling of bees in the hair. Such is the glorious city of Madurai, also known as Madhurapuri. We will continue to contemplate the glories and Sthala Purana of Madurai. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 03 — Murthi Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 182, Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam, Murthi Nayanar Puranam. The country where Murthi Nayanar was born and lived was the Pandya country, and the city was Madurai. Therefore, Sivakavimani Ayya, who wrote the commentary on Periya Puranam, wherever Sekkizhar Peruman's verses about the birthplaces and liberation places of Nayanmars appear, he would mention the important aspects and special features of those places. Here, it is our tradition to contemplate the greatness and legends of those places as much as possible, based on their Sthala Puranas. In that manner, we must systematically contemplate the special features of Madhurapuri, also known as Madurai, which is both the birthplace and liberation place of Murthi Nayanar. This world is like the form of Lord Vishnu. The Pandya country is like the lotus from Lord Vishnu's navel. The Podhigai Hills are like the pericarp of that lotus flower. Sage Agastya, who lives on the Podhigai Hills, is like Brahma residing in that lotus pericarp. The three forms of Tamil nurtured by Sage Agastya are like the Vedas revealed by Brahma. The incomparable Mount Meru will be like the Podhigai Hills. The river Sambunadam that flows around Mount Meru will be like the Thamirabarani River where the gods bathed, surrounding the Podhigai Hills. The land of enjoyment surrounded by the Sambunadam River will be like the Pandya country surrounded by the Thamirabarani. Thiru Aalavaai, also known as Madurai, with its own Tamil, is the very form of Shivlokam. All the cities outside Thiru Aalavaai are forms of the cities of gods like Indra, Brahma, and Vishnu. All dharmas originate in the cities surrounded by the Thamirabarani River. Devotion to Lord Shiva's feet arises in the minds of the people of those cities. Why does Lord Shiva come to the South and perform His divine dance here? Is it not to receive the breeze from the Podhigai Hills on His divine face and to savor the taste of Tamil with His divine ears? Lord Shiva revealed the grammar sutras for the Sanskrit language to Sage Panini. Similarly, He instructed Sage Agastya, who was seated on the Podhigai Hills, in Tamil grammar. This verdant Tamil, which Lord Shiva Himself, as one of the Sangam poets, resided in and researched, becomes divine Tamil. Such a glorious Pandya country, where Shiva devotion, true love, and Saivite enlightenment never diminish, and where righteousness is always practiced, is the land ruled by Goddess Uma, Lord Shiva, and Lord Muruga. Lord Shiva Himself, as Sundareswarar, came here and ruled with Madurai as His capital. The all-powerful Parasakti Herself, as Meenakshi Devi, incarnated and ruled with Madurai as Her capital. Their divine son, Lord Muruga, also incarnated here as the divine son of Sundareswarar and Meenakshi Devi, and He too wielded the scepter. One can say that there is no other city or country in this world, in the heavens, or in any other world, where Lord Shiva, Ambika, and Lord Muruga have ruled in this manner. The city of Madurai is a red lotus abode for Goddess Lakshmi. It is a white lotus abode for Goddess Saraswati. It is a yogic seat for Goddess Uma, the power of knowledge. The glories of Madurai are extensively described in Sanskrit texts like Halasya Mahatmyam, Neepavana Mahatmyam, and in Tamil texts like Thiru Aalavaaiyudaiyar Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam, Kadambavana Puranam, Sundara Pandiyam, Paranjothiyar's Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam, and other prabandhams. Among the Thevaram-praised temples, 14 are in the Pandya country. Madurai is the foremost among them. There is no other place equal to this Madurai, also known as Thiru Aalavaai, no other sacred pond equal to the Potramarai Theertham, and no other Shiva Lingam equal to the one enshrined there, in all three worlds. In all worlds, sacred places are the most excellent. Among those sacred places, Shiva temples are the most excellent. Among those Shiva temples, 68 Shiva temples are most excellent. Among them, 16 Shiva temples are excellent. Those 16 Shiva temples are Kailash, Thirukkedaram, Srisailam, Gokarnam, Kasi, Thirukalathi, Thiru Alangadu, Kanchipuram, Thiruvannamalai, Virudhachalam, Chidambaram, Thiruvenkadu, Thiruvarur, Vedaranyam, Madurai, and Rameswaram. Among these 16 Shiva temples, four Shiva temples are most excellent. They are Chidambaram, Kasi, Thirukalathi, and Thiru Aalavaai. Among these four, Thiru Aalavaai, also known as Madurai, is the most excellent. Chidambaram grants liberation to those who visit it. Kasi grants liberation to those who reside there and die. Thirukalathi grants liberation to those who worship with love. But Thiru Aalavaai, also known as Madurai, grants enjoyment and the supreme abode of bliss merely upon hearing its name. Lord Shiva, the Supreme Being, first created this Thiru Aalavaai before creating all other places, and resided therein. Those who hear "Thiru Aalavaai" attain righteousness. Wealth will flourish. Those who think of "Thiru Aalavaai" attain prosperity. Those who behold the Lord of Gods, Thiru Aalavaai, attain happiness and well-being. And those who reside in Thiru Aalavaai always attain the supreme abode. This is from Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam. Shiva temples like Kasi, surrounded by the Ganges, grant liberation in the afterlife. Thiru Aalavaai, surrounded by the Vaigai River, grants jivanmukti (liberation while living) in this life and paramukti (supreme liberation) in the afterlife to those who reside there. Therefore, this Thiru Aalavaai is incomparable to itself. The benefits derived from performing many sacrifices like Ashwamedha, Vajapeya, Agnishtoma, Rajasuya in other places, the benefits from performing rituals like Darshapoornam, the benefits from giving donations of sesame, ghee, food, etc., the benefits from residing in Shiva temples like Kasi, and the benefits from performing penance by bathing in rivers like Ganges, Yamuna, Saraswati, Kaveri, Krishna, Tungabhadra, Pali, Thamirabarani, are easily attained by those who live in this Thiru Aalavaai. Sins like murder, theft, drinking alcohol, coveting another's wife, committed in other places, all depart from those who always live in Thiru Aalavaai. The benefit of observing Chandrayana Vratam monthly in other places is obtained by eating at night in this place. The benefit of a month-long fasting vow in other places is obtained by a one-day fasting vow in this place. The benefit of observing a four-month vow in other places is obtained by reaching this place and observing Ashtami Vratam. The benefit of observing a six-month vow in other places can be attained by observing Somavara Vratam in this place. The benefit of observing a year-long fasting vow in other places can be obtained by observing Shivaratri Vratam in this place. Even if ascetics who have conquered the five senses live in this Thiru Aalavaai, eating fully three times a day, it is equivalent to having performed great penance by subsisting on air. In this Thiru Aalavaai, whoever has given a handful of rice to Brahmins and a handful of food to ascetics will attain the benefit of having performed Shodasa Danam (sixteen types of charity) from other places. Those who always reside in Madurai attain the benefits of residing in various places, bathing in various sacred waters, performing various charities, performing various pujas, completing all kinds of penances, chanting various mantras, researching and understanding various scriptural texts, and practicing all kinds of yoga and gnana to realize the ultimate truth and subdue their ego. Therefore, there is no place equal to this place anywhere. Who else but Lord Shiva can fully comprehend the greatness of this place? Therefore, those who wish to conquer the two karmas should come and live in this place. Once upon a time, Lord Brahma placed all places, including Thirukailayam, on one pan of a scale, and this Madurai on the other pan. When he lifted both, he saw that Thiru Aalavaai, also known as Madurai, was much heavier, and thus confirmed that this is the most supreme place in all three worlds. Agastya says that great ones call this place by many names: Sivanagaram, Kadambavanam, Jivan Muktipuram, Kannipuram, Thiru Aalavaai, Madurai, Bhuloka Shivalokam, Samashti Vichapuram, Naanmadakkoodal, Dwadasanta Thalam. It is named Kadambavanam because this place was formerly a forest filled with Kadamba trees. Later, at the command of the Lord, Kulasekara Pandiyan cleared the forest and made it a country. As a symbol of its past as Kadamba Vanam, the Kadamba tree is the Sthala Vriksham (sacred tree) of this place. Hence, it is called Kadambavanam. It has another name, Aalavaai. To indicate the boundary of Madurai city to the king, the Lord took His serpent bracelet and let it go. It coiled in a circle, biting its own tail. That circular shape was taken as the boundary. Since the serpent showed the boundary by biting its tail, this place is called Aalavaai and Thiru Aalavaai. It has another name, Madurai. The Lord sanctified this place by sprinkling the nectar (madhu) from the crescent moon on His matted hair, hence it is called Madurai. It has another name, Naanmadakkoodal. To stop the seven clouds sent by Varuna to destroy Madurai, Lord Shiva released four clouds from His matted hair. These clouds rose like mountains and gathered as four mansions, protecting the city, hence it is called Naanmadakkoodal. This is Jivan Muktipuram. It is named so because it grants Jivan Mukti to those who reside here. It is also called Kannipuram, Kanni Puri, Kannika Kshetram, and Kanni Nadu. This is because the all-powerful Parasakti, as Thadathagai Pirattiyar, ruled here as a maiden. It is named Samashti Vichapuram. It is called Samashti Vichapuram because it bestows all kinds of knowledge, including Shiva Jnanam. Samashti Vichapuram means the complete city of Shiva Jnanam. It is named so because it bestows the four paths of Charya, Kriya, Yoga, and Jnana upon souls. Furthermore, in the form of Aruvuruv Linga, there are two types: Vyashti Lingam and Samashti Lingam. Among these, the Sokkanathar Peruman, the Sokkalinga idol here, is a Samashti Lingam, hence this place is called Samashti Vichapuram. It is named Dwadasanta Thalam. This place is called Dwadasanta Thalam because it is located 12 inches above the head of the Virat Purusha. Since it is a Niradhara Paravali Thalam beyond Adhara, it is also called Dwadasantapuram and Dwadasanta Thalam. It has another name, Sarva Theerthapuram. Goddess Uma established all 136 sacred waters of Thirukailayam as sacred waters here. Hence, it is called Sarva Theerthapuram. Besides these, since Lord Shiva Himself ruled as Sundara Pandiyan, it is also called Shivarajadhani, Shivanagaram, and Bhuloka Kailasam. This is the place where Sangam poets resided and researched Tamil. Therefore, Madurai also has names like Tamil Madurai, Tamil Koodal, Sangam Tamil Koodal, Muchchangam Valarkkoodal, Viri Tamil Koodal, Muthamizh Madurai. The sacred water of this place is Potramarai Theertham. This Potramarai Theertham is a sacred water created by Lord Somasundarar for His own ablutions even before rivers like Ganga and Kaveri, which are like kings among rivers, and oceans and clouds and other water bodies came into existence. Since it appeared before all other sacred waters, it is called Adhi Theertham. Since it shines above all other sacred waters, it is called Parama Theertham. Since it bestowed auspiciousness upon those who bathed in it, it is called Shiva Theertham. Since it bestowed knowledge, it is called Jnana Theertham, and since it bestows liberation, it is called Mukti Theertham. Lord Shiva Himself says that those who bathe in this sacred water, perform tarpana, give charity, chant the sacred five letters from its bank, lovingly approach, worship, circumambulate, and praise Him, will attain the benefit of having received human birth. Furthermore, the Lord Himself says that if we perform ablutions with this sacred water, we will attain the benefit of having performed ablutions with all the exalted sacred waters in all 14 worlds. Merely beholding the Potramarai Theertham, located inside the Meenakshi Amman Temple in Madurai, removes sins. Bathing in it fulfills all desired objects. The blessings attained by those who perform Jnana Snanam, Agneya Snanam, Mantra Snanam, Mrittika Snanam, Divya Snanam, Kapila Snanam, Vibhuti Snanam, Manasa Snanam, Vayu Snanam, Nirmalya Snanam, and other such purifying baths, are not comparable to the blessings attained by bathing in the Potramarai Theertham. Those who bathe in this Potramarai Theertham and worship Lord Somasundarar attain supreme liberation. Those who bathe in it without faith attain heaven. This is the glory of the sacred water of Madurai. First, we saw the glory of the place. Then we saw the glory of the sacred water. Now let us contemplate the glory of the Murthi, the Moolalinga Murthi. The glory of Lord Somasundarar, the Moolalinga Murthi, who resides and bestows grace in Thiru Aalavaai, also known as Madurai, is immeasurable. The Shiva Lingam appeared here under the Kadamba tree even before Shiva Linga Murthis appeared in places like Mount Meru, Thirukailayam, Mandara Mountain, Thirukkedaram, and Kasi. Only after this did all the Shiva Lingams in other places appear. Therefore, this Shiva Lingam, which is the form of the light of knowledge, is called Moolalingam. This can be understood from the Thevaram lines of Appar Peruman, "Mulaiththane Ellaarkkum Munne Thondri." The lines from Thiru Aalavaaiyudaiyar Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam, "Medhini Thondrum Munnan Sevviyan Mulaiththezhundha Divya Lingam," also convey the same. This Linga Thirumeni, endowed with such greatness, is a Shiva Linga Thirumeni that possesses the beautiful form of Jnana, with 32 auspicious marks, and always bestows grace with Goddess Uma. All the Devas named Him Somasundara Kadavul. Somasundara Lingam means a beautiful Shiva Lingam accompanied by Goddess Uma. This Somasundara Peruman pervaded the entire world. That is why when the Pandya king struck Him on the back with the golden cane in his hand, that mark appeared on all animate and inanimate objects in all three worlds. Those who have had the darshan of this Samashti form of Somasundara Kadavul have had the darshan of all the Vyashti forms of Shiva Linga Murthis in other sacred places. Lord Shiva, who is without beginning or end, who stands as the all-pervading fullness, and who bestows bondage and liberation upon souls, always remains manifest in this Sokkalingam, praised by the Vedas, in order to bestow the three – this world, the next world, and liberation – upon those who worship Him. Those who have had the darshan of this supreme Murthi are those who have had the darshan of the supreme light of Shiva that appears within when speech and mind subside. Here, the pure souls who regularly worship Lord Somasundarar with love four times a day are freed from the karmas of their previous births, attain purity, receive divine wealth and other riches, enjoy worldly pleasures, and then, freed from the impurity of anava, attain liberation. Hearing the sacred name of Somasundara Kadavul removes the karmas of ten births. Thinking of Him removes the great sins of a hundred births. If one rises and steps out of the house saying, "Let us now go and worship Lord Sokkalinga," the heinous sins of a thousand births are removed. Merely having the darshan of Somasundara Kadavul yields the benefit of having performed a thousand Ashwamedha Yagas. Those who praise and worship Him with Rudra Sukta mantras attain the benefit of having performed a thousand Rajasuyam Yagas. Offering one flower on the sacred head of Somasundara Kadavul yields the benefit of having worshipped with a hundred golden flowers in other places. Those who offer incense to Somasundara Kadavul once are freed from a thousand faults committed by mind, speech, and body. Those who offer sacred food (Thiru Amudu) to the Lord will enjoy a yuga of Shiva Loka life for each grain of rice in the offering. Those who offer betel leaves will live in Shiva Puram for a hundred crore divine years. Those who light camphor lamps attain Shiva Sarupyam (the form of Shiva). Those who worship Lord Sokkalinga once, either with Ashtanga (eight-limbed) or Panchanga (five-limbed) prostration, will live as kings wielding the scepter. Homam, penance, japa, charity, etc., performed in the presence of such a glorious Somasundara Kadavul, even if atomic in size, will be considered as vast as Mount Meru. Who can measure and describe all the glories of this Shiva Linga Murthi, who possesses such immense greatness? Sage Agastya is quoted in Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam as saying, "O sages, we have spoken a little of what we know." Thus, we have contemplated the glory of Thiru Aalavaai, the glory of Potramarai Theertham, and the glory of Lord Somasundarar, the Murthi. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 04 — Murthi Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 183, Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam, Murthi Nayanar Puranam. We are continuing to contemplate the Sthala Purana of Madurai, the sacred city where Murthi Nayanar was born. Lord Somasundarar, enshrined here, has many sacred names such as Karpoora Sundarar, Kadambavana Sundarar, Kalyana Sundarar, Abhirama Sundarar, Shanbhaga Sundarar, Maguda Sundarar, Kasturi Sundarar, Pazhiyanchiya Sundarar, Somasundarar, Aalavaai Sundarar, Naanmada Koodal Nayagar, Madhurapathi Vendhar, Samashti Vichapura Nadhar, Jivan Mukthipura Nadhar, Bhuloka Shivalokadhipar, Kannipuresar, Moolalinga Murthi. All these glories of the Sthala that we have seen are said to have been taught by Lord Muruga to Sage Agastya in the Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam. In inscriptions, the Lord is referred to by names such as Azhagiya Sokkanathar, Thiru Thiru Aalavaaiyudaiya Nayanar, Madurai Udaiyar, Athira Veesi Vilaiyaaduvar, Sokkanatha Eswara Udaiyar, Mahadevar Azhagiya Sokkanar, Sundara Pandiya Chozha Konar, Thiru Aalavaaiyudaiya Thambiranar. Here, the Goddess's name is Meenakshi, and in Tamil, Angayarkanni. The Goddess is also known by various names such as Thadathagai Piratti, Abhisheka Valli, Karpoora Valli, Maragatha Valli, Sundara Valli, Abhirama Valli, Kayarkan Kumari, Kumarithuraiyaval, Komagal, Pandiya Piratti, Manikka Valli, Madhurapuri Thalaivi, Mudumalai Thiru Vazhudhigal. In inscriptions, she is referred to as Thirukkamakkottathu Aaludaiya Naachiyar. This Sthalam is known as Velliambalam (Silver Hall) among the Pancha Sabhas (five halls) where the Lord performs His dance. The 64 Thiruvilaiyadals (divine sports) performed by the Lord here can be seen in detail in the Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam. There is also a temple here where Lord Somasundarar Himself, to make the world understand the glory of Shiva Puja and attain salvation, consecrated Himself as an Atmaartha Murthi and worshipped, known as "Immaiye Nanmai Tharuvar" (He who bestows good in this life). Other sacred waters in this place include Vaigai Theertham, Ezhu Kadal Theertham, Krithamalai Theertham, Kondali Theertham, and Theppakulam. The Madurai temple offers a unique opportunity to see together hundred-pillared halls, thousand-pillared halls, etc., which are not found in any other texts except Saiva Agamas, as well as the 25 forms of deities found in Shiva Agamas, their meditative forms, the five types of Nritta Murthis (dancing forms) and their postures, and divine images made in five materials: gold, silver, copper, stone, and stucco. Furthermore, one can observe the various types of Upapeetha structures like Padabandham, Padmabandham, Pratyantaram found in Saiva Agamas and Shilpa Shastras, and the diverse and artistic variations of Vimanas like Kailasam, Meru, Mandaram. Thus, the Madurai temple stands as an art gallery for the world. The Ganesha of this place is Siddhi Vinayagar. Murugan is Koodal Kumarar. The Vimana is Indra Vimana. Now let's look at the temple structure. The city of Madurai itself is shaped like a lotus flower. In its center is the Meenakshi Sundareswarar Temple, surrounded by beautifully laid out streets. Here, the Lord's Vimana is called Indra Vimana. This Indra Vimana was built and worshipped by Indra to be freed from his curse. The Madurai temple is located in the center of the city, facing east. There are four large Rajagopurams (temple towers) on all four sides of the temple. First, the East Gopuram. This is located directly opposite the Swami Sannidhi, in the eastern entrance area adjacent to the East Aadi Veedhi. Among the four gopurams, this is the oldest. It also has another name, Sundara Pandiyan Gopuram. Next, the South Gopuram. This is the tallest among the four gopurams. It has nine tiers and contains 1511 stucco sculptures depicting mythological stories. Next, the West Gopuram. Its height is 154 feet. It has 1124 stucco sculptures. Next, the North Gopuram. This also has nine tiers and is 152 feet high. Compared to the other gopurams, it has the fewest stucco sculptures, only 404. Besides these, there is a gopuram called Raya Gopuram. The gopurams of the Swami temple include Sannidhi Gopuram, Nayak Gopuram, Nadukkattu Gopuram, Palagai Gopuram, and Idabakkuri Gopuram. The Vimana of the Swami's sanctum sanctorum was built by Indra. Eight Dikgajas (elephants of the cardinal directions), two on each side, are depicted supporting this Vimana on all four sides of the sanctum sanctorum. Next are the Amman temple gopurams: Chithira Gopuram, Sannidhi Gopuram, Kadaga Gopuram, and the sanctum sanctorum Vimana. The Meenakshi Sundareswarar temple has three circumambulatory paths (prakarams). The first two circuits are inside the temple, separately for Sundareswarar and Meenakshi Amman. The third circumambulatory path is inside the compound wall. This is called Aadi Veedhi. The area outside the compound wall is called Chithirai Veedhi. The Amman temple has a direct entrance. Generally, it is customary in this temple to have darshan of Meenakshi Amman first, and then Lord Sundareswarar. We enter the temple through the Ashtashakti Mandapam in front of the Meenakshi Amman temple. It contains sculptures of eight Shaktis. On the south side are the forms of Kaumari, Raudri, Vaishnavi, and Mahalakshmi, and on the north side are the forms of Yagnaroopini, Shyamala, Maheswari, and Manonmani, each on separate pillars. The history of Sri Meenakshi Amman is depicted in paintings in this mandapam. To the right of the entrance next to this mandapam is Vallabhai Ganapathi, and to the left is Shanmugar. After worshipping them and entering, there are sculptures of a hunter on the north and a huntress on the south. Next to this is the Meenakshi Nayakkan Mandapam, built by Meenakshi Nayakkar, one of Thirumalai Nayakkar's ministers. On the ceiling of this mandapam, the 12 zodiac signs are arranged in a square shape. In the next gopuram entrance, large Thiruvasi lamps are lit daily. Passing this, one finds the Mudali Mandapam. In its northern row are sculptures of Bhikshadanar, Rishipattini, and Tharuka Munis, and in the southern row are sculptures of Munivar, Mohini, and Kadanthai Mudaliyar. Proceeding further inside, one sees the Potramarai Kulam (Golden Lotus Pond). This is the place where the Sangappalagai (Sangam board) appeared, which upheld the greatness of Thirukkural. This is also the place where Sri La Sri Gurugnanasambandar, the Adhi Gurumurthi of Dharmapuram Adheenam, received the Sokkalingam by the grace of Lord Shiva. To the west of the Potramarai Kulam, opposite the Sri Meenakshi Sannidhi, is the Kilikkattu Mandapam. It is also called Yali Mandapam or Sangili Mandapam. This mandapam houses the main entrance to the Meenakshi Amman Sannidhi. Here, the Siddhi Vinayagar Sannidhi, specific to this temple, is on the right, and the Kumarar Sannidhi is on the left. From here, there is the Nadukkattu Gopuram entrance leading to the Swami temple. The entrance to the Madappalli (kitchen) is located here. Near the Madappalli entrance is the flower-tying area. Next to it is the sandalwood grinding stone used by our Murthi Nayanar. From here, near the entrance to the Amman Sannidhi, are the Siddhi Vinayagar Sannidhi and Murugan's Sannidhi. One must go through the Vembathoorar Gopuram to enter the Amman temple from here. Entering through the Vembathoorar Gopuram from the Kilikkattu Mandapam leads to the second prakaram. In this prakaram are the Thirumalai Vinayagar Sannidhi, Thirumalai Subramaniar Sannidhi, Kodimaram (flagstaff), Thirumalai Nayakkar statue, Kolu Mandapam, Vinayagar Sannidhi, Amman temple West Gopuram, Koodalingar Sannidhi, Koodal Kumarar Sannidhi, and Aarukaal Peeda Mandapam. Directly opposite the Amman Sannidhi, the Kodimaram is located in this second prakaram. In most temples, the Balipitham (sacrificial altar) and Kodimaram are located in the same place. However, here the Kodikkambam is in the second prakaram, and the Balipitham is in the Kilikkootu Mandapam. At the entrance to the inner prakaram from this prakaram, there are two dwarapalika (female guardian) sculptures. Entering through the main hall of this second prakaram leads to the Mahamandapam. The first prakaram of the Amman temple begins and ends in this mandapam. To the west of the Mahamandapam are the Ardhamandapam and the sanctum sanctorum of Mother Meenakshi. In this prakaram, to the left of the Mahamandapam, there is a Vinayagar, and on the way to the sanctum sanctorum, there are two Vinayagar sculptures. In the southwest corner, there is a small double Vinayagar temple, and in the northwest corner, there is a Murugan temple. Furthermore, Chandikeswari is also enshrined in this prakaram. The Palliyarai (bedchamber) is also in this prakaram. The mandapam in front of the Moolasthanam (sanctum sanctorum) is the Ardhamandapam. This is where the priests performing puja chant Vedas and worship. From here, at the entrance to the Garbhagriham (sanctum sanctorum) of Meenakshi Amman, there are sculptures of Vinayagar and Murugan on both sides. There is the sanctum sanctorum mandapam. In this mandapam, during Deeparadhana (lamp offering) times, Odhuvar Murthis stand and sing Thevaram Thirumurai songs. Going further inside from here, there is the Moolasthanam, the Garbhagriham. There, the Moola Vigraham (main idol) of Meenakshi Ambika, the Moolasthana Murthi, is enshrined in an exquisite form, embodying compassion with a smiling face. The divine form of Meenakshi Amman, my mother, is seen here in a standing posture, holding a flower in her right hand and her left hand hanging down. A green parrot is perched on the Amman's right shoulder. The divine form of Ambika, made of green emerald stone, appears with a childlike face and eyes brimming with grace. The old Thiruvilaiyadal sings of the Amman's divine form: "Ponguru selvam kalvi porivilaa vaaimai thuymai ingu emakku arulavalla inaiyilaa empiraatti pangayaththadan sool koodal pavalamal varaiyai neengaa angayarkanni mangai adikkamalangal potri." Paranjothi Munivar's Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam praises Meenakshi Ambika as: "Surumbu mural kadimalar poonguzhal potri uththariya thodiththol potri karum puruvach chilai potri kavuniyarkku paal surandha kalasam potri irumbu manam kuzhaiththennai eduththaanda angayarkkan empiraatti arumbum ilanagai potri aarana noopuranjilambum adigal potri." As praised, we too praise and worship the Amman. Circumambulating her, in the Deva Koshtam, around the Amman's sanctum sanctorum, are enshrined the Ambikas of Iccha Shakti, Kriya Shakti, and Jnana Shakti. Then, Chandikeswari Devi is also enshrined. After having their darshan, to the right of the first prakaram is the Palliyarai (bedchamber). After this, coming out, one can reach the second prakaram of the Swami temple from the Amman temple's Kilikkootu Mandapam in the second circumambulatory path. In the southern part of this, near the Nadukkattu Gopuram, is the Mukkuruni Vinayagar temple, about eight feet tall. In the western part of the Swami temple's second prakaram, many Lingams are consecrated. In the northwest of this prakaram is the Sangappulavar temple. It contains the idol of Iraiyanar and the statues of 49 Sangam poets, starting with Nakkirar, the head of the Sangam. There are eight flagstaffs in this second prakaram alone. Apart from this second prakaram belonging to the Swami, this prakaram also contains the West Gopuram, Idabakkuri Gopuram, Mandapa Nayagam (a hundred-pillared mandapam), and the Gnanasambandar temple. Adjacent to the northern prakaram of the second prakaram is the Kalyana Sundareswarar temple. After that, the hundred-pillared mandapam is located in the northeast corner of the second prakaram. In the southeast corner is the Nalvar temple. In the eastern part of the second prakaram is the Kambathadi Mandapam. The pillars of this mandapam have sculptures of Lord Shiva's 25 forms, along with Agni Veerabhadrar, Aghora Veerabhadrar, Urdhva Thandavar, and Kali. From here, at the entrance to the Swami's first prakaram, on both sides are Anugnai Vinayagar, Pazhani Andavar, Thanigai Kumarar, and Swamimalai Kumarar. The Nandi Mandapam is located directly opposite the Swami Sannidhi, between the pillar sculptures of the Kambathadi Mandapam. In the center of this mandapam are the flagstaff and the Balipitham. Entering the first prakaram from the second prakaram, on the eastern side are a Lingam and the Sun with his consorts. Vandiyamai also appears. Passing them, if one comes to the southern side, one can see the 63 Nayanmars. Next to them are the divine forms of the Santhana Acharyas, Sahasralingam, Kalaivagal, and Saptamatar. In the southwest corner of the first prakaram is the Somasakandar Mandapam. It houses the Utsava Murthis (processional deities) of Meenakshi Sundareswarar. In its western row, the Utsava Murthi of Murugan, Shodasa Lingam, Murugan, Juradevar, Juralingam, Panchamukha Lingam, and Bhikshadanar are seen. In the right Arkka corner are the Kasi Viswanathar Sannidhi and Medha Dakshinamurthi Sannidhi. In the northern part of the first prakaram are the Ellaam Valla Siddhar temple and the Chandikeswarar temple. Near it is the Kadamba tree, the Sthala Vriksham of Madurai. Near this is the Kanaka Sabhai (Golden Hall). Next to this hall are the 51 Akshara Lingam and the Mahalakshmi Sannidhi. Next to Lakshmi are the Potpadiyan Sannidhi and Lord Shiva as Nataraja in dancing posture, with Sivakami Ambal nearby. This is called Rathina Sabhai (Jewel Hall). There is a small covered well here. Here are a stone Vanni tree and a Lingam. In the eastern part of the prakaram are the Sannidhis of Bhairavar, Bhuvalingam, and Chandra with his consorts Karthigai and Rohini Deviyar. From this first prakaram, we proceed towards the Swami's sanctum sanctorum. Here is the Aarukaal Peeda Mandapam. At its entrance, on both sides, are installed two dwarapalaka sculptures made of stucco. Next is the Mahamandapam. Here, the copper idols of the 63 Nayanmars are placed. Here, Velliambala Natarajar appears in a dancing posture with 10 arms. This is called Velliambalam among the five halls where Lord Shiva dances. Here, the Lord performs the Ananda Thandava (dance of bliss), unusually lifting His right leg and resting on His left leg. This uniqueness of the Lord dancing with a changed leg is exclusive to Madurai. Next is the Ardha Mandapam. This mandapam is the front hall connected to the Moolasthanam. Then comes the Moolasthanam. The Swami temple's Moolasthanam is situated with an Upapeetam. Lord Somasundarar, enshrined here as Sokkalinga Thirumeni, is the primeval Lingam, having appeared before all other Shiva Lingams in all other places. He shines as the Samashti Lingam. As Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam praises and glorifies Lord Sokkalinga: "Venrule pulangal aindhaar mey unar ullam thorum senrule amudham ootrum thiruvarul potri etru kunrule irundhu kaatchi koduththarul kolam potri manrule maazhi aadum maraichchilambadigal potri," we too worship Him. Coming out, in the Deva Koshtam, to the south is Dakshinamurthi, to the west is Lingodbhavar, and to the north is Durga. We can worship them and come out. Thus is the structure of the Meenakshi Sundareswarar temple. Madurai and this temple are places abundant in festivals. Madurai is foremost among the places where festivals occur in all 12 months of the year. Here, daily, the Thiruvandhal festival puja, Kaalasandhi, Thrikalasandhi, Uchchikalam, Saayaratchai, Ardha Jamam, and Palliyarai pujas are performed. Here, in the month of Chithirai, the Thirukalyana festival; in Vaigasi, the Vasanthotsavam; in Aani, the Oonjal Utsavam; in Aadi, the Mulaikkottu Utsavam; in Aavani, the Pittu festival; in Purattasi, the Navaratri festival; in Aippasi, the Kolattam festival and Pavithrotsavam; in Karthigai, the Deepa festival; in Margazhi, the Ennai Kappu festival, Ashtami Sapparam; in Thai, the Theppa festival; in Maasi, the Maasi Magam festival for one mandalam (48 days); and in Panguni, the Kodai Vasantha festival are celebrated. The number of saints in this place is immeasurable. The number of Tamil and Sanskrit texts singing about this place is also immeasurable. Thus, we have contemplated a little about the Sthala Purana and glories of Madurai. Contemplating the 64 Thiruvilaiyadals performed by Lord Somasundarar in Madurai, we conclude this Sthala Purana and will continue with the Purana of Murthi Nayanar. The 64 Thiruvilaiyadals performed by Lord Somasundarar in Madurai are: Curing Indra's curse, Curing the white elephant's curse, Discovering the sacred city, Incarnation of Thadathagai Pirattiyar, Marriage of Thadathagai Pirattiyar, Performing the sacred dance in Velliambalam, Feeding Kundodharan, Invoking the Annakkuzhi and Vaigai, Invoking the seven seas, Invoking Malayathuvajan, Incarnation of Ukkira Pandiyan, Giving spear, conch, and discus to Ukkira Pandiyan, Releasing the spear to dry up the sea, Casting net over Indra's crown, Striking Meru with a stick, Revealing the meaning of Vedas, Selling rubies, Drying up the sea released by Varuna, Becoming Naanmada Koodal, Becoming Ellaam Valla Siddhar, Giving sugarcane to a stone elephant, Shooting an elephant, Becoming an old young boy, Dancing with changed leg, Fearing sin, Removing great sin, Cutting off a limb, Shooting a serpent, Killing a magical cow, Showing the true form, Bestowing the ploughman's parrot, Selling bangles, Teaching Ashtama Siddhi, Imprinting the bull emblem, Setting up a water pandal, Performing alchemy, Defeating the Chola king in battle, Bestowing the ploughman's fort, Coming as a maternal uncle to arbitrate, Showing Shivlokam to Varaguna Pandiyan, Selling firewood, Giving a sacred letter, Placing a plank, Winning a music debate, Giving milk to piglings, Making piglings ministers, Teaching a crow, Granting liberation to a stork, Becoming Thiru Aalavaai, Shooting the beautiful arrow, Giving the Sangam plank, Giving a golden purse to Dharumi, Rescuing Keeran, Teaching grammar to Keeran, Resolving the dispute of the Sangam poets, Resolving Idaippadan's displeasure, Casting a net, Teaching Vaathavoor Adigal, Turning foxes into horses, Turning horses into foxes, Carrying earth, Curing the Pandya king's fever, Impaling the Jains, Invoking the Vanni tree, well, and Lingam. The primary meaning is that the texts are those that reveal the ultimate truth, the profound meaning that shines in Tamil texts. Here, "meymmai porul" refers to the state of certainty of truth, and "vaaimai semmai porul" within it refers to the yogic liberation attained through experiential realization of that certain truth. Next, taking "semmai porul" to mean "semporul" (ultimate truth), it signifies the truth that bestows liberation. The experiential state of liberation, which is the ultimate truth, is conveyed by Tamil Akapporul (love poetry) texts. This is clearly explained by the prefaces to Thirukkovaiyar and Iraiyanar Agapporul. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman sings, "Thiru Aalavaayil Iraivar, who bestows the truth and profound meaning that shines in the true philosophical Tamil texts." The Lord in Thiru Aalavaai explained the true nature of Tamil texts. This can be known from Iraiyanar Agapporul and histories like Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam's "Sangathar Kazhagam Theerthathu." "Semmai porulum tharuvaar" means that He bestows not only the knowledge of scriptures and worldly affairs, but also the subtle truth of Jnana Yoga, the experiential state of liberation, which is difficult for anyone else to convey through the path of Agamic texts. "Thannar Tamil alikkum thanpaandi naattanaai" is from Thiruvachagam, Thiru Ammanai. "Sangam" (academy) implies the giving of texts, and "bhavam theerppavar" (remover of sins) implies the giving of ultimate truth. "Iraiyanar, the remover of sins, was in the Sangam" means He presided as the head of the Sangam. Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 186, Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam, Murthi Nayanar Puranam. Thirumoola Deva Nayanar has stated what kind of harm will come if the hearts of Shiva's devotees are distressed. If he is to govern, Murthi Nayanar says that the sacred ash that shines on Lord Shiva's body must be his coronation anointing. This sacred ash is the one that bestows greatness upon all who wear it with devotion. The Nayanar says that he will accept such sacred ash as his anointing. "Abhishekam" means ritual bathing. It is customary to pour sacred water for coronation. Therefore, it means ritual bathing. It means ritual bathing performed for coronation. The mantra yogis had arranged pure water in golden kalasams and pots for the ritual bathing. But Murthiyar considered the application of sacred ash as his anointing, above that pure water. To bathe with sacred ash, to consider sacred ash as the anointing substance, means to apply sacred ash profusely. Because it purifies more than bathing in water, applying sacred ash is specially referred to as Bhasma Snanam (ash bath), Agneya Snanam (fire bath), bathing in ash, and bathing in fire, in Saiva tradition. Regarding this, elders say, "After bathing in water, bathe in sacred ash." The Nayanar says that he will accept this sacred ash as his anointing substance. Now, he says that he will accept the Rudraksha garland, which is Lord Shiva's sacred symbol, as his ornament. Rudraksha is said to have originated from the compassionate tears of Lord Rudra's eyes. Therefore, it is also called "kanmani" (eye-gem). Just as a woman wearing a thali (mangalsutra) is a symbol of her being married to a husband, so too a soul should wear Rudraksha as a symbol of its belonging to Lord Shiva, its soul's beloved, according to Saiva tradition and rules. It is also implied that people who do not wear Rudraksha are like women who do not know their husband. It is also known from the divine utterance "Poopatharkku kandiyinai koosi idum pulliyarai kaanpatharkku koosum aran kaiththu" that Lord Shiva Himself would hesitate to see people who do not wear Rudraksha. Murthi Nayanar says that sacred ash should be the anointing, the Rudraksha, which is Lord Shiva's symbol, should be the ornaments, and matted hair should be the crown. Matted hair is also called "Jatabharam." Its meaning is to bear the weight of matted hair. This matted hair is a special symbol unique to Lord Shiva. The Nayanar says that this matted hair should be the jeweled crown worn by kings. Murthiyar, who held onto the feet of his Lord, who protects the entire world, took the divine symbols of His sovereignty as the symbols of his own worldly kingship. Murthiyar says, "Maamudiye mudiyavathu" (The great matted hair itself is the crown). Therefore, it is implied that he was already a great ascetic with long matted hair. The three mentioned here – sacred ash, Rudraksha, and matted hair – are called Shiva Sadhanas (symbols of Shiva). Murthiyar, who previously wore these and shone as a great devotee of Shiva, even when he became a great emperor ruling the world, did not change but adopted these very same things as symbols for that as well. His devotion is worthy of worship. We must also consider the ideas that "those who have transcended both destruction and creation, who view gold and copper equally" and "those who worship the feet of the universal cause are worthy to rule all worlds." Hearing Murthi Nayanar's words, the ministers, who possessed immeasurable knowledge, firm intellect, scriptural wisdom, and growing truthfulness, bowed to Murthi Nayanar and said that what he had commanded was good. Then, to suit the mind of Murthi Nayanar, the king of ascetics, they performed all the rituals pertaining to his right to rule. Various auspicious musical instruments played. The sounds of people's blessings were heard everywhere. Amidst all these sounds, Murthi Nayanar, who had assumed kingship, went to the Thiru Aalavaai temple and bowed low to Lord Sokkalinga enshrined there. The first act Murthi Nayanar did after being crowned king was to go and worship Lord Aalavaai. This was due to the immense compassion the Lord had shown him earlier, and also to seek His divine grace to assist and guide his future reign. It is the duty of worldly people, who become deluded and arrogant even with a little wealth, forget Lord Shiva, and commit many sins, to understand this and attain salvation. Sekkizhar Peruman says, "Sendru thaazhndhu Thiru Aalavaai sendru thaazhndhu" (went and bowed, went to Thiru Aalavaai and bowed), referring to Murthi Nayanar going to the Thiru Aalavaai temple after his coronation. When describing his departure from the temple after worshipping the Lord there, Sekkizhar Peruman says, "Murthi Nayanar kalitrin misai ponthaar" (Murthi Nayanar went on an elephant). Therefore, it is understood that Murthi Nayanar walked without a vehicle when going from the coronation hall to worship at the Thiru Aalavaai temple, and after worshipping the Lord in the temple, he mounted an elephant and went on a procession through the streets. The rule that one should not go in a vehicle when going for darshan at Lord Shiva's temple is derived here. Thus, Murthi Nayanar departs from the Thiru Aalavaai temple, mounts an elephant, and circumambulates the sacred streets of Thiru Aalavaai. The sacred streets of Thiru Aalavaai are the sacred streets that extend due to the divine grace of Lord Sokkalinga, the sacred deity of Thiru Aalavaai, who performed many divine sports. It is said that there are no streets in this city of Madurai where Lord Shiva, who performed 64 divine sports, did not set His divine foot, to the extent that the sacred streets of Madurai are filled with divine grace. For some reason or another, the Lord performs divine sports in all the sacred streets here. Thus, Murthi Nayanar, who went on a procession through the sacred streets, goes to the royal palace. There, he reaches the hall where the royal court is held and sits on the throne, the royal seat supported by lions and adorned with gold. Fly-whisks are waved on both sides. He rules from that throne under the white royal umbrella. Ministers, who administer justice in various departments, act according to Murthi Nayanar's indications. The control of the rebellious Jains was removed. The rebellion caused by Jains in Madurai was not only their following their own religion but also their causing trouble to other religions. Such rebellion caused by them was stopped there, and all the troubles arising from it were removed. Just as a soul flourishes only when its bonds of attachment are removed, so too, when the Jain control that prevented Saivism from flourishing in Madurai was removed, all the souls living there turned towards Saivism. Sekkizhar Swamigal states, "Nilavum Thiruneetru Nerithurai Needu Vaazha Ulagengum Nirambiya Saivam Uyarndhu Manna" (May the path of sacred ash flourish forever, and may Saivism, which pervades the world, rise high and endure). The path of sacred ash is the path of Shiva. It is praised in various ways as the path of light, the great path, the sacred path, the path of grace, in Tamil scriptures like Thevaram and Thiruvasagam. The Pandya country became a place where such a path existed, and where activities like entering, staying, and residing in that path of sacred ash took place. Saivism, which pervaded the world, became firmly established. What does it mean that the state of Saivism pervades the entire world? It means that the symbols of Shiva Shakti become the entire world. The truth that the symbols of Shiva Shakti become the entire world is known from the Thirugnanasiddhiyar verse beginning "Sathiyum Shivamumaay thanmai ivvulagamellaam oththavvaa aanum pennum uyar guna guniyumaagi," and from the Thirugnanasiddhiyar verse "Yaathorutheivam kondir attheivamagi ange maathorubaganar thaam varuvar," and from Appar Swamigal's Thiruvaiaatru Thiruppathigam "Kaathan madappidiyodum kaliru varuvana kanden kanden avar thiruppatham kandariyathana kanden," and from the Thirunavukkarasar Puranam verse "Sathiyum Shivamumaam sarithai panmai yonigal yaavaiyum payilvana panindhe," and from the Vedas.
