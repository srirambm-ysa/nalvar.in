Thiruchitrambalam Periyapuranam Part 218 Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar Puranam. Sekkizhar Peruman, who sings about the sacred places located in the Neythal land of Thondai Nadu, has combined and sung about Mylapore and Thiruvanniyur in a single sacred verse. Thiruvanniyur is the 25th sacred place among the song-honored places of Thondai Nadu. Here too, Vanmeeganathar is enshrined facing west. Since the Devas churned the milk ocean and consecrated and worshipped a Shiva Lingam with the obtained Amrutham, the Lord here is also known by the name Marundeesar. The Moolalingam (main deity) of the Lord is a Swayambhu Lingam with streaks of white ash, enshrined by the name Paalvannanathar. The name of the Goddess here is Tripurasundari Sokkanayaki. The Sthala Vriksham (sacred tree) is the Vanni tree. The Theerthams (sacred waters) include Panchatheertham, Paapanaasini, etc. Those who worshipped here include Thirugnanasambandar Peruman, Appar Swamigal, Sekkizhar Peruman, Valmiki Muni, Agathiyar, Bringi Muni, Surya, the Vedas, Devas, Kamadhenu, and others. The Sthala Puranam states that five drops of water from Ganga fell from the head of Lord Shiva in five directions around the Thiruvanniyur temple, and these became the five Theerthams. These five Theerthams are Janmanasini, Kamanasini, Papanasini, Gnanadayini, and Mokshadayini. Besides these, Surya Theertham, Brahma Theertham, and Yama Theertham are also located here. A beautiful Thyagarajar shrine is located in the Thiruvanniyur temple. The Thiruvanniyur Sthala Puranam is in Sanskrit, and Sri Ashtavadanam Poovai Kalyanasundara Mudaliar has translated it into Tamil and composed it. This Sthala Puranam consists of 19 chapters and 1022 viruthams (verses). Valmiki Muni, who composed the Ramayana, came here and worshipped Lord Shiva as per the advice of Markandeya Muni. Lord Shiva showed him his dance form. Therefore, the Lord is known by the name Vanmeeganathar. Rakshasas worshipped the Lord here. Parthasarathy Peruman worshipped the Lord here. Surya worshipped here. Chandra worshipped here. Indra worshipped here. The Vedas worshipped the Lord here. Kamadhenu worshipped here and received blessings. Sri Rama worshipped here. On the fifth day of the Panguni festival, the event where Arulmigu Thyagarajar blessed Rama is celebrated as the fifth day procession. During the divine marriage of Parvati and Parameswara, when the North tilted and the South rose, Agathiya Mamuni, to balance it, came towards the South and worshipped the Lord in Thiruvanniyur. At that time, Lord Shiva taught Agathiyar about the types of naturally occurring medicinal plants in this world, their properties, which medicine is for which disease, and the greatness of medicines. Lord Shiva taught Agathiyar all these things. Humans have bodies according to the deeds they have done. The diseases that occur to them are 4448, and as a solution to these diseases, four types of plants – trees, shrubs, creepers, and grass – and their parts such as bark, root, leaf, fruit, seed, wood, and juice, and six tastes – astringent, pungent, bitter, sour, salty, and sweet – have been classified. Medicines can be made by combining these. A physician must possess honesty, purity of mind, compassion, good conduct, devotion to God, masculinity, and other qualities. A physician should be able to understand the condition of the pulse of the patients who come to them and diagnose the nature of the disease through the eight types of examinations mentioned in the texts. The physician should examine the patient's body condition, their diet, their age, their sleep, and then treat the patient for Vatham, Pitham, and Silettumam (three humors). The physician should speak kindly to the patient and provide appropriate medicines. These are the qualities of a good physician, Lord Shiva teaches Agastya about medicines here. Furthermore, diseases can be cured using three methods: Mani (gems), Mantram (incantations), and Aushadham (medicine). Mani refers to the root essence. Through the methods derived from it, one can travel anywhere. It helps in acquiring wealth like mountains and the eight great siddhis. 63 sub-essences like mercury are secondary to this root essence. These are not suitable for pills. They are suitable for medicines. Only sages know their properties and greatness. It is difficult for ordinary physicians to know them, Lord Shiva teaches Agastya Peruman all these things. Among mantras, the Panchaksharam, which includes Pati (Lord), Pasu (soul), and Pasam (bondage), is the most supreme. Seven crores of great mantras originated from it. If one thinks of or sees those who have thoroughly chanted this five-letter mantra, all troubles will disappear like dew before the sun. Earlier, when the milk ocean was churned, the nectar that appeared spilled on the shores. As a result, nine types of herbs sprouted. Medicines can be made from herbs and plants. We are giving you complete knowledge about these. You will discover new herbs with your intelligence, Lord Shiva teaches Agastya and bestows complete knowledge of medicines upon Agastya Muni in Thiruvanniyur. Therefore, Agastya Muni bowed down to Lord Shiva and prayed, "Oh Lord, you who have taught the world about the types of medicines through me, should be worshipped here by the name Marundeesar." Agastya Mamuni made this request to Lord Shiva. Furthermore, Agastya Muni requested the Lord that those who come to this place and worship you, Marundeesar, with sincere love, should live without disease. And the diseases they had should also disappear. Lord Shiva granted his wish. He also showed Agastya Muni his wedding form. Therefore, the Lord of Thiruvanniyur is known by the names Marundeesar and Aushadheeswarar. We all know the history of Bringi Muni. Bringi Muni would perform penance on Bringi Malai, located near Thiruvanniyur. In the evening, he would worship at the Thiruvanniyur temple. On the fourth day morning of the Panguni festival, during the Chandrasekarar Vimana festival, the event where the Lord showed Bringi Muni the right path is celebrated as a traditional festival. We say that Bringi Muni performed penance on the mountain near Thiruvanniyur, which was called Bringi Malai. That is now known as Parangi Malai. All these are found in the Thiruvanniyur Sthala Puranam. Now, we all know about Srimath Appayya Dikshitar very well. Chinnabomman, a king who ruled in Vellore, performed Kanakabhishekam for Appayya Dikshitar. The Vijayanagara kings and the Thanjavur Nayak kings supported Srimath Appayya Dikshitar. Appayya Dikshitar, a great scholar, stayed in a place called Vedachireni near Thiruvanniyur and performed yagnas. Every day, he walked from Vedachireni to Thiruvanniyur and worshipped Marundeeswarar. The place where Srimath Appayya Dikshitar stayed and performed yagnas daily, Vedachireni, is now known as Velachery. This is hearsay. Now, information about the temple festivals. Generally, in temples located along the coast, festivals are traditionally held based on the full moon day. It is very rare to hold festivals based on star calculations in such coastal temples. Thiruvanniyur, being a coastal temple, the festivals for Marundeesar and Thyagar are held on the full moon day according to Agamic rules, even though they should be held according to star calculations. During the Panguni festival, the full moon is considered the tenth day, and the Dhvajarohanam (flag hoisting) is performed accordingly. The Brahmotsavam in Panguni is a grand festival. On the seventh day, the chariot festival, on the ninth day, the Vanni tree service, on the tenth day, the divine dance performed by the Lord for Valmiki Muni, and on the eleventh day, the float festival take place. In the months of Chithirai, Vaikasi, Aani, and Aadi, the Nallvar Thirunakshatram and Adipatta Nayanar Utsavam are celebrated. On Aadi Pooram, Tripurasundari Ambal comes in procession on a Kamadhenu vehicle. In Avani, Vinayagar Chaturthi, in Purattasi, Navaratri, on Aippasi Pournami, Annabhishekam for Marundeeswarar, Kandashashti, Karthigai Deepam, and Arudra Darisanam are all celebrated grandly. On Karthigai Somavaram, 108 Sangabhishekam is performed. These are all the special features and Sthala Purana stories of Thiruvanniyur. Sekkizhar Peruman, who has thus sung about the Neythal land, now sings about the Thinaimayakkam (mixing of landscapes) in the Puranam of Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar. First, Neythal and Kurinji are mixed. Thinaimayakkam means regions where two types of landscapes are intertwined. For example, Kurinji is a mountain and its surrounding areas, and Neythal is the sea and its surrounding areas. When Neythal and Kurinji are mixed in Thinaimayakkam, it means places where mountains and their surrounding areas, and the sea and its surrounding areas, are intertwined. It is our tradition to call such places Thinaimayakkam. Sekkizhar Peruman, who described the five Thinais – Kurinji, Paalai, Mullai, Marutham, and Neythal – and their respective Mudal Porul (primary elements) and Karupporul (secondary elements), and the glories of the Shiva temples located in them, now begins to describe the richness of the regions where these five Thinais are intertwined. In Thinaimayakkam, "mayanguthal" means to join or mix. Scholars say that there are six types of this. Kurinji mixes separately with Neythal, Marutham, and Mullai, making three. Then Mullai mixes separately with Neythal and Marutham, making two. Then Marutham mixes with Neythal, making one, totaling six types. First, Sekkizhar Peruman sings about the Thinaimayakkam where Neythal and Kurinji are combined. In Thondai Nadu, the places where Kurinji (mountainous region) and Neythal (coastal region) are combined are called Kadal Mallapuram, which is known as Mamallapuram. It is also known as Kadal Mallai, Mamallapuram, and Mahabalipuram. Vaishnavites also consider Mahabalipuram as one of their sacred places. Mahabalipuram is one of the 108 Divya Desams praised by the Alvars. In Mahabalipuram, Lord Shiva is enshrined in a sanctum made of sandalwood. The temples here and the five chariots belonging to the Shiva temple were carved out of individual rocks on the hillside. Ordinary people refer to these five chariots as Panchapandavar Ther. Since it is a mountain on the coast, Sekkizhar Peruman sings about it as a land where Neythal and Kurinji are intertwined. Next, Sekkizhar Peruman sings about the land where Marutham and Kurinji are combined, and its richness. Next, he sings about the richness of the regions where Mullai and Kurinji are combined, then the glories and richness of the regions where Neythal and Mullai are combined, then the glories of the land where Marutham and Mullai are combined, and then the richness of the regions where Neythal and Marutham are combined. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman sings about the glories, richness, and greatness of Thondai Nadu. From the 1083rd verse to the 1124th verse, Sekkizhar Peruman, with his divine poetic skill, describes the four lands and five Thinaigal of Thondai Nadu in great detail, showing the boundaries of Tamil grammar and following the path of righteousness. Madhava Sivagnana Yogigal, in his Kanchipuranam, in the Thirunattu Padalam, praises Sekkizhar Peruman's divine poetic quality, saying, "Who else but the poet of Kundrai, surrounded by Madhava Sivagnana Yogigal, can describe the richness of the four lands and five Thinaigal of Thondai Nadu, and how can devotees like us understand and describe it?" Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman, who sang about the richness of Thondai Nadu in the Puranam of Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar, now begins to sing about the glories of Kanchipuram, especially as a city. Sekkizhar Peruman, who sang about all the glories of Thondai Nadu, now sings in great detail about the glories of Kanchipuram and the greatness of the Goddess's penance there. Sivakavimani Ayya mentions the following as the special features of Kanchipuram: The Mother, who is the mother of all worlds, worshipped the Lord in Kanchipuram, following the Agamic path, which she received from the Lord, for the salvation of the world. She came from Mount Kailash to Kanchipuram, found and worshipped a Shiva Lingam that miraculously sprouted under a mango tree on the banks of the Kampa river, and demonstrated Shiva Agamic worship for all living beings in the world. Afterwards, she is enshrined in Thirukamakottam, nurturing righteousness and protecting all living beings. Such is the glorious place, Kanchipuram. When the Mother was worshipping in Kanchipuram, the Kampa river swelled and overflowed. Because the Mother, trembling, embraced the Lord, the temple here came to be known as Ekambaram. "Kampam" means trembling. As the Mother embraced the Lord tightly with her bangled hands, the Lord's body softened, showing the marks of her bangles and breast, and he is enshrined here as Thazhuvak Kuzhaintha Nathar (the Lord who softened when embraced). Ekambaram is also the name given to this temple. Ekambaram can be separated as "Ekam Amram," where "Ekam" means one and "Amram" means mango tree, thus meaning "the one with a single mango tree." This name is given because the Lord is seated under a mango tree. Kanchipuram is the foremost among the seven places of liberation (Mukti Sthalams). Kanchipuram is the first of the five elemental shrines (Pancha Bhoota Sthalams), representing the element of Earth (Prithvi). In Kanchipuram, there are countless temples such as Thirukamakottam, Thirukumarakkottam, Kachchapesam, etc. In Thiruvekambam, Vikata Chakra Vinayagar is prominently enshrined in front of the thousand-pillared hall at the southern entrance. Here, the Lord's name is Thiru Ekambar, and the Goddess's name is Elavaar Kuzhali Kamakshi Ambal. The Sthala Vinayagar is Vikata Chakra Vinayagar, Murugar is Mavadi Kumara Nayagar, the Sthala Vriksham is the mango tree, and the Theerthams are Sivagangai, Kampa Nadi, etc. The elephant, rescued from the grip of a crocodile, became a mountain called Athigiri, on which Lord Vishnu is enshrined as Varadarajar in Kanchipuram. Kanchipuram is the place where Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, who lost both his eyes due to breaking a vow in Thiruvottiyur, was granted his left eye. Kanchipuram is the place where our Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar attained the feet of the Lord whom he served. Kanchipuram is also the place where Sakkiya Nayanar and Aiyadigal Kadavarkon Nayanar received blessings. The Linga Murtis named Vellai Kambam, Kallak Kambam, and Nallak Kambam, worshipped by Brahma, Vishnu, and Rudra respectively, are enshrined for darshan in Ekambaram. The Thalam called Thirukachi Mayanam, where Lord Shiva performed a sacrifice using the Devas as fuel during the time of deluge, is located inside Thiruvekambam, in front of the flagstaff. Thirukachi Mayanam is a Thevara Vaippu Thalam (a place mentioned in Thevaram hymns). Thirukachi Metrali, where Vishnu, who performed penance to attain Shiva Sarupam, melted upon hearing Thirugnanasambandar Peruman's hymn and became a Shiva Lingam; Thiruvonakanthan Thali, worshipped by the Asuras Onan and Kanthan; Thirukachi Anekathangavatham, worshipped by Vinayagar, Kubera, etc.; and Thirukachi Nerikaraikkadu, worshipped by Indra, Budhan, etc. - these are the four Thevaram song-honored places located within the city limits of Kanchipuram. Among the 32 Thevaram song-honored places in Thondai Nadu, five are located within the city limits of Kanchipuram. The place where Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar received blessings is now called Mukteeswarar Temple. These are all special notes about the place written by Sivakavimani Ayya. Now, according to our tradition, we must contemplate the glories and special features of Kanchipuram. The Sthala Puranam of Kanchipuram is the Kanchipuranam, composed by Sivagnana Yogigal of Thirukailaya Paramparai Thiruvavaduthurai Adheenam, who is the author of Dravida Mahabhashyam. Kanchipuranam is a very extensive Sthala Puranam, encompassing Vedic concepts, Upanishadic concepts, Agamic concepts, and Thirumurai concepts. In Kanchipuranam, Sivagnana Yogigal has described all the Shiva temples located in Kanchipuram and their glories. Kanchipuranam is structured as Suta Mahamuni narrating the greatness of Kanchipuram to other munis in Naimisaranyam. Suta Mamuni teaches the other munis. The greatness of Kanchipuram is found in the Theertha Manmiyam of the Kalika Khanda, in the Sanatkumara Samhita, which is one of the six Samhitas in the Skanda Mahapurana, one of the 18 Puranas composed by Vyasa Muni, an incarnation of Vishnu. Suta Mahamuni states that Sanatkumara Muni heard this greatness of Kanchipuram from Thirunandi Devar, and Sanatkumara Muni taught it to Vyasa Muni, who then taught it to him. Once, on Mount Kailash, Parvati Devi, the Mother of the World, worshipped the feet of Lord Shiva and asked the following: "Oh Lord, the bliss of liberation is obtained by the darshan of your sacred feet. Only wise sages who have researched and understood the essence of the Vedas, and who have matured through penance, meditation, and samadhi, can attain the darshan of your sacred feet. It is difficult for others to attain the darshan of your sacred feet. So, how will other ignorant beings, animals, birds, and other living beings in this world attain the bliss of liberation by seeing you?" Parvati Devi, the Mother of the World, asked this question out of great compassion for living beings and made this request to Lord Shiva. Hearing this, Lord Shiva, filled with compassion for all living beings to survive and prosper, spoke with his divine mouth: "Oh Uma, we reside in a state of transcendence. We are pervading everywhere in the world. But no one will know us in that form. We will live in the supreme places of unchanging glory, visible to all. In all the supreme places on this earth, you and I will live, visible to all," the Lord spoke with his divine mouth. Hearing this, Uma Devi asked the Lord which those places were. Lord Shiva then listed the places where he lives with Uma Devi: Sethu, Madurai, Thiruchirappalli, Thiruvazhuvur, Thiruvaiyaru, Thiruvaidaimarudur, Thillai, Virudhachalam, Annamalai, Thiruvinjai, Thiruvothur, Kanchi, Thiruvalangadu, Kalahasti, Srisailam, Sithavadam, Virupaksham, Thriyambakam, Thiru Gokarnam, Ujjayini, Mahakalam, Kasi, Kedaram, Prabhasam, Himalayas, Mandaram, Kailash, and countless other places where we reside, the Lord said. Hearing this, Parvati Devi asked, "Oh Lord, which is the best among all these places?" The Lord replied that the holy city of Kanchi is the best among all places. He said that even thinking of that city of Kanchi, or hearing its name spoken by others, or speaking it oneself, or bowing towards its direction, will grant everyone the supreme bliss of liberation. The Lord told Parvati Devi that he and she would live happily in that place forever. Kanchipuram is superior to Kasi, and just as he resides in Kasi by the name Viswanathan, in the city of Kanchi, under a mango tree, he performs the five divine functions of creation, preservation, destruction, concealment, and grace, and resides as a Lingam by the name Ekambanathan, the Lord told the Goddess. If one comes to Kanchipuram and stays there for at least one day, one can attain liberation. Even if one passes through Kanchipuram on a journey to other places, or stays there for a moment out of desire for wealth, they will attain liberation. It is true that if one utters "Kanchi" even once by its very nature, all five great sins will be removed, and one will attain the supreme state. The Lord said that he created the city of Kanchi to destroy Kali. In Tamil, when counting the letters of a word, it is customary to exclude the consonant-vowel combinations. Accordingly, if we remove the consonant-vowel combinations 'm' and 'n' from the word 'Ekambanathan', we get five letters. Therefore, the Lord says it becomes a Panchaksharam. This sacred mantra, Ekambanathan, is to be taught only to those who have studied and understood all the texts that explain the four types of benefits: dharma, artha, kama, and moksha. The Lord states that even those who utter this mantra, even in times of distress or by mistake, will become dear to him. Hearing this, the Devas request Lord Shiva, "Oh Lord, please explain to us, your humble servants, the glory of this Kanchi Sthalam and the glory of the mango tree where you are enshrined." Since I, who am Brahma, have been embraced (anjikkappattathal), meaning attained, this city will achieve unchanging glory. Because it was embraced by 'Ka,' which is Brahma, it is named Kanchi. Wise people refer to the navel of women as Kanchi. Kanchipuram is the navel of Mother Earth, who is clothed by the ocean. Therefore, it is also called Kanchi. Furthermore, 'Ka' signifies head, bliss, and Brahma Devan, among other things. Kailash mountain is called "Velli maal varai Kailai" (great silver mountain Kailash). Kailash mountain is the most excellent among the high peaks of the great silver mountain. It is also known as "Nodiththan Malai." "Nodiththal" means to destroy. "Nodiththan" means one who destroys. "Oozhithorum oozhi mutrum uyar pon nodiththan malai" (The high golden mountain that destroys Yuga after Yuga) is a Thevaram by Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. We have seen the glories of Thirukailash mountain in the "Thirumalai Sarkam" in the section "Thirumalai Sirappu." Lord Shiva is seated on such a glorious Thirukailash mountain. He is seated with Parvati Devi. At that time, Umaiyammaiyaar, who rules the world, worships Lord Shiva and asks him. Sekkizhar Peruman refers to her as "Ulagai aludaiyaal" (one who rules the world), which indicates that Uma Devi asked for the benefit of all living beings in the world. Umaiyammaiyaar asks Lord Shiva, worshipping him, to graciously reveal all the clear truths of the Agamas for the salvation of all living beings. Here, Sekkizhar Peruman has sung "Thellu vaaimaiyin agamat thiranellam" (all the clear truths of the Agamas). Sivakavimani Ayya writes the following commentary on this: The Veda says, "Isana Sarva Vidyanam" (Isana is the Lord of all knowledge). Accordingly, Isana is the chief of all arts. That Lord has revealed countless Shastras according to the different levels of maturity of living beings. Among all those Shastras, the Vedas and Agamas are the most supreme. Gnana Shastras say that there are two types of texts: Vedic texts and Saivite texts, and that the Veda and Agama are the true texts of the Lord. Among these two texts, the Veda is a general text. It speaks of various things like "Annam Brahma," "Vishnu Brahma," etc., according to the capacity and maturity of living beings. The Agama, however, without being general, clearly states the ultimate truth that Shiva is the supreme God for those who have attained Shakti Nipada (descent of divine grace). Therefore, what the Veda generally calls Brahma, the Agama specifically calls Shiva. "Nool unarnthor" (those who know the texts) say that the clear truths are taught to those who have attained Shakti Nipada by the Agama, as mentioned in Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam. Kumaraguruparar Swamigal praises this beautifully, saying, "Only a few have tasted the honey-nectar of Saiva Siddhanta, which is the essence squeezed from the ripe fruit of endless bliss at the peak of Vedanta." Thus, there are 28 Agamas that teach clear truths. Among them, eight appeared from the Isana face of Maha Sadashiva Murthy, and 20 from the other four faces like Tatpurusha, five from each, totaling 28 Agamas. Among these 28 Agamas, 10 are Shiva Bhedam and 18 are Rudra Bhedam. It is said that 30 persons, including Pranavavar, who are the leaders among the Anu Sadashivas, heard the ten Shiva Bheda Agamas, three persons from each. And 36 persons, starting from Anadi Rudra, heard the 18 Rudra Bheda Agamas, two persons from each Agama. "Anjana meni arivaiyor bhagaththan anjodu 23la agamam anjali kooppi 66varum anju mugaththil arumporul kettathe" (The 66, with folded hands, heard the precious meaning of the 23 Agamas, along with the five, from the five faces of the Lord with the dark body and the wise Goddess in one part) is the 57th verse in the Payiram of Thirumandiram by Thirumooladevar Nayanar. "66var Pranavaraathi arivura kettar agamam" (The 66, starting with Pranavavar, heard the Agama with wisdom) is the 337th verse of Saiva Samaya Neri. Thus, Sivakavimani Ayya writes a commentary on "Thellu vaaimaiyin agamat thiranellam" with various textual examples and references. We will contemplate this in more detail. Thiruchitrambalam.
