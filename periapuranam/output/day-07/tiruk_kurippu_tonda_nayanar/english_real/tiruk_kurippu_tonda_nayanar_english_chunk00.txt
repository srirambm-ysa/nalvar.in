Chapter 01 — Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 209. Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam. Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar Puranam. Following the Thirunaalai Povaar Nayanar Puranam in the Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam, the Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar Puranam is placed. First, we will reflect on the brief history of the Nayanar, and then we can delve into the detailed Puranam of the Nayanar. The country where Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar was born is Thondai Nadu. This land is renowned for its greatness, eminence, and antiquity. It is the land that attained the blessing of Uma Devi performing penance to embrace her Lord. Thondai Nadu is a place where great families, excelling in righteous conduct and impartiality, flourish. Sekkizhar Peruman has extensively sung about Thondai Nadu, the birthplace of Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar, in the Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar Puranam. Sekkizhar Peruman sings about the various landscapes within Thondai Nadu, namely Kurinji, Mullai, Marudham, Neidhal, and Paalai, and also about the Shiva temples located in these regions that are glorified in Thevaram hymns. He then describes the mixing of these landscapes (thinai mayakkam). Following this, he extols the greatness of Kanchipuram, the city where the Nayanar was born. He also elaborates on the history of Ammaiyaar (Goddess Parashakti) performing penance there, specifically detailing the story of Kamakshi Ammai, the all-powerful Parashakti, coming here to perform penance and the Kachi Thirukkamak Kottam. Subsequently, he describes the prosperity of the city. After singing about all these aspects, Sekkizhar Peruman, having extensively narrated the Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar Puranam, then begins the Nayanar's story. Our Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar was born in Kanchipuram, in the Ekaaliyar (washerman) community. He possessed a loving heart, adherence to righteous principles, and a hereditary devotion to Shiva. From the day he was born, the Nayanar dedicated his mind, speech, and body to the sacred feet of Lord Shiva. Due to his nature of understanding and honoring the intentions of Shiva's devotees, the Nayanar came to be known as Thiruk Kurippu Tondar. The Nayanar lived in the great city of Kanchi, born into the washerman community, washing clothes for the people, and was excellent in his truthfulness. He had a heart that would melt at the mere mention of Lord Shiva's name. With great eagerness, he would wash the clothes of devotees whose hearts melted at the mere mention of Lord Shiva's name. He would remove the impurities from the clothes of Shiva's devotees, just as he was removing the impurities of his own three-fold birth (mummalap piravi). One day, Lord Shiva, intending to bestow grace upon the Nayanar, decided to test his sincere devotion. In a very cold season, Lord Shiva appeared before Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar in the guise of a Shiva devotee, wearing a very old, dirty, and tattered cloth. Upon seeing the Shiva devotee, the Nayanar was overjoyed and welcomed him with reverence. Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar, understanding the intention of the visiting Shiva devotee, asked, "Oh Lord, your body seems weary. Please grant me this tattered cloth you are wearing so I may wash it." He humbly requested, "If you give me the garment you are wearing, I will clean and wash it for you." The Shiva devotee who had come replied, "This tattered cloth is so dirty that it can no longer be worn. Moreover, the cold is very severe now.

Therefore, if I give you this garment, I will not be able to withstand the cold, so I will not give it to you." He then added, "If you can wash and dry this cloth and return it by this evening, then take it and quickly wash it." Hearing this, Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar said, "Swami, as you have commanded, I will return it before evening." Hearing this, the Shiva devotee who had come said, "If you do not quickly take it, wash it, dry it, and bring it back by evening, you will be harming this body," and he handed the tattered cloth to Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar. Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar took the garment to wash it properly in the water, intending to return it at the appointed time. As he was washing and drying it, the Lord had a divine intention. At that moment, by the grace of the Lord, rain began to fall incessantly. The rain poured heavily without even a moment's pause. Therefore, the Nayanar was unable to dry it. But he had promised the ascetic that he would wash and dry this garment and return it by this evening. The rain was falling without stopping. "What shall I do?" he wondered. He waited for a while, thinking that the incessant rain might stop, but the rain continued to fall without ceasing. Therefore, Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar was deeply distressed, thinking, "My service to the devotee has been ruined! The rain is falling incessantly! Even if I were to take the washed cloth home to dry it in the shade, the rain is not allowing even that!" He was deeply saddened. Not only that, but he also thought, "The body of that great ascetic, soaked in this rain, will become very cold, and that cold will harm his body, causing him suffering. I have become the cause of his suffering!" He was filled with great sorrow. Therefore, he resolved to strike his head against the washing stone, lamenting, "I have caused harm to the body of Lord Shiva's devotee, making his body shiver!" He prepared to strike his head against the stone where clothes were washed. Just as his head was about to strike the rock, Lord Shiva's bangle-adorned hand, the lotus-like red hand of Ekambara Nayagar, who resides in Kanchi, reached out and held the Nayanar's head. It held the Nayanar's head so that it could not strike the rock. The rain that had been falling until then ceased, and a shower of flowers fell. Ekambaranathar, along with his consort Kamakshi Ammaiyar, appeared on his Rishabha (bull) vehicle and bestowed his grace upon the Nayanar. Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar stood with folded hands, worshipping the Lord. Then, the Lord addressed Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar, saying, "We have made known your steadfast devotion to all three worlds. You shall now live in our eternal Shiva Loka, never to be separated from us." With these words of grace, he vanished into Thiruekambaram. This is the brief history of Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar. Now, according to our tradition, we shall continue to reflect extensively on the sacred history of Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar, following the Thondathogai (summary work), Thiruthondar Thiru Andhadhi (type work), and Sekkizhar Peruman's Periya Puranam (expanded work). Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 02 — Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 210. Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam. Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar Puranam. In the Periya Puranam, also known as Thiruthondar Puranam, the Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar Puranam is placed after the Thirunaalai Povaar Nayanar Puranam. This section describes the life history and character of the Nayanar named Thiruk Kurippu Tondar. In the Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam, following the Puranams of Murthi Nayanar, Muruga Nayanar, Rudra Pasupathi Nayanar, and Thirunaalai Povaar Nayanar, the Puranam of Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar is the fifth in sequence. Sekkizhar Peruman has composed this Puranam with 128 verses. First, we can begin to reflect extensively on the Nayanar's history according to the order of the summary work, then the type work, and then Sekkizhar Peruman's expanded work. Firstly, in the Thiruthondathogai composed by Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, Swamigal has sung, "Thiruk Kurippu Tondar tam adiyaarkkum adiyen" (I am a servant to the servants of Thiruk Kurippu Tondar). The meaning of this is that "I am a servant to the devotees of Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar." The Nayanar came to be known by this name because he understood the intentions of Lord Shiva's devotees and served them according to their wishes. For this reason, it is also understood that Lord Shiva came to him with a dirty garment, and the Nayanar, understanding the devotee's intention, requested and received the garment to wash and clean it for him. Now, in the type work, Thiruthondar Thiru Andhadhi, Nambi Andar Nambi composes the following verse about the Nayanar: "Mandhum punal sadaiyaan thamar thoo chetri vaattum vagai vindu mazhai mugil veedaadhu ozhiyin yaan veevan ennaa mindum padar paarai muttum ezhilaar Thiruk Kurippu Tondar kulam Kachi Ekaaliyar thangal thol kulame" (If the clouds, which are like the water-laden matted locks of Shiva, do not cease their tormenting rain, I shall die, said the beautiful Thiruk Kurippu Tondar, who would strike the spreading rock; his lineage is the ancient lineage of the Ekaaliyars of Kanchi). Now, let us reflect on the sacred history of the Nayanar through Sekkizhar Peruman's Periya Puranam, the expanded work. The tradition that Sekkizhar Peruman adopted in the Nayanar's histories is to describe the country and town they lived in, the rivers of that country, its prosperity, the prosperity of the town, the temples located there, the Lord enshrined there, and also the Nayanar's lineage, family, and tradition, and then to sing about the Nayanar's sacred service. That is the tradition Sekkizhar Peruman adopted. He has composed this Puranam according to that tradition. The country where the Nayanar was born is known as Thondai Nadu. This Thondai Nadu is a country that has performed a greater penance than any other country. Therefore, it is a country of immense glory. What was that penance? The penance performed by that good Thondai Nadu was the great penance where Uma Devi, the mother who bestows boundless compassion on all beings, performed severe penance towards Lord Shiva, standing in the path of the Agamas, praised by the four Vedas, to embrace her Lord. This is the good Thondai Nadu. In Kanchipuram, located in this Thondai Nadu, our Mother Parashakti, with the names Elavaar Kuzhali and Kamakshi, performed penance towards the Lord. She performed worship according to the Agamic tradition. Therefore, Thondai Nadu possesses such greatness. Hence, it is called Thondai Nannadu (good Thondai Nadu). Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman introduces Thondai Nadu in the first verse. The verse is as follows: "Eyum maaru pal uyirkalukku ellaiyil karunai Thaayaan thaniyaayina thalaivarai thazhuva Aayu naanmarai potra nindru arunthavam puriya Thooya maathavan seythathu Thondai nannadu." This is that sacred verse. Sekkizhar Peruman begins to sing, "Eyum maaru pal uyirkalum" (as it suits all beings).

The meaning of this is that the compassion of our Goddess, though boundless, is bestowed upon each being in different ways, according to their karma and maturity. Parashakti, the one who bestows compassion on all beings according to their karma, is therefore the Mother of Compassion. While other mothers in the world give bodies to souls and nurture them, that body becomes the root cause of the soul's ailments, leading to further physical burdens. However, Parashakti, the mother of all, gives bodies to beings to remove the impurities of their ailments, nurtures them through their karmas, removes those karmas, and ensures that no further physical burdens befall them. Therefore, she becomes the unique leader who bestows compassion on all beings as it suits them. Thus, she becomes the unique mother of compassion. Such Uma Devi, Parashakti, performed penance here for her unique Lord. "Thani" means unique or singular. Furthermore, since Parashakti Ammaiyar came to Kanchipuram, separated from the Lord to perform penance, the meaning that the Lord became "thani" (alone) also applies here. Thus, Thondai Nadu is the country where Ammaiyar performed penance to embrace her unique Lord, Lord Shiva, who was separated from her. The city where Ammaiyar performed penance is the great city of Kanchi. Therefore, this country performed great penance. When Ambika requested to perform worship according to the Agamic tradition, the Lord revealed that Kanchi was the suitable place for it. Thondai Nadu has earned the merit of having Kanchipuram within it. If a country has earned merit, it means that great Shiva dharma flourished there, bringing well-being and prosperity to its inhabitants. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman has described the penance performed by Thondai Nannadu, categorizing it differently from Kurinji to Neidhal, and presented it in this Puranam. The Goddess, who performed such penance, performs the penance of Shiva Puja here. Sekkizhar Peruman sings about it as "Aayum naanmarai potra nindru" (standing praised by the discerning four Vedas). "Aaythal" means to discern or clarify. The Vedas, having discerned the characteristics of the Lord, held Lord Shiva as the supreme reality in their hearts. Thus, the Mother, who bestows compassion on all beings, performs Shiva Puja here to show beings the path to worship and attain that supreme reality held within the hearts of the Vedas. The Vedas stand praising this. The penance and worship performed by Parashakti Ammaiyar to embrace the Lord are extensively explained in this history from verse 1135 to verse 1142. Thondai Nadu possesses such greatness, penance, and merit. Sekkizhar Peruman praises it as Thondai Nannadu. "Nannadu" (good country) implies that the goodness lies in the people living there without deviating from righteous conduct. Sekkizhar Peruman elaborates on this in the subsequent verses. Now, in the next five verses, he sings about the greatness of Thondai Nadu, the Nayanar's country. Then, from verse 1083 to 1126, he describes the nature and prosperity of Thondai Nadu. By doing so, he explains that Thondai Nadu encompasses all four land regions and mixed land regions, and that all of them bestow the great life of Shiva's merit, as explained by our teacher Sekkizhar Peruman. Beyond that, he describes the glory and prosperity of Kanchipuram, the Nayanar's city, from 1127 to 1189, as a description of the city. After that, from 1190 to 1207, he narrates the history of Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar. This is the structure of this Puranam. Therefore, although this Puranam, the Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar Puranam, consists of 128 verses, Sekkizhar Peruman has described the glories of Thondai Nadu, the Shiva temples located there, the greatness of Kanchipuram, the penance performed by Ammaiyar, and other historical events in 110 verses, and then narrated the sacred history of the Nayanar in the subsequent 17 verses. In Periya Puranam, it is Sekkizhar Peruman's tradition to describe the glories, prosperity, and reasons for Shiva's merit of a country in great detail and depth when he first sings about it. Therefore, this Nayanar Puranam is the first place in Periya Puranam where Thondai Nadu is mentioned. Hence, he describes Thondai Nadu in such detail here. The description of the country and city is more extensive than the length of Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar's history. Sekkizhar Peruman was born in Thondai Nadu. Kunnathur, where Sekkizhar Peruman was born, is located in Thondai Nadu. Therefore, some say that Sekkizhar Peruman sang in this manner due to his love for his own country. While that is partly true, during Sekkizhar Peruman's time, Thondai Nadu was the country of the Cholas who commissioned the Thiruthondar Puranam. Kanchipuram flourished as a Chola city. Moreover, if we compare the descriptions of Chola Nadu and Pandya Nadu in Periya Puranam, the extent of description given to Thondai Nadu here is relatively small. Then why did he describe Thondai Nadu so extensively in this history? The ultimate purpose of Periya Puranam, the Thiruthondar Puranam, is the sacred form of Lord Shiva's devotees and the Shiva Puja they performed. This is the place where Ammaiyar, the ruler of the world, demonstrates Shiva Puja according to Agamic rules. Therefore, it was essential for Sekkizhar Peruman to describe the greatness of Thondai Nadu in great detail. Thus, to illustrate the Shiva Puja performed by Ambika, the descriptions of the country's glory and prosperity, meaning the glories of Thondai Nadu, were all related to Lord Shiva, and therefore, it became necessary to sing about them extensively. Therefore, the prosperity of the four land regions of Kurinji, Mullai, Marudham, and Neidhal, which are parts of Thondai Nadu where Ammaiyar performed penance, and the prosperity of the land regions where these parts are mixed, and the necessity to categorize them according to Tamil grammatical rules, and how all these regions are associated with Lord Shiva, and how the prosperity of this country is all related to Lord Shiva, and how it was a country rich in Shiva's merit and Shiva dharma – to explain all these, Sekkizhar Peruman sang in this manner. The nature of this epic, Periya Puranam, is to exemplify the rules of Tamil literature by singing about the five landscapes (thinai) of Kurinji, Mullai, Marudham, and Neidhal, and their intermingling (thinai mayakkam). Sekkizhar Peruman has composed it in this Puranam as an example of that rule. Because, while other countries sung in Periya Puranam have parts of these four landscapes, Thondai Nadu is the country that possesses all five thinai regions and their mixed thinai regions. Therefore, Thondai Nadu is worthy of being an example of Tamil literary rules. For these reasons, too, we must understand that Sekkizhar Peruman has sung so extensively. Moreover, how a country, city, and people should be, and why a country, city, and people should be respected, is exemplified here and should be contemplated. Furthermore, in the Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar Puranam, Sekkizhar Peruman's skill in presenting the essence of Agamas to inspire Shiva Puja in the first section with five-lined Kalinilai Thurai, and then the section describing the worship of the Ashtamurthi with eight qualities using eight-lined Viruthams, and the history of removing the impurities of birth by following the four-footed path of Chariyaadi with four-lined Kochaga Kalippa, should also be noted. We will continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 03 — Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 211. Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam. Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar Puranam. Thondai Nadu is a land of boundless and ancient glories. It is a country with many prosperous cities possessing strong, great walls. The prosperity of a country is not about new wealth that comes and goes, but rather the true prosperity that has endured from ancient times without diminishing. Many such prosperous cities are located in this country. In this Thondai Nadu, citizens of excellent conduct and noble character reside. "Excellent conduct and noble character" refers to truthfulness and the quality of never intentionally harming others. The citizens of Thondai Nadu live without deviating from impartial conduct. What is impartial conduct? It is to act without considering "this person is mine" or "this person is a stranger," or "this is good" or "this is bad," but rather to adhere to righteousness, standing impartially like the beam of a balance scale. To illustrate that the citizens of Thondai Nadu live without deviating from impartiality, Sekkizhar Peruman cites an ancient story prevalent in Thondai Nadu and incorporates it into his verses. The story of Pazhaiyanur Neeli is known to everyone. Specifically, the story of Pazhaiyanur Neeli has been passed down through generations among the people of Thondai Nadu, especially in the northern districts of Tamil Nadu. In the story of Pazhaiyanur Neeli, there is an account of how the Vellalars acted impartially and upheld justice. The Vellalars of Pazhaiyanur, who desired overt goodness, were courageous enough to sacrifice their lives for justice when blame fell upon them, and indeed they did. Sekkizhar Peruman sings that Thondai Nadu is a country of such Vellalars who sacrificed their lives to uphold their word. This story of Pazhaiyanur Neeli is still prevalent among the people of the northern districts of Tamil Nadu as an example of how the Vellalars kept their word. The story of Pazhaiyanur Neeli is as follows: A long time ago, a merchant lived with his wife. However, the merchant had an affair with a courtesan. So, his wife tried to reform her husband. At that time, the merchant, her husband, took his wife aside and killed her. The blame for killing his wife, for killing a woman, became a demon named Neeli. This demon Neeli pursued the merchant to kill him. To escape from this demon Neeli, he went to a sorcerer and asked for a way out. The sorcerer gave the merchant a magical sword. He said, "Wherever you go, carry this magical sword with you. As long as this magical sword is with you, the demon Neeli will not be able to harm you." So, he always kept the magical sword with him. One day, he came to a village called Pazhaiyanur. At that time, the demon Neeli took the form of a woman. She broke a dry stick, made it into the shape of a child, carried it on her hip, and followed the merchant. In Pazhaiyanur, there was an assembly of 70 Vellalar elders. The merchant, knowing that the demon Neeli, in the form of a woman with a baby, was following him, sought refuge before the assembly of 70 Vellalars. He sought refuge, asking them to protect him from that woman. Neeli, the woman, also came before their assembly and cried out, "This is my husband, and this is our child. He has abandoned us, falling for courtesans, and has run away. So, you must reunite us and help us live together." The demon in the form of a woman thus pleaded. But the merchant denied the woman's claim, saying, "This is not a woman, but a demon named Neeli," to the Vellalar elders. At that moment, Neeli let go of the child she had on her hip, and it ran to the merchant, calling "Father." Therefore, the 70 Vellalar elders could not understand what the truth was. So, they said, "We will give our verdict tomorrow." Therefore, the assembly members told them, "Stay here together tonight." But the merchant had the magical sword in his hand, didn't he? So, the demon Neeli, in the form of a woman, said, "He has a sword in his hand. If we stay together, he will cut us. So, take the sword from his hand and make him stay here without the sword." But the merchant said, "If I don't have that sword, the demon will kill me." There was also the child between them. So, the deceived Vellalars took the sword from his hand and asked the merchant to stay with her without the sword. Furthermore, the 70 Vellalars also promised, "If any harm comes to his life, we, all 70 of us, will give our lives in exchange." So, as they requested, Neeli stayed with the merchant that night. At that time, the demon Neeli tore the merchant apart, killed him, and avenged herself, then left. Knowing this, the Vellalars were deeply saddened. Hadn't they promised to give their lives if any harm came to the merchant? Therefore, they now weighed their lives and the promise they had made, considering the promise they had given to be superior to their lives. So, the 70 Vellalars lit a fire, plunged into it, and attained the sacred feet of Lord Shiva at the Thiruvallangadu Shiva temple. Thus, Pazhaiyanur was the village where 70 Vellalars lived, who, without deviating from impartiality, sacrificed their lives for their word. It is located in Thondai Mandalam. Pazhaiyanur is near Thiruvallangadu. This is the story of Pazhaiyanur Neeli. This very ancient and true history has been sung by Thirugnanasambandar Peruman in the Thiruvallangadu Thevaram. Thirugnanasambandar Peruman sang, "Munai natpaai vanchap padutthu orutthi vaazhnaal kollum vagai kettu anjum Pazhaiyanur" (Pazhaiyanur, which fears hearing how a woman, pretending to be a friend, deceitfully took a life). Umapathi Sivachariyar, who sang the history of Sekkizhar Peruman, has narrated this story of Pazhaiyanur Neeli in the Thiruthondar Purana Varalaru, singing about the 70 Vellalars who, without failing in their word, sacrificed their lives by plunging into fire, their sacrifice, and how they attained the sacred feet of Lord Shiva. The pond where the Vellalars plunged into fire is still known as Neeli Kulam (Neeli Pond) there. On the bank of that pond, there is a temple with idols of the 70 Vellalars and Neeli. Both the temple and the pond are located in Pazhaiyanur. Pazhaiyanur is near Thiruvallangadu. The descendants of the Vellalar community still serve as excellent devotees, bearing the sacred feet of Thiruvallangadu Appar. The fragrance of the truthfulness of these Vellalars still permeates this Thondai Nadu. The Vellalars there desired the well-being of the merchant and his wife, wishing them to live together in harmony. Moreover, they always desired good. These 70 Vellalars made themselves devotees of God's dharma and truth, and unitedly sacrificed their lives. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman refers to them as Siruthondar (small devotees) in this verse. When the action they intended for good ended in evil, they considered that the blame for the merchant's loss of life, due to his adherence to their word, fell upon them. That fault came to them unknowingly. Therefore, they weighed their lives, their word, their promise, and their truthfulness, and considering which was superior or which was worth protecting, they chose their word. They had said that if any harm came to the merchant's life, they would give their 70 lives in exchange. They considered that word to be superior to their own lives and protected it without faltering, descending into the pit of fire and sacrificing their lives. This is their special quality. Pazhaiyanur was the village where such great people, who did not deviate from justice, lived. Thondai Nadu is the country that encompasses it. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman praises the greatness of Thondai Nadu, filled with such noble people. Furthermore, Sekkizhar Peruman sings that the people of Chera Nadu claim kinship with the people of Thondai Nadu, calling them "maithunar" (brother-in-law). Thondai Nadu possesses such greatness. Chera Nadu is the present-day Malai Nadu, Kerala. Sekkizhar Peruman, when singing about Chera Nadu, sings about Cheraman Peruman Nayanar. Cheraman Peruman Nayanar was one who revered the sacred ash, considering it the very form of Lord Shiva's grace. One day, while he was riding an elephant, a washerman came towards him. He was carrying alkaline earth on his head. At that moment, it rained. The alkaline earth on the washerman's head dissolved and spread all over his body, making him appear white. Cheraman Peruman Nayanar, who was riding the elephant, saw the washerman covered in alkaline earth. This reminded him of sacred ash. So, he dismounted from the elephant and worshipped the washerman. The washerman replied, "I am Adiyen Adiyannan (your servant, the washerman)." Hearing this, Cheraman Peruman Nayanar, even after realizing that the white substance on his body was not sacred ash but alkaline earth, still remembered the sacred ash form when he saw the washerman's appearance. So, he said to the washerman, "Adiyen Adichcheran (I am your servant, Cheran). You reminded me of the sacred ash form. Go without worry." He sent him off. The country ruled by such a great Cheraman Peruman was Malai Nadu. Sekkizhar Peruman calls this Malai Nadu "Thirumalai Nadu" here. "Thirumalai" refers to Mount Kailash. By the command of Lord Shiva, the Lord of Thirumalai (Mount Kailash), Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, the Lord of Souls, ascended Mount Kailash on a white elephant from this Malai Nadu. Following him, Cheraman also rode a horse and reached Thirumalai. Therefore, this Malai Nadu is connected to Thirumalai, Kailash. Hence, Sekkizhar Peruman also refers to it as Thirumalai Nadu. The warriors of this Chera Nadu maintained a special relationship of kinship with Thondai Nadu. There is also a history for this. Once, a rebellion broke out in Chera Nadu. So, the people of that country scattered in many directions. Among them, a woman from a merchant family sought refuge in a Vellalar's house in Thondai Nannadu. Those Vellalars raised that woman with love, as if she were their own daughter. After the rebellion in Chera Nadu subsided, her relatives came looking for her and wished to take her back. At that time, the Vellalars, who had raised her as their own daughter, said that she was indeed their daughter, and they sent her off with dowry and arranged her marriage. Therefore, it is a tradition for the people of Chera Nadu to celebrate kinship with the people of Thondai Nadu. A verse in Thondai Mandalam Sathagam also explains this: "Koththalar Kodhai viyan Chera mandala kombai thampaal Vaitthirundhu aangu aval than kelir nedi vara avarkke Uyththiruvorkkum varisaiyum maatru udanum sendru Maithunak kenmai padaiththathu andro Thondai Mandalam" (Did not Thondai Mandalam, by keeping the beautiful woman from the great Chera Mandalam with them, and when her relatives came searching, send her to them with honors for both, and thus establish kinship?). There is also another history: Karikala Chola, the king who improved Kanchipuram and ruled in Thondai Mandalam, married women from the Chera lineage for his son and grandson. Because of this relationship, the Chera warriors did not fight against Karikala Chola's warriors but stood by them, defeating other kings and celebrating their kinship. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman sings that Thondai Nadu is a country that celebrates kinship with such a great Chera Nadu. We will continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 04 — Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 212. Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam. Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar Puranam. Thondai Nadu is renowned throughout the world for its adherence to the principles of Shiva Dharma. Sekkizhar Peruman now begins to sing about the general characteristics of the four landscapes of this Thondai Nadu. Until now, in the first five verses, Sekkizhar Peruman has praised the nature of Thondai Nadu, highlighting its ancient historical glories. Now, he will describe the fivefold and fourfold land divisions of Thondai Nadu in a general way in five verses. In Tamil tradition, it is customary to divide the lands of this world into five categories (thinai). This has been defined since Tholkappiyam. Tamils divided this world into four land regions. They divided it into five thinai. Kurinji is mountains and mountainous regions, Mullai is forests and forest regions, Marudham is cultivated fields and their surrounding areas, and Neidhal is the sea and coastal regions. Thus, Tamils divided this world into four land regions. The world is characterized by these four divisions. Therefore, the world is referred to as "Naanilam" in Tamil. Besides these, there is a fifth land, Paalai, which arises from the natural transformation of Kurinji and Mullai. Tamils have included this as the fifth, forming five thinai: Kurinji thinai, Mullai thinai, Marudham thinai, Neidhal thinai, and Paalai thinai. Here, Thondai Nadu is characterized by these five types of land regions. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman first describes the fivefold and fourfold land divisions in a general way in five verses. After that, he describes each of the five thinai separately with their specific characteristics. From 1088 to 1091, he describes Kurinji thinai in four verses, from 1092, Paalai thinai, from 1093 to 1096, Mullai thinai in four verses, from 1097 to 1109, Marudham thinai in 13 verses, and from 1110 to 1117, Neidhal thinai in eight verses. After this, he describes the mixing of these thinai (thinai mayakkam). He describes the combination of Neidhal and Kurinji in verse 1118, Marudham and Kurinji in verse 1119, and Mullai and Kurinji in verse 1120. He describes the mixing of Neidhal and Mullai in verse 1121, and Marudham and Mullai in verse 1122. He describes the combination of Neidhal and Marudham in verse 1123.
