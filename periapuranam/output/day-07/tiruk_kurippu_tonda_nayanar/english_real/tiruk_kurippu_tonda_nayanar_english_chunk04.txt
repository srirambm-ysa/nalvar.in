Chapter 20 — Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 228. Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam, Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar Puranam. In Kanchipuram, the Lord manifested as a Lingam under a mango tree, and the Goddess bowed down to worship Him. Sekkizhar Peruman composed the verse: "Seeing the Lord, the fruit of great penance, who resided in Ekambam, the Goddess, with hair adorned with buzzing bees falling forward as she bowed, her desire surging with love, resolved to perform a specific worship without limit. The fair-mouthed Uma Devi, with lips like a kovai fruit, began her pure worship." The beautiful-mouthed Goddess, whose lips resemble the kovai fruit, thought in her mind that the Lord resides in Ekambam as a result of her great penance. She praised Him with her words and bowed down to Him with a Panchanga Namaskaram. With her hair, bound and buzzing with bees, falling forward, the Goddess performed the Panchanga Namaskaram there. We can see here the sight of her hair falling forward as she bowed, with five limbs touching the ground, as is customary for women. Now, the next verse: "To the worship of the Lord of the heavens, our Empress, understanding the truth of the Agamas He Himself proclaimed, prepared to offer worship. Her maidens, like tender creepers, brought baskets of flowers. She gathered pure, fragrant, fresh flowers from the Ambika Vanam, the sacred garden, avoiding the tender sprouts at their feet." This is the next verse. The Empress has come here to perform Shiva Puja. She advises her maidens to bring the necessary materials for Shiva Puja, understanding the truth of the Agamas that Lord Shiva Himself taught her, and according to those Shiva Agamas. The Goddess herself established a garden there for Shiva Puja. Therefore, that garden is named Ambika Vanam. It is an excellent rule that those who perform Shiva Puja should establish their own gardens for the flowers required for their worship and gather the appropriate flowers themselves. Thus, the garden established by Ambika came to be known as Ambika Vanam. Furthermore, it received the name Ambika Vanam because it had the great fortune of having its flowers plucked by Ambika's own hands. Now, in the next verse, Sekkizhar Peruman sings about how Ambika's maidens assisted her in Shiva Puja. "The Empress, who gave birth to all beings, performed the Shiva Puja according to all the Agamic rites, with the maidens providing all that was needed at the right time: the many flowers gathered, the sacred water from the Kamba River, the fragrant anointing paste, the ghee-laden incense and lamps, and abundant love in her full heart." This is that verse. Ambika's maidens bring the many flowers they have gathered for Ambika to perform Shiva Puja. They bring water from the Kamba River for the sacred ablution. The Kamba River is a river that flows in Kanchipuram. The Kanchi Puranam states that it now flows underground. It is a general Shiva Agamic principle that river water is excellent for sacred ablutions. Now, the maidens bring the ghee-laden incense and lamps to Ambika. Ghee-laden incense is an incense smoke item made by adding ghee to frankincense and other ingredients. We have discussed the method of offering incense according to the Agamas in the Puranam of Kungiliyak Kalaya Nayanar. Thus, Ambika's maidens assist her by bringing the flowers, materials for sacred ablution, materials for anointing Lord Shiva, incense, and lamps required for Ambika's Shiva Puja. It is noteworthy that these five – flowers, sacred water, anointing paste, incense, and lamps – are indispensable for Shiva Puja. With all these, Ambika performs Shiva Puja according to the Agamas. The Kanchi Puranam elaborates on how Ambika performed Shiva Puja there according to the Agamas, as follows: Ambika, about to begin Shiva Puja, instructs her maidens to keep providing her with the necessary items for the puja. As soon as they heard the divine words of the Goddess, her maidens were overjoyed. They fell at the lotus feet of the Goddess, bowed down, and then said to the Goddess, "Please tell us individually the services we need to perform." Thus, the maidens of the Goddess stood requesting her. Then the Goddess said to her maidens as follows: "Maidens, I must bathe and perform my daily rituals without fail. Therefore, create a well suitable for that. Create a well for the anointing of our Lord Shiva, Thiruneelakanta Peruman." Thus, the Goddess said to some of her maidens. And to some other maidens, the Goddess said, "Cultivate gardens on the banks of the Vegavathi River. The banks of the Vegavathi River will be filled with flowers, green leaves, and fruits. Cultivate gardens with many trees like Mayilai, Vilvam, Pala, and so on." Thus she said. And to some other maidens, she said, "Bring fragrant sandalwood paste, kalabak kasturi, green camphor, saffron, and other fragrant substances, divine gem ornaments, priceless and invaluable parivattam, and so on." Thus, the Goddess said to some. And to some other maidens, she said, "Bring Panchagavya along with Panchamirtham." And to some other maidens, she said, "Decorate the temples and mansions of Kanchipuram everywhere with navamanis, beautiful flowers, and embroidered silk cloths." Thus, the Empress explained to her maidens individually how to make arrangements for Shiva Puja. As soon as the Empress commanded, all the maidens fell at the feet of the Goddess, worshipped, and rose, saying, "We will do it first, we will do it first," each striving in their respective tasks with great love, surpassing one another in speed. Among the maidens of Uma Devi, Umapathrai and Keerthimathi, two maidens, created two wells, one in the north and one in the south, and filled them with sacred waters like Ganga and Kalindhi. Some other maidens, to prepare sacred food for Ekambaranathar, who has matted locks, created a well in the Agni corner and established beautiful kitchens. Some maidens, to adorn Ekambaranathar's matted locks, cultivated gardens called Ambika Vanam on the banks of the Vegavathi River, west of Kanchi city. That Ambika Vanam shone as a glorious garden, pure and worthy of worship by many. Some other maidens arranged a wondrous canopy. That canopy was adorned with long-leaved plantain trees, areca nut trees with fruits that ripened red like fire, and red sugarcane that yields jaggery, tied to each pillar. They hung radiant gem garlands and strung fragrant flower garlands in rows on that canopy.

Some other maidens applied fragrant civet, added pollen powder, sprinkled scented rosewater, raised fragrant agarwood smoke, and crushed gold into powder, dissolved it, and painted all the walls of the beautiful mansion golden. Some other maidens prepared various offerings: fragrant rice mixed with spices, plain rice, sweet pongal, curd rice, and other delicious rice dishes, as well as fried curries seasoned to release their aroma. These offerings, which included four types of food – those that are sucked, licked, bitten, and swallowed – were prepared with six tastes: bitter, pungent, salty, astringent, sour, and sweet. Some maidens gathered honey-laden flowers and wove them into various garlands like Chuttu, Kanni, Indai, Thongal, and placed them in golden baskets, sprinkling rosewater and adding fragrant smoke. Some, chanting divine mantras, filled golden pots with water, tied their mouths with golden ribbons, and poured the purified water, drawn from the surging waves, into many large vessels. Some maidens, on golden plates, purified puja vessels like handbells and arranged them. Some maidens carefully selected all the sacred leaves and flowers, ensuring they were free from worms and insects, and mixed fragrant cardamom and other spices into the sacred water, purifying it with joyful hearts. Some placed clusters of hanging jackfruits, ripe bananas, mangoes, oranges, pomegranates, fragrant lemons, coconuts, and areca nuts there. Some maidens collected cow's milk in many golden pots. Similarly, they brought countless pots of ghee, honey, sugarcane juice, curd, and divine nectar. Some brought beautifully embroidered silk garments, white silk garments from various islands, green silk garments, red silk garments shining like the Indragopa insect, golden silk garments, blue silk garments, and many silk garments from the Pancha Tarukkal. Some brought navaratna crowns, ear ornaments, golden ornaments, headbands, shoulder ornaments that dispel darkness, rings, bracelets, neck chains, and necklaces, all bestowed by the Kalpaka tree. They brought pots filled with a fragrant sandalwood paste, into which civet was squeezed, powdered green camphor was added, and saffron was mixed, all blended and strained. Some brought mirrors, white whisks, moon-shaped umbrellas, fans, sacred ash, camphor, incense, golden gem pots, plates, and many other items for the puja. Some maidens commanded the celestial damsels to play their melodious veenas, to play various musical instruments, and to dance in accordance with the songs, instructing both gods and celestial damsels. Thus, the maidens of the Empress were preparing all the materials required for the Empress's Shiva Puja. Now, Kamakshi Devi, the mother of all living beings, prepares to perform Shiva Puja. We will continue to contemplate this. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 21 — Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 229. Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam, Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar Puranam. All the maidens of the Empress have prepared all the materials required for the Shiva Puja that the Empress is performing, as we have seen. Now, the Empress prepares to perform the puja. Sekkizhar Peruman explains this in the following verse: "Understanding that this is the fruit of having hands, with a heart filled with constant love for the Lord of the heavens residing in Ekambam, the mountain daughter, the bestower of boons, whose peerless love blossomed, bowed her head in worship. Her daily puja grew in acceptance by the Lord's divine will." This is the verse composed by Sekkizhar Peruman. Its meaning is as follows: Uma Devi, the mountain daughter, the bestower of boons to beings, performs Shiva Puja. Understanding that the benefit of having hands is to perform Shiva Puja, Ambika, whose heart is filled with great love for Lord Shiva, the God of gods residing in Ekambam, performs Shiva Puja there. As a result, all good things flourished. The puja performed by Ambika, the puja performed with her head bowed in great love, grew in acceptance by Lord Shiva's divine will day by day. This is the explicit meaning of this verse. Now, let us see the commentary. Ambika performs Shiva Puja, understanding that "this is the fruit of having hands." Why did the Lord give hands to living beings? It is so that all living beings may attain Him by performing Shiva Puja. Lord Shiva gave hands to living beings, especially to humans, so that they may perform Karanyasam and perform Shiva Puja to the Lord with their hands. Thirunavukkarasar Peruman sings, "The Lord who gave a mouth to praise, a mind to think, and a head to bow." The Lord created mouths for living beings to praise Lord Shiva. He created minds to think of Lord Shiva. He gave heads to living beings to bow down to Lord Shiva. Thirunavukkarasar Peruman has blessed us with a separate Thiruppathikam called Thiruvangamalai. In it, starting with "Kankal Kanminoh," he has illustrated how each part of the body should be used to worship Lord Shiva. In it, he has blessed us with the divine words, "Join hands and worship, offering great flowers; join hands and worship." Thirunavukkarasar Peruman has said that one should take many flowers with one's hands and offer them as worship in Shiva Puja. Furthermore, he emphasizes the necessity of performing Shiva Puja with hands by saying, "The hand that does not seek and worship is a stone embedded in the earth." In the 42nd verse of Ponvanna Thanthathi by Kalaritrarivar Nayanar, the objective of the Shiva Puja performed by the Mother is for all living beings to attain good and liberation. That is why Sekkizhar Peruman has described the Mother as "Ulagai Aludaiyal," "Pennin Nallaval," "Bhogamartha Poonmulaiyinal," "Ulagu Anaithaiyum Eendraal," and "Man Ulagathu Uyir Thazhaippa Uyirkal Yavaiyum Eendra Empiratti." We can recall all these verses here. The essence of Ambika's Shiva Puja is that living beings should attain Lord Shiva and experience bliss. Ambika possesses Maya Shakti. Therefore, using her Maya Shakti, she provides suitable bodies and senses to living beings, enabling them to know and worship the Lord, and thus all living beings experience bliss with Lord Shiva. The Mother and other living beings are the Nayakis (heroines) who experience the Lord. The Lord Himself is the Anma Nayakan (soul-hero). It is the Mother who enables all living beings, as Nayakis, to experience bliss. This is its meaning. Now, Sri Madhava Sivagnana Swamigal describes the Shiva Puja performed by Kamakshi Devi in the Kanchi Puranam as follows: We have seen in detail how Ambika's maidens made all the necessary arrangements for Ambika's Shiva Puja. With the materials for Shiva Puja and other arrangements ready, Parashakti, the ocean of great compassion, prepares to perform Shiva Puja there. We saw that there is a well created by Umapathrai, one of the Empress's maidens. Now, the Empress goes to that well called Umapathrai. The Empress meditates on the lotus feet of Parameswara Murthy, who shines as the boundless, complete, blissful light. Meditating on the Lord, she bathes in that well, strictly adhering to the Vedic and Agamic rules. Then, she dries her divine body with the cloth given by her maidens, removing the moisture from her body. Then, she wears two embroidered silk garments and then smears sacred ash all over her body. She applies sacred ash as a Tripundram on her forehead. She lovingly wears a Rudraksha garland and completes her daily rituals. With an overflowing desire to worship her Lord, and with her maidens carrying the necessary items and walking around her on both sides, the Empress prepares for Shiva Puja. Everyone, knowing that the Empress is ready to perform Shiva Puja, praises the Goddess and the Lord. Conches, trumpets, drums, ekkalams, thiruchinnams, veenas, flutes, musical pipes, perigais, murasams, and thindimams resound everywhere. Kinnaras, Garudas, and Kimpurushas sing hymns. Celestial maidens and Rudra Kannikas dance. Sages and Brahmins chant the Vedas. The elephant-faced Ganesha Murthy, who holds the five Vedas and six Angas along with the Mahabharata in one hand, is present there, growing with divine power. Subramanya Swami, adorned with a fragrant Kadamba flower garland, is seated there with great joy. Ambika, having pleased Ganesha Murthy in the Niruthi direction and Velayudhapaani (Subramanya Swami) in the Vayu direction with her worship, reaches the sacred entrance where Nandi Devar stands guard. After performing the necessary pujas to the doorkeepers, she sees the Lord who resides gloriously under a mango tree, which has grown and risen as a majestic Vedic form, the Thriyekambam. With the love that arose as soon as her two eyes saw Him, she falls down, bows, rises, and praises the Lord. With a concentrated mind, she chants the Sri Panchaksharam, and with her hands joined above her head, a flood of supreme bliss overflowing, she enters and approaches the Lord, bowing and praising Him many times. Thus, praising the Lord, she saw with her own eyes the wondrous, nectarine grace that spontaneously manifested under a mango tree, emanating the fragrance of knowledge. She absorbed it into her heart, praised Him in various ways, marveled at the Lord's great compassion, was filled with supreme joy, and with tears of bliss flowing all over her divine body, her heart melting, her hair standing on end, her words faltering, the Empress began to perform Shiva Puja, an act of supreme wisdom. The Empress, addressing the Lord, says, "Chandrasekhara, by Your grace, I will perform Your puja without forgetting. Please bestow Your grace, remove all obstacles to Your puja, and You Yourself preside over this puja."

Thus, the Empress makes a sankalpam. She performs Pushpa Shuddhi to Bhuta Shuddhi, then performs Manasa Puja, and purifies the Padhyam, Achamanam, and Arghyam with clear water mixed with fragrant substances, causing her mind to blossom. The Empress praises Sadashiva Murthy, who is seated on the throne, shining as the primordial Shakti. The Empress, with the eye of true knowledge capable of dispelling the darkness of ignorance, clearly sees and praises Ekambaranathar, who is the all-pervading Supreme Light, who shines as the divine lamp in Thriyekambam, and who is the boundless, complete, pure, eternally blissful wealth, without beginning, middle, or end, pervading everywhere. She offers Padhyam at His feet, Achamanam at His divine face, and Arghyam at His divine crown. She offers Madhuvarkam and again offers Achamanam according to the Shiva Agamic rules. She offers the sacred food, cooked with ghee and milk, to the Lord and performs the puja. She applies sacred oil, sacred gooseberry paste, and sacred turmeric paste. She performs ablution with Panchagavya, Panchamirtham, and milk, curd, ghee, honey, and tender coconut water from many pots. She performs ablution with sugarcane juice. She performs ablution with various fruits. She performs sandalwood ablution. The Empress, with the clear water from the well dug by her maiden Keerthimathi, performs a complete ablution to Ekambaranathar according to the rules, chanting the Sri Rudra mantra. Then, she takes a soft silk cloth and gently wipes Lord Shiva's divine body, removing the sacred moisture from His divine body. Then, she adorns the Lord with a red silk garment and a white sacred thread. She adorns Him with ornaments and flower garlands. She offers incense. She offers lamps. She offers the four types of food with six tastes as sacred offerings, then cleanses the Lord's divine hands and coral-red divine mouth with cool water and anoints them. Then, she offers betel leaf and areca nut, chanting mantras, and properly offers incense and lamps. With a joyful mind, she performs the Arathi, a ritual to remove evil eye, circumambulates many times, bows, praises, and completes the Shiva Puja, which she began with immense love, meditating on the Sri Panchaksharam, which remains unwavering in her mind. Thus, Kamakshi Devi, the Empress, has been worshipping Sri Ekambaranathar in this manner for a long time. While the Empress was performing Shiva Puja daily according to the Shiva Agamic rules, on an auspicious day, Ekambaranathar decided to perform a divine play. The Lord decided to enact a divine play filled with love for the Empress. The meaning here is that the Lord performed this divine play, filled with love, to bestow grace upon the Mother and out of His immense love for her. This love that the Lord had for Ambika should not be compared or thought of as the love that we, as living beings (cows), have. The love that we, bound by imperfections, have is a love connected to attachment. To think that the Lord and the Goddess had love like this is a Shiva Aparadham (offence against Shiva). Because the love that the Lord and the Goddess had signifies the state of Tattva, where they are united with Shakti to uplift and save living beings. Living beings have love for themselves, but the Lord undertaking this divine play filled with love is for the sake of living beings, for their salvation. This is its meaning.

We will continue to contemplate the divine play that the Lord performed with the Empress. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 22 — Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 230. Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam, Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar Puranam. In Kanchipuram, Ambika performs Shiva Puja daily according to the Shiva Agamas. At that time, the Lord desires to perform a divine play with the Empress. Now, the next verse: "Seeing the flood of grace of the Lord, the fish-eyed one, fearing that a great flood would come upon her Lord from the heavens, with an inner trembling love, embraced the Lord of the flower-adorned tresses, who was like herself, preventing the flood with her divine hand." This is the next verse. Ambika is peerless; there is no one equal to her. The Lord is incomparable; similarly, the Goddess is also incomparable, having no equals or superiors. The Empress, as before, bathed in the Kamba River, adorned her body with Rudraksha garlands and sacred ash, completed her daily rituals, and began Shiva Puja, without any deviation in her Shiva service. When she was performing the sacred ablution to Ekambaranathar, filled with Shiva's essence, Lord Shiva, who resided under the mango tree blossoming with the fragrance of knowledge, decided to bestow His grace upon the Empress through a compassionate divine play. He willed that all the sacred waters from countless universes, both within and without, should unite and come forth. As soon as the Lord willed it, all those waters mingled with the Kamba River, and a flood surged forth from the Kamba River, like the deluge that comes during the time of cosmic dissolution. The Devas, the Ashtadikpalakas (guardians of the eight directions), and the inhabitants of the earth, who were present in all the universes, cried out in fear, thinking that the end of the age had come. Even the tears that arose from their cries mingled there as tributary rivers. Thus, the Kamba River, united with the waters of all worlds, began to swell with great speed. The river flood rose to touch the sky. That river flood surged forth like Vishnu, who once incarnated as a boar and lifted the earth. That flood leaped into the sky like Brahma, who once transformed into a swan and searched for the Lord's divine crown. The flood that came in the Kamba River carried along bananas, jackfruits, mangoes, coconuts, agarwood, saffron, sandalwood, flowers, honey, and sugarcane pieces. This was as if the maidens were carrying the items needed for Parvati Devi's Agamic puja. Seeing that flood, which carried along mountain treasures like sandalwood trees, saffron trees, honey, and other substances, as well as many gems, surging with waves, roaring with sound, covering the entire sky, and rising thickly upwards, the Goddess, whose face was like the full moon born of the mountain king, was startled and perturbed. With her maidens surrounding her on both sides also terrified, she looked at the relentlessly approaching flood and wondered, "What is the reason for this flood, which is like the surging ocean, suddenly appearing in the midst of Shiva Puja?" The Empress thought, "Perhaps this flood has come as an obstacle to my puja due to my fault," and feeling distressed, she meditated on the lotus feet of Lord Shiva, Chandrasekhara. But before she could meditate, the great flood had approached very rapidly and was very near. So, filled with distress, she looked at it, trembled with fear, and stood bewildered, thinking, "Oh, this great flood, reaching the sky, will surely come upon the Lord! What can I do now?"

Indeed, the entire golden body of Ambika, whose mind was overflowing with unwavering devotion to the Lord, trembled. Beads of sweat appeared on the forehead of the Goddess, adorned with a tilak. The pearl necklaces resting on her lotus bud-like breasts swayed. The Empress's slender waist became very weak and wilted. She intended to stop the flood with her divine hands. But the flood could not be stopped. The act of the Goddess trying to stop the flood with her hand implies that she, being the causal power of the universe, believed she had the ability to stop it herself. However, the fact that the flood did not stop but surged even more is explained as having happened by the grace of the Lord. That is why Sekkizhar Peruman first states here, "the flood of the Lord's grace." The Empress worried that the flood, which had approached very closely, would surge over the divine body of Ekambaranathar, whom she was worshipping. What to do now? She stood bewildered. Her devotion to the Lord intensified and soared. Her divine body trembled. Small drops of sweat appeared on her forehead. Pearl necklaces swayed on her breasts. Her slender waist wilted. Buzzing bees, immersed in honey, hummed in her hair. The Mekala ornament around her waist jingled. Golden anklets and bells jingled. Golden bracelets on her hands clinked. Like a flowering creeper that sprouted and sent out two tender shoots on either side, embracing a golden mountain peak, the Goddess, like a statue of emerald, quickly rose. She knelt on her right knee on the fragrant altar, and with both her hands, she tightly embraced her Lord, her husband. As soon as the Goddess embraced Him tightly with her mountain-like breasts and her red hands adorned with bangles, with overflowing, endless love, Ekambaranathar, the Lord, revealed His wondrous, blissful, luminous divine body, which dispels the darkness of ignorance, bearing the marks of the bangles on the Empress's red hands and the marks of her breasts. How wondrous, wondrous, wondrous it is! Our Lord, whose divine body is the supreme, blissful light, who is beyond form, formless, atomic, mountainous, existent, non-existent, darkness, light, and everything else, pervading everywhere, bore the marks of the bangles on the red hands and the marks of the breasts of the Goddess, whose hair was adorned with fragrant flowers. How astonishing! Our Lord, whose divine body is the supreme, blissful light, who resides in the mind as the size of a thumb, and who effortlessly moves all the universes, bore the marks of the bangles on the red hands and the marks of the breasts of the Goddess, who possesses great compassion and a gentle smile. How astonishing! Our Lord, whose divine body is the supreme, blissful light, whom Shiva yogis, who can control the Rechaka and Puraka breaths and sleep without sleeping, behold with their eye of true knowledge, bore the marks of the bangles on the red hands and the marks of the breasts of the Goddess, who speaks sweetly like a female cuckoo. How wonderful! My Father, whose divine body is the supreme, blissful light, who is always of one nature, free from thought and forgetfulness, immersed in the Self, and easily accessible to the love of Jnanis, bore the marks of the bangles on the red hands and the marks of the breasts of the Goddess, who has tender, sprout-like small feet. How astonishing! By merely thinking of His divine feet in the mind, the Lord, whose divine body is the supreme, blissful light, casts away the great karmic births of those who think of Him. How wonderful that He bore the marks of the bangles on the red hands and the marks of the breasts of the Goddess, the mother of all worlds. It is a great wonder that the red coral mountain, which bent the golden mountain as a bow, the eternal, beginningless, and endless Vedic form, became soft and slender due to the embrace of a flowering creeper's red hands and breasts. As soon as Thiruekambamudaiyar (Ekambaranathar) bore the marks of the bangles on His red hands and the marks of the breasts on His divine body, overflowing with compassion and attaining supreme bliss, all living beings in the heavens and on earth rejoiced greatly. The earth and the heavens shone without blemish. Countless musical instruments resounded. The gentle breeze wafted with a new fragrance. The dust settled throughout the ocean-bound world. The homa fire rose in a swirling column, radiating light. A shower of golden flowers fell incessantly. The Vedas, with their many meters, all rose individually, rejoiced, and resounded. All the worlds, without suffering from delusion, rejoiced. As all this was happening, the Devas, led by Indra, and the Asuras, all came there with their consorts, overjoyed. Brahma, seated on a lotus, came there with Saraswati. Vishnu, adorned with a cool, fragrant Tulasi garland, came there with Lakshmi. They came rejoicing, saying, "We have received supreme bliss today." The glorious Narada Muni and other Devarishis, and the Brahmarishis led by Vasishta, came in countless joyful crowds, with their tridents and other staffs, and their matted locks shining. Many Shaktis, the daughters of Diti, Aditi, and Daksha, Yoginis, Arundhati, Lopamudra, Anasuya, and other women, all came. The four great sages, Sanaka, Sanatkumara, Sanandana, and Sanatana, came. Agastya Muni, Durvasa Muni, Upamanyu Muni, the heroic Bhutaganas, Rudras, Ganadhipathis, Thirunandi Devar, and everyone else gathered there. Thus, everyone gathered there and continuously beheld the wondrous, luminous, blissful divine body of Ekambaranathar, who had softened and yielded to the Empress's embrace, and they roared "Hara Hara, Jaya Jaya." They plunged into the ocean of supreme bliss, rolled and tossed, and with their eyes, which had attained a great, rare fortune, their flower-like eyes shed tears of joy. Their bodies were covered with goosebumps due to the overflowing love, and they mingled like water absorbed by iron. Reflecting on the great compassion of the Lord's divine grace, their five senses were immersed in bliss. With their hands joined above their heads, Vishnu, who holds the chakra shining like a thousand suns, and Brahma, regretted that they did not have a thousand eyes to behold this wondrous sight. Indra, whose entire body had a thousand eyes, also wept, saying that even these thousand eyes were not enough. In this manner, everyone bowed down, rose, danced in joy, and praised and blessed with a roar like the seven oceans. At that time, only Lord Shiva, the deity adorned with the Konrai flower, knew the supreme bliss they experienced. Who else could know it? At that moment, Lord Paramashiva, who is difficult for the Trimurtis – Brahma, Vishnu, and Rudra – to behold, appeared from the beautiful, luminous Lingam, shining with the wondrous divine body bearing the marks of the Goddess's bangles on her red hands and the marks of her saffron-smeared breasts. The Lord appeared from the beautiful, luminous Lingam. With a gentle smile that radiated moonlight, the Lord established the great flood that came in the Kamba River there by the name of Sarva Teertham. The Lord said to the Empress, who had embraced Him, "See, I am supremely pleased because you have embraced Me." The Empress, the sole sovereign, her two eyes filled with joy at the wondrous divine body of her Lord, released her tightly embracing hands, rose from the earth, and with overflowing love, made an anjali. She plunged into the boundless ocean of supreme bliss, which surged as she beheld the pure, radiant, gem-like divine body, and experienced the bliss of Shiva. Then, the Empress praised Lord Shiva. "Salutations to the pure flame of knowledge, whom the tall one (Vishnu) and Brahma could not see. Salutations to the ocean of great grace, who bestowed fearlessness upon me, Your servant. Salutations to the Supreme Being, who is distinct from the 36 Tattvas, from the earth upwards. Salutations, salutations to the divine being who resides under a mango tree, emanating the fragrance of knowledge. Salutations, I, Your servant, have received a fortune that no one has ever received. Salutations to the one who creates, protects, and sustains all the universes in arrays. Salutations to the Lord who performs the Panchakritya Tandavam in the golden hall, with the heroic anklets resounding. Salutations, salutations to the eternal one who resides under a mango tree, emanating the fragrance of abundant flowers. Salutations, salutations to the one who has the eight forms: earth, water, fire, air, ether, moon, sun, and the master. Salutations, salutations to the one who has three eyes: Soma (moon), Surya (sun), and Agni (fire). Salutations to the Gnana Murthy who resides under a mango tree, blossoming with the fragrance of knowledge. Salutations to Lord Neelakanta. Salutations to the apple of my eye. Salutations to the one who bestows great grace even upon those who worship Him forgetfully. Salutations, salutations to the benevolent one who resides under the Sutha tree, which is the form of the Vedas. Salutations to the ancient one who existed before all things that were born first. Salutations to the new one who will be born after all things that will be born later. Salutations to the boundless, complete one whom the ignorant cannot comprehend. Salutations to the pure, conscious Thiruekamban. Shiva Shiva, praise, praise!" Thus, the Goddess, with her fragrant, flower-adorned hair, praised Him with a melting heart and boundless love. The Lord showered His flood of grace there. "Praise to my Father who resides in Ekambam! Praise to the one whose body is half woman, half man!" We will continue to contemplate this. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 23 — Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 231. Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam, Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar Puranam. In Kanchipuram, on a day when the Empress was performing Shiva Puja according to the Agamas, the Lord, intending to perform a divine play, caused a great flood to surge in the Kamba River. Unable to stop the surging flood, the Empress embraced Ekambaranathar tightly with her bangle-adorned hands and her divine breasts. Ekambaranathar softened His divine body to be gentle to the Mother's divine breasts and bangle-adorned divine hands. Sekkizhar Peruman explains this in the 64th verse as follows: "When the Mountain Princess, out of loving fear, tightly embraced the Lord of the Devas residing in the mango tree, pressing Him with her mountain-like breasts and bangle-adorned hands, with surging love, the Lord, who wears the hide of a murderous elephant, softened His divine body to be gentle to her beautiful forehead, her divine breasts, and her tender sprout-like hands, as if He were a person of resolute principles." Now, in the 65th verse, he composes as follows: "Seeing the Lord of Ekambam soften His body as His beloved embraced Him, all stationary and moving beings, from the Devas to all seven types of living beings, their souls and bodies melted and became one, praising Him as 'Ekambar became gentle for our Empress.' The sky showered fragrant flowers, and the Kamba River bowed down and its flood subsided." This is the next verse, the 65th verse. Seeing Ekambaranathar soften His body as His beloved Mother embraced Him, all stationary and moving beings, all seven types of living beings including the Devas, their souls and bodies melted and became one. They praised Him, saying, "Ekambar became gentle for His Empress." The celestial beings showered fragrant Kalpaka flowers. At that very moment, the Kamba River bowed down and its flood subsided. We can recall here what we have already seen: that the Lord stopped the surging flood of the Kamba River by naming it Sarva Teertham and made the river flow underground. When the Empress embraced her beloved Lord, why did joy arise in all living beings? Because the Lord, the thief who hid within every living being as the life of life, was embraced. Therefore, all living beings experienced His softening in their consciousness. This is its meaning. If "soul and body melted and became one," it means the body melted, and the soul within it also melted. Becoming subject to the single emotion of melting, "they themselves became one, and without the cessation of the command, they performed actions of coming and going in the two deeds," as per the second sutra of Sivagnana Bodham. The Lord is present, mingled as all living beings themselves. Therefore, all living beings melted and became one due to the Lord's softening. When Lord Shiva was struck by the Pandyan king's cane on the banks of the Vaigai, and when He was struck while carrying earth for the Puttu, and when the Lord was struck by Arjuna's arrow in the Kailasa mountain range, the stories say that the blows the Lord received were felt by all living beings. Similarly, here, due to the Lord's softening, all living beings have now melted. Now, the 66th verse: "Having bathed in the sacred ash, adorned with matted locks intertwined with the surging Ganga, wearing white ear-ornaments and Rudraksha beads hanging low, with a mind absorbed in the yoga that mingled with them, the Primal Lord, as if performing great penance, bore the marks of the bangles and the impression of the breasts, as the Mother who gave birth to and nourished all worlds bestowed the true fruit." Its meaning:

The Saivite wears pure sacred ash all over his divine body. He has bound his matted locks, intertwined with the surging Ganga River, as his crown. He wears white conch ear-ornaments and Rudraksha beads hanging low in his divine ears. The sacred ash smeared on the Lord's divine body is called Vibhuti. Bhuti means wealth, and Vibhuti means excellent wealth. Sacred ash is the best wealth of all. The Vedas praise "Bhutihi Bhutir Aishwaryam" (sacred ash is wealth, prosperity). The Vedas praise the Lord wearing sacred ash as "Bhasmoddhulita Sarvangam" (one whose entire body is smeared with sacred ash). Now, the Lord wears matted locks. He wears ear-ornaments and beads in his divine ears, so that they hang low and sway. He wears conch ear-ornaments. All these are ascetic forms. These are all the ascetic forms of Lord Shiva. Because in Kanchipuram, the Mother performed Shiva Puja and penance. As a result of that Shiva Puja, the Mother attained the Lord and embraced Him. It is as if the Father also performed penance to be embraced by the Mother and received the fruit of that penance, thus both together bestowed divine grace upon living beings. "What penance did the maiden perform to embrace the Lord?" they would say. "What penance did the generous one perform to marry the maiden?" they would say. This is the 120th verse of the Thirumanappadalam in the Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam. When Meenakshi Ammai and Sundaresa Peruman were married in Madurai, all the people who attended were amazed at the two of them. Some said, "What penance did the generous Sundaresa Peruman perform to marry our Thadathagai Pirattiyar, Meenakshi?" Others said, "What penance did our Meenakshi Devi perform to marry Sundaresa Peruman?" This is how the verse is structured in the Thirumanappadalam of the Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam. Recalling that here, the Mother performed penance and as a result of that penance, she attained the Lord and embraced Him. This verse implies that it is as if the Father also performed penance to be embraced by the Mother and received that fruit, thus both together bestowed divine grace upon living beings. Because the true benefit of the Mother and Father being together is indeed the divine grace they bestow upon living beings, isn't it? Thus, the Mother, who gave birth to all worlds, gave the benefit of touching and embracing her divine body. And the Ekambaranathar bore the marks of her bangles and the impressions of her breasts, which are the distinguishing marks of the Mother's divine body, as His own. Even today, in Kanchipuram, Ekambaranathar appears with the marks of the Mother's bangles and the impressions of her breasts, displaying them as signs of grace. Thus, this verse composed by Sekkizhar Peruman is structured. Now, the next verse. Ekambaranathar, who softened for the Mother's divine breasts, reveals His beautiful bridal form to the Mother. The Lord grants grace to the Mother, saying, "Receive the boons you desire." Then the Mother asks Lord Shiva. To indicate that this Shiva Puja, today's Shiva Puja, is not yet complete, the Mother bows down at the fragrant lotus-like divine feet of Thiruekambar, who is the form of the Vedas. Lord Shiva, who was hidden within the Shiva Linga, now appears and shows the Mother His form as He resides in Kailasa, saying, "Receive the boons you desire." As soon as He said this, the Mother bowed to Lord Shiva, and then she realized that the time to ask for boons, as granted by the Lord, would come only after the Shiva Puja was completed. But today's Shiva Puja has not yet taken place. It is not yet complete. The Mother reveals this to the Lord.

She reveals this by bowing down at the Lord's divine feet with folded hands. The Lord granted, "Receive the boon." But without receiving the boon, the Shiva Puja is now halfway. Therefore, the Mother requests the Lord that the Shiva Puja should be completed. When the Lord said, "Receive the boon," the Shiva Puja is now halfway. Therefore, Ambika requests the Lord that the Shiva Puja should be completed. At that time, Lord Shiva looks at Ambika's lotus-like divine face and says, "Your worship to Us never ends." Lord Shiva blesses, "The Shiva Puja you perform never ends." First, He accepted the portion of the puja performed so far and then appeared openly, revealing His divine form. Now, when Ambika asks for the remaining puja to be completed, He blesses, "Your puja never ends." Sekkizhar Peruman uses the phrase, "Nin poosanai endrum mudivathu illai nambal" (Your worship never ends for Us). The Shiva Puja performed by Ambika never ends. Because Lord Shiva and the Empress are eternal. Ambika performs Shiva Puja for the sake of the lives of the beings she gave birth to, for the benefit of their liberation. And those beings are also eternal. Ambika performs this Shiva Puja for the sake of those eternal beings. Therefore, Ambika and the Lord, being together eternally, should always bestow their divine grace upon living beings eternally. Therefore, Ambika's puja continues eternally without end. This is the meaning of this verse. Not only that, but in the upcoming verse, we should remember that Ambika's first boon is that this puja should remain eternal, and the Lord should joyfully accept that puja. Thus, as Lord Shiva joyfully blessed, now the Empress, our Mother, asks Lord Shiva for some boons for us. We will continue to contemplate them. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 24 — Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 232. Mummaiyaal Ulaganda Sarukkam, Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar Puranam. In Kanchipuram, after performing Shiva Puja according to the Shiva Agamas, when Lord Shiva appeared, Kamakshi Devi requested three boons from Lord Shiva. The first boon was that the Shiva Puja she performed should be eternal, and the Lord should accept and bless that Shiva Puja. As the second boon, Kamakshi Devi requested the Lord that she should be able to perform all dharmas in Kanchi city and that He should bless her for it. As the third boon, she requested that even if the residents of Kanchipuram commit any actions other than Shiva Aparadham (offence against Shiva), all those actions should be considered as great penance, and the fruit of that great penance should be bestowed upon them. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman sings that Ambika stood requesting these three boons from Lord Shiva. We have already seen that Lord Shiva blessed that the Shiva Puja performed by Ambika is an eternal puja. Accordingly, as the first boon, the Mother requests that she should eternally perform Shiva Puja in Kanchipuram, and that the Lord should accept and bless that Shiva Puja. Now, as the second boon, Kamakshi Devi asks the Lord that she should be able to perform all dharmas in Kanchipuram. It is the Mother's duty to protect all living beings through the household life she leads with Lord Shiva. Furthermore, it is the Mother's duty to guide her people in this world in household life. Therefore, the Mother requests this boon. Now, as the third boon, Ambika asks that whatever actions the residents of Kanchipuram perform, whatever karmas they perform, for all those actions, the fruit of great penance should be bestowed upon them. It is a general rule of the Lord's karmic division that each person receives the appropriate fruit for the actions they perform. The Lord bestows the fruits of actions upon each living being according to the actions they have performed. However, in this third boon, Ambika asks Lord Shiva that the residents of Kanchipuram, without considering the rules and exceptions of what they should do and what they should not do, whatever actions they perform, all those actions should be considered as great penance, and the fruit of great penance should be bestowed upon them. Even though it is the Lord's general rule to bestow fruits according to actions, Ambika requests that by the special grace of the Lord, whatever actions the residents of Kanchipuram perform, the fruit of great penance should be bestowed upon them. Because Ambika is the one who removes the births of living beings. She is the mother of all. Therefore, she requested these boons for the sake of her children. The only exception here is that Ambika asks that any actions other than Shiva Aparadham should be considered as penance and the fruit of that penance should be given. What is Shiva Aparadham? Shiva Aparadham includes slander of Shiva, slander of Shiva's devotees, slander of Vedas and Shiva Agamas, and so on. There is no remedy for Shiva Aparadham. Shiva Aparadhams are offenses for which there is no atonement. It is the conclusion of authentic texts that those who commit Shiva Aparadhams will suffer severe punishments and terrible hellish torments, just as Daksha lost his head. Shiva Aparadhams are unforgivable offenses. Therefore, Ambika asks that excluding these offenses, whatever other actions are performed, the fruit of penance should be bestowed upon them. Thus, when Kamakshi Devi requested these three boons, Lord Shiva, who comes on the Rishabha Vahana, granted all three boons to Ambika. Therefore, Kamakshi Devi eternally performs Shiva Puja in Kanchipuram according to the Shiva Agamas, and Lord Shiva accepts and blesses it. This is how the first boon was granted. Now, as the second boon, she requested that she should be able to perform all dharmas in Kanchipuram. Accordingly, the Lord grants this second boon to Ambika. To enable Kamakshi Devi to perform dharmas in Kanchipuram, the Lord gives her two measures of paddy. These two measures of paddy are called "Iga Parathu Iru Nazhi" (two measures for this world and the next). He gives one measure for the dharmas related to this world and one measure for the dharmas related to the next world. With these two measures of paddy given by the Lord, Kamakshi Devi performs 32 dharmas there. The paddy required for the worldly life of living beings is one measure. The second measure of paddy is for performing dharmas like creation, which are necessary for the bliss of the next world. The Lord gives paddy to promote dharmas. Because paddy is not just a seed for food. It is also the seed for the dharmas performed by living beings. "I will give you two measures of paddy as a seed to cultivate truth and dharma every year. With that paddy, may the green crops of life in this world and the next flourish," says the 426th verse of Thazhuva Kuzhaintha Padalam in Kanchi Puranam. Its meaning is that Lord Shiva says to Ambika, "I will give you two measures of paddy as a seed every year for you to cultivate dharmas. With that paddy, may the green crops of life in this world and the next flourish." This is how it is structured in the Kanchi Puranam, granting that boon. Therefore, even today in Kanchi city, Ambika, with the two measures of paddy given by the Lord, cultivates 32 dharmas. Tamil texts like Sendan Divakaram describe what these 32 dharmas are. Sendan Divakaram, a text, has structured these 32 dharmas in verse form. "Athular Salai, Odhuvaarkku Unavu, Arusamayathorkku Unavu, Pasuvukku Vayoorai, Siraichoru, Ayyam, Thinpandam, Makachoru, Makapperu Viththal, Maka Valarththal, Makappal, Aravai Pinam Suduthal, Aravai Thuriyam, Vannar, Navithar, Vaduvai, Poonool, Noi, Marundhu, Kannadi, Kadhoolai, Kan Marundhu, Thalaikku Ennai, Pen Bhogam, Sunnam, Pirar Thuyar Kaaththal, Thanneer Pandhal, Madam, Thadagam, Kaa, Aa Urinchi Naduthari, Eru Viduthal, Vilai Koduthu Kolai Uyir Meettal, Ichcheyal 32 Aram Enba." This is that verse. The first among these is Athular Salai. Establishing a hospital for the very poor is the first dharma. Next, secondly, Odhuvaarkku Unavu. Providing food to students, that is, to Brahmacharis in Gurukulams. Thirdly, Arusamayathorkku Undi. Providing food to people of all six religions, divided into six types. Next, Pasuvukku Vayoorai. Providing food to cows. This is the fourth dharma. Fifth, Siraichoru. Providing food to prisoners. Sixth dharma, Ayyam. Giving alms. "Ayyam ittu un" (Eat after giving alms) is Avvaiyar's saying. Seventh dharma, Thinpandam Koduthal. Giving food items. Eighth dharma, Aravai Choru. That is, providing food to the helpless and unsupported. Ninth dharma, Makapperu Viththal. That is, providing all the necessary items for childbirth. Tenth dharma,

Makam Valarththal, which means raising other people's children. The 11th dharma is Makappal Vaarththal, meaning giving milk to small children to drink. The 12th dharma is Aravai Pinam Suduthal, meaning performing the final rites, such as cremation, for the bodies of those who died without support, according to their traditions. The 13th dharma is Aravai Thuriyam, providing clothes and other necessities to the helpless. The 14th dharma is Sunnam, giving fragrant powders, lime, and so on. The 15th dharma is Noi Marundhu, buying and giving medicines for diseases. The 16th dharma is Vannar, giving donations to laundry workers. The 17th is Navithar, giving donations to barbers and those involved in that profession. The 18th is Kannadi, giving mirrors. The 19th is Kadhoolai, buying and giving ear ornaments to wear in the ears. The 20th dharma is Kan Marundhu, giving medicine to those with impaired eyesight. The 21st dharma is Thalaikku Ennai Koduthal, giving oil for the head. The 22nd dharma is Pen Bhogam, meaning providing necessary assistance to those involved in household life for their household life to flourish. The 23rd dharma is Pirar Thuyar Kaaththal, alleviating the suffering of others in any way possible, speaking comforting words to those who are suffering, mentally wishing for their suffering to cease, and physically helping them to alleviate their suffering. The 24th dharma is Thanneer Pandhal Vaiththal, establishing water stalls. The 25th dharma is Madam, establishing monasteries for the poor and travelers to stay. The 26th dharma is Thadam Amaiththal, meaning establishing water bodies like ponds. The 27th dharma is creating trees and groves, creating forests. The 28th dharma is Aa Urinchi Naduthari, meaning planting stones along roads and other places where animals travel, so that the animals can rub their bodies against these stones and find comfort. The 29th dharma is feeding animals and providing them with necessary provisions at appropriate times. The 31st dharma is Vilai Koduthu Uyir Kaaththal, saving a life that is about to be killed, at any cost. The 32nd dharma is Kannikathanam. Thus, various Tamil texts like Sendan Divakaram and Abhidhana Chintamani describe these 32 dharmas. However, in the Kanchi Puranam, the author of Kanchi Puranam describes the following as the 32 dharmas performed by Ambika. First among them is establishing places for performing the five yagnas (five types of sacrifices) performed by householders: Deva Yagam, Pitru Yagam, Bhuta Yagam, Manuda Yagam, and Brahma Yagam. Giving charity to the poor, establishing monasteries for renunciates, providing homes for householders, giving them necessary items for household life, establishing water stalls, creating groves, giving to beggars what they ask for, protecting the blind and other physically challenged from their sufferings, giving medicines to cure terrible diseases, raising small children, giving lime, giving betel leaf and areca nut (thamboolam), giving oil for the head, giving bathing powders like araippu and shikai, giving sleeping mats and other bedding materials, giving land (bhoothanam), giving cows (gothanam), giving away daughters in marriage (kannikathanam), arranging marriages, lighting lamps, settling someone's endless debts and freeing them from it, giving necessary assistance to Shiva's devotees,

Giving Rudraksha, giving sacred ash, giving items for Lord Shiva, giving Shiva Puja vessels, teaching Vedas and Shiva Agamas, teaching Shiva Puranas (i.e., making them read), giving Shiva Upadesam to develop true devotion to Lord Shiva's feet, and giving refuge to those who seek it. Kamakshi Devi performs these 32 dharmas in Kanchi with the two measures of paddy given by the Lord, as the Kanchi Puranam elaborates. This is the boon related to the second request made by Ambika, that she should be able to perform all dharmas in Kanchi city and that He should bless her for it. Now, as the third boon, we saw that she asks that any actions performed by living beings in Kanchi city should be accepted as penance, and that any actions other than Shiva Aparadham should be accepted as penance and grace should be bestowed. Lord Shiva also grants this boon. Sekkizhar Peruman, while singing the glories of Thondai Nadu in this Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar Puranam, sings that Thondai Nadu is a land where pure people, whose minds never even dream of evil, reside. Is that not due to the boon received by Kamakshi Devi? If this applies to the entire Thondai Nadu, then it also applies to Kanchipuram, doesn't it? Therefore, the Lord also grants the third boon requested by the Mother. But if one asks if the residents of Kanchipuram commit evil deeds, if they do commit evil deeds, then there are possibilities for such incidents to occur in Kanchipuram, such as committing evil deeds forgetfully, committing them due to others' influence, committing them losing self-control, or committing them by those of other religions who are not connected to Shiva, or by people from other countries who come here. Therefore, out of compassion for such people, the Mother asks that even those evil deeds should be transformed into penance and yield the fruit of penance, and the Father also grants it. We will continue to contemplate this. Thiruchitrambalam.
