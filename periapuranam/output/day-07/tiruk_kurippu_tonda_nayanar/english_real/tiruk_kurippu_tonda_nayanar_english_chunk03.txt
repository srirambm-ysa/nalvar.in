Chapter 15 — Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 223, Mummaiyal Ulaganda Sarga, Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar Puranam. In Mount Kailash, Lord Shiva imparts the secrets of the Agamas to Goddess Uma. The Goddess listened, understood, and received His grace. Lord Shiva, as the Acharya, seated in Sukhasana, imparts the secrets of the Agamas to Goddess Uma. We are contemplating the commentary on the statement by Sekkizhar Peruman, "the truth of the Agamas with clear words." The Vedas and Agamas are revealed by the all-powerful Supreme Being, Shiva. The Lord has revealed countless scriptures according to the spiritual maturity of all souls. However, among all of them, only the Vedas and Agamas are considered to be great scriptures. Since these were revealed by the Primal Lord, they are called primal texts. The nature of reality described in the Vedas and Agamas, composed by the omniscient Primal Lord, is the complete nature of reality. The nature of reality described by other religions and other philosophies are partial and contained within these. Since the Vedas and Agamas encompass all of them and were directly revealed by the Primal Lord, they are complete texts. Furthermore, when one speaks of "texts," it generally refers only to the Vedas and Agamas. Generally, texts or scriptures are not all of the same kind or equal. Texts are usually classified into various types: primal texts, derivative texts, auxiliary texts, and purvapaksha texts. The Vedas and Agamas are called primal texts because they describe the true nature of all things. Moreover, the Vedas and Agamas were directly revealed by God, hence they are called primal texts. Souls who cannot truly comprehend the real meaning contained in these Vedas and Agamas will take a small portion of the concepts within the Vedas and Agamas according to their understanding and spiritual maturity. Taking a small portion in this manner, they will compile and elaborate on their chosen religious texts using logical arguments and question-and-answer formats. Such texts are called purvapaksha texts. So, first we saw the Vedas and Agamas as primal texts. Now we have seen purvapaksha texts. Next, the third type of texts are called derivative texts. Smritis, Puranas, Kalas, and Upagamas are all called derivative texts. Those who truly understood the real meanings of the Vedas created these texts to explain them to others. These are the Smritis, Puranas, Kalas, and Upagamas. These derivative texts are further divided into four types: compendiums, elaborations, compendium-elaborations, and translations. All these derivative texts were created by following the Vedas and Agamas, hence they are called derivative texts. These are the third type of texts. Now, there are six angas (limbs) for the Vedas: Shiksha, Kalpa, etc. Similarly, there are anga texts for the Agamas: Garuda, Dakshina, etc. These are all created to aid in truly understanding the primal texts, the Vedas and Agamas. Therefore, these are all called auxiliary texts. So, the Vedas and Agamas are the primal texts. All other shastras can be categorized into derivative texts, compendiums, elaborations, compendium-elaborations, translations, and auxiliary texts. Among these, all texts belonging to categories other than the primal texts, the Vedas and Agamas, are based on the primal texts. Therefore, in reality, only the Vedas and Agamas are truly called texts. Thus, the conclusion is that there is no independent text that takes a concept not covered by the Vedas and Agamas and elaborates on it. In short, the Vedas and Agamas, directly revealed by God, are the texts that describe all things and their true nature. That is why only these two are truly called texts. There is nothing that is not contained in them. Therefore, the idea is that there is no text that speaks of a new concept apart from these. This is what the 267th verse of Sivagnana Siddhiyar states, that there are only two texts: Vedic texts and Saiva texts. That verse is as follows: "Vedic texts, Saiva texts, these are the only two texts; other texts are elaborations of these. The primal texts, given by the eternal, pure Lord, are two: the Vedic texts are general, and the Saiva texts are specific. Righteously revealed for the world and for those with Shakti Nipada, the hidden meaning of the great Vedas, the flawless essence of Vedanta, is expounded in Saiva texts; other texts are Purvapaksha, while Sivagamas are Siddhanta." This is that verse. In the world, what are called texts are only these two: the Vedas and the Sivagamas. All other texts are elaborations of the ideas in these two texts. The first part of this verse explains that these two texts were revealed by Lord Shiva. The Veda and Sivagama are the primal texts given by the eternally liberated Lord. As we have already seen, all other texts are elaborations of these two and are named derivative texts, auxiliary texts, or purvapaksha texts. That is why the Veda and Agama are exalted as texts. If God himself revealed these first two texts, why did He not give them as a single text? Why did He reveal them as two texts, the Vedas and the Agamas? To this question, Madhava Sivagnana Yogigal, who wrote the commentary on Sivagnana Siddhiyar, explains as follows: God is supremely compassionate. Therefore, for the salvation of the people of the world, He revealed the Vedas, and for the salvation of those with Shakti Nipada, He revealed the Sivagamas. That is why the Veda is revered by Saivites as a general text, and the Agamas as specific texts. The Veda is like a sutra. Just as a sutra can be structured to appear to have many different meanings, so too are the Vedas structured like sutras. But the Sivagamas are structured like a bhashya, explaining the true meaning. Just as a bhashya is structured to explain a sutra, so too are the Vedas like sutras and the Agamas like bhashyas. Therefore, the Veda is considered a general text and the Agama a specific text, just as a sutra and a bhashya are structured differently. Let us see how the Veda is like a sutra and the Agama is like a bhashya. The Agamas will elaborate in more detail on the concepts mentioned in the Karma Kanda section of the Vedas. For example, the Vedas state that one should perform Shiva Archana. But the methods and explanations of how to perform that Shiva Archana are extensively described in the Agamas. The Agamas extensively describe how Shiva Pujas, such as Atmarttha and Pararttha, should be performed. Similarly, if we take the Jnana Kanda section of the Vedas, the Agamas take the essence of the Upanishads and elaborate on them. For example, the Upanishads mention the essence of the Upanishads: Pati, Pasu, Pasam. But the Jnana Pada of the Agamas explains these philosophical concepts in great detail. Therefore, texts based only on the Vedas, which expound Purvapaksha concepts, are considered Purvapaksha texts, and the Siva Agamas, which expound the Siddhanta meaning of the Vedas, are considered Siddhanta texts. That is why the Vedas are for those who practice general dharma, and the Agamas are revealed for those with Shakti Nipada who accept Lord Shiva as the Supreme Being and follow the four paths of Charya, Kriya, Yoga, and Jnana. That is why the Lord revealed the primal texts in two types: Vedas and Agamas. Among these, Lord Shiva imparted the meaning of the Agamas, which are special texts, to Goddess Uma in Mount Kailash, and Goddess Uma, having heard and understood from the Lord, bestowed them upon us. There are a total of 28 Agamas. The creations performed by Lord Shiva are of two types: one is creation directly by Lord Shiva, and the other is creation through Anantadeva. The Vedas and Agamas are subject to Lord Shiva's direct creation. The knowledge in the form of Avabodha first manifested as Nada. Then, from Lord Sadashiva, who is in formless-form, the Nada form emerged as Shastra forms. When Bindu, the first tattva of Shuddha Maya, is moved by the divine grace of Lord Shiva, Nada appears. That Nada is called Omkara and Kutila. From this, the 50 aksharas, starting with A, arise. Then, from these aksharas, padas (words) arise. Then, shastras arise. This is the general method of how shastras arise. To explain this in a specific way, it will be clear to us if we say that Lord Shiva, assuming the form of Sadashiva, manifests the Vedas and Agamas. The form of Sadashiva refers to a body made of knowledge. God performs actions by mere संकल्प (resolve). However, this form of Sadashiva assumed by Lord Shiva is not a body connected to Ashuddha Maya like our body. The body assumed by the Lord is a pure body formed by mantras. The Kamika Agama calls this body Vidya Deham. The Ajita Agama calls it Sabda Brahma Rupam. From such a Lord Sadashiva, the Vedas and Agamas emerge. The Lord manifests them. Sadashiva Murti has five faces and ten hands. Each face has three eyes. In the right hands, He holds a trident, vajra, sword, axe, and bestows the abhaya mudra. In the left hands, He holds a snake, noose, gem, fire, and goad. He wears a snake as a sacred thread. He has a jata-makuta adorned with the crescent moon. He has a serene appearance. Thus, the Kamika Agama describes the form of the Supreme Being Sadashiva. Among the five faces of Lord Sadashiva, the upward-facing face is called Ishana. This face is beautiful, clear, crystal-like, possesses pure light, and has a childlike quality. Among the five divine functions, this face performs the act of grace (anugraha). The eastward-facing face is called Tatpurusha. It has the color of molten gold, is majestic, serene, and has the appearance of early youth. This face performs the function of concealment (tirodhana) among the five divine functions. The southward-facing face is called Aghora. It is dark in color, has furrowed brows, a terrifying appearance, and protruding fangs. This face, with trembling lips, is in the prime of early youth. Among the five divine functions, it performs the act of destruction. The northward-facing face is called Vama. It has the radiance of new coral, shines with curly hair like a swarm of dark bees, is captivating to all, appears like Parvati's face, and has creeper-like eyebrows. Among the five divine functions, this face performs the act of preservation (sthiti). Now, the westward-facing face is called Sadyojata. It appears with the radiance of the moon, possessing royal characteristics, adorned with lunar ornaments, and having unwavering eyes. Among the five divine functions, this face performs the act of creation (srishti). Although these five faces bear the names Ishana and so on, in reality, the five, namely Ishana, Tatpurusha, Aghora, Vamadeva, and Sadyojata, are referred to as the Pancha Brahma Mantras in the Siva Agamas. Among these, the Ishana mantra has five kalas, the Tatpurusha mantra has four kalas, the Aghora mantra has eight kalas, the Vamadeva mantra has thirteen kalas, and the Sadyojata mantra has eight kalas. The five kalas of the Ishana mantra are the five heads of Sadashiva Murti. The four kalas of the Tatpurusha mantra are the faces of the Lord. The eight kalas of the Aghora mantra are His heart, neck, shoulders, navel, stomach, back, and chest. The thirteen kalas of the Vamadeva mantra are the private parts, thighs, knees, ankles, upper hips, hips, and two sides. The eight kalas of the Sadyojata mantra are the legs, hands, nose, crown of the head, and shoulders. Thus, the body of Lord Sadashiva is perfectly formed by the five mantras beginning with Ishana. That is why it is said that this body, this Sadashiva body assumed by the Lord, should not be thought of or considered like our body. The Linga form we see in the sanctum sanctorum of all temples is this Sadashiva form we have just contemplated. If God did not assume such a mantra-filled body, there would be no way for meditation, puja, and so on to take place. If meditation and Shiva puja do not take place, there is no way for the multitude of souls to attain salvation. Therefore, the Kirana Agama states that the formless Supreme Being Shiva assumes the form of Sadashiva only to bestow grace upon the multitude of souls. Thus, the Supreme Being Shiva, having taken form, resolves to create the shastras solely for the reason that all souls should attain liberation. Now, we will continue to contemplate how the primal texts, the Vedas and Agamas, which are called shastras, originate from Lord Sadashiva. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 16 — Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar

Periya Puranam, Part 224, Mummaiyal Ulaganda Sarga, Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar Puranam. We contemplated that the Supreme Being Shiva, in His essential state (Swarupa Nilai), assumed the form of Sadashiva out of compassion, intending to reveal the primal texts, the Vedas and Sivagamas, for the salvation of souls. The Supreme Lord, who resolved to create the shastras to unite all souls with Himself, assumes the form of Sadashiva. Now, resolving to create the Vedas and Sivagamas, from His eastward-facing face, the Tatpurusha face, He brought forth the Rig Veda with 21 shakhas. From His southward-facing face, the Aghora face, He created the Yajur Veda with 101 shakhas. From His westward-facing face, the Sadyojata face, He brought forth the Sama Veda with 1000 shakhas. From His northward-facing face, the Vamadeva face, He manifested the Atharva Veda with nine shakhas. We saw that there are only two types of texts, Veda and Agama. Lord Sadashiva thus brought forth the four divisions of the Vedas from His four faces. Now, let us see how Lord Sadashiva brought forth the Agamas, which are the second type of primal texts. What we have just seen is the form of Sadashiva Murti assumed by the Supreme Being Shiva. Similarly, the Lord exists and assumes the form of Maha Sadashiva Murti. Maha Sadashiva Murti has 25 faces. That is, we already saw that Sadashiva Murti has five faces. Maha Sadashiva Murti has 25 faces, with five faces for each of the five faces of Sadashiva Murti. From the five faces within the Ishana face of this Maha Sadashiva Murti, the Lord brings forth 28 Agamas. From the Sadyojata face belonging to the Ishana face of Maha Sadashiva Murti, the five Agamas – Kamikam, Yogajam, Chintyam, Karanam, and Ajitam – emerged. Maha Sadashiva Murti brought forth the five Agamas – Deeptam, Sukshmam, Sahasram, Amshuman, and Suprabhedam – from the Vamadeva face belonging to His Ishana face. Maha Sadashiva Murti brought forth the five Agamas – Vijayam, Nisvasam, Svayambhuwam, Analam, and Veeram – from the Aghora face, one of the five faces in His Ishana face. Maha Sadashiva Murti brought forth the five Agamas – Rauravam, Makutam, Vimalam, Chandragyanam, and Bimbam – from the Tatpurusha face belonging to His Ishana face. Now, Maha Sadashiva Murti brought forth the eight Agamas – Protkritam, Lalitam, Siddham, Santanam, Sarvoktam, Paramesvaram, Kiranam, and Vatulam – from the Ishana face, one of the five faces belonging to His Ishana face. Thus, Maha Sadashiva Murti manifested a total of 28 Agamas from His Ishana face: 20 Agamas (five each from the other four faces belonging to His Ishana face) and eight Agamas from His Ishana-Ishana face. We have seen that the Supreme Being Shiva, assuming the form of Maha Sadashiva Murti, thus brought forth the primal texts, the Vedas and Sivagamas, out of compassion for souls and to bestow grace upon them. Now, let us also contemplate how these Agama shastras reach souls. Lord Sadashiva, realizing that any shastra would only be excellent if it came through a lineage of instruction, created ten Sivas by His mere thought and bestowed upon them the ten Sivagamas from Kamika to Suprabheda.

He imparted this through His Sadyojata face. Then, He made the 18 Rudras, starting with Anadi Rudra, who are in the Ishvara Tattva, knowledgeable and imparted to them the 18 Agamas from Vijaya to Vatula through His Aghora face, Tatpurusha face, and Ishana face. Those who received this instruction are of two types: Sivas and Rudras. Therefore, the first ten Agamas, that is, from Kamika to Suprabheda, are called Sivabheda because they were imparted to the Sivas. The other 18 Agamas, that is, from Vijaya to Vatula, are called Rudrabheda because they were imparted to the Rudras. Thus, the 28 Agamas are divided into two types: 10 Sivabheda Agamas and 18 Rudrabheda Agamas. However, it is very important for us to note that there is no hierarchy among the Agamas. Thus, Lord Sadashiva imparted each Agama individually to the ten Sivas, starting with Pranava, and to the 18 Rudras, starting with Anadi Rudra. Sivagnana Munivar and Sivagra Yogigal have mentioned in their respective commentaries on Sivagnana Bodham that there are also two methods of imparting this instruction. Furthermore, it is said that when the Sivagnana Shastras, also known as Siva Agamas, are transmitted through a lineage of instruction, six types of relationships arise. We said that Lord Sadashiva imparted 10 Agamas, called Sivabhedas, to 10 individuals. From them, three individuals each later received instruction. So, a total of 30 people. Now, we saw that He imparted 18 Agamas to 18 Rudras, called Rudrabhedas. These 18 individuals instructed another person. So, 18 times 18 is 36. Adding the 30 who received instruction in Sivabheda, a total of 66 people thus heard the Agamas, as stated in Tirumantiram, Payiram 57, composed by Tirumoolar Devanayanar. Now, Lord Sadashiva imparted the Agamas to Anantesvara. Anantesvara instructed Srikanta Rudra. Srikanta instructed Nandi Bhagavan. Nandi Devar instructed the Devarishis. The Devarishis instructed the Manudarishis. The Manudarishis instructed the spiritually mature among humans. Thus, the primal texts, the Vedas and Agamas, revealed by God, reach souls through the Lord's unique great compassion. Thus, Goddess Ambika, who rules the world, heard all the clear truths of the Agamas from Lord Shiva and bestowed them upon souls. For, is not Ambika the very form of the Lord's power of grace? Have we not seen that the Lord's grace, His desire to bestow grace upon souls, is Shiva Shakti? Thus, on Mount Kailash, Lord Shiva sits as the Acharya, imparting the Agamas, and Ambika hears all the clear truths of the Agamas from the Lord and bestows them upon us. Thus, we have contemplated the explanation for Sekkizhar Peruman's phrase "the clear truth of the Agamas," based on the commentary written by Sivakavimani Ayya and various other texts. Thus, we have contemplated the explanation for the 50th verse in Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar Puranam, which is "Velli Mal Varai Kailayil Veetrirundu Aruli Thulluvar Punal Veniyar Arul Seyya Thozhudu Thellu Vaimaiyin Agamathiran Ellam Theriya Ullavaru Kettu Arulinal Ulagai Aludaiyal." Now, the glorious 51st verse in Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar Puranam. "Ennil Agamam Iyambiya Iraivan Tham Virumbum Unmai Yavathu Poosanai Ena Uraithu Arula Annnalar Thamayi Archchanai Puriya Atharithal Pennil Nallavalayina Perum Thavakkolundu." This is the 51st verse in Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar Puranam. Its direct meaning is: Lord Shiva, who revealed countless Agamas, declared that what He desires is only puja. Understanding this, Goddess Uma, who is the best among women and like a great ascetic sprout, desired to perform puja to Lord Shiva. This is the meaning of this verse. Here, the verse is structured as "Ennil Agamam Iyambiya Iraivar Tham." "Ennil Agamam" means Agamas that have been measured and established. We already saw that there are 28 Agamas in total. Similarly, there are 207 Upagamas. Each of the 28 Moola Agamas contains many crores of granthas. Therefore, "Ennil Agamam" here can also mean countless Agamas, in the sense of number. Alternatively, "Ennil" can be split as "En nil," meaning Agamas that reside within one's thought or concept. Now, the meaning of the word "Agama." Saiva elders explain the word "Agama" by splitting it into "A," "Ga," and "Ma," which respectively denote Sat, Sat-Asat, and Asat, referring to the three entities: Pati, Pasu, and Pasam. The meaning is that Agama confirms these three entities – Pati, Pasu, and Pasam – and leads souls to salvation. The text Pingala Matam states that the name "Agama" originated from the combination of the first letters of the three words: Agatam, Gatam, and Matam. "Agatam" refers to its emanation from Lord Shiva. "Gatam" refers to its being heard by Goddess Uma. "Matam" refers to its propagation in the world as the doctrine of Shiva devotees. Furthermore, "Agama" also means "that which has come." From where has it come? It has come from the Supreme Aptar (trustworthy one). That Aptar is Paramasivan, the creator of all worlds. It means it has come from His divine tongue. Moreover, as we have already seen, "A" signifies Pasam, "Ga" signifies Pasu, and "Ma" signifies Pati. Therefore, the name Agama came about because it specifically explains these three: Pati, Pasu, and Pasam. They also explain it this way. Furthermore, some texts explain that "A" signifies Jnana (knowledge), "Ga" signifies Moksha (liberation), and "Ma" signifies the destruction of Anava Mala (egoic impurity). Thus, Agama is the text that removes impurities through knowledge and bestows liberation. As we saw above, the Lord who revealed countless Agamas, that is, the Lord who expounded them, tells Ambika that what is most dear to Him is Shiva Puja. Therefore, Ambika also desired what was dear to her Lord, Shiva. A woman desires and does what her Lord desires. That is the characteristic of virtuous womanhood. Our Ambika is the best among women. Our Umaiyamma is the best among all who are called women. Therefore, when her Lord declared that what He desires is Shiva Puja, she also desired to perform Shiva Puja. Ambika, who thus desired to perform Shiva Puja, is praised by Sekkizhar Peruman as "Perum Thavakkolundu." "Perum Thavakkolundu" means one who is like the sprout of great penance. What does that mean? It refers to a state that has resulted from penance, will perform many more penances, and will fully establish the penance performed, and further, will cause penance to flourish in the world. Therefore, Ambika's performance of penance and Shiva Puja signifies the flourishing of Shiva Puja and penance in this world.

This indicates the idea that it should flourish. God said that Shiva Puja is most dear to Him, and hearing this, Ambika also desired to perform Shiva Puja. We saw that Ambika heard what God said, didn't we? By saying "hearing," the act of learning is also included within it. By Sekkizhar Peruman's phrase "heard and received grace as it is," the meaning of "flawlessly" is conveyed here. Therefore, the secrets of the Agamas are to be learned, and by "desired to perform archana," it is implicitly understood that one should act accordingly. In accordance with the Kural, "What is the use of learning if one does not worship the good feet of the Pure One?" Ambika, who learned the Agamas from God through the process of instruction, resolved in her heart to abide by the Agama knowledge she had acquired. Therefore, she desired to perform Shiva Puja. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman has placed this verse, which conveys that Lord Shiva told Ambika that what He truly desires is Shiva Puja, and that Ambika, thinking that what her Lord Shiva liked was also what she desired, wished to perform Shiva Puja, as the 51st verse in Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar Puranam. Texts elaborately describe the Goddess as "51 Akshari." Parasakti herself embodies the 51 aksharas as her form. Thus, this verse, which speaks of what Ambika, known as "51 Akshari," desires, is placed as the 51st verse in Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar Puranam by Sekkizhar Peruman. This divine arrangement is also good evidence for the authenticity of Periya Puranam. Furthermore, the truth that Lord Shiva shines within the waves of Nada and that the benefit of knowing letters (education) is Shiva Puja is also mentioned here. Let us continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 17 — Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 225, Mummaiyal Ulaganda Sarga, Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar Puranam. Lord Shiva stated that Shiva Puja performed according to the Agamas is most dear to Him, and hearing this, the Goddess also desired to perform puja according to the Agamas. Now, the next verse, the 52nd verse. "Nangai ulnirai kadhalai nokki nayakan thiruvullathu magizhnthe Angan eithiya muruvalum thondra aduthathu enkol ninpal ena vinava Ingu natha nee mozhintha agathin iyalinai unai archchanai puriya Pongukindrathu en asai endru irainji Pogamartha poonmulaiyinal potra Deva Devanum athu thiruvullam seithu thentisai mikka seithavathal Yavarum thanai adaivathu manmel endrum ullathu Kanchi matru athanul Ma amarnda nam irukkaiyil anainthu mannu poosanai magizhndhu seivai endru eva Emperumattiyum piria isaivu kondu ezhundarutharku isainthal." Thus are the 52nd and 53rd verses. Now, their meaning. "Nangai" means the best among women. The Lord (Nayagan) observes the love that arose in the heart of such a Nangai, Ambika. "Nayagan" means the best among men. So, Lord Shiva, observing the love that arose and filled the heart of Ambika, the Nangai, rejoiced in His divine heart, and with a smile appearing on His divine face, asked, "What is in your heart?" To this, the Empress replied, "O Lord, my desire overflows to worship You according to the nature of the Agama that You have expounded here." The phrase "the nature of the Agama that You have expounded" appears. The "nature" referred to here is the performance of Shiva Puja. Performing Shiva Puja is the natural state for souls; everything else is a deviation. This is the implication. Thus, she says, "My heart desires to worship You according to the Agama; I yearn for it greatly." Who is that Empress? Sekkizhar Peruman refers to the Empress in this verse as "Pogamartha Poonmulaiyinal." "Pogamartha Poonmulaiyinal" means one whose breasts are adorned with ornaments that nourish souls with enjoyments. Thirugnanasambandar Peruman, in his Thevaram, Thirunallar Thevaram, begins that Thirunallatru Thirupathikam by praising Ambika with the phrase "Pogamartha Poonmulaiyal." "Pogamartha Poonmulaiyal thannodum pon agalam Pagamartha paingann vellatru annal parametti Agamartha thol udaiyan kovana adaiyin mel Nagamartha nambiraman meyathu nallare" is the first verse of Thirunallatru Thirupathikam. In this, Thirugnanasambandar Peruman has sung of Ambika as "Pogamartha Poonmulaiyal." Thirunallar is a sacred place located in the Chola country. Here, the Lord's name is Darbharanyesvarar, and the Goddess's name is Pogamartha Poonmulaiyal. Thirugnanasambanda Murthigal begins this Thirupathikam with this divine name. In the history of Thirugnanasambandar Peruman, he went to Madurai, cured the fever of Koon Pandiyan, and then engaged in Anarvadam and Punarvadam with the Jains. Anarvadam means lighting a fire and placing our Thirugnanasambandar Peruman's pathikam, written on a palm leaf, into that fire. Similarly, the Jains would write their mantras on palm leaves and place them in the fire. The one whose leaf remained unburnt in the fire would be declared the winner.

Thus, the Anarvadam was concluded. At that time, Thirugnanasambandar Peruman praised Lord Shiva, declared that our Lord Shiva is the Supreme Being, and worshipped Him. He untied the string binding the palm leaf in the Thirumurai and, with his divine hand, marked a line on that Thirumurai. The pathikam that came at that time was the Thirunallatru Thirupathikam, beginning with "Pogamartha Poonmulaiyal" as mentioned above. Thirugnanasambandar Peruman worshipped Lord Shiva, took that leaf from the leaves of the Thirumurai into his divine hand, and to make everyone understand that Paramasivan is the ultimate truth, he sang the Thirupathikam "Thalarila Valaroli" and then, for everyone to see, placed this Thirunallatru Thirupathikam into the fire. After the appointed time, when they looked, the Jains' leaf had turned to ashes, while the divine leaf placed by Thirugnanasambanda Murthi Swamigal shone green. Therefore, this pathikam, beginning with "Pogamartha Poonmulaiyal," came to be known as "Pachai Pathikam" (Green Hymn). Thus, in the Pandya country, Thirugnanasambandar Peruman, who re-established the Saiva religion by singing this Thirupathikam, sang of Ambika as "Pogamartha Poonmulaiyal" in the first line of the first verse of that Thirupathikam. Sekkizhar Peruman has used that phrase here. At this point, which shows that the ultimate conclusion of Saivism, the ultimate truth of Saivism, is Shiva Puja, Sekkizhar Peruman uses the phrase "Pogamartha Poonmulaiyal" to recall this Thirupathikam, which helped establish Saivism and demonstrated it to the world. We should also note here that the phrase is used to indicate the feminine form, the divine body of grace, of the Goddess. "Pogamartha Poonmulaiyal" means Goddess Uma, whose breasts are adorned with ornaments that nourish souls with enjoyments. "Pogam artha" means nourishing souls with enjoyments. "Poonmulaiyal" means Goddess Uma, whose breasts are adorned with ornaments. The two breasts of Ambika are revered as the two states of knowledge, Para and Apara. The conclusion of Saiva Siddhanta is that these two types of knowledge yield the fruit of Shiva's enjoyment. In the Skanda Purana, the breasts of Goddess Uma are referred to as "Paramabodha Neerathai" (filled with supreme knowledge). This is mentioned in the 23rd verse of the Saravana Padalam, Utpatti Kandam, of the Kanda Puranam composed by Kachiyappa Sivachariyar. That 23rd verse is as follows: "Adhi Nayakan Karunaiyai Amalamai Paramabodha Neerathai Irundathan Kongaiyil Pozhi Pal Ethilathathor Kurumani Vallal Meethu Etru Kadhal Ma Magarku Anbinal Aruthinal Gauri." This verse is set in the context when Lord Muruga incarnated and was growing up with the Karthigai women in Saravana Poigai, and Lord Shiva and Goddess Parvati went on their Rishabha vehicle to bestow grace upon Lord Muruga. The meaning of this verse is that Goddess Gauri, with love, offered the milk that flowed from her breasts, which were full of the grace of the primal Lord Shiva, pure and in the form of supreme knowledge, in a beautiful golden bowl to her beloved great son, Lord Muruga, and made him drink it. In this verse, Kachiyappa Sivachariyar has referred to the milk flowing from Ambika's breasts as the grace of God and the form of knowledge. Knowledge is of two types: Para Jnana and Apara Jnana. The meaning here is that Ambika's two breasts are the states of knowledge for these two types of knowledge, Para Jnana and Apara Jnana. Therefore, in this verse, where Ambika, intending to perform Shiva Puja according to the Agamas, presents her request to God, Sekkizhar Peruman has composed it as "Pogamartha Poonmulaiyinal" so that the meaning that Ambika performs Shiva Puja solely to impart knowledge to souls is implicitly conveyed. Lord Shiva listens to this with a smile. Because He had imparted the Agamas to Ambika. All souls in this world are going to receive the benefit of that instruction. Due to the penance that Ambika is about to perform, due to the Shiva Puja, all souls in the world are going to receive the benefit of the instruction given by Lord Shiva. Therefore, the Lord smiles. Now, Lord Shiva, the great God, the God of gods, the chief of all gods, takes Ambika's request into His divine heart and speaks to Ambika as follows: "The southern direction is endowed with great merit from penance. It performed great penance. Therefore, by the merit of that penance, everyone will reach the southern direction of that Bharatadesa. In that southern direction, which performed such great penance, the city of Kanchi remains eternally. In that Kanchi, under the mango tree, we reside with pleasure. Go to that abode and perform Shiva Puja with great joy." Thus, Lord Shiva commands. The Lord refers to the southern direction as "the southern direction that performed great penance." We have contemplated this in the beginning of Periya Puranam, in the Thirumalai Sarga, in the section on the glory of Thirumalai, in the 35th verse. In that verse, "Madhavam seitha thentisai vazhnthida theethila thiruthondar thokai thara pothavar mel manam pokkida kadhal madharum katchiyil kanninar," we contemplated all the explanations for "the southern direction that performed great penance." Recalling all that here, when describing the glories of such a southern direction that performed great penance, Sekkizhar Peruman also mentioned Kanchipuram there. At that time, we saw the explanations that Kanchipuram is a Prithvi Sthalam filled with Shakti. Therefore, we should recall all that and contemplate it here. Furthermore, we can contemplate here all the glories of Kanchipuram from the Kanchi Puranam that we have just seen in Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar Puranam. "Ka" means Brahma, and "anjuthal" means desiring, thus meaning the place desired by Shiva, who is Brahma. Or, taking "Ka" as Brahma Deva and "anjuthal" as reaching, it means the place where Brahma Deva reached and performed puja. Just as the ornament "Kanchi" is worn at the navel of a woman, so too Kanchipuram is located at the navel of the woman that is the earth. Therefore, it is named Kanchi. We saw all this in the summary of the Thiruvekamba Padalam in Kanchi Puranam. We also contemplated that in such a Kanchipuram, the Vedas themselves stood as a mango tree, and the Lord appears beneath it. That mango tree embodies the four Vedas and yields four kinds of fruits. Lord Shiva says, "We reside under such a glorious mango tree." He tells the Goddess, "Go there and perform Shiva Puja with joy." When Lord Shiva says this, He says, "Mannu poosanai magizhndhu seivai" (Perform the lasting puja with joy). "Mannu poosanai" means lasting puja. The Shiva Puja performed by our Empress is a lasting Shiva Puja, a Shiva Puja that will continue forever. Furthermore, in Shiva Puja, both the souls who worship and the God who is worshipped are eternal. "Were you not my servant then?" is the saying of Thayumanavar Swamigal. According to that, even in the state of liberation, souls will serve Shiva. Thus, the Goddess performs the lasting puja so that souls may eternally worship God and attain salvation. This is the implication here. When the Lord said this, Goddess Uma, though unwilling to be separated from the Lord, nevertheless, according to the Lord's command, prepares to depart from Kailash and go to Kanchi. Chapter 18 — Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 226. Mummaiyal Ulaganda Sarga, Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar Puranam. According to Lord Shiva's divine command, Ambika departs from Mount Kailash for Kanchipuram to perform Shiva Puja. The 54th verse is as follows: "Ethamil pala yoni enbathu nangu noorayiram thannul vaitha Bethamum purantharum a karunai piran mozhintha agama vazhi peni Podhu neermaiyil thozhuthana pothap poruppil vendhanum viruppil vandheithi Madhavam purintharuthal amaintha valathodum parisanangalai viduthan." This is that verse. Its meaning: Goddess Uma, following the path of the Agamas expounded by Lord Shiva, who is endowed with compassion that protects and nurtures all beings placed in the 84 hundred thousand (8.4 million) types of wombs (yoni bedhas) to remove their impurities, proceeds with devotion, worshipping the Lord. Just as when Parvati Devi incarnated as the daughter of Himavan and performed penance towards Lord Shiva, Himavan provided Parvati Devi with the materials for puja and maidservants, similarly now, with puja materials carried by maidservants accompanying her, Ambika departed from Mount Kailash. This is the meaning of that verse. First, Sekkizhar Peruman mentions "Ethamil." "Etham" means fault or blemish. The puja performed by our Empress is for the purpose of nullifying the bondage of Anava Mala that binds souls, to liberate souls from the bondage of Anava Mala. Thus, to liberate souls from the bondage of Anava Mala that has eternally gripped them, God bestows grace by causing them to be born in various types of wombs according to their karmas. Thus, Lord Shiva bestows such grace so that souls, born in various types of wombs, experience karmas, exhaust karmas, and thereby sever the bondage of Anava Mala and attain liberation. Therefore, Lord Shiva arranges for souls to be born in 8.4 million types of wombs according to their karmas. Generally, our shastras state that souls have seven births and four types of origins. The seven types of births are: gods, humans, animals, birds, reptiles, aquatic beings, and plants. Our shastras state that there are a total of 8.4 million types of wombs for these seven births. Among these 8.4 million types of wombs, gods have 1.1 million types, humans 0.9 million, quadrupeds 1 million, birds 1 million, reptiles 1.5 million, aquatic beings 1 million, and plants 1.9 million. Thus, the soul is born in these 8.4 million types of wombs, in these seven types of births, and experiences the karmas associated with them, according to our shastras. In the Thirukkural, in the chapter "Puthalvaraip Peruthal" (Having Children), Parimelazhagar, in his commentary on the Kural "Ezhupirappum theeyavai theenda pazhipiranga panpudai makkad perin" (If one gets children of good character who are not blamed, evil will not touch them in seven births), states that "Ezhupirappum theeyavai theenda" means that the seven births that occur due to karma. In such seven births, troubles will not reach a person. How? If he gets children of good character who are not blamed by others, then troubles will not approach him in all seven types of births. In his commentary, he specifically quotes an old Tamil poem that speaks of these 8.4 million types of wombs in these seven types of births. "Oorva onram onpathu manidam neer paravai narkal or pappathu see Piriya panthamam devar 14 ayan padaitha anthamil seer thavaram nalainthu enpathu" is that poem. Furthermore, Thirugnanasambandar Peruman has expressed the same idea in the fourth verse of the Thiruveezhimizhalai Thirupathikam in the First Thirumurai. He sings: "Uraiserrum enbathu nangu noorayiramam yonibhedam Niraisera padaithu avatrin uyirkku uyirai angange nindran koyil Veraiserrum mukin muzhava mayilgal pala nadamada vandu pada Viraiserrum pon ithazhi thara menkanthal kaiyerkum Mizhalaiyame." In the first line, he sings about Lord Shiva: "Uraiserrum enbathu nangu noorayiramam yonibhedam Niraisera padaithu avatrin uyirkku uyirai angange nindran." The meaning of this is that Lord Shiva systematically created 8.4 million differences in birth, known as yoni bedhas, and stands within them as the life of those lives. Thus, many of our shastras and great sages have sung about the 8.4 million yoni bedhas. Thus, Ambika departs to perform Shiva Puja according to the Agamas revealed by the compassionate Lord Shiva, who causes souls to be born in 8.4 million yoni bedhas to experience their karmic actions. At that time, attendants also accompany Ambika. This is similar to how, when our Empress once incarnated as Parvati Devi, the daughter of Himavan, and began to perform penance, her father Himavan provided Parvati Devi with the necessary materials for Shiva Puja and maidservants. He received grace by doing so. Similarly, now, as Ambika departs from Mount Kailash for Kanchipuram, maidservants carrying the materials for Ambika's Shiva Puja and other necessities also depart with Ambika. Ambika performs Shiva Puja for the salvation of souls. Therefore, various souls, knowing that Ambika is coming to perform Shiva Puja for their salvation, accompany Ambika on her journey. The Goddess comes to perform Shiva Puja with compassion for souls in her heart. Therefore, many souls accompany the Empress to receive the benefits of the Shiva Puja performed by the Goddess. How Ambika departed from Mount Kailash and came to Kanchipuram, and how all souls accompanied Ambika, are described in verses 112 to 121 of the Thazhuvakkuzhaintha Padalam in Kanchi Puranam. Sivakavimani Ayya writes in his commentary, "See that." Accordingly, our Empress, who resides with Lord Shiva on Mount Kailash, rises from that seat, bows at the Lord's feet, and receives His consent. Having thus received the Lord's consent to go to Kanchipuram and perform Shiva Puja, she begins to walk from there. On one side, there is the sorrow of leaving Lord Shiva from Mount Kailash. On the other side, there is the joy of having to worship Ekambaranathar in Kanchipuram. The sorrow of leaving Mount Kailash pulls Ambika back. But the joy of having to worship her Lord Ekambaranathar in Kanchipuram pulls Ambika forward towards Kanchipuram. Therefore, Ambika bows to Lord Shiva and repeatedly prostrates at the Lord's feet, saying, "O Lord, wherever I am, You must bestow Your grace," and then stands up and repeatedly says, "I am going, I am going," worshipping the Lord, taking leave, and walking slowly. She walks like a creeper gently swaying. As she departs from Mount Kailash in this manner, she walks backward slowly, looking at Lord Shiva's divine face, without showing her back to Lord Shiva.

She comes, bowing to Lord Shiva. After visiting the temple and completing the darshan of the Lord, we too should walk backward, looking at the Lord's divine face, without showing our back to the Lord. This is a rule for visiting Shiva temples. The Goddess reminds us of this here. Our Empress, who shines as the boundless, perfect, blissful, luminous light, seen by great ascetics and yogis who have subdued the three impurities of desire, anger, and delusion, and who have controlled the five sensory hunters of body, mouth, eyes, nose, and ears, and escaped their plunder, walks backward, looking at Lord Shiva's divine face, so that Lord Shiva appears before her divine eyes. Walking thus, she crosses the first gopuram gate of Mount Kailash, and continues to walk backward until Lord Shiva's divine face is visible to her eyes. After crossing the first gopuram gate, she begins to walk forward towards Kanchipuram. With difficulty, she parts from Mount Kailash, unwilling to leave, and by the divine grace of Lord Shiva, she ascends and sits on a great luminous vimana carried by many bhoota ganas. Thus seated, she begins her journey towards Kanchipuram. Let us continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 19 — Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 227, Mummaiyal Ulaganda Sarga, Thiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar Puranam. From Mount Kailash, having taken leave of the Lord, Ambika ascends and sits in a great luminous vimana. Ambika's retinue of female companions also ascend and sit in that vimana. Thus seated, Ambika, with her companions, travels in the vimana towards Kanchipuram. At that time, with supreme joy, every soul accompanies Ambika on that journey, thinking that our Mother, the Empress, is going to perform puja to Thiruvekambanatha according to Agama rules. Auspicious sounds are heard everywhere. Rudra Kannikas, with boundless joy, make joyous sounds and gather in crowds, surrounding and walking with Ambika's vimana. Lakshmi Devi, who resides in the red lotus, also joins. Saraswati Devi also joins, surrounded by crores of women on her side. Indrani, with her female companions, joins Ambika. Surrounded by countless women, the Sapta Matrikas – Brahmi, Maheswari, Kaumari, Narayani, Varahi, Indrani, and Kali – also join. Among the celestial women, crores of women from Garuda, Gandharva, Siddha, and Vidyadhara also join Ambika and journey to Kanchipuram. The deities Dakini, Kali, and Yogini, wielding tridents, also join with their retinues. Rishi Patnis also join Ambika. Thus, everyone joins Ambika and begins the journey. All these women who joined serve Ambika in various ways as they come. Furthermore, Uragar, Gandharvar, Rakshasar, Yakkar, Rudrar, Maruthar, Vasukkal, Garudar, Kimpurushar, Charanar, Siddhar, Devar, Brahma, Vishnu, 48,000 munis, and deities of various religions all come, prostrate at the lotus feet of the Empress, and with great joy, chant "Jaya Jaya Potri" and walk ahead. Percussion instruments like tannumai, murudu, kudamuzha, monthai, thaguniccham, perigai, thakkai, pathalai, murasam, etc., are played. Wind instruments like thiruchinnam, ekkalam, kombu, sangu, kuzhal, etc., are blown. Other instruments also play their own sounds. The sounds of these various instruments surpassed the roar of the seven seas. Thus, the roar of the seven seas was subdued by these instrumental sounds. The women accompanying Ambika wave white chamaras inlaid with navaratnas. They hold white silk umbrellas. They carry fans and alavattams here and there, accompanying the Empress. Along their path, Kalpaka trees shower flowers. The women accompanying the Empress sing sweetly like cuckoos. They sing pallandu (long life) for the Empress. The warrior women in the Empress's army, dressed in their masculine attire, walk ahead, wielding shining swords. Miraculous items like kulikai that can transmute the five metals accompany them. The Pancha Tarus (five celestial trees) that bestow abundant wealth also accompany them. All sacred tirthas accompany the Empress. All sacred rivers accompany Ambika. Dwarfs and bhootas walk ahead eagerly and quickly, waving their hands and making "guru guru" sounds. The clanging sounds from various instruments, the "kalakala" sounds, the "kalin kalin" sounds, the "salasal" sounds, the "sadadasa" sounds, and other sounds all resound there. Yet, above all these sounds, "Hara Hara" is heard everywhere. "Jaya Jaya" is heard everywhere. Thus, many sounds reverberate in all eight directions. The chants of the thiruchinnams praising the Empress, who is coming to Kanchipuram to perform penance, also spread everywhere. The chants of the thiruchinnams spread everywhere, proclaiming that she who is united with the Lord of the Vedas in one half has come, that she who is dear to the gods has come, that the great compassionate Empress has come, that the pure supreme light has come, and that the Empress who gave birth to all worlds in ancient times has come to worship Thiruvekambar of Kachi, rich in water. The Empress, who stands by her husband's command, comes like a boundless ocean of overflowing compassion. Ambika comes as if all the penance performed by the southern direction has gathered and is coming. She comes through the sky with boundless joyous adornments. Because Ambika comes through the sky, the sun and other luminaries are obscured, and the radiance of those who come in front of Ambika's procession shines there. The radiance of the bodies of those who walk in front of Ambika's procession appears like many lightning flashes appearing together. Their shouts of praise and "Jaya Jaya" resound like thunder. All of them in the procession walk very closely together. They wear many ornaments made of pure gold. As they walk so closely, the ornaments on their bodies rub against each other, and the clusters of navamani (nine gems) fall down like a rain of navaratnas. All who hear the thiruchinnams raised in praise of Ambika rejoice greatly, thinking that the World Mother is coming. They are filled with joy, immersed in a flood of supreme bliss, shedding tears, standing with folded hands and worshipping. The inhabitants of the earth celebrate, hearing that Ambika is coming to Kanchipuram to perform penance, to perform Shiva Puja, to remove all our birth-related ailments, and that the time for us to attain blissful liberation has come. They rejoiced greatly, thinking in various ways: what penance this earth has performed for Ambika to come here and perform Shiva Puja, what penance this Bharat Khanda has performed, what penance this southern direction in Bharat Khanda has performed, and what penance Kanchipuram has performed. Thus, Ambika approaches Bharat Khanda in a great luminous vimana through the sky. Upon arriving in Bharatadesa, Ambika visits all the sacred places where Lord Shiva graciously resides. Our Empress first descends in Kasi in Bharatadesa, installs a Shiva Linga there, performs puja, and then worships Kasi Viswanathar. But the desire for Kanchipuram pulls Ambika, so she departs from Kasi. Departing south from Kasi, she lovingly performs puja at every sacred place where Lord Shiva resides, and then reaches Thondai Nadu, which is like the beautiful face of the auspicious land of Tamil Nadu, and our Empress arrives at Kanchipuram. In the month of Aippasi, Krishna Paksha, Somavaram (Monday), Ekadasi, Puram Nakshatra, on an auspicious day when the Sun is in Thula Rasi and the Moon is in Kataka Rasi, our Mother, Parasakti, entered Kanchipuram bearing the name Kamakshi Devi. For the prosperity of all worlds, the divine Empress Kamakshi Devi entered the limits of the city of Kanchipuram. Seeing the city of Kanchi, which remains indestructible even at the end of yugas, she rejoiced more and more, clasped her red hands above her head, her whole body thrilled, shed tears of joy like rain, bathed in a tirtha there, made a sankalpa, wore a golden bangle and a turmeric thread on her luminous hands, installed a linga at that spot, and performed puja.

She performed puja. Since Ambika tied a protective thread and performed puja there, that place is called Kappu Tirtham. That linga is called Kappu Lingam. At the place where she wore the navaratna bracelet, she worshipped Sivaparanjothi, known as Kadakeesan, and wore the bracelet on her hands. Those who worship these two lingas installed by Ambika, Kappu Lingam and Kadaka Lingam (Kadakeesam), will be freed from the three impurities, overcome the sufferings of birth, and live in liberation. Our Empress, who has taken me as her own, thus performed puja and praised, and then entered the city of Kanchipuram, which removes the sufferings of birth for those who remember her. Ambika enters the city, delightedly observing the prosperity of Kanchipuram and showering her divine grace. As our Empress thus enters Kanchipuram, the great serpent Padman places her divine feet on his head and pleads, "O Mother, who gave birth to all worlds, please reside in the bilam (underground abode) where I live and establish your temple." There are eight serpents in the eight directions, one for each direction. They are Vasuki, Anantan, Takshan, Sangapalan, Kulikan, Padman, Mahapadman, and Karkotakan. Among them, the great serpent Padman resided in a bilam in Kanchi. It was he who now bowed to Ambika and requested her to reside in that bilam. That bilam is now located inside the Thirukamakottam temple, where Kamakshi Amman resides, which is centrally located in Kanchipuram. According to Padmanaga's request, Kamakshi Devi resided in that bilam. She established her temple there. Now, Ambika desired to perform puja to Lord Shiva, with boundless joy and grace in her heart, for the salvation of all souls on earth. Lord Shiva had already said on Mount Kailash, "We reside under the mango tree in Kanchipuram. Go there and perform puja." Accordingly, Kamakshi Devi now searches for the place where Lord Shiva resides. At that time, Lord Shiva performs a divine play. He hides Himself without appearing to Ambika, who is searching for Him. Is not hiding and appearing the divine play of God? Is not the Lord's hiding in this manner to further increase devotion in Shiva devotees? Therefore, the Lord hides Himself without revealing Himself. So, Ambika, with great love, performed intense penance to see Him. The Goddess, who is the inseparable power of grace from God, thus performs puja as if she is separate from God. The Lord accepts her puja. Thus, the Lord and the Goddess manifest as two. Then, when the Goddess embraces the Lord, they appear as one. Thus, it is one of the unique glories of Kanchipuram that the one Supreme Being Shiva manifests as two, as Ambika and the Lord, and then Ambika and the Lord appear as one again, giving the vision of unity, the unity of Shiva-Shakti. Thus, our Empress searches for the Lord with great love to perform puja to Him. The Lord hides there and performs a divine play. "Nenjam Eesanai Kanpathe Virumbi Nirantharam Thiruvakkinil Nigalvathu Anjezhuthume Aga Aludaiya Ammai Semmalar Kai Kuvithu Irainji Thanjamaghiya Arunthavam Puriya Tharippare Aval Thaniperum Kanavar Vanja Neekkiya Mavvin Moolathin Vanthu Thondrinar Malaimagal Kana" is the next verse composed by Sekkizhar Peruman.

No. As the Goddess yearned, unable to see the Lord, Kamakshi Devi's heart desired only to see the Lord. The sacred Panchakshara always resounded on Ambika's divine tongue. Ambika worshipped with her lotus-like hands clasped together. Since Ambika thought of the Lord in her heart, it signifies the mind; since she chanted the sacred Panchakshara with her divine tongue, it signifies speech; and since she clasped her hands, it signifies the body. Thus, the penance performed with the three instruments of mind, speech, and body, all united in discipline, is described there. Kamakshi Amma performs that penance. Lord Shiva, who resides under the mango tree in the form of the Vedas, was hidden at the root of that mango tree to remove the impurities of souls. The mango tree there is the form of the Vedas. Just as the Vedas keep the Supreme Being Shiva very secretly within their heart, placing the Aksharatvaya (two letters) within the Sri Rudra, and then reveal Him, similarly, Lord Shiva was hidden at the root of that mango tree. But seeing His consort Ambika thinking of Him with her whole heart, chanting the sacred Panchakshara with her divine tongue, and worshipping Him with her lotus-like hands, would He endure any longer? Lord Shiva, her unique great husband, unable to bear it, appeared at the foot of that mango tree for Ambika to see. He gave darshan to Ambika in the form of a Shiva Linga at the foot of that mango tree. Seeing the Lord appear there according to the promise He made in Kailash, "I will give you darshan at the foot of the mango tree in Kanchipuram," Ambika was overwhelmed and bowed to Lord Shiva, her life's Lord.
