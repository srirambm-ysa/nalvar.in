Chapter 35 — Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 243. Mummaiyal Ulaganda Sarukkam. The Puranam of Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar. Before Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar, Lord Shiva appears in the guise of a devotee of Shiva. He comes before the Nayanar, wearing dirty rags on his sacred body, with a emaciated form, bearing the sacred guise of Shiva, which is a form of great penance. The clothes worn by Shiva's devotees are called 'kandai' (rags). 'Kandai' refers to a garment like a quilt, made by stitching together four or five layers of fabric with threads. 'Kandai' also has another meaning: torn clothes. Wearing such ragged clothes, he comes in the sacred guise of a Shiva devotee, adorned with sacred ash, rudraksha, and matted hair, in a form of great penance. With a very emaciated body, wearing these dirty rags, he walks with faltering steps, befitting the guise he has assumed. The time of his arrival is described by Sekkizhar as 'Seethamali Kalam,' meaning a period of intense cold. The sacred day of Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar's liberation is Chithirai month, Swathi star. However, Sekkizhar Peruman refers to it here as a period of intense cold. Therefore, it is understood that even in Chithirai, there were days in the past with heavy rainfall, and days when heavy rains in Chithirai brought intense cold. Thus, Lord Shiva comes in the guise of a Shiva devotee, with dirty rags covering his sacred body, which is radiant with sacred ash, like a black cloud covering a bright form. He appears with a radiant sacred body. Sekkizhar Peruman compares the dirty rags worn by the Shiva devotee to a dark black cloud. We all know that a dark black cloud indicates impending rain. Here, that sacred form is the cause for the great rain of the world and the rain of great grace to fall upon Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman compares the clothes worn by the Shiva devotee on his sacred body to a dark cloud. Upon seeing such a Shiva devotee, Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar's hair stands on end. Seeing the devotee, he rejoices, bows, prostrates, and rises. When seeing, speaking to, or thinking of great people and deities, goosebumps are a prime characteristic of devotion. Thus, upon seeing the Shiva devotee, Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar approaches him and, with many sweet words, asks with folded hands, "O great ascetic, what is the reason for your emaciated sacred body?" The Shiva devotee comes before Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar with a slow, faltering gait. Therefore, recognizing the devotee's condition, Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar speaks sweet words to him, asking the reason for his emaciated sacred body. The Shiva devotee comes with dirty rags before Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar, who is an Ekali (washerman) dedicated to serving devotees. Lord Shiva, who is Digambara (naked), makes his unsoiled clothes appear soiled. This is because Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar's profession is washing clothes. Thus, he comes with soiled clothes to cleanse Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar's birth through his profession of washing clothes. Furthermore, he conceals his blue throat. If he had revealed his blue throat, Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar would have prayed to Lord Shiva to cleanse the impurities of his birth. Therefore, without doing so, Lord Shiva comes in the guise of a Shiva devotee with the intention of removing and cleansing Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar's birth impurities through the profession he had undertaken.

He says, "The clothes of that Shiva devotee are ragged and full of dirt particles." Therefore, Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar, with folded hands, humbly requests the Shiva devotee, "Please give me those ragged clothes you are wearing so that I may wash them for you." The Shiva devotee who had come replied, "These rags I am wearing are indeed very dirty. However, it is winter now. If I give you these rags to wash, the cold will trouble me. Therefore, I will not give them. If, however, you can wash and dry these rags for me by this evening, and give me that assurance, then take these rags, wash them, and return quickly." The Shiva devotee says this to Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar. Why does he say it must be done by evening? Because at sunset, the cold intensifies. Therefore, he gives the clothes with the condition that they must be returned before the cold can cause distress. Furthermore, the Shiva devotee says, "Bring them quickly, wash them quickly and bring them." Then Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar says to the Shiva devotee, "Please give me these rags. I will wash and dry them without any delay for you to wear, and bring them before evening." Then the Shiva devotee says to Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar, "If you do not quickly wash and dry these rags and return them today, you will have caused harm to this body," and he gives the rags. We must note the words spoken by the Shiva devotee here. We must note the place where Sekkizhar Peruman has placed the words spoken by the Shiva devotee. He says to Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar, "You will have caused harm to this body." Suffering comes to the soul only through the body. Here, the Shiva devotee refers to "this body." It is the nature of great souls to not get entangled in karmic experiences but to limit those experiences to the body, to the extent of the body, and to exhaust all those sufferings and karmic experiences through the body. Therefore, it is customary for Shiva devotees and great souls to refer to "this body." Devotees separate their soul and body and attribute pleasures and pains to the body. It is the nature of great souls to stop all pleasures and pains at the level of the body and to think and speak without being affected by those pleasures and pains. Furthermore, the one who came as a Shiva devotee here is none other than God himself. Therefore, he says to Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar, "You will have caused harm to this body that I have assumed." The inner meaning of this is that the trouble or suffering will affect "this body that I have assumed," and not me, the God within. Not only that, all the beings in this world are my body. Without being confined within them, I stand as the life within lives, taking beings as my body. The joys and sorrows that come to my body, which are beings, do not affect me – this is the inner meaning embedded in the philosophical truth. Having received the ragged cloth from the Shiva devotee, Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar washes the cloth at one of the washing ghats in the water tank. In those days, there were separate areas in water tanks and rivers specifically for washermen to wash clothes. They would bring dirty clothes to those areas and wash them until all the dirt was gone. As a result of washing clothes, the dirty water would mix with the water in the tank and the surrounding areas. Therefore, the water in that area would not be suitable for drinking or bathing. Thus, they would allocate separate areas for washing clothes. Even today, in many places, one can find separate areas called 'Vannar Thurai' (washermen's ghat) in rivers and ponds, as written by Sivakavimani

Ayya. However, this good practice has been abandoned in the name of reform in these times, and people are polluting water bodies, rivers, and ponds in all possible ways, without any discipline or justice, causing distress to the world. This is regrettable, writes Sivakavimani Ayya. Sekkizhar Peruman describes Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar washing the Shiva devotee's rags with three phrases: 'maserindhu,' 'miga pulukki,' and 'piritholikka.' These three are important stages in removing dirt and whitening clothes. They are separate actions. First, the cloth must be rubbed to remove dirt. Then, soda ash must be applied to the cloth, and it must be steamed and whitened. After whitening with steam, the cloth must be thoroughly rinsed in water to remove both the dirt and the soda ash. These are the three stages of washing clothes. Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar performs these actions. As he performs the third action, which is steaming and whitening, and then rinsing the cloth to remove dirt and soda, noon passes, and evening arrives. Then, by the grace of Lord Shiva, heavy rain falls. This heavy rain is about to separate the Nayanar from this mortal world and unite him with Shiva's eternal world, the world of knowledge. The rain that came then was by the grace of Lord Shiva. Because, as we have already seen, the Nayanar's sacred star of liberation is Swathi in the month of Chithirai. At that time, in the month of Chithirai, rain is generally not expected. But if heavy rain falls during a time when it is not expected, we must understand that it happens by the grace of Lord Shiva. Furthermore, this falling rain appears to be an obstacle to the sacred service being performed by Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar. Nevertheless, in reality, this rain that came was meant to unite the Nayanar with the sacred feet of Lord Shiva. Therefore, it becomes a rain of grace. How heavily and densely does the rain fall? The rain falls so densely that one's eyesight cannot penetrate the downpour. As this heavy rain falls, Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar's heart breaks as he remembers the promise he made to the devotee. He laments, "What shall I do now?" The devotee's sorrow and anxiety melt and grow even more. It causes a state that displaces the mental fortitude of Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar. As his mental fortitude is displaced, his intellect ceases to function. His mind is distressed by the situation where he cannot fulfill the promise he made. Therefore, he stands motionless, without mental or external action. Devotees are those whose state does not waver even if the five elements are disturbed. But now, the devotee is distressed, thinking, "Oh, the sacred service to the sacred feet of Lord Shiva's devotees has been troubled, an obstacle has arisen." He waits, thinking the continuous rain will stop. But the rain shows no signs of stopping. Then night also falls. As night falls, Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar, distressed that his sacred service to the ascetic whose body would suffer from the cold has failed, collapses to the ground. We will continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 36 — Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 244. Mummaiyal Ulaganda Sarukkam. The Puranam of Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar. By the time Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar finished washing the Shiva devotee's ragged cloth, evening had arrived, heavy rain had fallen, and then night had also set in. He was distressed and saddened, thinking that the promise he had made to the Shiva devotee to wash and dry the cloth by that evening had been broken. He was disheartened and grieved that his sacred service to the devotee had been hindered, and that the devotee's body would suffer from the cold. His mind was so distraught that his strength to stand diminished, and he fell to the ground. The rain continued to fall without any sign of stopping. The time limit given by the true ascetic had passed. Because of this, Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar's heart was filled with anxiety. He lamented, "I failed to wash the cloth quickly beforehand and take it home to dry it indoors. I missed that opportunity." Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar was deeply pained. The rain was such that all the people of the world would pray for it, yet here, that very rain became an obstacle to the Nayanar's sacred service. He deeply regretted, "I could have washed the clothes before the rain, taken them to my house, and dried them in the air indoors without getting them wet. Why didn't that thought come to me?" Not only that, but he was now unable to return the cloth to the Shiva devotee. The ascetic already had an emaciated body. He lamented, "I have committed the evil of causing such an ascetic's body to suffer from the cold. What can I do now?" The Nayanar's lamentation here shows his deep involvement and melting devotion towards Shiva devotees, who embody the sacred form adorned with sacred ash. Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar intended only good; he never intended to harm the Shiva devotee. He did not harm the Shiva devotee, but he was distressed and blamed himself, thinking that harm had come to the ascetic because he had not returned the ragged cloth on time, and that he was responsible for it. As he grieved, Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar resolved to strike his head against the stone slab used for washing clothes, until his head was shattered. That stone slab, used for washing clothes, had previously been helpful to Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar in washing the clothes of Shiva devotees. Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar's head and the body that bore his head had been instruments for washing the clothes of Shiva devotees. But now, his body had become the cause of harm to the devotee's sacred form. Therefore, the Nayanar resolved to strike his head against that stone slab and shatter it. Previously, that very slab had aided in the sacred service of devotees. Now, when harm came to the Shiva devotee's service, that same slab was also helping to provide a solution. Previously, this sturdy slab had helped in beating and washing the ragged clothes of Shiva devotees. Now, it also served as an aid when the Nayanar intended to strike his head against it. As he swiftly brought his head forward to strike it against the slab, the lotus-like red hand of Ekambaranathar, adorned with a beautiful bangle scar, emerged from the slab and caught the Nayanar's head. Sekkizhar Peruman refers to this slab as a 'sezhumpaarai' (sturdy slab). Previously, this slab had helped in washing the ragged clothes of Shiva devotees. This same slab stood as an aid when the Nayanar intended to strike his head against it. And then, the sacred hand of Ekambaranathar emerged from that same slab to catch his head. Thus, that same

slab was the reason for the emergence of the Lord's sacred hand. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman refers to it as a 'sezhumpaarai' (sturdy slab). He thinks in his mind of striking the slab. As soon as he thinks it, he performs the action. There is no delay whatsoever between his thought and the execution of the action. As he immediately resolved to strike his head, the sacred hand of Ekambaranathar caught his head. "If there is any desired gift, that too is your will," says the sixth verse of Kuzhaitta Pattu in Tiruvachagam. Accordingly, the action of the devotee and the action of the Lord occurred in unison. The sacred hand of God, as known from Tiruthandagam and other scriptures, and from the words of God in the Vedas, is omnipresent. Thus, the sacred hand of God is omnipresent. However, when it comes to love, its nature is to intensely manifest itself. The red hand emerged between the Nayanar's head and the slab before the Nayanar could strike his head against the slab. Not only did it emerge, but it caught the Nayanar's head. Just as the Lord's sacred hand caught the hand of Kannappa Nayanar in the Puranam of Kannappa Nayanar, when he, seeing the wound, feared and tried to pluck out his second eye to apply it, saying, "The hand of the one who rides the bull caught the hand of the one who tried to pluck out his eye," similarly, here, the Lord's sacred hand caught the head of Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar. The Nayanar resolved to strike his head against the firm stone slab. Therefore, to support and hold his head, the Lord, with his lotus-like soft hand, caught and supported the Nayanar's head there. Just as he showed his firm sacred body to the Goddess by softening it, so too, with that softened body, with the bangle scar, he extends his lotus-like hand and supports the Nayanar's head. At that moment, the rain falling from the sky stops, and a shower of flowers falls. Lord Shiva, adorned with a garland of Konrai flowers on his sacred crown, appears in the sky on his Rishabha mount with Umaiyamman, giving darshan to Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar. The rain that fell from the sky was a rain of grace that came to show compassion upon seeing the state of Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar. As soon as the Nayanar's sacred service was illuminated by grace, that rain also stopped. Then, whenever the divine grace of the Lord manifests, the Devas shower Kalpaka flowers. Lord Shiva, who a moment ago only extended his lotus-like hand to support the Nayanar's head, the omnipresent Lord Shiva, now appears in the sky on his Rishabha mount with Parvati, giving darshan to the Nayanar in the sky of knowledge. With abundant love overflowing in his heart, and all his senses melting, the Nayanar, who was previously in the rain of grace of the Tirodhana Shakti, now stands in the manifested rain of grace, the shower of flowers, and worships the Lord. Then, Lord Ekambaranathar says to Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar, "We have made the three worlds know the state of your love. You shall henceforth remain forever in our eternal Shiva Loka, never to be separated." To bestow grace, the eye becomes the instrument. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman refers to the Lord here as the three-eyed one. Lord Shiva, with his three sacred eyes, bestowed full grace upon Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar, the devotee who performed a great and difficult deed. The third eye is the eye of fire. It is the gaze that burned Manmatha. But here, it looked upon the Nayanar with direct grace, showering him with the rain of grace. Lord Ekambaranathar, who bestowed grace saying, "We have made your greatness known to the three worlds. Henceforth, you shall remain forever in Shiva Loka," then manifested from that state of appearance into his omnipresent state in Ekambaram, Kanchipuram. Thus, Sekkizhar Swamigal, having sung the sacred history of Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar, praises the sacred service of Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar and now begins to sing the sacred Puranam of Chandeswara Nayanar. Who can fully comprehend its greatness? No one can, saying this, Sekkizhar Swamigal concludes the Puranam of Tiruk Kurippu Tonda Nayanar and now begins to sing the Puranam of Chandeswara Nayanar. We too shall continue to reflect on the sacred Puranam of Chandeswara Nayanar by the grace of God.
