# English

Chapter 01 — Idangazhi Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Volume 988, authored by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Idangazhi Nayanar Puranam, from the Kadal Soozhndha Sarukkam. Following the Puranam of Kalarsinga Nayanar in the divine Periyapuranam by Sekkizhar Peruman, the Puranam of Idangazhi Nayanar is the 54th. It is the second Puranam in the sequence of the Kadal Soozhndha Sarukkam. Firstly, in the collection of works known as Thiruthondathogai, this Nayanar is praised in the ninth verse composed by our Lord Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. He sang, "I am a devotee of Nambi Idangazhi, who wears a garland of petals, and of the devotees of Seruthunai, the king of Thanjai." This should be interpreted as "I am a devotee of Nambi Idangazhi, who wears a garland of petals." The meaning of this is, "I am also a devotee of Idangazhi Nayanar, who is adorned with a garland of petals." A garland of petals is a garland belonging to the Chola kings. The one who wears it is Nambi, the best among men. The sacred name of this Nayanar is Idangazhi Nayanar. The meaning of this is, "I am also a devotee of Idangazhi Nayanar." Now, in the detailed work, Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi, in the 65th verse, Nambiandar Nambigal Peruman praises this Nayanar. The verse is, "The one who defeated the lion-like one, the first of the lineage of Adithan who adorned the roof of Chidambaram with gold, the one whose wealth is the devotees of Shiva with the moon in their matted locks, our Lord is Idangazhi, the king of Irukku Velir." The Lord Shiva is the great one who, as Sarabeshwarar, removed the arrogance of Vishnu who took the form of Narasimha. The king Adithan Cholan adorned the roof of the Ponnambalam in Chidambaram, where Shiva resides, with gold. Idangazhi Nayanar was the progenitor of this Adithan Cholan. Adithan Cholan was born in the lineage of Idangazhi Nayanar. Such a king, Idangazhi Nayanar, declared that all his treasures belonged to the devotees of the Lord. He was a king from the steadfast Velir clan. His sacred name is Idangazhiyar. This is the meaning of this verse. Now, in the detailed work, Periyapuranam, authored by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, in 11 verses, as the 54th Puranam, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman describes the Puranam of Idangazhi Nayanar. The first verse describes the land, "The great earth, adorned with the great ocean as its garment, has the Ko Nadu as the ornamental design on its chest, where the female cuckoos, hidden by the lush young shoots, rejoice, and the male cuckoos, having plucked and eaten the tender shoots of the mango trees, sing joyfully." The sacred land where this Nayanar was born is Ko Nadu. This Ko Nadu is a part of the present-day Pudukkottai region. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman sings about its prosperity in this verse. This earth-maiden wears the great ocean as her garment. On the chest of this great earth-maiden, who wears the great ocean with its waves as her garment, women would draw designs of leaves, flowers, etc., with sandalwood paste, which is called Thoyyil. This Ko Nadu region is like the chest of this earth-maiden. Since this Ko Nadu is surrounded by groves, it is like the Thoyyil drawn on the chest of this earth-maiden. There are countless groves. In these groves, cuckoos pluck and eat the tender shoots of mango trees, rejoicing with delight. The male and female cuckoos play and make love very happily among themselves. When the female cuckoo playfully hides, the male cuckoo searches for it, and seeing itself being searched for, the female cuckoo rejoices and delights. Similarly, the children of that country also play and rejoice in the streets. Such is the prosperous Ko Nadu. The second verse describes, "The bees, gathered around the honey-filled red lotus flowers, drink the nectar. In the clear-water ponds, water birds called Kurugu, both male and female, eat their fill and rejoice. They seek shelter from the cold wind and sleep in the groves of Kurukkathi. Kodumbalur is the capital city of Ko Nadu, surrounded by such ponds and groves." The bees drink the honey from the lotus flowers. In those ponds, water birds called Kurugu, both male and female, gather, eat their fill, and rejoice. They take shelter from the blowing cold wind and sleep in the Kurukkathi groves. Thus, Kodumbalur, the capital city of Ko Nadu, is surrounded by ponds and groves. This is called a Kodinagaram, a royal capital. This city of Kodumbalur is now seen as a village. It is in Pudukkottai district. It is on the way to Pudukkottai, southeast of Manapparai. It is a short distance southwest of Viralimalai. The temple here is very ancient. This temple should be renovated, and the idol of the Nayanar should be installed and worshipped. That would be a supreme Shiva Punya, writes Sivakavimani Ayya in his commentary. The third and fourth verses describe, "In that city, in the Velir clan, ruled the king with the great name of Idangazhiyar, who was the progenitor of the glorious lineage of Adithan, who adorned the beautiful roof of the Ponnambalam with pure gold, like a golden pillar. He never thought of any path other than the path of devotion to the feet of Shiva, who burned the three cities of the enemies, not even in his dreams. With constant great love, he always fulfilled the wishes of the devotees." In this city of Kodumbalur, the Nayanar named Idangazhiyar, who was born in the Irukku Velir clan, ruled. Adithan Cholan, who came later in the lineage of Idangazhiyar, covered the beautiful roof of the Ponnambalam in Chidambaram with pure gold tiles. This Irukku Velir clan is also said to be a small branch that later separated from the Chola lineage. Idangazhiyar was the first to be born in that lineage. Adithan Cholan came later in his lineage. He covered the Ponnambalam of Chidambaram with gold. This is an Ambalam next to the Chidambaram, to the south, different from the Chidambaram. It is one of the five Ambalams. Adithan Cholan covered its roof with gold. The act of covering the roof of the Kanakasabhai with gold is mentioned in inscriptions. King Idangazhiyar was the first to be born in that clan. He was renowned in the world with the great name of Idangazhiyar. He always thought of and praised only the path of devotion to the sacred feet of Shiva, who burned the three cities of the enemies, and never even in his dreams thought of any other external path. He never even forgot and thought of other external paths. External paths here refer to sinful paths that contradict the Vedas and Shivagamas. He not only did not think of them in his waking state but also never forgot and thought of them even in his dreams. With constant great love, he always fulfilled all the wishes of the devotees. The fifth verse describes, "In those days, he ruled in such a way that the Saiva path flourished with the Vedic path of Dharma, and in all the temples where the Lord with the dark throat resided, true worship and rituals, according to the scriptures, shone brighter and brighter, and his abundant and growing fame spread." Idangazhi Nayanar ruled in such a way that the Saiva path flourished with the Vedic path of Dharma. In the temples where the Lord resides, he ensured that true worship and rituals, according to the Vedas and Shivagamas, shone brighter and brighter. We see here the idea that the Vedic path flourished, and the great Saiva path shone. Dharma is worldly dharma, which brings benefits in this world. The Saiva path leads to liberation. He ruled in such a way that both these paths, this world and the other, flourished, and in the temples where Shiva resides, the pujas, according to the Shivagamas, shone brighter and brighter.

Such was his rule, and with such generosity and method of governance, he gained great fame. As he was ruling in this manner, a miraculous divine event occurred. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman continues to sing about it, and we too shall continue to contemplate it. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 02 — Idangazhi Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Volume 989, authored by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Idangazhi Nayanar Puranam, from the Kadal Soozhndha Sarukkam. We saw the manner in which Idangazhi Nayanar ruled. The sixth and seventh verses describe: "There was a great devotee who had the penance of feeding the devotees of Shankaran daily. One day, when he could not find the provisions to prepare food for a devotee, and his usual service was about to be interrupted, he was filled with great desire to fulfill his duty. Without knowing what he was doing, he entered the king's granary, which was filled with many rice stacks, in the deep darkness of midnight, and tried to scoop out rice. The guards, who were beating drums, caught him and brought him before the king." While King Idangazhi Nayanar was ruling, a miraculous divine event occurred. In that country, there was a very great devotee who had the penance of daily offering sacred food to the devotees of Shankaran. He was very dedicated to offering sacred food to the devotees, never missing a single day of this sacred service. One day, however, he could not obtain the provisions needed to prepare sacred food for a pure devotee. Therefore, he was greatly distressed and saddened that his sacred service of offering food to devotees was about to be interrupted. With an overwhelming desire to somehow offer sacred food to the devotees, he did something without knowing what he was doing. He entered the king's granary, where there were rows of rice stacks. These rice stacks were filled with rice. This was called a Kottakaram (granary). He tried to enter this Kottakaram in the middle of the night and take out rice. He did not even realize the ignominy of this act, which was theft, and without knowing what he was doing, he attempted it. It is a worldly truth that when a person has a very great desire for something, they will pursue it without considering whether the act is noble or ignoble, determined to achieve that thing. Great desire and great anger are destructive of wisdom. Therefore, the book of ethics states that "to examine an object without attachment or aversion is the mark of the wise." But here, this devotee, due to his distress that he could not perform Maheswara Puja for the devotees and offer them sacred food, and due to his elevated love for offering sacred food to the devotees, entered the king's granary at night, where there were rows of rice stacks filled with grain, without even realizing that what he was doing was theft. As he was taking the rice, the guards saw him, caught him, and brought him before the king. The eighth and ninth verses describe: "When the king, who held a spear, saw the true devotee and questioned him, he replied, 'I did this because my service of feeding the Lord's devotees was interrupted.' The king, deeply moved, said, 'Is this not my treasury, not this devotee?' With a heart full of compassion, he ordered that not only the rice granary but also all the treasures of gold, jewels, and gems be opened for the Lord's devotees to take as much as they wished, and had this proclaimed everywhere. Thus, he made use of the wealth he possessed." The guards caught the devotee and brought him before the king. As the king was holding court, King Idangazhi Nayanar questioned him. At that time, the true devotee said, "I did this because my Maheswara Puja, my service of feeding the Lord's devotees, was interrupted, and I had no provisions." The devotee said that he did this. With the determination that he must feed Shiva's devotees even if it meant stealing, the true devotee did this without even considering that he would face royal punishment for the theft. Even after doing so, he stood before the king without the slightest fear and told him what had happened, just as it was. Such is the valor of devotees. As soon as the true devotee said this, King Idangazhi Nayanar was deeply moved, thinking that such a situation had arisen where a true devotee had to suffer. The king was moved by the thought of how much this devotee must have suffered when his service of feeding the devotees was interrupted, and how much he must have suffered when the guards caught him. Immediately, Idangazhi Nayanar said, "This devotee is my treasury!" He meant that the rice stacks in the granary were not the real treasury. The treasury is the reserve. Therefore, the rice stacks filled with rice were not the treasury. He said, "This devotee is the treasury." Because the treasury containing these rice stacks holds the rice as it is, and it does not yield any benefit. But this devotee, by holding the rice within himself and using it to feed the devotees, brings benefit to me. This is what Idangazhi Nayanar thought. A commodity is a thing. If someone steals it, and the thief uses that stolen thing to do good, then the benefit of that good goes to the rightful owner of that thing. If someone uses a stolen thing to do evil, then the benefit of that evil comes to the thief. This is what the books of ethics say. Therefore, this devotee stole this rice and performed Maheswara Puja for the devotees. He offered sacred food. Is not the benefit of that for me? Therefore, Idangazhi Nayanar praised this devotee as his true treasury with wonder. In addition, Idangazhi Nayanar announced, "Not only this rice granary in my country, but all the treasures filled with gold, jewels, and gems, let the devotees of the Lord take them." He announced this, regretting that in his reign, Shiva's devotees were suffering due to lack of provisions for Shiva's service. The idea of Idangazhi Nayanar, revealed here, is that establishing the performance of Shiva dharmas and meritorious acts as desired by each individual is the goal of a true ruler. Therefore, he opened not only the granary containing rice and other grains but also the treasury containing gold, jewels, and other valuables for the devotees of the Lord. He proclaimed that the devotees of the Lord could come here, take what they needed, and thereby perform sacred service. He had this news proclaimed everywhere in his country. Thus, King Idangazhi Nayanar attained the true benefit of the wealth he possessed. The benefit of possessing wealth, according to the Vedas and Shivagamas, is to use those riches for Shiva and his devotees. The Lord is the one who created this world, and all the objects and philosophical principles within it, from Maya. Moreover, he is present in all of them. Therefore, the idea that this world and its wealth are under the control of humans is, in fact, a mistaken notion. Worldly possessions are not the property or right of humans. They are the property of the Lord, which is the truth stated by the Vedas and Shivagamas. Therefore, people who are destined to live with the help of these things created by the Lord, in reality, should realize that all these belong to Shiva and are his right.

In accordance with this, the Saiva tradition includes the discipline that worldly possessions and wealth, which one desires, should first be offered to the Lord with honor, dignity, and reverence, and only then should one accept them. Even in Shiva Puja, fragrant substances, food items, and other enjoyable things are offered to the Lord and then received back, as prescribed by the Agamas, so that this practice continues. Therefore, Shiva devotees should be aware that enjoying these possessions, which belong to the Lord, with the thought that "these are my possessions, they belong to me," falls under the crime of theft. Therefore, whatever the object or property, one should accept it with the feeling that "we receive it from the Lord," and not seize it with the thought that "this is my possession, my right." This is the idea expressed in the Isha Vasya Upanishad. The first verse of the Isha Vasya Upanishad states, "Isha Vasyam Idam Sarvam Yatkincha Jagatyam Jagat Tena Tyaktena Bhunjitha Ma Gridhah Kasyasvid Dhanam." The Lord created this world and entered into it, mingling within and without. "Isha Vasyam Idam Sarvam," this entire world is pervaded by the Lord. "Tena Tyaktena Bhunjitha," you are allotted a small portion by Him, and you enjoy it. Therefore, if you enjoy it with the thought that it is your exclusive right, it falls under the crime of theft. Therefore, when enjoying worldly possessions, one should consider them as belonging to the Lord, receive them, and offer them back to the Lord, accepting them as enjoyable things. The scriptures state that one who enjoys without offering to the Lord is a thief. Therefore, it is a rule that all property and possessions should be offered to Shiva, the Lord. Therefore, offering to the moving Shivas, the Jangamas (wandering ascetics), who are Shiva's devotees, is also acceptable. The feeling that "the Lord is the creator of everything," and the love that "those possessions belong to the Lord's devotees," and that "this is not my nature, but the nature of those who are pure and whose determination is like dust," as described by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman in the Puranam of Thirugnanasambandar, we reflect upon here. Therefore, if all possessions are to be offered to Shiva's devotees, do Shiva's devotees need possessions? They are those who desire nothing for themselves. They are endowed with boundless dedication to Shiva's service. Therefore, what they primarily wish to do is Maheswara Puja. They need possessions only for this puja, which involves feeding the devotees, and they do not desire any personal possessions. This puja is performed by realizing Shiva's devotees as Maheswara himself. Therefore, any donation made by seeing everything as Shiva is truly an offering to Shiva and is gladly accepted by Shiva. Thirumooladeva Nayanar also praises this in Thirumandiram, saying, "Giving to the moving temple, the Nambars, is like giving to the Lord in the fixed temple." In another way, we see that giving to Shiva's devotees yields special benefits. By offering everything received as an offering to Shiva, without even thinking of it as one's own, even in thought, the giver also attains the blessed state of Shiva, which is Shiva-related. That is why, in the Puranam of Thiruvathavooradigal, in the Puranam of Srimat Manikkavasagar Swamigal, when the Pandyan king initially became angry for giving his wealth to Shiva's devotees and Shiva's temple in Thiruperunthurai, and later understood the truth, he said that if his wealth was in the hands of Shiva's devotees, that was true dharma. We see in the Puranam of Thiruvathavooradigal, in the Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam, that the Pandyan king's face brightened when his wealth was used by Srimat Manikkavasagar Swamigal for Shiva's devotees and Shiva's temple service.

Here, in the Puranam of Idangazhi Nayanar, we see all these truths. We see that the true devotee entered the king's granary and took rice without even thinking that he was stealing. He was a true devotee who had completely surrendered himself to Shiva's service, without the slightest ego of "I" and "mine," having completely lost his self-awareness. If he did this when there was no other way to obtain the essential provisions for his service, it means that due to his overwhelming desire to fulfill his regular duty of feeding the devotees, he entered the granary and took rice without even knowing what he was doing, as mentioned by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Realizing this, Idangazhi Nayanar opened all his treasures and granaries for Shiva's devotees who perform Shiva's service. If the former is the nature of a true devotee, then the latter is the nature of Idangazhi Nayanar, we see. Now, the tenth verse describes: "With boundless love in his heart, he made the countless great treasures available for the Lord's devotees to take, and for a long time, he showered his grace on earth, causing the path of sacred ash to flourish, and then attained the shade of the Lord's lotus feet." Idangazhi Nayanar, with boundless love in his heart, made the countless great treasures available for the Lord's devotees to take, and stood firm in his sacred service. He had it proclaimed. Hearing that proclamation, many Shiva devotees came. As he had announced, he opened all the granaries and made all the treasures and wealth available for Shiva's devotees to take. This happened out of his boundless love. Having showered such grace and cool compassion, Idangazhi Nayanar, for a long time, caused the path of sacred ash to flourish on earth, and then attained the shade of Shiva's sacred feet. He attained the liberation that is the path of no return. The eleventh and final verse describes: "Having worshipped the feet of Idangazhiyar, who attained great distinction in the path of devotion to the Lord with the dark throat adorned with jewels, we shall now speak of the sacred service of Seruthunaiyar, the great ascetic who knew no other path than the true path." In this way, having worshipped the sacred feet of Idangazhi Nayanar and with their help, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman concludes the sacred Puranam of Idangazhi Nayanar and is about to begin the Puranam of Seruthunai Nayanar, saying that he will now speak of the sacred service of Seruthunai Nayanar, the great ascetic who knew no other path than the path of Shiva. We too, having bowed to the sacred feet of Idangazhi Nayanar, and with their help, shall now continue to contemplate the sacred Puranam of Seruthunai Nayanar. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of our Lord Idangazhi Nayanar. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of our Lord Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of Nambiandar Nambigal Peruman. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the commentators.
