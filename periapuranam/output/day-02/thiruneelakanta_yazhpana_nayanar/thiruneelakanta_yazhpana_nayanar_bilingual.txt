Chapter 02 — ThiruNeelaKanta Yazhpana Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Part 1018, authored by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Manniyasir Sarukkam, ThiruNeelaKanta Yazhpana Nayanar Puranam. ThiruNeelaKanta Yazhpana Nayanar reaches the Thiru Alavai temple in Madurai. The second sacred verse: "He reached the temple gate of the Lord residing in Alavai, stood there, tightened the strings of the instrument to produce the tune of the fourteen Paalai scales, and with his hands, repeatedly tested the strings to find the tune appropriate for the season, then played the songs of the Lord on the Yazh, which was beloved by the Goddess with fragrant tresses." ThiruNeelaKanta Yazhpana Nayanar stands at the entrance of the temple of the Lord who lovingly resides in Thiru Alavai. The meaning of this verse is that he tightened the strings of the instrument to achieve the proper tune of the 14 types of Paalai scales, and among the many tunes, he repeatedly tested the strings with his fingers to find the tune suitable for that particular time. After achieving the perfect tune on the instrument, he played the musical hymns of the Lord on the Yazh. The verse states: "Paalai irezhu koththa panninil karuvi veekki kaalam aadhariththa pannil kai palamuraiyum aaraayndhu." There are two types of songs: vocal songs (Kandappaadal) and instrumental songs (Karuvipadal). Among these, Kandappaadal is special because it is produced by the parts of the body, which are the instruments of the soul. It is sung through the throat, using the throat. Therefore, Kandappaadal, being produced by the parts of the body, which are the instruments of the soul, is more special than Karuvipadal. Karuvipadal is produced by inanimate musical instruments. Therefore, compared to Kandappaadal, Karuvipadal is not as special. This Karuvipadal, or instrumental song, is special to the extent that it can be compared to a vocal song. Among musical instruments, the flute (Kuzhal) and the Yazh are the most excellent. Among these two, the flute is given precedence because it sounds similar to words. The Yazh is placed after it. Therefore, the order of excellence is vocal song, then among instrumental songs, the flute, then the Yazh. Accordingly, in the Puranam of Thaduththaatkontar, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman explained the musical harmony of the vocal song in the 212th sacred verse. He described the characteristics of the flute, a wind instrument, in the Puranam of Ana Nayanar, in verses 947 to 953 and 938. We can apply these characteristics to the Yazh instrument here as well. This is because the characteristics described for the flute (Kuzhal) are mostly applicable to the Yazh instrument. Among these, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman briefly highlights some characteristics that are unique to the Yazh instrument. These characteristics are the tightening of the strings of the Yazh and repeatedly testing them with the hands, which are specific to the Yazh and not to the flute. Therefore, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman has explained them here. The phrase "Paalai irezhu koththa panninil" appears. Paalai is of four types: Aayam, Chathuram, Thirikkonam, and Vattam. From these, four types of tunes are born: Paalai Yazh, Kurinji Yazh, Marutha Yazh, and Neythal Yazh. Within Paalai Yazh, seven Paalai melodies are born: Sempalai, Padumalappaalai, Sevvazhippalai, Arumpaalai, Kodipaalai, Vilaripalai, and Mersempalai. These are of two types based on the order of variation: Varanmurai and Idamurai. Many tunes are born from these. These seven, including Sempalai, which are established in the 16 types of Aayappaalai, are referred to as Paalai irezhu. That is what the divine Sekkizhar Peruman means by "Paalai irezhu koththa panninil." Now, he sings "panninil karuvi veekki." To play on the Yazh, the strings belonging to a particular tune, which are of the types inai, kilai, pagai, and natpu, must be known and tightened to play. This is called Pannal among the eight artistic skills. Therefore, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman sings "panninil karuvi veekki." He says "kai palamuraiyum aaraayndhu." As mentioned before, examining the tightened string by touching it with the inner and outer fingers is called Parivattanai. Understanding the music by ascending and descending (aarohana avarohana) is called Aaraaythal. Anuśruti is called Thaaivaral. Along with these, performing the eight types of musical actions such as Vaarthal, etc., is the meaning of "kai palamuraiyum aaraayndhu." The eight musical actions mentioned are Vaarthal, Vadiththal, Undhal, Urazhthal, Uruttal, Theruttal, Allal, Pattadai. Vaarthal is performing with the index finger. Vadiththal is examining the string inside and out by joining the index finger and thumb. Undhal is plucking the strings to know their strength, weakness, and order. Urazhthal is plucking strings by inserting one or two fingers. Uruttal is of four types: rolling with the left index finger, rolling with the right index finger, rolling by joining the index finger and thumb, and rolling by joining both thumbs. Theruttal is touching and twisting the refined string. Allal is plucking with the index finger and thumb. Pattadai is plucking the entire string from the edge. Further details of these are extensively described in musical texts. They can be found in Silappathikaram, Arangetru Kadhai, Kaanal Vari, and in the commentaries of Adiyarkku Nallar. The subtleties and details can also be found in the chapter on Kannan's mind in the Kurma Puranam. These commentaries written by Sivakavimani Ayya are mostly based on the commentary of Silappathikaram. Such a tune, "kaalam aadhariththa pann," means a tune suitable for the time and season of singing. There are temporal divisions such as day, night, watch, and major seasons. Tunes appropriate for each of these temporal divisions must be played. Therefore, he chooses the time and plays tunes appropriate for it. It is very regrettable that these traditions are abandoned today, and any tune can be sung or played at any time. ThiruNeelaKanta Yazhpana Nayanar played these tunes with vocal accompaniment. "Paani" means musical songs or hymns. Therefore, it is understood that he also sang with vocal accompaniment. In the section of vocal accompaniment, the four arrangements of Selavu, Vilaiyattu, Kaiyool, and Kurumpokku, and in the section of vocal singing, the five elements of Eduththal, Paduththal, Nalithal, Kampitham, and Kudilam, and other grammatical rules are followed. ThiruNeelaKanta Yazhpana Nayanar skillfully expresses the verbal arrangement of such hymns through the Yazh music. The third sacred verse: "When the Lord, who vanquished the enemies, heard his instrumental song in the great Alavai of Madurai, He took it to heart and commanded all His devotees in a dream that day to bring the great Paananar, who had vanquished the enemies, before Him." As ThiruNeelaKanta Yazhpana Nayanar sang standing at the entrance of the Thiru Alavai temple, the song played by ThiruNeelaKanta Yazhpana Nayanar on the Yazh instrument was taken to heart by King Soundarapandiyanar, Lord Sokkanatha Peruman, who resides in Thiru Alavai in Madurai. He appeared in a dream to all His devotees that day and commanded them. What did He command? He commanded the devotees of Thiru Alavai in a dream, saying, "Bring ThiruNeelaKanta Yazhpana Nayanar, who is singing at the entrance, before me, for he is a great devotee of grace, and let him sing." He appeared in a dream and graciously commanded them. Even though ThiruNeelaKanta Yazhpana Nayanar was supposed to enter the temple and sing before the Lord, he did not do so, but sang standing at the outer entrance. Because the Lord desired that Yazh song, Somaskanda Peruman went into the dreams of the devotees of Alavai and commanded them to bring ThiruNeelaKanta Yazhpana Nayanar before Him. Paanars are of two types: Perum Paanar (great bards) and Siru Paanar (minor bards). This classification relates to the type of Yazh they possess. Now, some people will interpret this in a perverse way. Just as this custom became the Lord's command, so too did the devotees, hearing that it was the Lord's command to enter the temple and sing before the Lord, and realizing this through divine grace, understood it. Therefore, he consented to transcend the customary discipline of his Paanar lineage and sing before the Lord. The fifth sacred verse: "He sang with devotion about the Lord who burnt the three cities, who stood on the chariot, who flayed the elephant, who consumed Kama, who is difficult for Hari and Brahma to know, and yet is easy for His devotees, and by the grace of the Supreme Lord, He listened." The meaning of the songs sung by ThiruNeelaKanta Yazhpana Nayanar is that he sings about the great compassion of the Lord. He sings about the Lord's great heroic deeds, all of which embody His easy grace to His devotees, as the inner meaning of his songs. He sings with love about the Lord's act of burning the three cities, His standing on the chariot for that purpose, the history of His flaying the elephant, His consuming Kama, and the history of His being difficult for Vishnu and Brahma to know, and yet His great compassion in being easy for His devotees. Hearing that song, a voice arose in the sky by the grace of the Lord. An ethereal voice arose. The sixth sacred verse: "A voice arose in the sky, saying, 'If the melodious Yazh, played by the Paanar with love, touches the cold ground, its strings will loosen.' So the devotees placed a beautiful plank before him. The Paanar of sacred Tamil, having received divine grace, then sat there." A voice arose in the sky. It said that if the melodious Yazh, played by the Paanar with love, touches the cold ground, the tension of its strings will be lost. Therefore, the voice commanded, "Place a beautiful plank." ThiruNeelaKanta Yazhpana Nayanar had in mind a "Santham Yazh," which means a Yazh made of wood that has the proper maturity according to musical rules. It is a Yazh capable of producing that "Santham" (melodious sound). Therefore, the "Santham" will emerge properly from the wood only when the temperature and humidity are balanced. Here, ThiruNeelaKanta Yazhpana Nayanar was singing from the ground. Therefore, his Yazh was also placed on the ground. The coldness of the ground, through the Paanar's fingers and the wooden body of the Yazh, would affect the strings of the Yazh, causing them to loosen and lose their tension. If the tension of the strings is too much or too little, the correct music will not be produced. Therefore, a voice arose from the sky, saying, "Place a beautiful plank." "Thaaram uyththathu Paanarkku arulodae" (The Lord bestowed grace upon the Paanar) is a sacred saying from Pillaiyar's Thevaram. In that, ThiruGnanasambandha Peruman has praised this miraculous event of divine grace. The Paanar, who sang sacred Tamil songs both instrumentally and vocally, was given a beautiful golden plank by the devotees, according to the Lord's command. He sat upon it and sang the songs. He received divine grace and sat there. The seventh sacred verse: "He ascended the golden plank and played the instrument, praising the generosity of the Lord, who has Uma as one half, so that the whole world might know. ThiruNeelaKanta Yazhpana Nayanar ascended that golden plank and played the Yazh. "Thamaniya palagai" refers to a plank adorned with golden carvings. Seated on it, he played the Yazh and praised the generosity of the Lord, who has Uma as one half, so that the whole world might know. Here, the generosity of the Lord refers to the fact that caste, lifespan, and enjoyment are determined by the Lord's command according to one's actions. The divine grace that allowed him, who should have stood at the entrance according to this moral code, to enter before the Lord, to sing, and to be given a golden plank, is what is praised here as the Lord's generosity. The sacred words of Thiruvachakam, "He made me also a thing, and placed me in a palanquin, who else would receive such grace as He bestowed upon me, alas!" come to our mind. ThiruNeelaKanta Yazhpana Nayanar, who sang such sacred songs, was praised by all the celestials. The celestials, seeing the easily accessible grace bestowed upon this devotee, glorified him. Afterwards, he received gracious leave from Lord Sokkanatha Peruman of Alavai, visited many sacred places where Lord Shiva manifests, and reached Thiruvarur. Departing from Thiru Alavai, he worshipped many sacred places along the way to Thiruvarur and finally reached Thiruvarur. There, we see that the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, in this sacred verse, quotes the Thevaram verse, "Amarar naadu aalaadhu Aarur aandavar Thiyagesa Peruman Amarar naadu aalaadha Vairavaname" (Lord Thiyagesa, who rules Thiruvarur, not the land of the celestials, is indeed Vairavan). Lord Thiyagaraja was placed in the celestial world by Indra and received their worship. However, leaving that state where he was worshipped by the celestials, he graciously manifested in Thiruvarur on this earthly plane through Muchukunda Chakravarti, to bestow grace upon him. Therefore, Lord Thiyagesa preferred ruling in Thiruvarur to ruling the land of the celestials, and manifested to bestow grace upon the people of this world. We have already contemplated all these historical accounts. This is extensively sung in the Kanda Puranam. The eighth sacred verse: "Standing before the temple gate, he played on his instrument, describing the great power of the Lord who vanquished Yama, and His cool compassion, which bestows great grace, better than a mother's, upon His devotees. When the Lord heard this, He created another gate in the northern direction, through which he entered and worshipped." In Thiruvarur, he reached the temple and, according to the custom of his lineage, stood before the temple gate and sang. He played on his instrument, describing the great power of the Lord who vanquished Yama, His heroic deed of kicking Yama with His foot, and His cool compassion, which bestows great grace, better than a mother's, upon His devotees.

ThiruNeelaKanta Yazhpana Nayanar. The great power that vanquished Yama and the cool compassion that bestows grace upon devotees, better than a mother's, are praised as "Kopa Prasadam." These are the gracious qualities of the Lord. We know that a text named "Kopa Prasadam," authored by Nakkiradeva Nayanar, is included in the 11th Thirumurai. Both of these, that is, anger and grace, are the Lord's blessings, common to all souls and specially distinct for devotees. All praises to the Lord are contained within these two. Therefore, He is more compassionate than a mother. "More compassionate than a mother who remembers to feed her child" is a sacred saying from Thiruvachakam. "The Lord who is more compassionate than a mother" and "Sankaran, who is better than a mother" are also sacred sayings. "Thannali" (cool compassion) means that even the Lord's actions that seem like anger are also compassion. Now, bestowing great compassion is especially praised as "Thannali." This is called "Arakkarunai" (righteous compassion) and "Marakkarunai" (compassion that transcends righteousness). He sings these qualities, bringing out the meaning of the words with musical accompaniment on the Yazh. He performs all the appropriate Yazh techniques and also sings with vocal accompaniment. The Lord listens to it. Lord Thiyagaraja, having listened, in order to bestow grace upon ThiruNeelaKanta Yazhpana Nayanar, created another gate in the northern direction of the Thiruvarur temple specifically for ThiruNeelaKanta Yazhpana Nayanar. Thus, in Thiru Alavai, Lord Somaskanda commanded the devotees and had ThiruNeelaKanta Yazhpana Nayanar enter the temple and come before Him. Here in Thiruvarur, the Lord graciously created a separate gate in the northern direction specifically for ThiruNeelaKanta Yazhpana Nayanar to enter. ThiruNeelaKanta Yazhpana Nayanar entered through that gate and worshipped the Lord. The ninth sacred verse: "He worshipped the Lord, the first one, who resides in the Moolaththanam, stayed there for a long time, and by the grace of his Lord, departed from Thiruvarur, where virtuous devotees never leave, and worshipped many sacred places where the Lord with the dark-throated neck resides." Inside the Thiruvarur temple, in the sacred Moolaththanam, resides Lord Vanmeeganathar, the Lord who resides in the sacred anthill. He worshipped Him and stayed there for a long time, worshipping the Lord. The virtuous devotees, the true devotees in the Devasiriya Mandapam, were born in Thiruvarur, worthy of liberation. He worshipped the Lord in that Thiruvarur, from which they never depart, for a long time, throughout the periods of worship, and having received gracious leave from the Lord there, he continued to worship many sacred places where the Lord resides. The tenth sacred verse: "By the grace of the Lord who resides in ThiruThoni, surrounded by the ocean, the Paanar of melodious Yazh, who received grace, came to Sirkazhi, the city of the Brahmins, to worship the lotus feet of our Lord ThiruGnanasambandha Peruman, who was fed the nectar of wisdom by Uma, whose language is sweeter than the music of the Yazh, and who is the Lord of Kaali country and the chief of the Kauniyar clan." By the grace of the Lord who lovingly resides in ThiruThoni, surrounded by the ocean, ThiruNeelaKanta Yazhpana Nayanar, the Paanar of melodious Yazh, came to Sirkazhi, the city of the Brahmins, to worship the lotus feet of our Lord ThiruGnanasambandha Peruman, who was fed the nectar of wisdom by Uma, whose language is sweeter than the music of the Yazh, and who is the Lord of Kaali country and the chief of the Kauniyar clan. The eleventh sacred verse: "When the one who received wisdom heard about him, he bestowed special grace upon the great Paanar of good music and Yazh. From that day, the Paanar received the great fortune of setting the music of the glorious hymns sung by him to the Yazh. After that, the Paanar, with a heart full of joy, immediately attained the feet of the Lord with the dark throat." ThiruNeelaKanta Yazhpana Nayanar went to worship in the temples of the Lord in Thiru Alavai and Thiruvarur. Here, he came to Sirkazhi to worship the feet of Aludaiya Pillaiyar. When Pillaiyar, who had received divine wisdom, heard that ThiruNeelaKanta Yazhpana Nayanar had arrived, he bestowed special grace upon the great Paanar of good music and Yazh, as was fitting. From that day, when he met and worshipped Pillaiyar in the sacred city of Sirkazhi, ThiruNeelaKanta Yazhpana Nayanar received the great fortune of setting the music of the glorious hymns sung by Pillaiyar to the Yazh. From then on, he accompanied ThiruGnanasambandha Peruman on his sacred journeys and set all the sacred hymns composed by ThiruGnanasambandha Peruman to the Yazh. We contemplated this part of his sacred history, which is intertwined with the sacred history of ThiruGnanasambandha Peruman, in the Puranam of ThiruGnanasambandha Peruman. Therefore, from this day until the marriage of ThiruGnanasambandha Peruman, and until he attained the feet of the Lord in Thirupperumanallur, ThiruNeelaKanta Yazhpana Nayanar remained inseparable from ThiruGnanasambandha Peruman, and received the great fortune of setting his sacred hymns to the Yazh as commanded, wherever they were. Such a great soul, along with ThiruGnanasambandha Peruman, attained the feet of the Lord, who has a neck the color of a blue flower, in Thirupperumanallur. Thus concludes the sacred history of ThiruNeelaKanta Yazhpana Nayanar. The twelfth and concluding sacred verse in this Puranam: "We now proceed to narrate the greatness of Sadaiyanar, who received the grace of the Lord, wearing the beautiful crescent moon and snake, a Shiva Vedic scholar of the Shiva Agamic tradition, who came from ThiruNavalur, a land of fields abundant with paddy and sugarcane, having worshipped the lotus feet of the great Paanar, ThiruNeelaKanta Yazhpana Nayanar, who came in such a manner." This is the 12th and concluding sacred verse. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman, following his tradition, concludes this sacred history that he has been narrating so far, and introduces the next history, saying that he will now proceed to narrate the greatness of Sadaiyanar, who received the grace of Lord Shiva, wearing the beautiful crescent moon and snake, a Shiva Vedic scholar of the Shiva Agamic tradition, who came from ThiruNavalur, a land of fields abundant with paddy and sugarcane, having worshipped the lotus feet of the great Paanar, ThiruNeelaKanta Yazhpana Nayanar, who came in such a manner. Concluding the Puranam narrated so far is called "Upasamharam" in Sanskrit, and introducing the next Puranam is called "Upakramam." We observe this method in the Puranams of the Nayanmars in the Periyapuranam authored by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. In that regard, this is the concluding section structured in this way. The next Puranam to be narrated is the greatness of Sadaiyanar. The greatness of Sadaiyanar, the greatness of his consort Isaignani Ammaiyar, and the history of their divine son, Nambigal Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, who incarnated and became the first of the original Thiruthondathogai, and who came to uplift this world, are also included. The place where ThiruNeelaKanta Yazhpana Nayanar is praised in the Thiruthondathogai is "I am a devotee of ThiruNeelaKanta Paananar." With this, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal concludes his praise of individual devotees. The concluding part of the Thiruthondathogai is "Sadaiyan, the lover of Isaignani, who attained the feet of the Lord, the King of ThiruNavalur." Accordingly, we see that the Puranam of Sadaiya Nayanar and the Puranam of Isaignani Ammaiyar are arranged, and this Sarukkam concludes. We will also see that the next Sarukkam, Vellana Sarukkam, will be the concluding part of the Periyapuranam. Therefore, having worshipped the feet of ThiruNeelaKanta Yazhpana Nayanar and with his support, we will continue to contemplate the Puranam of Sadaiya Nayanar. Praise, praise to the feet of our Lord ThiruNeelaKanta Yazhpana Nayanar. Praise, praise to the feet of our Lord ThiruGnanasambandha Peruman. Praise, praise to the feet of our Lord Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Praise, praise to the feet of Nambiyandar Nambigal Peruman. Praise, praise to the feet of the divine Sekkizhar Peruman.
