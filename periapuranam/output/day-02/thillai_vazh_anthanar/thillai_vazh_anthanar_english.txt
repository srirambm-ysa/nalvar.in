Chapter 01 — Thillai Vazh Anthanar

Thiruchitrambalam. We conclude the section called Thirumalai Sarukkam, the first chapter in Periyapuranam, and step into the second chapter, Thillai Vazh Anthanar Sarukkam. Sekkizhar Swamigal elaborates on the 11 verses of Thiruththondaththogai composed by Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, structuring them into 11 chapters in Periyapuranam. Therefore, Sekkizhar Swamigal narrates the history of the devotees mentioned in the first verse of Thiruththondaththogai, which begins with "Thillai Vazh Anthanar," as the Thillai Vazh Anthanar Sarukkam. The first verse of Thiruththondaththogai, "Thillai Vazh Anthanartham Adiyaarkkum Adiyen," is as follows: Thiruchitrambalam. "Thillai Vazh Anthanartham Adiyaarkkum Adiyen, Thiruneelakandaththu Kuyavanaarkkum Adiyen. Illaiye Ennaatha Iyarpaigaikkum Adiyen, Ilaiyaan Than Kudimaaran Adiyaarkkum Adiyen. Vellumaa Migavalla Meypporulukkadiyen, Viripozhil Suzhntha Kunraiyaar Viranmindarkkadiyen. Allimen Mullaiyanthaar Amarnithikkadiyen, Arooran Arooril Ammaanukku Aale." Thiruchitrambalam. Sekkizhar Swamigal expands this very first verse of Thiruththondaththogai as the Thillai Vazh Anthanar Sarukkam, the second chapter of Periyapuranam. The devotees mentioned in this first verse are the Thillai Vazh Anthanars, Thiruneelakanda Nayanar, Iyarpaigai Nayanar, Ilaiyaankudi Maara Nayanar, Meypporul Nayanar, Viranmindar Nayanar, and Amarnithi Nayanar – a total of seven individuals. The histories of these seven devotees are discussed sequentially within this chapter. Similarly, Sekkizhar Peruman narrates the histories of the devotees mentioned in the other 10 verses of Thiruththondaththogai in their respective chapters. Thus, Sekkizhar Swamigal composes separate Puranas for each of the 63 Nayanmars and nine Thogaiyadiyavars. Accordingly, the Thillai Vazh Anthanar Puranam is the first Purana within the Thillai Vazh Anthanar Sarukkam. The essence of this Purana is the section that describes the qualities of the Thillai Vazh Anthanars, who are praised in Thiruththondaththogai. Thillai refers to the sacred place of Chidambaram. Thillai Vazh Anthanar refers to the three thousand Brahmins residing in Thillai. "Vazh Anthanar" is a Vinai Thogai (compound verb) according to Tamil grammar. Therefore, the Thillai Vazh Anthanars lived in the past, continue to live in the present, and will live in the future. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal sings that he is a servant to the devotees of these glorious Thillai Vazh Anthanars. We can find the explanation for this within this Purana. In this Thillai Vazh Anthanar Purana, Sekkizhar Swamigal composes 10 verses. In the first two verses, he praises and extols Sri Nataraja Peruman, who is considered the foremost among the three thousand Thillai Vazh Anthanars. The first verse is: "Aathiyaai Naduvumaagi Alavilaa Alavumaagi Sothiyaai Unarvumaagi Thontriya Porulumaagi Pethiyaa Ekamaagi Pennumaai Aanumaagi Pothiyaa Nirkum Thillai Pothunadam Potri Potri." Its meaning is: "Salutations, salutations to the common dance in Thillai that stands as the beginning, the middle, the immeasurable measure, the light of consciousness, the manifested substance, the undivided oneness, both female and male, and that which teaches." For this verse, Sivakavimani writes the following commentary. Firstly, for the term "Aathiyaai" (as the beginning), he states that Lord Shiva stands as the creator for the emergence of all worlds. During the time of Maha Sangaram (great dissolution), all worlds merge into the Lord, who is the destroyer, and then re-emerge from Him during the time of creation. Thus, by causing all worlds to merge into Himself as the destroyer, He is the destroyer, and by causing them to re-emerge, He is also the creator. This is implied by "Aathi." Sekkizhar then states that the Lord stands as the middle. Since the term "Aathi" implies that the Lord is the cause of both creation and dissolution, "Naduvum" (and the middle) here indicates that the Lord also performs the act of sustenance (Sthithi). The Lord not only creates and dissolves the world but also sustains it. Therefore, the phrase "Aathiyaai Naduvumaagi" signifies that the creation, sustenance, and dissolution of the world are all due to this sacred dance in Thillai. Furthermore, the fourth and fifth actions, Tirobhava (concealment) and Anugraha (grace), are included within these. Thus, we must understand that this dance is the dance of the fivefold actions. Next, Sekkizhar describes the Lord as "Alavilaa Alavumaagi" (the immeasurable measure). This means that the Lord is a being who can only be known through Shiva Gnanam (divine wisdom), and not through Pasu Gnanam (individual soul's knowledge) or Pasa Gnanam (bondage-related knowledge). Now, he states that the Lord stands as "Sothiyaai Unarvumaagi" (as light and consciousness). "Sothi" is the external light that reveals. "Unarvu" is the consciousness of the soul that perceives what is revealed by that light. Therefore, its meaning is that the Lord Himself exists as both the external light and the consciousness of the soul, enlightening everything. Next, he says "Thontriya Porulumaagi" (and the manifested substance). This means that the Lord pervades the entire universe. Following this, "Pethiyaa Ekamaagi" (the undivided oneness) also means that the Lord exists as a substance that is indivisibly mingled with the objects He has created. Both the world of words (Sorprabanjam) and the world of objects (Porutprabanjam) exist within the Lord's pervasiveness. Furthermore, Sekkizhar Swamigal here refers to the state where the Lord, who creates these worlds from pure and impure Maya, mingles with them without any separation. Then Sekkizhar Swamigal describes the Lord as "Pennumaai Aanumaagi" (both female and male). This is the nature of Shakti and Shiva pervading all worlds. Because the Lord holds these two distinct natures of Shakti and Shiva within Himself, all diverse forms of life, by their characteristics and qualities, are named male and female, contained in two categories, and live in union and joy. Thiruvachagam refers to this form of the Lord, standing as Shiva and Shakti, as the ancient form. Why does the Lord stand in this state of being the beginning, the middle, the immeasurable measure, the light of consciousness, the manifested substance, the undivided oneness, both female and male, and teaching? The wise say it is for the salvation of all beings. This is praised as Thirukkooththu (sacred dance) and Thirunadanam (sacred performance). Sivakavimani writes that this teaching through the sacred dance should be clearly understood by us, as enlightened by good teachers, through the wisdom scriptures like Thirumoolar's Thirumanthiram and Sivagnana Botham. Now, in this verse, some interpret "Aathi" as Brahma, "Nadu" as Vishnu, "Alavu" (measure) as Rudra, who is the end, and "Sothiyaai Unarvumaagi" as Maheswara, who possesses the Tirodhana Shakti (power of concealment) that obscures beings through the power of Maya. And through "Thontriya Porul" (manifested substance), they interpret it as Sadashiva Murthy, known as Sadakkiyam, which is the place from which these powers arise. Such a great Lord performs this sacred dance, the Pothunadam (common dance), in Thillai. Thillai is a city. Thillai was once a forest of Thillai trees. Therefore, it got the name Thillai. This city got the name Thillai. The Sanskrit word "Thilvam" became "Thillai" in Tamil. The name of the Thillai tree became associated with the forest abundant with them, and then that name was given to the city of Thillai, which was located where the Thillai forest once stood. Thus, Sekkizhar Swamigal praises the Lord who performs the Pothunadam in Thillai with "Potri Potri" (salutations, salutations) in the first verse. Now, the second verse: "Karpanai Kadandha Sothi Karunaiye Uruvamaagi Arputhak Kolam Needi Arumarai Sirathin Melaa Sirpara Viyomamaagum Thiruchitrambalaththul Nindru Porpudan Nadam Seigindra Poongazhal Potri Potri." Its meaning is: "Salutations, salutations to the lotus-like, anklet-adorned feet that gracefully dance within the Thiruchitrambalam, which is the form of the Gnanaakasham (sky of wisdom) revealed at the pinnacle of the rare Vedas (Upanishads), standing beyond all that is conceived by Pasu Gnanam and Pasa Gnanam, as a light that is compassion itself, enduring in a wondrous form that is difficult to comprehend." Now, Sivakavimani's commentary on this verse. "Karpanai Kadandha Jothi" (the light beyond conception) is first interpreted as the form of wisdom that transcends Maya, which is the primary cause of conceived forms. "Karpanai" refers to everything that is conceived. Similarly, it also has a second meaning: a light that transcends the conceptions fabricated by various religious proponents according to their own thoughts. Another meaning is that it is the luminous form that transcends the distinctions of Nishkala (formless) and Sakala (with form) manifestations like Sadashivam and Maheswaram. Thus, elders offer various interpretations. Furthermore, the idea is that the Lord is a self-effulgent light who conceives everything Himself, without being conceived by anyone above Him. This Lord, who is the light beyond conception, becomes the embodiment of compassion. Sekkizhar refers to this aspect as "Karunaiye Uruvamaagi" (becoming the embodiment of compassion), where the Lord, desiring nothing for Himself, takes the divine grace He bestows upon living beings as His very form. That very Lord stands as the "Sirpara Viyomam" (supreme sky of consciousness) above the "Arumarai Siram" (pinnacle of rare Vedas), which are the Upanishads. This "Sirpara Viyomam" is the Gnanaakasham, the substratum for the birth and re-absorption of the Vedas and other scriptures. The author of Koyil Puranam states this as "Sirparamam Ambaram." Therefore, "Sirpara Viyomamaam Thiruchitrambalam" refers to the Ambalam (hall) that is the Gnanaakasham. In this second verse, Sekkizhar Swamigal praises the anklet-adorned feet of such a Lord. Through these first two verses, Sekkizhar Swamigal composes a hymn of praise to the Lord of Dance. Sekkizhar, who came to sing the Purana of the three thousand Thillai Vazh Anthanars, who are the sacred devotees, praises and extols the glory of Nataraja Peruman, who resides in Thillai, in the first two verses. This is because this Ananda Nataraja Peruman, this Lord of Dance, is one of the three thousand Thillai Vazh Anthanars and is considered the foremost among them. Therefore, Sekkizhar Swamigal, who came to sing the Purana of the Thillai Vazh Anthanars, dedicates the first two verses to praising Nataraja Peruman, who is the foremost among those three thousand. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 02 — Thillai Vazh Anthanar

Thiruchitrambalam. In the Periyapuranam, within the Thillai Vazh Anthanar Sarukkam, we are contemplating the section called Thillai Vazh Anthanar Puranam. In this section, which came to sing the glory of the three thousand Thillai Vazh Anthanars, Sekkizhar Swamigal composes the first two verses as praises to Nataraja Peruman. We are going to contemplate this Periyapuranam, which describes the greatness of devotees, through the commentary of Sivakavimani and the Periyapuranam Soosanam (summary) by Arumuga Navalar of Jaffna, Sri Lanka. Therefore, praising and submitting to these two great devotees, we will continue our contemplation. Now, let us contemplate the summary that Arumuga Navalar provides for this section called Thillai Vazh Anthanar Puranam. Since the immovable (Sthavaram) universe (Andam) and the movable (Jangamam) body (Pindam) are equivalent, the Sushumna Nadi, located between Ida and Pingala in the body (Pindam), is equivalent to the Thillaivanam (forest of Thillai), located between Sri Lanka and the Himalayas in Bharat Khanda within the macrocosm (Brahmandam). In the Chandogya Upanishad, the Dahara Vidya (knowledge of the small space) states that one should know the extremely subtle consciousness residing in the space within the lotus-like abode (Pundarika Veedu) in the Brahmapura. Here, Brahmapura refers to this body, Pundarika Veedu refers to the lotus of the heart, Akasha refers to Parasakthi, and the extremely subtle consciousness refers to Param Brahma Shiva. If we apply this to the universe, this Brahmandam is called Brahmapura, the Thillaivanam within this Brahmandam is called Pundarika Veedu, the Akasha within Thillaivanam is called Thiruchitrambalam, which is Parasakthi, and the Param Brahma Shiva performing the dance in that Thiruchitrambalam is called the extremely subtle consciousness. This Akasha is not inert like the elemental Akasha but is consciousness itself. Therefore, this Gnanaakasham is called Chidambaram. This Chidambaram, in this place called Thillai, shines eternally without cessation. Hence, this Thillaivanam is also called Chidambaram. In that greatly glorious Thillaivanam, Lord Shiva, who is older than the oldest things, is enshrined in the form of a Shivalinga as the Thirumoolasthanam (main deity) to bestow grace upon all souls. To the south of that Thirumoolasthanam, there is the Kanakasabai (golden hall), which is the form of divine grace. In that Kanakasabai, Lord Shiva, who is an ocean of supreme compassion, performs the Ananda Thandavam (dance of bliss) eternally, witnessed by His divine consort, Sivakami Ammaiyar. The Brahmins who perform the Pooja and other rituals for the Thirumoolasthanam Lingam and the Sabhanayakar (Lord of the Hall) according to the Vedas and Shivagamas are called the Thillai Vazh Anthanars. There are three thousand of them. They are born in an unblemished lineage. They are entirely free from sin. They are like the embodiment of all virtues. They adhere to the Grihastha Ashrama (householder's stage of life). They have studied and understood the four Vedas – Rig, Yajur, Sama, Atharva – and the six Vedangas – Shiksha, Vyakarana, Chandas, Nirukta, Jyotisha, Kalpa – and the four Upangas – Mimamsa, Nyaya, Purana, Smriti. They meticulously maintain the three sacred fires – Ahavaniyam, Dakshinagni, Garhapatyam – according to scriptural injunctions. The Thillai Vazh Anthanars regularly perform the six duties prescribed for Brahmins: Adhyayanam (studying), Adhyapanam (teaching), Yajanam (performing sacrifices), Yajanam (causing sacrifices to be performed), Danam (giving gifts), and Pratigrahanam (receiving gifts), thereby averting the Kali Yuga's influence. These six duties are referred to in Tamil as Othal (studying), Othuviththal (teaching), Vettal (performing sacrifices), Vetpiththal (causing sacrifices to be performed), Eethal (giving), and Ettal (receiving). The Thillai Vazh Anthanars meticulously wear the Shiva symbols of Vibhuti (sacred ash) and Rudraksha beads. They consider their wealth to be the unwavering love fixed upon the sacred feet of Lord Shiva. They have received the four types of initiation: Samaya Deeksha, Vishesha Deeksha, Nirvana Deeksha, and Acharya Abhishekam. They have studied and understood all 28 Shaiva Agamas, from Kamikam to Vathulam. They diligently practice the four paths – Chariya, Kriya, Yoga, and Gnana – which are taught as the path to liberation by those Shaiva Agamas, without deviation. These Thillai Vazh Anthanars possess the greatness of having been declared by Sabhanayakar, Lord Nataraja Himself, as being one with Him. Not only that, when Lord Thyagaraja commanded Sundaramoorthy Nayanar to compose the Thiruththondaththogai, He Himself mentioned them with the sacred words, "Thillai Vazh Anthanartham Adiyaarkkum Adiyen." These Thillai Vazh Anthanars, true to their name, reside in Chidambaram, also known as Thillai. We have seen the greatness of Thillai as described in the Chandogya Upanishad. The Chandogya Upanishad states that one should worship the extremely subtle consciousness, Lord Shiva, who resides in the Gnanaakasham (sky of wisdom) in Chidambaram, Thillai, through the Dahara Vidya. The Kaivalya Upanishad describes the method of practicing this Dahara Vidya. The Kaivalya Upanishad states the following on how the Dahara Vidya should be practiced: One should sit in a comfortable posture in a secluded place, pure, with neck, head, and body straight, as an Atiyashramasta (one who has transcended the ashramas), controlling all senses, worshipping one's guru with devotion, considering the lotus of one's heart as pure and spotless, and in its center, meditate on the all-powerful Lord Shiva, who is clear, sorrowless, unthought of, unmanifest, of infinite forms, Shiva, immortal, the source of Brahma, the essence of That, without beginning, middle, or end, blue-throated, peaceful, the source of all beings, the witness of all. By doing so, one becomes a Rishi and transcends darkness. Furthermore, this Dahara Vidya states that one should understand that Lord Shiva is Brahma, He is Shiva, He is Indra, He is imperishable, supreme, self-effulgent, He is Vishnu, He is Prana, He is Kala, He is Agni, He is Chandra, He is all that exists and has existed, and He is eternal. By knowing Him thus, one will transcend death, and there is no other path to liberation. It is clearly stated that Mrityu (death) here refers to Anava Malam (ego impurity), as mentioned in the Mrigendra Agama. In this glorious sacred place of Chidambaram, the three thousand Thillai Vazh Anthanars, along with Vyagrapada Muni and Patanjali Muni, are experiencing supreme bliss by witnessing the Ananda Thandavam performed by Lord Shiva, an ocean of supreme compassion, in the Kanakasabai, which is pure Maya, sanctified by Parasakthi. At that time, Brahma began a Yagna (sacrificial ritual) in Antarvedi on the banks of the Ganga. For the purpose of that Yagna, he came to Chidambaram and, with the permission of Vyagrapada Muni, invited the Thillai Vazh Anthanars to the Yagna. After the Yagna was completed, Brahma obtained permission from Emperor Hiranyavarman to bring the three thousand Thillai Vazh Anthanars back to Chidambaram. Therefore, Hiranyavarman worshipped the three thousand Thillai Vazh Anthanars, seated them in his chariot, and brought them to Chidambaram. The chariot reached the northwest direction of Chidambaram. At that time, Vyagrapada Muni, who had come to receive the three thousand Thillai Vazh Anthanars, began to count them to show that all three thousand had returned. However, there were only 2999 people who disembarked from the chariot, and one was missing. As everyone was bewildered by the absence of one, Lord Shiva, who is easily accessible to devotees, declared for all to hear, "All these Thillai Vazh Anthanars are equal to Me. And I am equal to these Rishis. Therefore, they consider Me as one of them."

Chapter 03 — Thillai Vazh Anthanar

Thiruchitrambalam. We are contemplating the Thillai Vazh Anthanar Puranam within the Thillai Vazh Anthanar Sarukkam of Periyapuranam. Arumuga Navalar explained how the Thillai Vazh Anthanars live according to the Vedas, studying and understanding them, and adhering to the Vedic path without deviation. Now, he explains how the Thillai Vazh Anthanars live with an understanding of the Agamas. There are 28 Shaiva Agamas. These are Kamikam, Yogajam, Chintyam, Karanam, Ajitam, Deeptham, Sukshmam, Sahasram, Anshuman, Suprabhedam, Vijayam, Nisvasam, Svayambhuva, Analam, Veeram, Rauravam, Makutam, Vimalam, Chandragnyanam, Bimbam, Prothgeetham, Lalitam, Siddham, Santanam, Sarvoktam, Paramesvaram, Kiranam, and Vathulam. These Agamas are also known as Mantram, Tantram, and Siddhantam. For each of these 28 Shiva Agamas, there are one crore granthas (texts), totaling 28 crore granthas. Each of these 28 Agamas has four distinct sections: Gnana Padam, Yoga Padam, Kriya Padam, and Chariya Padam. Among these, Gnana Padam describes the nature of the three entities: Pati (Lord), Pasu (soul), and Pasam (bonds). Yoga Padam describes Shiva Yoga, which includes Pranayama and other practices. Kriya Padam describes the recitation of mantras, Sandhyavandanam, Pooja, Japa, Homam, and other rituals, as well as Samaya, Vishesha, Nirvana, and Acharya Abhishekas (initiations). Chariya Padam teaches the conduct appropriate for a devotee. The word Agama means that which came from a supremely trustworthy source. Furthermore, since 'A' means Pasam, 'Ga' means Pasu, and 'Ma' means Pati, the best meaning for Agama is a text that teaches the characteristics of the three entities. Also, 'A' signifies Shiva Gnanam (divine wisdom), 'Ga' signifies Moksha (liberation), and 'Ma' signifies Mala Nasham (destruction of impurities). Therefore, it is also said that Agamas are called Agamas because they destroy the impurities of souls, generate Shiva Gnanam, and grant Moksha. In addition to these 28 Agamas, there are 207 Upagamas (sub-Agamas) from Narasingam to Visvanmakam. The Thillai Vazh Anthanars, having received Shiva Deeksha (initiation), study and understand these Shaiva Agamas and Upagamas, and they practice the four paths taught by them. The Thillai Vazh Anthanars perform daily Pujas, festivals, and other rituals in the Chidambaram temple according to the rules of the Shaiva Agamas. The installation of Parartha Lingams, Parartha Pujas, and festivals are prescribed by the Shaiva Agamas. The Agamic rule is that one must receive Shiva Deeksha before studying the Shaiva Agamas. It is stated in the 25th chapter of the Navamsha of Shiva Rahasyam and in the Chidambara Mahatmyam that the Thillai Vazh Anthanars have received Samaya Deeksha, Vishesha Deeksha, Nirvana Deeksha, and Acharya Abhishekam, and that they have studied and understood the Moola Agamas and Upagamas. Agama is an authoritative statement. Agamas are of five types: Laukikam (worldly), Vaidakam (Vedic), Adhyatmikam (spiritual), Atimargam (transcendental), and Mantram (mantric). The Kamika Agama states that the Rig, Yajur, Sama, and Atharva Vedas originated from the other faces of Shiva, and that the 28 Agamas, including Kamikam, originated from the Urdhva Mukha (upward-facing face) of Lord Shiva, which is the Ishana Mukha. Thus, the Thillai Vazh Anthanars are those who have learned, understood, and recited the Vedas and Shaiva Agamas, and who live according to them. When Thirugnanasambandar visited Thillai, these three thousand Thillai Vazh Anthanars appeared to him as Shiva Gananathars (chiefs of Shiva's retinue). Thirugnanasambandar pointed out this nature to Thiruneelakanda Yazhpanar. Sekkizhar begins by saying, "I am going to narrate the history of these greatly glorious Thillai Vazh Anthanars, praising the sacred feet of the Lord of Dance, considering Him as their leader." The Thillai Vazh Anthanars possess a sacred form adorned with sacred ash. They have the great fortune of being devoted servants dedicated to the Lord of Dance. They are the limit of all greatness and are foremost in the conduct that enables one to worship and live. Driven by their love for the Lord, which grows ever stronger, they live a life of penance, clinging to the sacred feet of the Lord as their sole support. The Thillai Vazh Anthanars have the great fortune of adorning the Lord's sacred form with beautiful divine ornaments according to scriptural injunctions. They praise the Lord with Vedic hymns and perform inner services within the Lord's temple. The Thillai Vazh Anthanars are blessed with the glory of praising and singing about the Lord using Vedic hymns such as Namakams, Chamakams, Rudram, Upanishadic verses describing the Lord's nature, Shivagama verses, and Tamil scriptures like Thevaram and Thiruvachagam. They perform all aspects of Pooja, including Avahanam (invocation), according to Agamic rules. They also perform services such as waving chamaras (whisks) and fans. Sekkizhar Swamigal refers to these as "Mangala Thondugal" (auspicious services). Furthermore, "Mangala Thondugal" also refers to daily Pujas and special festival celebrations. "Agampadi Thondu" refers to the inner services performed within the temple, such as Abhishekam (ablution) and other sacred duties related to the Lord's sacred form. "Purath Thondu" refers to external services performed in the outer courtyards and outside the temple, such as sweeping, cleaning, lighting lamps, singing hymns, dancing, and decorating the streets. The Thillai Vazh Anthanars, considering righteousness as their wealth, diligently study the four Vedas and six Angas, which follow the path of truth, becoming proficient in them. For the welfare of the world, they meticulously maintain the three sacred fires—Ahavaniyam, Dakshinagni, and Garhapatyam—according to the Vedic injunctions. Ahavaniyam is maintained in a square fire pit in the northeast of the Yagna hall for the Devas. The second fire, Dakshinagni, is maintained in a crescent-shaped pit in the south, and it is said to be for the Pitrus (ancestors). The third fire, Garhapatyam, is maintained in a circular pit next to the Ahavaniyam and is nurtured by householders practicing the Grihastha Ashrama. Furthermore, fire is categorized as Vaidika Agni, Shaiva Agni, and Vaindhava Agni. Thus, the Thillai Vazh Anthanars perform the Yaga and other karmas found in Purva Mimamsa and the Brahma Upasana found in Uttara Mimamsa. The Thillai Vazh Anthanars follow the good conduct known through generations in both their maternal and paternal lineages. They perform the six duties of Brahmins and have prevented the arrival of Kali. The Thillai Vazh Anthanars live by the understanding that the Brahmananda (bliss of Brahman) mentioned in the Vedas is indeed Shivananda (bliss of Shiva). They live contemplating Shiva according to the Vedic injunctions: "Shivam, Chaturtham, Shantam, Advaitam, Anyante." They live according to the four paths of Chariya, Kriya, Yoga, and Gnana, as taught in the Agamas. Furthermore, they have learned and understood these without any doubt or error. Due to their inherent Shiva-nature, the Thillai Vazh Anthanars have shed impurities like Anava Malam and have attained a divine state, living as three thousand divine Brahmins, praising the Lord in this very birth. They have received that blessed life of penance. Therefore, there is no greater blessing for them to receive. Thus, the Thillai Vazh Anthanars are incomparable to themselves. There is no one else equal to them. They consider Lord Nataraja Himself as one of the three thousand. Therefore, some say that they are divine Brahmins who have attained the state of Jivanmuktas (liberated while living). The ultimate goal for souls, according to spiritual texts, is Shiva-peru (the blessing of Shiva). The Thillai Vazh Anthanars have attained this Shiva-peru in this very birth. There is no greater blessing than this. Therefore, the Thillai Vazh Anthanars have nothing more to achieve. Thus, there is no one else equal to them. When Sundaramoorthy Swamigal prayed to the Lord to compose the Thiruththondaththogai, Lord Shiva, the Thyagaraja Peruman of Thiruvarur, Himself praised them with the phrase "Thillai Vazh Anthanartham Adiyaarkkum Adiyen." Sekkizhar Swamigal wonders if he can truly describe their greatness. The fivefold dance of the Lord, which pervades and enlightens this entire world, is praised. The lotus-like feet that perform that dance are praised. The Thillai Vazh Anthanars, who live praising this, are dedicated to serving the Lord's feet. They adorn the Lord's sacred form. They praise the Lord with Vedas. They perform the inner services within the temple. They diligently maintain the three sacred fires according to scriptural injunctions for the world to flourish. They have learned the four Vedas and six Angas and are proficient in them. They are of pure lineage and possess pure conduct. They have averted Kali by performing the six duties. They hold the wealth of sacred ash as their support. They consider the love fixed on Lord Shiva as their greatest blessing. They have understood the four paths, including Gnana. They are skilled in charity and penance. They live happily in a householder's life, embodying honor and patience. They have attained the Lord in this very life. They are incomparable. They are glorified by the sacred words of Lord Thiruvarur, placed first in Thiruththondaththogai. Sekkizhar, marveling, bows down, wondering if he can truly describe the greatness of these Thillai Vazh Anthanars. May these highly exalted Thillai Vazh Anthanars in this vast world eternally cherish, praise, and live by the gracious dance of the Lord in Thillai Chitrambalam. With this blessing, praise, and adoration, Sekkizhar Swamigal concludes this section of Thillai Vazh Anthanar Puranam. Next, Sekkizhar begins to sing the history and glory of the first Nayanar in Periyapuranam, Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar, in the second Purana, Thiruneelakanda Nayanar Puranam. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.
