# English

Chapter 01 — KazharChinga Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Part 986, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Kadal Suzhntha Sarukkam, KazharChinga Nayanar Puranam. Following the Karaikandan Sarukkam, which is the ninth sarukkam in the Periyapuranam composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, the tenth sarukkam is Kadal Suzhntha Sarukkam. We know that for each verse in the Thiruthondathogai Thiruppathikam composed by our Lord Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman has named the sarukkam after the initial lines of that verse. In that manner, the ninth verse of Thiruthondathogai Thiruppathikam, which begins with "Kadal Suzhntha," is "Kadal Suzhntha ulagelam kaakkindra peruman kadavar kon KazharChingan adiyarkkum adiyen, madal suzhntha thar Nambi Idangazhikkum Thanjai mannanam Seruthunai than adiyarkkum adiyen, pudai suzhntha puli athal mel aravada aadi ponnaddikke manam vaitha Pugazhthunaikkum adiyen, adal suzhntha vel Nambi Kotpulikkum adiyen, Aruran Aruril Ammanukku ale." This is the ninth verse of Thiruthondathogai. The tenth sarukkam, Kadal Suzhntha Sarukkam, narrates the puranas of the Nayanmars mentioned in this verse. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman has named this sarukkam "Kadal Suzhntha" after the first part of this sarukkam, which begins with "Kadal Suzhntha ulagelam kaakkindra peruman." In that way, the sacred history of the Nayanmars in the tenth Kadal Suzhntha Sarukkam is structured as the sacred puranas of five Nayanmars: KazharChinga Nayanar Puranam, Idangazhi Nayanar Puranam, Seruthunai Nayanar Puranam, Pugazhthunai Nayanar Puranam, and Kotpuli Nayanar Puranam. These five sacred puranas are included in this sarukkam. Among these five puranas, the first is KazharChinga Nayanar Puranam, which is the 53rd puranam in Periyapuranam. First, the section that praises this Nayanar in the Thogai Nool (compilation text) is in the ninth verse of Thiruthondathogai, where our Lord Sundaramoorthy Swamigal praises this Nayanar as "Kadal Suzhntha ulagelam kaakkindra peruman kadavar kon KazharChingan adiyarkkum adiyen." Its meaning is, "I am a servant to the devotees of KazharChingar, the king of the Kadavar lineage, who rules and protects the entire world surrounded by the sea." Since it is stated in the present tense "kaakkindra" (protects), it is believed that this king might have reigned during Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's time. "Kadavar Kon" means he belonged to the Kadavar lineage. This is the Pallava dynasty; the Pallava lineage and the Kadavar lineage are one and the same. He was named KazharChingar. Inscriptions and other sources suggest that he might be Nandivarman III. Here too, historians believe that his queen, Sanga, was a Jain woman. Thus, the Thiruthondathogai, a compilation text, praises this Nayanar. Now, in the Vagai Nool (classification text), the 64th verse of Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi, composed by Nambiandar Nambigal Peruman, praises KazharChinga Nayanar. That verse is as follows: "Madhavaththor thangal vaippinukku Arur manikku vaitha pothinai than mondha devi than mookkai ariya porkai kaadhi vaithanro arivathu endru angaval kaiththadindhan nadha moiththar vandu kindu paingothai KazharChingane." In Thiruvarur, the queen, the chief queen of KazharChinga Nayanar, took a flower placed for the Lord of Thiruvarur and smelled it. For this offense of smelling a flower kept for Shiva puja, Seruthunai Nayanar cuts off the queen's nose. KazharChinga Nayanar, seeing this, immediately asks, "If the nose smelled the flower kept for the Lord, wasn't it the hand that took it?" and cuts off her hand as well. Nambiandar Nambigal Peruman explains the history of the Nayanar, stating that such a great one is KazharChingar, in this verse. Now, in the Viri Nool (detailed text), Periyapuranam composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, KazharChinga Nayanar Puranam is the 53rd puranam, explained by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman in 13 verses. In order, it is the 53rd puranam and the first puranam in the 10th sarukkam, Kadal Suzhntha Sarukkam. Now, the first verse: "Padimisai nigazhntha thollaip pallavar kulathu vandhar kadimathil moonrum setra gangaivar sadaiyar seyya adimalarandri veru ondru arivinil kuriya neermai kodinedum thanai mannar kokkazharChingar enbar." He was born in the ancient Pallava lineage that flourished on earth. He was one whose mind always focused on the red lotus feet of the Lord and nothing else. He was known as KazharChingar, the king with a victorious banner. The second verse: "Kadavar kurisil aram kazhar perumChinganar tham adagam meru villar arulinal amaril sendru koodalar munaigal saya vadapulam kavarnthukondu nadara neriyil vaiga nanneri valarkkum nalil." KazharPerumChinganar, the chief of the Kadavars, by the grace of the Lord who has Meru as his bow, went to war, defeated his enemies, conquered the northern lands, and ruled the country righteously, fostering good dharma. The Kadavar lineage is a branch of the Cholas. That this lineage branched off from the Pallavas is understood from the phrase "Pallavar kulathu vandhar." Since this lineage is distinct from the three great kings (Chera, Chola, Pandya), Srimath Umapathi Sivachariyar Swamigal has classified this king as one of the minor chieftains among the Thiruthondars. The Nayanar's name "Perum KazharChinganar" appears here. KazharChingam means one who is like a lion wearing anklets (kazhal). Thus, this is a descriptive name. This king conquered the northern countries through war by the grace of the Lord. He conquered these lands not out of greed for territory, but in accordance with royal justice and divine grace. Although the conquered lands were in the north, they are specifically considered to be the lands of the Chalukya kings. This is because history tells us that many long wars occurred between the Pallavas and the Chalukyas. We saw about the Battle of Vatapi in the north in the Siruthonda Nayanar Puranam. Thus, having conquered these lands, he ruled well, fostering both the worldly justice of "aram neriyil" and the Saivite path of "nanneri." He promoted both paths. The third verse: "Kuvalayathu aranaar mevum koilgal palavum sendru thavalarum anbil thazhndhu thakka meiththondru seivar Sivapuri enna mannum then Thiruvarur eythi pavamaruththu atkolvar tham koilum paniyap pukkar." While ruling in this manner, KazharChinga Nayanar visited many temples where Lord Shiva resides on this earth, bowed with unwavering devotion, and performed true services. He undertook a pilgrimage to Shiva temples. In doing so, he arrived at Then Thiruvarur, renowned as a city of Shiva. Thiruvarur is a city called Sivapuri. It is a city that makes one feel as if it is Kailasa itself. Since all those born in Thiruvarur attain liberation from rebirth, it is named Sivapuri. There, in Then Thiruvarur, he entered the temple of the Lord, who is capable of cutting off rebirth and taking one as His servant. It is the place where the Lord resides as Puttidam Konda Nathar and Thyagesar. He went there to worship. His queen also accompanied him on this pilgrimage. KazharChinga Nayanar was a great devotee of Shiva, so he went into the temple to worship the Lord. However, his queen, though accompanying the king, did not worship the Lord but walked around the temple, enjoying its beauty. We will continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.

King KazharChinga Nayanar, accompanied by his five types of royal entourages, who are called the five groups (Aivagai Kuzhukkal), consisting of ministers, priests, commanders, envoys, and spies, entered the temple of the Lord who resides in the anthill (Puttidam Konda Peruman) and worshipped Him. At that time, the queen who accompanied the king—the king had many wives, and among them, the chief wife was the Pattathu Arasi (crowned queen)—instead of going with the king to worship the Lord inside the temple, went to admire the beauty of the Thiruvarur temple. Historians mention her name as Sanga. She was very beautiful, like a mother to her subjects, and as if the king's fortune had taken form. This queen of the king was the daughter of Amoghavarsha Nripatunga, a great Jain emperor. Although she was inclined towards Jainism, the king married her for political reasons. Therefore, the queen was inclined towards Jainism. Hence, instead of engaging in worshipping Shiva, she engaged in admiring the temple's beauty and went to see it. She was the king's chief queen, so she accompanied her husband to Shiva temples. While doing so, she observed the various mandapams and sculptures within the temple. The fifth and sixth verses: "Koyilai valam kondu angadku uzhaviya perumaiyellam sayan ma mayile polvan thaniththani kandu vandhu thuyamen palliththamam thodukkum mandapaththin pangar meyathor puthuppu angu vizhundhathu ondru eduththu mondhal. As the queen circumambulated the temple, observing all its glories one by one, like a beautiful peacock, she came to the mandapam where soft garlands were made. This was the Pushpa Mandapam, where flowers for the Lord were collected and garlands were strung. This Thiruppalliththamam Thirumandapam is said to be an inner mandapam of the Thiruvarur Araneri temple, located on the southeast side of the Thiruvarur Poongoyil. Inside this mandapam, a flower fell. Flowers that fall inside the flower mandapam are also meant to be offered to the Lord. So, as the queen came, a new flower fell there. The queen picked it up and smelled it. At that moment, Seruthunaiyar, a pure devotee, saw this. Seruthunai Nayanar is one of the 63 Nayanmars. His sacred puranam is yet to come. That great devotee, Seruthunai Nayanar, seeing the queen's action, considered it a Shiva-aparadham (offense against Shiva). He decided that an offense against Shiva must be punished immediately. For the offense of picking up this flower in the flower mandapam's sacred courtyard and smelling it, he took a weapon and cut off the queen's nose to punish her for the Shiva-aparadham she committed.
