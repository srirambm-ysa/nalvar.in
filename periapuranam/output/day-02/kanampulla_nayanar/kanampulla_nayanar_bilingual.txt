Chapter 01 — KanamPulla Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Part 980, authored by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Karaikkandan Sarukkam, KanamPulla Nayanar Puranam. Following the Poyyadimai Illadha Pulavar Sarukkam in the Periyapuranam authored by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, the ninth sarukkam is the Karaikkandan Sarukkam. The Karaikkandan Sarukkam is dedicated to the eighth verse of the Thiruthondathogai Thiruppathikam, composed by our Lord Sundaramurthy Swamigal. The eighth verse of the Thiruthondathogai is as follows: "I am a servant to KanamPulla Nambi, who held the ankleted feet of Karaikkandan as his protection, and to Kari. I am a servant to the devotees of Ninraseer Nedumaran, who conquered Nelveli with a full heart. I am a servant to the devotees of Vayilar, whose ancient Mayilai shines with light that dispels darkness like red coral from the sea. I am a servant to Munai Aduvar, the Nambi with a spear at his waist. Aruran is a servant to Amman in Arur." This is the eighth verse of the Thiruthondathogai. The Nayanmars praised in this verse are KanamPulla Nayanar, Kari Nayanar, Ninraseer Nedumaran Nayanar, Vayilar Nayanar, and Munai Aduvar Nayanar. These five Nayanmars are praised in this Thiruthondathogai verse. Accordingly, five Thiruppuranams are structured in this sarukkam. Among them, the first Thiruppuranam in this sarukkam is the KanamPulla Nayanar Puranam. In the sequential order, this ninth sarukkam, according to the Periyapuranam sequence, contains the KanamPulla Nayanar Puranam as the 48th Thiruppuranam. First, in the compilation work Thiruthondathogai, composed by our Lord Sundaramurthy Swamigal, KanamPulla Nayanar is praised. He praises this Nayanar by saying, "I am a servant to KanamPulla Nambi, who held the ankleted feet of Karaikkandan as his protection." The meaning of this is, "I am a servant to the great KanamPulla Nayanar, who considered the ankleted feet of Lord Shiva, the Lord with a dark throat (due to poison), as his protection." It says, "Karaikkandan kazhal adiye kaappuk kondirundha." Kaappu means protection. This indicates the profound nature of this Nayanar's devotion. KanamPullar is the sacred name of the Nayanar. This Nayanar, with great devotion to Lord Shiva and his devotees, performed the sacred service of lighting lamps daily in the temple. Through this service of lighting lamps, the Nayanar's great wealth diminished. He went to Thiruppuleeswaram in Chidambaram and regularly performed the service of lighting lamps there. As all his possessions and wealth dwindled, he even sold the items in his house to light the lamps. After that too was exhausted, he used his physical labor to cut a type of grass called Kanam Pul, sold it, and used the money to light the lamps. One day, the Kanam Pul he had cut did not sell. So, he put that very grass into the lamp and lit it. Even that was not enough to light the lamps. Therefore, with love that melted his bones, this Nayanar transformed his own head into a lamp and lit it. The Lord bestowed upon him great auspicious grace. Because he sold Kanam Pul to light lamps, and also because he put Kanam Pul itself into the lamp and lit it, this Nayanar received the sacred name KanamPullar. Now, in the 58th verse of the Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi, composed by Nambiyandar Nambigal, this story is very briefly presented in one verse, sung by Nambiyandar Nambigal. That verse is as follows: "In the good city of Irukkuvelur, though in poverty, he entered Thillai, the city of gold, and lit lamps with good grass for the Lord of Pulicharam, the King. He is KanamPullan, our father, the father of our father, whose Lord has a stone-like hide." This is the verse. KanamPulla Nayanar was born in a sacred place called Irukkuvelur. While living there and performing sacred service, poverty struck, so he went to the city of Thillai and lit lamps with good Kanam Pul for the Lord who resides in the Thiruppuleeswaram temple there. He is the great KanamPullar, our father and the chief of our fathers, who has strong, stone-like shoulders. This is its meaning. How does Nambiyandar Nambigal Peruman praise KanamPulla Nayanar in this verse? He praises him as "Endhai Thandhai Piran," meaning, "He is the Lord of my father, his father, and all my ancestors, who have served him generation after generation." This is how Nambiyandar Nambigal Peruman praises him. This shows his immense love for devotees. Nambiyandar Nambigal Peruman belongs to the Adisaiva lineage. Adisaiva Sivacharyas are servants only to Lord Shiva. Our Lord Sundaramurthy Swamigal was also born in the sacred lineage of Adisaiva Sivacharyas. When Sundaramurthy Swamigal praised the Nayanmars in the Thiruthondathogai, he declared, "I am a servant to all devotees, and we are servants to their servants." Following this same sentiment, the author of the lineage text, Nambiyandar Nambigal Peruman, who was also an Adisaiva, also stated, "Endhai Thandhai Piran." Since even the Adisaivas, who are superior to all other lineages, declared themselves servants to devotees, there is no impediment for others to also be servants to the Nayanmars and their devotees. This is why our Lord Sundaramurthy Swamigal, who composed the Thiruthondathogai, was born in the Adisaiva Sivacharya lineage, and through him, this was recited as a divine intention. This is the divine intention of the Lord. "Endhai Thandhai Piran" is a traditional way of speaking to refer to all ancestors. "En thanadhu sendru nilai endhai than thandhai amar inba nagardhan" is a sacred saying from Aludaiya Pillaiyar's Thevaram. We saw in the Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar Puranam, "Our Lord, my father, my father's father, and all our lineage, you are our Lord," indicating a life of continuous devotion, which is a superior life. Thus, the lineage text Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi praises this Nayanar in this manner. Now, in the extensive work Periyapuranam, authored by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, the KanamPulla Nayanar Puranam describes the historical account and character of KanamPulla Nayanar. The first verse in it is: "Irukkuvelur is a renowned city in this world, where magnificent mansions and prosperous families abound, on the southern bank of the great Vadavellaru, where groves of jackfruit trees overflow with honey from their long pods, filling the ponds and irrigating the fields." Here, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman sings about the sacred place where KanamPulla Nayanar was born. This place, called Irukkuvelur, is currently known as Belur, near Valappadi, close to Salem. This place is now called Belur. It is located south of Attur, on the way from Salem to Vriddhachalam. It is situated on the northern slope of the Pachaimalai hills. Here, there is a very ancient temple of the Lord. The sacred name of the Lord is Than Thondri Eeswarar. The sacred name of the Goddess is Aram Valartha Nayagi. In this temple, in front of Nandi Devar, there is a sculpture of KanamPulla Nayanar holding a lamp in his hand. KanamPulla Nayanar, due to poverty, left this place and went to Thillai, where he performed sacred service at Thiruppuleeswaram and attained liberation. There is also a Sthala Puranam for this place. Near this sacred place, on the mountain slopes, Kanam Pul (a type of grass) grows in abundance. Therefore, it is believed that even when our Nayanar was here, and before he left, when poverty struck, he was engaged in the business of cutting and selling this grass. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman praises Irukkuvelur as "Thirukkilarum Irukkuvelur," meaning a city rich in wealth and glory. It is a city with many mansions. It has the urban characteristic of many prosperous families living closely together. It is a city on the southern bank of the Vadavellaru, a river that brings great prosperity. There are two Vellaru rivers in Tamil Nadu. The Vadavellaru mentioned here originates in the Salem region and flows up to Thanjavur, the Chola Mandalam, forming the northern boundary of the Chola country, and merges with the sea near Parangipettai.

The other Vellaru, known as Thenvellaru, forms the northern boundary of the Pandya country and the southern boundary of the Chola country, merging with the sea near Mimisal. The Vadavellaru mentioned here has good groves on its southern bank. The honey dripping from the long pods of the jackfruit trees in these groves fills the ponds, thereby irrigating the fields. Such is the glory of the place called Irukkuvelur. Thus, the greatness of the Nayanar's birthplace is praised. Let us continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 02 — KanamPulla Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Part 981, authored by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Karaikkandan Sarukkam, KanamPulla Nayanar Puranam. The second verse in the KanamPulla Nayanar Puranam. "In that city, he was the chief among the heads of families, possessing immeasurable and endless great wealth, and he excelled in the world with incomparable noble qualities, holding the conviction that the true essence is the ankleted feet of the Lord." There were many families living in the sacred place called Irukkuvelur. While each family had its own head, KanamPulla Nayanar shone as the chief of all these heads. He was a great wealthy person, possessing immeasurable wealth. His wealth was so vast that its limits could not be ascertained. He lived with excellence in this world due to his incomparable noble qualities. He was deeply devoted, believing that the true essence, the ultimate reality, was the sacred feet of the Lord. The imperishable nature is the nature of the Lord. He believed that the ankleted feet of the Lord bestow that nature. Therefore, with the understanding that all this wealth was meant for the Lord's service, he performed sacred service. The third verse. "Believing that the imperishable great wealth's ultimate purpose was this, he incessantly lit lamps in Shiva's temples, praising Him with his tongue. When poverty befell him, he reached Thiruthillai, the abode of the Lord of Lords." Convinced that the greatest benefit of imperishable great wealth was this, he lit eternally radiant lamps in the temples of Lord Shiva. Our Nayanar praised Him. As a result, poverty came upon him. The sacred saying from Thevaram, "The wealth of praising the Lord's feet is true wealth," refers to the Lord who possesses great wealth that removes all poverty. Therefore, when poverty struck, our Nayanar went to Thiruthillai, where the Lord resides. In the city of Thiruthillai, with devotion fixed on the dancing feet in the beautiful sacred hall, the Nayanar worshipped and desired to remain there. In Thillai, there is the Thiruppuleeswaram temple, worshipped by Sage Vyagrapada. There, he performs the sacred service of lighting lamps. He sells all the items in his house and uses the money to light the lamps. This is mentioned in the fourth verse. Continuing with the fifth verse. "In the city of Thillai, he worshipped with devotion fixed on the dancing feet in the sacred hall and remained there. When the act of lighting lamps for the Lord, who burned the three cities, in Thiruppuleeswaram, by selling all household items, ceased, he, fearing to beg from others, gathered Kanam Pul through physical effort, sold it for a fair price, exchanged the money for ghee, and lit the pure sacred lamp, the steadfast true devotee." Having come from his village due to poverty, he sells all the items he brought with him to sustain himself and lights lamps in the Thiruppuleeswaram temple. When even that act ceased, meaning when there were no more items left to sell in his house, he, fearing to beg (to take alms), gathered a type of grass called Kanam Pul through his physical effort. He cut it, sold it, and used the money to perform the sacred task of lighting the lamps. "Ayalavarpal irappu anji" (fearing to beg from others). We contemplate here all the meanings expressed in the chapter on "Fear of Begging" in Thirukkural. In that chapter, Parimelazhagar explains that even acts of charity should be performed by earning money through one's own physical labor and effort, rather than by begging. Therefore, we see that our great Nayanmars performed these noble sacred services using the wealth earned through their own efforts, as seen in the Thiruppuranams, including the sacred history of Kaliya Nayanar. Thus, the professions undertaken by the Nayanmars were not for their own survival. They performed them so that the sacred service they had undertaken for the Lord would continue without interruption. Here, our Nayanar, who had until now performed that sacred service relying on other worldly possessions, now undertook to perform it with the help of his physical effort. Having performed worship with material possessions, he now performed sacred service through the fruit of his body's labor, toiling and striving, believing that this was the purpose of having a body. Kanam Pul is a type of grass. It is used for purposes such as roofing huts. It is found in abundance in small forests and hilly areas. This Nayanar was engaged in the business of cutting and selling this grass. With the money he earned, he would buy ghee and other necessities and perform the service of lighting lamps. The sixth verse. "As he continued to light the lamps in this manner, one day, the grass he had painstakingly cut and brought to sell did not find a buyer anywhere. Yet, unwilling to abandon this service, he placed that very grass in the lamp and lit the beautiful lamp." In this situation, as he was lighting the lamps, one day, the grass he had painstakingly cut and gathered did not sell at all. It remained unsold. Nevertheless, unwilling to abandon the sacred service of lighting lamps, he placed that very grass in the lamp and lit the beautiful lamp. The seventh verse. "Earlier, he would light the lamps without fail for the prescribed watch. When the soft grass was insufficient to light the lamp, the devotee, whose love was true, then lit the next lamp with his own sacred head, melting his bones, and thereby burned away the ties of his two karmas." He used to light the lamps for the prescribed watch, the period of time known as 'yamam,' without fail. However, this grass was not enough for that. Lighting the lamp for the prescribed watch was a regular sacred service he had undertaken, stating, "I will light the lamp for so many yamams." One yamam is a period of seven and a quarter 'nazhigai'. This grass was insufficient to light the lamp for that duration. When he could not light a ghee lamp, he lit a grass lamp, and now even that was exhausted. Therefore, as the next lamp, the Nayanar lit his own sacred head, melting his bones, setting it on fire. He became one who burned away the ties of his two karmas. He lit the lamp on his head. In earlier days, men also kept a tuft of hair on their heads. He burned that very hair as a lamp. He burned away the ties of his two karmas. The two karmas lead to taking birth in this world. Here, the act of lighting the lamp with his head did not merely end with lighting the lamp; it also served to burn away the ties of his two karmas, yielding the benefit of no more births. The eighth verse. "Our Lord, having taken to heart this sacred service, bestowed great auspicious grace upon the peerless devotee who lit the principal lamp with overflowing love. Our Lord, KanamPullar, then sat before Him in Shiva's world, worshipped by all." Our Lord, Lord Shiva, took this sacred service to heart. He bestowed great auspicious grace upon KanamPulla Nayanar, the peerless devotee who lit the principal lamp with overflowing love. Lord Shiva brought our great Lord KanamPulla Nayanar into Shiva's world and graciously allowed him to reside there joyfully. This is the great auspicious grace. Burning hair in front of the Lord, and burning the body in fire and giving up one's life, these acts, wherever they are done, are inauspicious and improper. That is the general rule. Moreover, doing these in front of a Shiva temple – burning one's hair, or burning one's body in fire and giving up one's life – is even more inauspicious and improper. However, here, because these acts were performed with overflowing love, they became auspicious and pleasing to the Lord. Therefore, the Lord bestowed great auspicious grace and placed him in Shiva's world, the path that leads to no more births. Regarding this Thiruppuranam, Pandit Mo. Kandaiya Asuchanam writes: "The extreme state of love sometimes leads to transgressing rules of conduct and rituals. In temples, there are prescribed and prohibited actions. Prescribed actions are 'acharam' (proper conduct), and they must be done. Prohibited actions are 'anacharam' (improper conduct) and must be avoided. These are fundamental in Saivism. For those who adhere to the path of Chariya and Kriya, these rules are very strict, and they are explained in the primary texts of Saiva religion. However, for great souls who have attained the ultimate state of true knowledge, realizing their intimate connection with the Lord, these rules sometimes become exceptions. We see this in the Puranas. Kannappa Nayanar, who attained liberation and supreme bliss by worshipping with 'uchishta neeru' (water from his mouth) for ablution and offering 'uchishta unavu' (food tasted by him) as 'naivedyam,' which are anacharam. Sakkiya Nayanar, who attained liberation by worshipping the Shiva Lingam with 'vanmalar' (hard flowers, i.e., stones) instead of soft flowers. Kaliya Nayanar, who attempted to light a lamp by filling the lamp with his own blood and attained supreme bliss by the grace of Lord Shiva. These are evident in the histories of such Nayanmars. Even adjusting one's hair in a temple is considered anacharam. But here, KanamPulla Nayanar's act of using his own hair as fuel in the lamp to light it was accepted by the Lord Himself because it was done out of love. The issue here is not whether it was oil or hair in the lamp. The special thing here is his true and overflowing love, which melted his bones, as he lit the lamp. That became his act. That became the profound specialty of the Nayanar's act. This is his spiritual high quality. Because it is stated that the Nayanar 'burned away the ties of his two karmas,' we understand that Lord Shiva, without considering whether this act was a fault or a virtue, took his steadfast love to heart and bestowed great auspicious grace. Therefore, it is said that in the context of the steadfast love of true devotees, even their faults are considered virtues by Lord Shiva. This demonstrates the supreme glory of Lord Shiva. That is why our Lord Sundaramurthy Swamigal, in one of his Thevaram verses praising the supreme glory of Lord Shiva, praises KanamPulla Nayanar among the eight Nayanmars. That verse is: "Nattramil valla Gnanasambandhan, Navinukku Araiyan, Naalai Povanaam, Katta Soodhan, Narchakkiyan, Kannappan, KanamPullan – seeing that even if these commit faults, You consider them virtues, I have taken refuge at Your ankleted feet, O Lord of Thiruppunkur, surrounded by ponds where golden lotus flowers bloom." This is the sacred saying of our Lord Sundaramurthy Swamigal, a Thevaram verse dedicated to the sacred place of Thiruppunkur. Now, the ninth and concluding verse of this Nayanar's Puranam: "Having praised the feet of KanamPulla Nayanar, who in this great Kali Yuga lit a lamp with his own head for the Lord who wears a great garland, we shall now narrate the sacred service performed by Kariyar, who was born in the sacred Kadavur, adorned with fragrant flower gardens." Here, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, following his tradition, concludes the sacred history he has been narrating and introduces the next history. Praising the sacred feet of KanamPulla Nayanar, who in this world lit a lamp with his own head for Lord Shiva, and with that support, he says, "We shall now speak of the sacred service performed by Kari Nayanar, who was born in Thirukkadavur." Thus, he concludes the Thiruppuranam of KanamPulla Nayanar and begins to compose the Thiruppuranam of Kari Nayanar. We too conclude the Thiruppuranam of KanamPulla Nayanar at this point and, with the support of his sacred feet, shall continue to contemplate the Thiruppuranam of Kari Nayanar. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of our Lord KanamPulla Nayanar. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of our Lord Sundaramurthy Swamigal. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of Nambiyandar Nambigal Peruman. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the revered commentator. Thiruchitrambalam.
