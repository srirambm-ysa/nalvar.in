Chapter 01 — Kulacchirai Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 478, Thirunindra Sarukkam, Kulacchirai Nayanar Puranam. In this Sarukkam of Periya Puranam, known as Thirunindra Sarukkam, the second Puranam, following the Puranam of Thirunavukkarasar Peruman, is the Puranam of Kulacchirai Nayanar. Sekkizhar Peruman has composed this extensive work, basing each Sarukkam on a verse from Sundaramurthy Swamigal's Thiruthondathogai, and narrating the history of the devotees praised in those verses within their respective Sarukkams. In this manner, the fourth verse in Thiruthondathogai is structured as "Thirunindra Chemmaiye Chemmaiya Konda Thirunavukkaraiyan Than Adiyarkkum Adiyen Perunambi Kulacchiraithan Adiyarkkum Adiyen Perumizhalai Kurumbarkkum Peyarkkum Adiyen Oru Nambi Appoothi Adiyarkkum Adiyen Olipunalsuzh Saathamangai Neelanakarkku Adiyen Arunambi Naminanthi Adiyarkkum Adiyen Aaruran Aaruril Ammanukku Aale." This Sarukkam, the fifth in order, is named Thirunindra Sarukkam, because this verse begins with "Thirunindra Chemmaiye." According to the line "Thirunindra Chemmaiye Chemmaiya Konda Thirunavukkaraiyan Than Adiyarkkum Adiyen," the Puranam of Thirunavukkarasu Nayanar is presented first. Now, according to the second line, "Perunambi Kulacchiraithan Adiyarkkum Adiyen," sung by Sundaramurthy Swamigal, the Puranam of Kulacchirai Nayanar, the great Nambi, is the second Puranam in this Sarukkam. It is the 22nd Puranam. In the sequence of Nayanmars, Kulacchirai Nayanar is the 21st Nayanar. Therefore, following the Puranams of 20 Nayanmars and the Thogaiyadiyar Puranam of Thillai Vazh Andanar, the 22nd Puranam in Periya Puranam is that of Kulacchirai Nayanar. As an introduction to Kulacchirai Nayanar's Puranam, Sekkizhar Peruman sets the stage for the upcoming Puranam of Kulacchirai Nayanar in the concluding verse of the preceding Puranam, that of Thirunavukkarasu Nayanar. This is Sekkizhar Peruman's method. It is one of Sekkizhar Peruman's unique characteristics to provide an introduction to the next Nayanar's Puranam as one Nayanar's Puranam concludes in a Sarukkam, thereby maintaining continuity throughout the Periya Puranam. How does he introduce Kulacchirai Nayanar's Puranam in the concluding verse of Thirunavukkarasu Nayanar's Puranam? He states, "Kulacchiraiyar mudivil pugazh thiruthondin muyarchiyinai mozhigindren." He says, "I am going to speak about the endless glorious efforts of Kulacchirai Nayanar's sacred service." Kulacchirai Nayanar's sacred service is an endlessly glorious sacred service. How does Kulacchirai Nayanar's sacred service become an endlessly glorious sacred service? The boundless glory of Kulacchirai Nayanar's sacred service is praised by the divine words of Aludaiya Pillaiyar, Thirugnanasambandha Peruman. Therefore, it shines as an endlessly glorious sacred service. Thirugnanasambandha Peruman has praised Kulacchirai Nayanar in the second, fourth, sixth, eighth, tenth, and eleventh verses of the Thiruppathikam that begins with "Mangaiyarkkarasi Valavarkon Paavai." Thus, Kulacchirai Nayanar is one whose boundless glory is praised by the divine words of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman. His sacred service becomes a sacred service of boundless glory.

"Pillaiyar tham thiruvakkil pirathalal aththalamum munbu ulla paasam vittagala ozhiya piravithanai ozhiththu kollu nirmaikaalangal kazhiththu sivame koodinaval vallalar matravar arulin vaaimai koorin varambennam" - this is the 983rd verse of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman Puranam. The verse states, "By being born from the divine words of Pillaiyar, the attachment that was there before will be removed, and the endless cycle of birth will be eradicated." According to this, it is said in this verse that if one is born even once from the divine words of Aludaiya Pillaiyar, Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, one can attain the fruit of liberation. Given this, if Thirugnanasambandha Peruman praises Kulacchirai Nayanar many times with his divine words and speaks of his greatness, can the greatness of Kulacchirai Nayanar's glory be measured? Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman describes Kulacchirai Nayanar's sacred service as an "endlessly glorious sacred service" and thus introduces Kulacchirai Nayanar's Puranam in the concluding verse of Thirunavukkarasu Nayanar's Puranam. Furthermore, instead of saying, "I am going to speak about the nature of Kulacchirai Nayanar's sacred service," he says, "I am going to begin narrating the history of Kulacchirai Nayanar, focusing on the nature of his efforts in sacred service." The event of sacred service occurs at a specific time. However, the effort of sacred service is something that is always and eternally continuous. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman very subtly states, "I will sing about the nature of the efforts in sacred service," rather than "I will sing about the nature of sacred service." Having thus introduced, Sekkizhar Peruman then presents the Puranam of Kulacchirai Nayanar. We saw that Kulacchirai Nayanar's Puranam is the 22nd Puranam. In this, Sekkizhar Peruman sings about the nature of Kulacchirai Nayanar's efforts in sacred service in 11 verses. In the comprehensive work Thiruthondathogai, Sundaramurthy Swamigal sings, "Perunambi Kulacchiraithan Adiyarkkum Adiyen." In the detailed work Thiruthondar Thiruvandathi, Nambiandar Nambigal praises Kulacchirai Nayanar in the 26th verse. Based on these comprehensive and detailed works, Sekkizhar Peruman sings very extensively in 11 verses in the elaborate work Thiruthondar Puranam, also known as Periya Puranam. This is the Puranam of Kulacchirai Nayanar, the second to appear in the Thirunindra Sarukkam, which describes the character and history of Kulacchirai Nayanar. First, in the comprehensive work, Sundaramurthy Swamigal sings, "Perunambi Kulacchiraithan Adiyarkkum Adiyen." Sundaramurthy Swamigal sings, "I am a servant to the devotees of Kulacchirai Nayanar, who is called Perunambi." "Perunambi" appears here as an epithet for Kulacchirai Nayanar. "Perunambi" was a distinguished family title in ancient times. "Raghu marabil ponnum Perunambi Kulacchiraiyum" is from the 211th verse of Thakkayaga Parani. We can learn this from its commentary. "Nambi" means a distinguished man. Here, "Perunambi" appears, referring to his lineage. This speaks of Kulacchirai Nayanar's lineage and name. This is the meaning of the line in the comprehensive work, "Perunambi Kulacchiraithan Adiyarkkum Adiyen." Now, in the detailed work, Nambiandar Nambigal praises Kulacchirai Nayanar in the Thiruthondar Thiruvandathi as follows: "Arunthamil Akaran vaathil amanai kazhunuthimel irunthamil naattidai yetruvithon ezhil sangam vaitha perunthamil meenavan than athikari pirasamalku kurunthavizh saaran manamerkudi man Kulacchiraye." This is the 26th verse in the detailed work, Thiruthondar Thiruvandathi, composed by Nambiandar Nambigal about Kulacchirai Nayanar. Kulacchirai Nayanar was the one who, in the debate conducted by Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, the repository of rare Tamil, caused the defeated Jains to be impaled on stakes in the Tamil land. He was a worthy minister of the Pandya king Nindra Seer Nedumaran, who established beautiful Tamil literary academies. We should note here that Nindra Seer Nedumara Nayanar is one of the 63 Nayanmars. He was the chief of the town Manamerkudi, which has groves where honey-filled Kuruntha flowers bloom. Such was Kulacchirai Nayanar. This is the concise meaning of the verse about Kulacchirai Nayanar in Nambiandar Nambigal's Thiruthondar Thiruvandathi. Now, let us reflect on how Kulacchirai Nayanar's Puranam is presented in the extensive work, Periya Puranam. The brief history of Kulacchirai Nayanar is as follows: In the ancient and glorious Pandya country, there was a prosperous town called Manamerkudi. Kulacchiraiyar was born there in the lineage known as Perunambi. He excelled in the steadfastness of sacred service and lived according to that path. If devotees of Shiva came, whether they belonged to the four castes or were casteless, whether they were worldly and prosperous or not, whether they came in large numbers or alone, Kulacchirai Nayanar would worship them, lovingly feed them ambrosial food, and adhere to this practice. He served as the chief minister among the ministers of Nindra Seer Nedumara Pandiyan, eliminating enemies and protecting the country peacefully. Kulacchirai Nayanar was one who always praised the devotees of Shiva. He became a servant to the sacred service of Mangaiyarkkarasi Ammaiyar, the Pandya Queen, and rejoiced in associating with the golden feet of Thirugnanasambandha Nayanar, so that the Pandya country might abandon Jainism and live according to the path of sacred ash. Kulacchirai Nayanar was the one who impaled the Jains who were defeated in the debate in the Pandya country. This is a summary of Kulacchirai Nayanar's history. Sekkizhar Peruman has elaborated on this in 11 verses. Following this, we will continue to reflect on Kulacchirai Nayanar's Puranam. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 02 — Kulacchirai Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 479, Thirunindra Sarukkam, Kulacchirai Nayanar Puranam. Sekkizhar Peruman begins the Puranam of Kulacchirai Nayanar by describing the greatness of the Pandya country where Kulacchirai Nayanar was born, and the prosperity and richness of Manamerkudi, the town of his birth, and then proceeds to narrate the Nayanar's sacred history. It is Sekkizhar Peruman's custom to begin the Puranam of each Nayanar by describing the greatness and prosperity of the country and city where the Nayanar was born, the greatness of the family and lineage in which the Nayanar was born, and the greatness of the Nayanar's sacred name. In this manner, the country where Kulacchirai Nayanar was born is the Pandya country, and the town where he was born is Manamerkudi. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman sings of their greatness. The Pandya country is an ancient and glorious land, spoken of by scholars, and a land of great goodness. There, fields full of red paddy and sweet sugarcane, surrounded by dense areca nut trees, and many other such places abound in this Pandya country. Among such places, one city is Manamerkudi. Manamerkudi is a city where all the aforementioned prosperities are established. Furthermore, Manamerkudi is a town where generous people reside and bestow grace. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman begins the Puranam of Kulacchirai Nayanar. By singing in this way, he praises the greatness of the Pandya country, the land where Kulacchirai Nayanar was born, and also the greatness of Manamerkudi, the town where Kulacchirai Nayanar was born. By stating that generous people reside there, the greatness of the lineage is also mentioned in this verse. The town of Manamerkudi is currently located near Aranthangi in Pudukkottai district. It is still known by the name Manamerkudi. Manamerkudi is an ancient town of great distinction, where Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, along with Nindra Seer Nedumara Nayanar and Pandi Madeviyar, traveled, stayed, and worshipped during his pilgrimage to the Pandya country. One can reach this town by traveling southeast from Avudaiyar Koil. From Thiruppunavayil, one can travel northeast to Mimisal and from there, further northeast to reach this site of Manamerkudi. Currently, Manamerkudi is a town predominantly inhabited by Muslims. The Vellaru river forms the northern boundary of the Pandya country. Therefore, this is designated as a Pandya country site. As the chief of this city, Manamerkudi, Kulacchirai Nayanar, who possessed extraordinary greatness, was born and lived there. Kulacchirai Nayanar, praised as Perunambi by Sundaramurthy Swamigal, was the chief of the city of Manamerkudi. Kulacchirai Nayanar is a Nayanar of immeasurable glory. He is one whose greatness is extolled in the Thiruthondathogai Pathikam, which was composed with the divine grace of Thyagaraja Peruman, who provided both the rhythm and the meaning. The greatness of the sacred service he performed can also be praised as "immeasurable glory." It is because of the greatness of that sacred service that the Nayanar himself was thus praised. The sacred name of such a Nayanar is Kulacchirai. Kulacchirai means "protector of the lineage." Kulacchirai Nayanar was filled with steadfastness, including mental fortitude, intellectual steadfastness, and steadfastness in action. Due to this steadfastness, Kulacchirai Nayanar lived without deviating from sacred service. This steadfastness of his was instrumental in the salvation of the Pandya king, the Tennavan, and in the flourishing of sacred ash in the Tennavan's kingdom and other countries, and in the disappearance of the darkness of Jainism. This is the point to note here. The sacred service performed by Kulacchirai Nayanar here is the service rendered to Shiva's devotees and to Lord Shiva himself through the three instruments: mind, speech, and body. He held the principle that Shiva's devotees are the cause of receiving the Lord's grace. With the conviction of such a principle, Kulacchirai Nayanar lived with love and joy for Shiva's devotees, reverently bowing at their feet with great affection, joining his hands in prayer, and speaking kind and loving words to them. The extent of Shiva's grace is proportional to the love shown to His devotees. Therefore, Shiva's devotees are the cause of receiving Shiva's grace. Thus, upon seeing Shiva's devotees, he would greet them, bow to them, speak kind words, and offer salutations. He shows us here that the worship of devotees is as worthy of performance as the worship of the Lord. Furthermore, Kulacchirai Nayanar had the practice of humbly bowing to Shiva's devotees, no matter who they were. Even if Shiva's devotees belonged to the four castes that arose due to the path of karma, or if they had deviated from that path of conduct, if they were known to possess steadfast knowledge and love for Lord Shiva, Kulacchirai Nayanar would humbly and sincerely bow to them. The four castes are Brahmins, Kshatriyas, Vaishyas, and Shudras. Kulacchirai Nayanar would worship Shiva's devotees regardless of which of these four castes they were born into. Sekkizhar Peruman mentions here, "even if they belonged to the four castes that arose due to the path of karma." According to Sivagnanabodham, the ripening of past karma, existing as subtle karma, takes hold of the Buddhi Tattva and lies in Maya, and when it comes to experience, it becomes the cause of three things: caste, lifespan, and enjoyment, which are respectively Janakam (birth), Tharakam (sustenance), and Pokkiyam (experience). Therefore, each soul, according to the path of karma, will be born into one of these four castes. Thus, Kulacchirai Nayanar would worship Shiva's devotees, regardless of which of these four castes they belonged to, even if they arose due to the path of karma. Even if they had deviated from that path, he would worship them. Kulacchirai Nayanar would bow to anyone, regardless of who they were, if he saw the sacred ash on their forehead and the sacred symbols. We can also see caste distinctions mentioned in the Agamas as Anuloma and Prathiloma. The core idea of the Thevaram section "Kulamilaraaga" is to emphasize the principle that one should worship a devotee of Shiva, regardless of their caste. "Pulayare eninum Eesan pulam kazhal adiyil punthi nilaiyarel avarkku poosai nigazhthuthal neriyee" is an Agamic rule. Even if all the people of this world possess the virtues that they consider superior, or if they are devoid of those virtues and possess immeasurable evils, if they are devotees of Lord Shiva, Kulacchirai Nayanar would prostrate himself before them and praise them. That is, the dharma of worldly conduct, which is generally considered good by worldly people, is related to the soul and its bonds. But the virtues related to the Lord are different. The soul clings to both the Lord and the world. The rules prescribed for it in its worldly state are called Pasu Dharmas. What is desired in the state of clinging to the Lord are the Dharmas based on love for Lord Shiva, and these are called Pathi Dharmas. These Pathi Dharmas are performed for the Lord and His devotees. These worldly merits, also called ordinary merits or Pasu Punya, are not related to Pathi Punya. Generally, killing living beings in this worldly state is considered evil in ordinary conduct and dharma, and it yields sinful results, leading to hell. However, when it is done for the Lord, it is considered a high Pathi Dharma and elevates both the doer and the killed beings to the fruit of liberation. Chandesa, who punished his father, became the son of Lord Shiva. His father, who was beaten by him, also attained Shivlokam. Similarly, the killings of living beings performed by Kannappa Nayanar during hunting were not considered sins, but were praised as "Arulipaadu" (act of grace) where light remained and killed. The incidents where Iyarpagai Nayanar and Kotpuli Nayanar killed relatives who obstructed Shiva's service did not result in the fruit of murder, but were histories that led to their respective good deeds, which we should consider here. Thus, whether they performed good deeds or evil deeds in the worldly state, if they were devotees of Lord Shiva, Kulacchirai Nayanar would prostrate himself before them and praise them. In the Vedas, good conduct for worldly beings is stated as "Dharmam Chara, Sathyam Vada" (Practice righteousness, speak truth). Doing both of these is good. Not doing both is evil. Therefore, those who walk on the path of dharma, as per "Dharmam Chara," and those who speak the truth, as per "Sathyam Vada," are truly virtuous and perform meritorious deeds. Those who act contrary to this are evil-doers. They are indeed wicked. However, whether they are good or evil, if they are devotees of Lord Shiva, Kulacchirai Nayanar would worship and praise them. This shows us the greatness of Kulacchirai Nayanar. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman continues to sing the greatness of Kulacchirai Nayanar's sacred service. We will continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 03 — Kulacchirai Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 480, Thirunindra Sarukkam, Kulacchirai Nayanar Puranam. Kulacchirai Nayanar, the noble one, possessed the virtue of lovingly welcoming devotees, even if they were a large group seeking food or a single person seeking food, with immense love beyond measure, and sharing sacred ambrosial food with them in friendship. Sekkizhar Swamigal states this in his verse: "Panbin mikkar palarai anaiyinum unba vendi oruvar anaiyinum enperukkiya anbal ethirkondu nanbu koornthu amuthu oottum nalathinar." Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, in the fourth verse of the 120th pathikam of the third Thirumurai, Thirukkadaikkappu, sang about Kulacchirai Nayanar: "Ganangalai varinum thamiyarai varinum adiyavar thangalai kandal gunangodu paniyum Kulacchirai kulavum." Sekkizhar Peruman elaborates on this very idea in this verse. In this verse, he mentions "Panbin mikkar palarai" (many virtuous ones). In the next verse, he explains what those virtues are. Sekkizhar Peruman states in the next verse that the virtues of a devotee are recognized by the sacred ash, rudraksha, loincloth, and other Shiva symbols. Kulacchirai Nayanar is described as "enperukkiya anbal ethirkondu nanbu koornthu amuthu oottum nalathinar." "Enperukkiya anbal" means with immense love, multiplied by constant contemplation and meditation. The idea here is that he would welcome devotees with great love that had grown in his mind through constant contemplation. Furthermore, Kulacchirai Nayanar would feed the devotees ambrosial food. "Oottuthal" implies feeding with love, like a mother feeding her child. He possessed such a virtue. This virtue is a Saivite virtue. The virtue mentioned in the previous verse was a worldly virtue. The one mentioned in this verse is a Saivite virtue. Among those who serve devotees, there are some who do so for worldly praise and some who limit their offerings. Therefore, they would act accordingly, doing more if many devotees came and less if one came. However, Kulacchirai Nayanar did not act in this manner; he treated everyone the same, whether one came or many. Now, the next verse: "Poothi kovanam sadanathar polinthathi thevartham anjezhutham avai othu naa vanakkaththal uraippavar paadham naalum paraviya panbinar." The devotees of Lord Shiva are those who shine with the sacred attire of sacred ash, loincloth, and rudraksha. They are those whose tongues, bowing, recite the sacred five letters of Lord Shiva, the primordial cause of all. Such are the great and virtuous devotees of Shiva. This verse begins with "Poothi." "Poothi" means sacred ash, wealth. To signify its special nature, the prefix "vi" is added to make "Vibhoothi," meaning special wealth. It is called Vibhoothi because it bestows the imperishable wealth of liberation. "Mukthi tharuvathu neeru" (sacred ash bestows liberation) is a saying from Thevaram. Therefore, devotees possess sacred ash. They wear rudraksha. They wear loincloths. Devotees of Shiva have no other support than Shiva. Therefore, they do not wear other clothes or ornaments, but wear only a loincloth. "Aaram kandigai aadaiyum kanthaye" is from the 144th verse of Periya Puranam. Not only that, wearing a loincloth is also a sacred form of Shiva. "Sambarpoochum keezhudaiyum konda uruvam" is from the first verse of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman's Thirukkadaikkappu, Thiru Kolakka.

"Kodungulai pugunthavan endrum kovanam araiyatheye" and "Kovanam uduththavarum kolaravu asaiththavarum" and "Theevana chambar poosi thiruvuruvil irunthavarum" are the words of Thirunavukkarasu Swamigal. "Mannu kalai thunnu porul marai naanke vaan sarada thannaiye kovanamasaaththinan kaan saazhalo" is from Thiruvachagam. Now, such devotees of Shiva are described as having tongues that recite the five letters. The verse mentions "Aathithevartham anjezhuththu." This is the sacred Panchakshara Maha Mantra, the holy name of Lord Shiva. Here, "anjezhutham avai" refers to the sacred five letters in the plural. Although the sacred five letters together form a single mantra, each of the five letters individually conveys a specific meaning, and therefore Sekkizhar Peruman refers to them in the plural as "anjezhutham avai." "Mannum anjezhuthakiya manthiram thannil onru vallar" is from Thirukkurunthogai, composed by Thirunavukkarasu Swamigal. Such devotees of Shiva repeatedly practice these five letters. The verse states, "Othum naa vanakkaththal uraippavar" (those who recite with a bowing tongue). "Othum naa" means a tongue that recites constantly through practice. "Naan marakkinum sollum naa namachivayave" is from Sundaramurthy Swamigal's Kodumudi Thevaram. According to this, the tongue of devotees always recites the sacred five letters. Thus, Kulacchirai Nayanar possessed the virtue of daily worshipping the sacred feet of Shiva's devotees, who shone with the sacred symbols of Vibhoothi, loincloth, and Rudraksha, and who always recited the sacred five letters of Lord Shiva. Sekkizhar Peruman, in this context, quotes the eighth verse of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman's Thiruppathikam, "Mangaiyarkkarasi Valavarkon Paavai," from Thiruvalavai, which states, "Navanangu iyalpam anjezhuththu othi nallaray nalliyalbagum kovanam poothi sadanam kandal thozhuthezhugu Kulacchirai potra," describing the virtues of Kulacchirai Nayanar. In the Puranam of Kulacchirai Nayanar, Sekkizhar Peruman sings about the steadfastness of Kulacchirai Nayanar's devotion to devotees and the nature of his sacred service in these five verses. He elaborates on this from verse 1697 to verse 1701. In verse 1697, he states that Kulacchirai Nayanar would worship them simply because they were devotees. In verse 1698, he states that Kulacchirai Nayanar would bow to them whether they were casteless or of a high caste. In verse 1699, he states that Kulacchirai Nayanar would approach and bow to them whether they possessed worldly virtues or not. In verse 1700, he states that Kulacchirai Nayanar would lovingly feed them ambrosial food whether they came in large numbers or alone to eat. Similarly, in this 1701st verse, he describes the nature of Kulacchirai Nayanar's sacred service, which stems from his steadfastness towards Shiva's devotees. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman extensively describes the nature of Kulacchirai Nayanar's sacred service in these five verses. Kulacchirai Nayanar, who excelled in such good conduct, lived as the foremost among the distinguished ministers of the Pandya king Nindra Seer Nedumaran, who possessed endless glory. Earlier, the author, Sekkizhar Peruman, described Kulacchirai Nayanar's virtues through various qualities such as steadfastness, dignity, goodness, and character. Now, Sekkizhar Peruman also states that Kulacchirai Nayanar excelled in conduct befitting those virtuous qualities. Sekkizhar Peruman, in narrating the history, states that Kulacchirai Nayanar became a minister, would bow with humility upon seeing devotees, and would rise and worship upon seeing the loincloth, sacred ash, and other sacred symbols. In doing so, Sekkizhar Peruman previously spoke about the underlying quality of love for those actions, referring to what Thirugnanasambandha Peruman had composed in the Thevaram of Thiruvalavai. Now, he speaks about the actions themselves, stating that he was "innel ozhukkaththinar" (one of good conduct). Such noble actions of Kulacchirai Nayanar are elaborated in Thirugnanasambandha Peruman's Thevaram as mentioned above. The histories of Kulacchirai Nayanar are to be seen in detail in the Puranam of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman. Therefore, we can see here Sekkizhar Peruman's skill in subtly conveying these matters and in structuring the Puranam based on the evidence of Thevaram. We will see Kulacchirai Nayanar's actions, his history, in great detail in the sacred history of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman. Here, Sekkizhar Peruman gives the epithet "Eeril Seer Thennavan Nedumaran" to the Pandya king Nindra Seer Nedumara Nayanar. "Vellai Neeru Aniyum Kottravan" is from Thevaram. Although Nindra Seer Nedumaran had not worn sacred ash when that Thevaram was composed, the truthful words of the Vedas, which stated "aniyuum kottravan" (the king who will wear), implying that he would later wear sacred ash, proved true, and he became Nedumaran, adorned with sacred ash and excelling. Sekkizhar Peruman, keeping this idea in mind, sings "Eeril Seer" and "Nedumaran" here. When Thirugnanasambandha Peruman composed the Thevaram, he spoke of the king's future glory as "Neeru Aniyum Kottravan." But by Sekkizhar Peruman's time, that glory and other glories had already been fully realized, and so the author praises Nindra Seer Nedumara Nayanar as "Eeril Seer Thennavan Nedumaran." Kulacchirai Nayanar shone as the foremost minister among the ministers of such a glorious king. Unlike other ministers who only sought worldly welfare for the king and his subjects, Kulacchirai Nayanar was intent on securing both the higher states of well-being for their bodies and souls simultaneously for both. Therefore, he shone as the best and most distinguished minister among all ministers. Similarly, it is noteworthy and venerable that Sekkizhar Peruman, as the foremost minister of Anapaya Chola, removed Anapaya Chola from Jain influence and brought him to Saivite influence, thereby becoming the cause of this Puranam, a high spiritual teaching, for the world. Sekkizhar Peruman sang about Kulacchirai Nayanar as "Manthirigatku Melagi Mannuvaar" (one who excelled among ministers). All the virtuous qualities of a minister mentioned by the divine poet Thiruvalluvar in Thirukkural were present in Kulacchirai Nayanar. Such a Kulacchirai Nayanar served the king by destroying enemies and ensuring the king's well-being. His standing in ensuring the king's well-being by destroying his enemies is understood in two ways. Firstly, it refers to the worldly enemies who did not obey the king's command and obstructed his righteous rule. Therefore, we can first consider that he was a helper to the king by conquering such enemies. Secondly, we can also consider here his ability to conquer the Jains, who were enemies to the king's life principle, in the context of the soul. Furthermore, he served in a way that ensured the king's well-being, meaning "uruthikkan nithral." "Uruthikkan" means in the path where the scepter and the banner attain ultimate meaning. The ultimate meanings are dharma, artha, kama, and moksha, which are righteousness, wealth, pleasure, and liberation. The highest good for people, as identified by the wise, is what we also consider as the ultimate meaning for the king.

They are Dharma, Artha, Kama, and Moksha, as stated in the commentary of Parimelazhagar on Thirukkural. Thus, in the path where the scepter and the umbrella attain ultimate meaning, that great person, Kulacchirai Nayanar, served the king as a loyal minister, following the path of righteousness and ensuring the king's well-being. Such a noble person was one who always praised the sacred feet of Lord Shiva, who holds the Ganga in His sacred matted locks. He was always contemplating and worshipping the greatness of divine grace. Kulacchirai Nayanar was a true devotee to the fitting sacred service of Mangaiyarkkarasi Ammaiyar, who spread the glory of sacred ash throughout the land. Kulacchirai Nayanar was the one who, with sincerity, performed all the necessary actions to ensure the success of the sacred service undertaken by Queen Mangaiyarkkarasi. Kulacchirai Nayanar lived with the distinction of joyfully bearing on his head the beautiful lotus feet of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, the generous one from Kazhi, in order to remove the falsehoods of the base-minded Jains and to foster the path of sacred ash in the Pandya country. The Jains were of base character. We can see all the evils committed by the Jains in the Puranams of Thirunavukkarasu Nayanar, Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, Thandiyadigal Nayanar, and Naminandiyadigal Nayanar. We can recall them here. Kulacchirai Nayanar was the one who, to remove the falsehoods of such Jains, joyfully bore on his head the beautiful lotus feet of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman. When Thirugnanasambandha Peruman was residing in Vedharanyam, Kulacchirai Nayanar sent his attendants to Thirugnanasambandha Peruman and requested him to visit the Pandya country. Later, Kulacchirai Nayanar possessed great distinctions, which are known through the actions he performed after Thirugnanasambandha Peruman's arrival. We will see all these historical accounts in detail in the Puranam of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman. Therefore, Kulacchirai Nayanar was one who lived with the distinction of joyfully bearing on his head the beautiful lotus feet of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman. It is implied here that this Nayanar attained liberation through the grace of his Guru. The phrase "Poi neekkavum neeru potravum" (to remove falsehood and to praise sacred ash) implies that liberation, which involves the removal of bonds and the attainment of Shiva, is the Saivite goal, and that the Guru's grace is the means to achieve it. These truths found in the Gnana Sastras are subtly conveyed by Sekkizhar Peruman in this sacred verse. Sekkizhar Peruman sings that such a Kulacchirai Nayanar possessed the power to impale the Jains, who were defeated in the three types of debates – Suravatham (debate by argument), Analvatham (debate by fire), and Punalvatham (debate by water) – in the Pandya country. Sekkizhar Peruman concludes the sacred history of Kulacchirai Nayanar by saying, "In what way have I praised and worshipped such a Kulacchirai Nayanar? I have not praised him in any single way." Having thus concluded the Puranam of Kulacchirai Nayanar, Sekkizhar Peruman then begins the Puranam of Perumizhalai Kurumbar Nayanar, saying, "Now, with the support of Kulacchirai Nayanar's sacred feet, I begin to praise the sacred feet of Perumizhalai Kurumbar, who cherished the virtues mentioned in the four Vedas." We too shall continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.
