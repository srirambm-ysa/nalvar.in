Chapter 01 — Kochengat Chozha Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Volume 1014, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. The Puranam of Kochengat Chozha Nayanar, from the Manniya Seer Sarakkam. In the Periyapuranam composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, the Puranam of Kochengat Chozha Nayanar is the fourth Puranam after the Puranam of Nesa Nayanar in the Manniya Seer Sarakkam. It is the 68th sacred Puranam according to the sequence of Periyapuranam. Kochengat Chozha Nayanar was a Chozha king who built countless Shiva temples on the banks of the Kaveri. Moreover, he built numerous temples for Lord Shiva, over 70 of them, as Maadakkoil temples, which elephants could not enter. His sacred Puranam has been composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Firstly, in the 11th verse of Thiruthondathogai, a collection of works composed by our Lord Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal sang, "Thennavanai ulaganda senganarkku adiyen," meaning "I am a servant to Senganar, who ruled the world as a Thennavan." The meaning here is that "I am a servant to Kochengat Chozha, who ruled this world." The phrase "Thennavanai" is interpreted as "Thennavanumai," meaning that Kochengat Chozha Nayanar, besides being a Chozha king and ruling the Chozha country, also ruled the Pandya country as a Thennavan. Therefore, "Thennavanumai ulaganda senganarkku adiyen" means "I am a servant to Kochengat Chozha Nayanar, who ruled this world not only as a Chozha king but also as a Pandya king, a Thennavan." This interpretation of the section from Thiruthondathogai has been given by Sivakavimani Sambandha Saranalaya Swamigal. We will see this history, where he ruled as a Pandya king and also the Pandya country, narrated by Nambiandar Nambigal Peruman in the work called Nimba Narunthongal. Senganan is the sacred name of the Nayanar. This sacred name is a cherished name given by his mother. Kochengat Chozha Nayanar, revered by devotees, is praised as Senganat Peyar Sembiyar Kon in various Thevaram verses. This is how the meaning of the collection of works is structured. Now, in the Thiruthondar Thiruvandathi, a descriptive work, Nambiandar Nambigal Peruman has praised Kochengat Chozha Nayanar in two verses, 81 and 82. Those verses are as follows: "Mai vaitha kandan neriyandri matror neri karuthatha theivak kudicholan munbu silanthiyay pandar seythu saivathu uru eythi vanthu tharani needu alayangal seyvithavan thirukochengannan enum sembiyane." And "Sempon aninthu chittrambalathai sivalogam eythi namban kazharkeezh irunthon kula muthal enbar nalla vambu malar thillai eesanai suzha marai valarthan nimba narunthongal kochengannan enum nithanaiye." These are the verses. The first verse, the 81st verse, means that the Chozha king, who belonged to a divine lineage and considered no path other than that of Lord Shiva, the blue-throated one, in his previous birth was a spider. He built a canopy over the Lord's idol with his silk threads to prevent dry leaves from falling and worshipped Shiva. Because of this devotion, he was born into the Saivite tradition and incarnated in this world as Kochengat Chozha Nayanar, building numerous grand temples for Lord Shiva. He is the Chozha king named Kochengannan. The 82nd verse refers to a Chozha king who covered the roof of the Chithrambalam with pure gold and attained Shivlokam, residing at the feet of the Lord. He is known as Hiranyavarma Chakravarti. This history is extensively narrated in the Koil Puranam.

This Hiranyavarma Chakravarti covered the Chithrambalam in Thillai with gold. Such was the tradition of the Chozha kings. Similarly, Adithya Chozha covered the Ponnambalam with gold. Anapaya Chozha covered the Perambalam with gold. Here, Hiranyavarma Chakravarti, who covered the roof of the Chithrambalam with pure gold, attained Shivlokam and resided at the feet of the Lord. Kochengat Chozha Nayanar was a descendant of his lineage. Kochengat Chozha Nayanar built many mansions for the Vedic scholars around the Thillai Ambalam of the Lord, which is adorned with fragrant flowers. He wore a garland of neem flowers. The neem garland is mentioned in the verse as "Nimba Narunthongal." The neem garland is characteristic of the Pandyas. Therefore, he wore the neem garland, indicating that he ruled the Chozha kingdom, including the Pandya country. Such was the glory of the eternal Chozha king named Kochengannan. He is Kochengat Chozha Nayanar. In his previous birth, he was a spider at the sacred site of Thiruvanaikka. He protected the Shiva Lingam from falling dry leaves and other debris by building a canopy over it with his silk threads. This history is also narrated by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman in the extensive work of Periyapuranam. This is how it is narrated by Nambiandar Nambigal Peruman in the descriptive work. Now, in the extensive work of Periyapuranam, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, the Puranam of Kochengat Chozha Nayanar is described in 18 verses by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. The first verse is: "Thulaiyil puravi nirai alitha chozhar urimai chozha nattil alaiyil tharalam akiladum santham ani neer ponni manikozhikkum kulaiyil perugum chandra theerthathin marungu kulir solai nilaiyil perugum tharumidaintha nedun then kanam ondru ulathal." The divine Sekkizhar Peruman describes a sacred site in the Chozha country. Sekkizhar Peruman begins this history by highlighting the glory of the Chozha country, stating that it was ruled by Sibi Chakravarti. He first narrates the story of Sibi Chakravarti, referring to him as "Thulaiyil puravin nirai alitha chozhar." Once, Sibi Chakravarti performed a rare yagna in Seerkazhi. At that time, Lord Shiva, wishing to make Sibi Chakravarti's greatness known to the world, sent Agni Deva and Indra. Agni Deva took the form of a dove, and Indra took the form of a hawk chasing the dove. As the hawk chased the dove, the dove sought refuge with Sibi Chakravarti. The dove sought refuge with Sibi Chakravarti, saying that the hawk was coming to eat it. The hawk came to Sibi Chakravarti and asked for the dove, stating it was its food. Seeing this, Sibi Chakravarti cut flesh from his own body, equal to the weight of the dove, and placed it on a scale. When it still did not balance the dove's weight, he himself climbed onto the scale, completing the balance, and offered himself. Because of this, he received divine grace, which is the history of Sibi Chakravarti. The glory of this Chozha lineage was known for its dharma, which would even sacrifice its own life to protect those who sought refuge. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman describes the glory of this sacred land. In this sacred Puranam, or rather, in the Puranam of Kochengat Chozha Nayanar, it is even greater. The spider, in order to protect the dharma of Shiva, sought to punish the elephant and, in punishing the elephant, gave its own life. It was this spider that incarnated as Kochengat Chozha Nayanar. To convey this, Sekkizhar Peruman narrates the history of Sibi Chakravarti first, highlighting the glory of that land. In Kambaramayanam, in the Kulmurai Kilaithu Padalam, the greatness of Sibi Chakravarti is spoken of, saying, "Ivar kulaththon mer paravai mannuyirkku thannuyirai maraga vazhanginanal."

The Kulmurai Kilaithu Padalam is a section in Kambaramayanam where the sage Kaushika narrates the glory of the Surya dynasty to King Janaka. In that section, the history of Sibi Chakravarti is thus spoken of. In such a sacred land, the Chozha country, the Kaveri river flows. The beautiful waters of the Kaveri bring pearls, agarwood, and sandalwood with its waves. On the banks of the Kaveri, which is rich in gems, there is a sacred pond called Chandra Theertham. Next to this Chandra Theertham, there is a long, cool forest with steadily growing trees in cool groves. This sacred site thus mentioned is Thiruvanaikka. The Chandra Theertham mentioned here is Chandra Pushkarani, a pond where Chandra (the moon god) is said to have installed a pond and worshipped to cure a diminishing illness caused by Daksha's curse, thereby regaining his well-being. This is the history associated with Chandra Theertham. The forest next to it, a beautiful grove, is described by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman in this first verse. Thus, the sacred site of Thiruvanaikka, where Kochengat Chozha Nayanar, in his previous birth as a spider, worshipped the Lord, is located in the Chozha country. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman begins this sacred Puranam by stating that it is a sacred site on the banks of the Kaveri. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 02 — Kochengat Chozha Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Volume 1015, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. The Puranam of Kochengat Chozha Nayanar, from the Manniya Seer Sarakkam. The second verse: "Appoonganil vennaval athankeezh munnal arithedu meyppoongazhalar velippadallum mikka thavathoru vellanaikaippoom punalum mukandu atti kamil poongothu aninthu irainchi maippoonguvalaikanatharai nalum vazhipattu ozhukumaal." In that grove, near the Chandra Theertham on the banks of the Kaveri, there is a white Jamun tree. This white Jamun tree is revered as the "Gnanachcharvam Vennaval" (the white Jamun tree of spiritual wisdom). This sacred site of Thiruvanaikka was once a white Jamun forest. Hence, it is also known as Jambuvana. In that white Jamun forest, the sage Jambu Muni performed penance. One day, a miraculously divine white Jamun fruit, which he had never seen before, fell before him. The sage offered it to the Lord in Kailash and ate the remaining seed. Just as a small offering to the deserving becomes immensely great, the Jamun seed that entered the sage's stomach began to sprout. It grew into a huge tree, bursting through the sage's head, and flourished abundantly. The sage, who had become a tree, danced in ecstasy. He praised the Lord. Everyone watched in wonder as a tree danced and praised the Lord. That tree, which emerged through Jambu Muni's head, had the Vedas as its roots, the Upanishads as its trunk, the Agamas as its branches, and the Chariyai, Kiriyai, and Yoga as its smaller branches. Its flower clusters represented the five types of knowledge, and it flourished as a tree bearing buds, flowers, unripe fruits, and ripe fruits. Seeing it grow sky-high and bestow spiritual maturity on everyone, the sage was astonished and prayed to the Lord. The Lord told the sage to go to the place where the fruit had fallen. The Lord also promised to come there. The sage, who had taken the form of a tree, set out from Kailash to reach Thiruvanaikka, the sacred site of wisdom. Many were amazed to see a tree walking. Some followed it. Thus, the sage passed through many places and reached Jambuvana, the place where the fruit had fallen, and performed severe penance there for a long time. The Lord, moved by his penance, manifested and resided under the Jamun tree' as the sage had wished. This glorious Jamun tree itself became the Sthala Vriksham (sacred tree of the temple). Since the Lord resided under the Jamun (Jambu) tree, he came to be known as Jambunathar. In such a sacred site, a white elephant, which had performed great penance in a previous birth, came there due to a curse. This is not Indra's elephant; it is another elephant. That white elephant, which had performed great penance, would draw beautiful water with its trunk, perform ablutions for the Lord, adorn the Lord's body with fragrant flower clusters, and worship Him. It would worship the Lord, whose neck was like a dark blue water lily, every day. The worship performed by the elephant is also praised in the Thevaram verses. "Pidiyellam pinsela perungai ma malar thazhuvi vidiyale thadamoozhgi vidhiyinal vazhipadum" (The elephant, with its large trunk, embraces the great flower, and at dawn, it bathes in the pond and worships according to custom) - this is from Pillaiyar Thevaram. "Adaintha Airavatham paniy mikka athanukku arul surakkum venkadu" (The Airavatha that reached there worshipped, and to it, grace flowed abundantly from Venkadu) - this is from Thiruvengadu Thiruppathigam. Such is the glory of Thiruvanaikka. In Mount Kailash, there were two Shiva Ganas named Maliyavan and Pushpadantan. These two competed with each other to see who was superior in serving Shiva. Even though each was superior to the other in serving Shiva, jealousy and anger arose due to the competition. At that time, Maliyavan cursed Pushpadantan to become an elephant. Pushpadantan cursed Maliyavan to become a spider. Thus, both, by the command of the Lord, came to Thiruvanaikka as an elephant and a spider, engaged in service to Lord Shiva, and attained liberation after their enmity. This is the history of the greatness of Thiruvanaikka. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman is going to narrate it in detail. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman refers to the elephant as "Mikka Thavaththor," meaning one who had performed great penance, referring to its previous state as one of Shiva's attendants. Similarly, Sekkizhar Peruman praises the other, the spider, as "Gnanmudiya oru silanthi" (a wise spider), referring to its previous state. Generally, in the sacred histories of the Nayanmars, it is not the tradition of the divine Sekkizhar Peruman to explicitly reveal their previous states in this manner. However, in the context of Kochengat Chozha Nayanar's story, it is essential to mention it. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman is going to explain that the spider itself became Kochengat Chozha Nayanar, thus clarifying the Nayanar's previous state. The third verse: "Ana cheyalal Thiruvanaikka endru atharku peyaraga gnanmudiya oru silanthi nambar sempon thirumudi mel kanal viravum sarugu uthira vannam kalantha vai noolal mel nal thiru merkkatti ena virinthu seriya purinthullathal." As we saw earlier, the place where the elephant worshipped is called Thiruvanaikka. A wise spider here at Thiruvanaikka built a canopy over the Lord's golden crown with its silk threads to prevent the sun's heat and dry leaves from falling on it. The spider built a canopy over the Lord's head to prevent sunlight from falling on the Lord's body and dry leaves from falling on it. The fourth and fifth verses: "Nandru mizhaitha silambi valaipparappai nathan adi vananga sendra yanai anuchitham endru athanaich chithaikka silambi than endru kalitrin karam suzhala sendru meela vizhaithu athanai andru kalitha pitrai nal adal vellanai azhithathal." And "Embirandhan thirumeniyin mel sarugu vizhamal yan varunthi imbar izhaitha nool valayam azhippathe endru urutthezhunthu vembi silambi thuthikkaiyinil pukku kadikka vegathal kumba yanai kai nilathin mothi kulainthu vizhnthathal." The divine Sekkizhar Peruman narrates the history of Thiruvanaikka, singing that the spider, in its anger, entered the elephant's trunk and bit it, causing the elephant to collapse and fall to the ground. The spider thus built a canopy with its silk threads. At that time, the elephant, which went to worship the Lord's feet, considered this inappropriate and destroyed the canopy. This was because the spider had built the canopy with its mouth. Therefore, the canopy made with saliva-stained threads was considered impure by the elephant, and it destroyed it. Seeing this, the spider thought that its canopy was destroyed because the elephant's hand had unknowingly swung around, and so the spider rebuilt the canopy. It built the canopy again with its silk threads. The next day, the elephant came to worship again and, seeing the canopy made of silk threads, destroyed it again. Now the spider thought, "Is this elephant destroying the canopy of threads that I painstakingly built to prevent dry leaves from falling on the Lord's body?" The spider became very angry, rose up, seethed with rage, and entered the elephant's trunk. It entered deep inside the trunk, near the elephant's brain, where there are very delicate tissues. It went to that area and bit there. Here, we see the truth that even the smallest can bring down the greatest. As the spider entered the elephant's trunk and bit the tissues near the brain, the elephant, unable to bear the pain, repeatedly struck its trunk on the ground, collapsed, and fell. This spider was a type of venomous spider called "Suthai Silambi." Therefore, it bit the elephant to death.

Next, the elephant fell and died. The sixth verse: "Tharai pirputaippa kaippukka silambi thanum uyir neenga maraiyir porulum tharum attran mathayanaiyukkum varam koduthu muraiyil silambi thannai chozhar kulathu vanthu mun uthithu niraiyir puvanam kathalikka arul seythu arula nilathin kan." As mentioned before, the elephant died by hitting the ground, and the spider, which had entered the elephant's trunk, also died. At that moment, the Lord, who is the essence of the Vedas, in His merciful way, granted a boon to the mighty elephant. He granted Shivapatham (the abode of Shiva) to the elephant. And to the spider, Lord Shiva granted the boon that it would be born in the Chozha lineage and rule this world righteously, protecting it. That very spider, as Kochengat Chozha Nayanar, incarnated as a Chozha king and built over 70 Maadakkoil temples for the Lord. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman continues to sing this sacred history. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 03 — Kochengat Chozha Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Volume 1016, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. The Puranam of Kochengat Chozha Nayanar, from the Manniya Seer Sarakkam. By the divine command of the Lord, the spider is to be born in the Chozha lineage and rule this world. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman sings the history of how it incarnated as Kochengat Chozha Nayanar and built temples. The seventh verse: "Thonmaitharu chozhar kulathu arasanan subadevan thannudaiya perumdevi kamalavathiyudan sarnthu mannum pugazh thiruthillai mandradum malarppatham senniyura paniythu etthi thiruvadikkeezh vazhipadunal." The Chozha lineage is an ancient one. In that Chozha lineage, there was a king named Subadevan. His chief queen was Kamalavathi. King Subadevan, along with his chief queen Kamalavathi, would bow their heads in worship and praise the lotus-like feet of the Lord who dances in the renowned Thillai Ambalam, and worship at the base of those sacred steps, the Panchakshara steps. On those days, as they had no children, the Queen Mother prayed for that boon. The eighth verse: "Makkalperu inmaiyinal madhevi varam venda sekkar nedunjadai mudiyar thiruvullam seythalinal mikka thiruppani seytha silambi kula venthu magizh ak kamalavathi vayitrin animaguvay vanthadaiya." As Queen Kamalavathi had no children, she prayed to the Lord. The Lord, whose long matted hair was like the evening sky, was moved by Kamalavathi's prayer and decided to grant her wish. Thus, the spider, which had performed great service by building temples for the Lord, came to be born as a beautiful child in Kamalavathi's womb. The great service performed by the spider at Thiruvanaikka was that while the elephant worshipped with water and flowers, the spider went beyond that by building a canopy over the Lord's head to prevent dry leaves from falling. Therefore, it was a great service. When the elephant destroyed it once, the spider rebuilt it again and again, making it even greater. The spider performed this great service not according to its small capacity, but beyond its capacity. It profusely spun its silk threads to build that sacred canopy. That very spider is now born as a beautiful child in Kamalavathi's womb. It entered the womb. The ninth verse: "Kalaiyar tholi kamalavathi thanpal karuppa na nirambi vilaiyar magavu pera aduththa velai athanil kalam unar pazhaiyar oru nazhigai kazhiththu pirakkumel ip pasunguzhavi uzhaiyar puvanam oru moonrum alikkum enna velizhaiyar." In Kamalavathi's womb, the pregnancy progressed, and the time for the child to be born arrived. Generally, the duration is calculated to be between 280 and 300 days. Kamalavathi was eagerly awaiting this day. Having prayed to the Lord for a child for a long time and finally receiving this boon, she was filled with great joy and awaited that moment. At that time, the astrologers, referred to as "kalam unar pazhaiyar" in this verse, who predict horoscopes based on the time of a child's birth, after understanding the planetary positions, said, "If this child is born one nazhigai later, this child will rule all three worlds." The tenth verse: "Piravatho oru nazhigai kazhiththu en pillai pirakkum parisu en kal uravu eduththu thookkum ena utra seyal matrathu mutri aravanargal solliya kalam anaiya pinivittu arumaniyai iravathu ozhival petru eduththu en kochengannano endral." Hearing what the astrologers said, Kamalavathi said, "Let my child not be born now. Bind my legs and hang me upside down so that my child is born one nazhigai later." By tying her legs and hanging her upside down, the child's birth, which would naturally occur by gravity, would be delayed by one nazhigai. So she asked them to tie her legs and hang her upside down. The maidservants did as she commanded. The astrologers, referred to as "aravanargal," are those who, according to the principles of astrology, understand the effects of planetary positions through calculations of time and guide people on the path of dharma. Therefore, they were called aravanargal. When the time they predicted arrived, and while her legs were still tied and held up, the knots were untied. Mother Kamalavathi then gave birth to a child like a rare gem and took it in her arms. Although Kamalavathi was about to attain Shivapatham in a short while, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman states in this verse, "iravathu ozhival," meaning she would not die. Even though Kamalavathi was to attain Shivapatham shortly after the child's birth, she would forever be immortalized by the fame of being Kochengannan's mother. Therefore, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman states it thus. As soon as she gave birth to that child, like a rare gem, Kamalavathi looked at the child. The child's eyes were red. Because the birth was delayed by one nazhigai, the child's eyes were red. Seeing this, the mother called out, "En Kochengannano?" (Is this my Kochengannan?). The mother's overflowing love led to that name being given to the child. When the astrologer spoke, and the Chozha king was confused as to what to do, the queen asked them to tie her legs and hang her upside down, and they did so. This is narrated in detail in the Thiruvanaikka Puranam by Udayar. Thus, Kamalavathi gave birth to a son like a rare gem. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman sings that she gave birth to a child like a rare gem, implying that just as gems and pearls emerge and their source materials are destroyed, so too was her life. The eleventh verse: "Devi puthalvar petru irakka sengor chozhan subadevan aavi anaiya varum puthalvan thannai valarthu angu ani magudam mevum urimai mudi kavithu thanum virumbu perunthavathin thavi neriyai sendradainthu thalaivar sivalogam sarnthan." As soon as his queen Kamalavathi gave birth to their son and passed away, Subadevan, the righteous Chozha king, raised his precious son, who was like his own life, and at the appropriate age, crowned him. He bestowed upon him the royal title. Having done so, he embraced the path of great penance, the Vanaprastha ashrama, which he desired, and then attained the Shivlokam of the Lord. It was a good old royal tradition for a father to crown his son at the appropriate age and then embark on penance. Subadevan, the father of Kochengannan, first embraced the worldly affairs of kingship, and with its help, later abandoned it to embrace the path of penance, and with its help, finally attained Shivlokam. The twelfth verse: "Kothai velar kochengat chozhar tham ikkuvalayathil athi murthi arulal mun arinthu piranthu man alvar puthanathan than magizhthu porunthum perunthan sivalayangal kathalodum pala edukum thondu puriyum kadan poondar." Kochengat Chozha Nayanar, by the grace of Lord Shiva, the primordial Lord, was born in this world with the knowledge of his previous birth. With that memory, he ruled this world. Generally, it is very rare for a child born in this world to be born with the memory of its previous state, or its parents' previous state. By the divine grace of Lord Shiva, Kochengat Chozha Nayanar was born with the memory of his previous birth. Kochengat Chozha Nayanar undertook the duty of building many large, cool Shiva temples, which Lord Shiva, the Lord of all beings, joyfully resides in, with great love. In his previous state as a spider, he had spun a web to form a canopy over Lord Shiva's idol to prevent dry leaves from falling on it. Therefore, the service he performed as a spider, to the extent of his ability, was not completed. Consequently, in this birth, he built many temples, and specifically strong Maadakkoil temples. He built many temples out of great enthusiasm and eagerness. It is said that he built 78 Maadakkoil temples. "Perungkoil ezhupathinetrodu" (with seventy-eight great temples) is from Thirunavukkarasu Peruman's Thevaram. Vaishnava Azhvar sang that he built 70 temples. All these great temples are Maadakkoil temples. He built all of them as Maadakkoil temples that elephants could not enter. The reason for this was his memory of his previous birth. That is, because the canopy he had built in his previous birth was destroyed by an elephant, Kochengat Chozha Nayanar built all his temples as Maadakkoil temples that elephants could not enter, so that this would not happen again. The thirteenth verse: "Anaikkavil tham munnam arulpetra athanai arinthu angu manai thariththa thirukkarathar magizhum koyil seykintrar. Gnanachcharvam vennavaludane kooda nalanchirakka panar kazhuthu tham peruman amarum koyil pani samaithar." He built temples in Thiruvanaikka, knowing that he had received grace there before, and rejoiced in doing so. What joy it was! The joy came from the thought that dry leaves would no longer fall on the Lord's body, and the elephant could not destroy it. He sat there with the joy of thinking that everyone would see and worship this temple and receive abundant grace. He built the temple for the Lord, associating it with the white Jamun tree, which is the source of spiritual wisdom. Even today, we can see and worship this sacred shrine, where the white Jamun tree and the Lord residing beneath it are seen as one, forming the temple. It is true that the Sthala Vriksham (sacred tree of the temple) and the Lord residing beneath it should be worshipped together. Without understanding this truth, in many places today, Sthala Vrikshams are removed for temple construction and relocated elsewhere. This is very regrettable. Sthala Vrikshams should not be damaged or removed in any way. Doing so is an offense against Shiva. The fourteenth verse: "Manthirigal thamaiyevi vallal kodai anapayan munthai varum kula muthaloraya muthal senganar anthamil seer sonattil aganadu thorum maniyar chandrasekaran amarum thanangal pala samaithar." Kochengat Chozha Nayanar, the ancestor of the generous Anapaya Chakravarti, is praised by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman in this Periyapuranam, among other places, for being the reason for the composition of this great Puranam, as he was the king who came in the lineage of Chozha kings who were devotees. Sekkizhar Peruman praises Anapaya Chozha not just as a generous giver, but as "Vallal Illalkodai," meaning one who is generous in all aspects, not just in giving. Anapaya Chozha Nayanar's generosity extended to upholding the path of sacred ash. That is why he was also known as Thiruneetru Chozha. He was instrumental in the composition of this Puranam. Kochengat Chozha Nayanar was the ancestor of such an Anapaya Chozha. He himself took the initiative to build the temple in Thiruvanaikka. Similarly, he commanded his ministers to build many temples throughout the Chozha country. Due to his eagerness to build many temples quickly, he sent his ministers. Kochengat Chozha Nayanar built many beautiful Maadakkoil temples where Lord Chandrasekara joyfully resides. He built many temples. There is a reference that he built 78 Maadakkoil temples. Thirumangai Azhvar, one of the Vaishnava Azhvars, has sung about the temples built by Kochengat Chozha Nayanar in the Divya Prabandham. The 1505th pasuram of Periya Thirumozhi by Thirumangai Azhvar praises him: "Irukku ilangu thirumozhivay enthol eesarkku ezhil madam ezhupathu seythu ulagandu thirukkulathu valacholan" (The Chozha king, who built seventy beautiful temples for the eight-shouldered Lord, whose sacred words shine in the Vedas, and ruled the world from the sacred lineage). Although Azhvar mentions 70 Maadakkoil temples, we saw that there is also a reference to 78. However, if a doubt arises as to why the divine Sekkizhar Peruman does not mention this number, we understand that according to Saivite tradition, good deeds should not be counted. Moreover, even though he built so many temples, he intended to do much more. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman did not mention the exact number, considering the benefit they would bring. We also see that he built many Vishnu temples, and the Thirunaraiyur Vishnu temple is one of them. The fifteenth verse: "Akkoil thorum sivanukku amuthupadi muthalana mikka perum selvangal viruppinal miga amaithu thikkanathum thani sengol murai niruthi thervendar mukkan muthal nadamadum muthal thillai munninar." Kochengat Chozha Nayanar established abundant wealth, including provisions for the Lord's offerings, in all those temples. At this point, the commentator Sivakavimani Aiyya notes: "All these endowments established by Kochengat Chozha Nayanar are not seen everywhere now. Many of the Maadakkoil temples built by Kochengat Chozha Nayanar are in a dilapidated state, needing renovation. It is very sad to see these temples without even a small amount of offerings or lamps. The Saivite world remains silent in the face of these conditions. This is the cruelty of time," writes Sivakavimani Aiyya in his commentary. However, Sivakavimani Aiyya wrote his commentary about 70-80 years ago. Now, in this century, due to Sivakavimani's great penance and fervent prayer, many devotees are renovating numerous Shiva temples, and all the Paadal Petra Sthalams (temples sung by the Nayanmars) are being renovated and shine with new splendor. We hope that the temples that still need renovation will also be quickly renovated and witness Kumbhabhishekam. Kochengat Chozha Nayanar, who established these, ruled justly, upholding the righteous scepter. Having ruled thus, he thought of Thillai, where the Lord performs His dance, and went there. The sixteenth verse: "Thiruvarantha semponnin ambalathe nadam seyyum perumanai adi vanangi perambu thalai sirappa uruga nindru ullam kalippa thozhuthu etthi uraiyinal varuvaimai maraiyavarkku maligaigal pala samaithar." In Thillai, he bowed at the feet of the Lord who performs the sacred dance in the golden Ambalam, and with overflowing love, with a melting heart, he rejoiced, worshipped, and praised. During his stay there, he built numerous mansions for the Thillai Brahmins, who were known for their truthfulness in speech. The Thillai Brahmins are those who possess the divine quality of Shiva, having been declared by Lord Nataraja Himself as "one of us." They are renowned for their practice of performing fire sacrifices. Therefore, the mansions where these Thillai Brahmins reside are also sacred sites. They are abodes where Lord Shiva resides. Thus, Kochengat Chozha Nayanar built mansions for the Dikshitars, the Thillai Brahmins. The seventeenth verse: "Devarpiran thiruthondil kochengat sembiyarkon pu valaiyum pothu neekki andaruli puvaniyinn mel eviya narttondu purinthu imaiyavargal adi potra mevinar thiruthillai venthar thiruvadi nizhal keezh." Kochengat Chozha, who was born as Kochengat Sembiyan, due to his service to Lord Shiva, the Lord of the Devas, ruled the entire world as if it belonged to him alone. He performed good services to Shiva in this world, as commanded by Lord Shiva, and attained the shade of the sacred feet of the King of Thillai, Lord Nataraja, worshipped by the Devas. We see that many of the Maadakkoil temples he built are also Paadal Petra Sthalams (temples sung by the Nayanmars). Some of the Maadakkoil temples built by the Lord include Thiruaakkur Thanthonri Maadam, Anaangur, Thiru Aavur Pasupatheeswaram, Ambar Perum Thirukkoil, Thiru Arisilkkarai Putthur, Gajaranyam, Ilangaadu, Iraivasal Nallur, Thiru Indalur Thanthonriappar, Kaichinnam, Keezhvelur, Koththavasal, Kudavayil, Koothappar, Mathali Velur, Maliyeeswaram, Marugal, Mookkeeswaram (Uraiyur), Nallur Perum Thirukkoil, Naduvananathar, Nallur, Nannilam, Nindriyur, Thiru Pananthal, Panamangalam, Pandaravadai, Pasupathi Koil, Pazhaiyarai Vadathali, Thiruthungaanai Maadam, Perumkadambur, Peruvelur, Rudra Gangai, Sattanathar, Seerkazhi, Sengalur, Sikkal, Chozhiya Vazhakkam, Thalaichangadu, Thirukkaruppariyallur, Thandalai Neelnery, Therur, Thiruchaykkadu, Thiruchempanpalli, Thirumanamedu, Thiruvanaikka, Thiruvezhimizhalai, Thiruviyattam, Vaikal Maadakkoil, Thiru Valivalam, Thiru Virkudi, and many other temples. We see that all these are Maadakkoil temples that elephants cannot enter. Due to this sacred service, this Nayanar has been praised by the great Acharayas like Thirugnanasambandha Peruman. We see this. Thirugnanasambandha Peruman has praised Kochengat Chozha Nayanar in the first Thirumurai, 48th Thiruppathigam, sixth verse; second Thirumurai, 23rd Thiruppathigam, fifth verse; second Thirumurai, 63rd Thiruppathigam, seventh verse; second Thirumurai, 120th Thiruppathigam, third and sixth verses; third Thirumurai, 18th Thiruppathigam, second, fourth, and sixth verses; third Thirumurai, 19th Thiruppathigam, first, second, fifth, and ninth verses; and third Thirumurai, 50th Thiruppathigam, ninth verse. Thirunavukkarasu Peruman has praised and sung in the fourth Thirumurai, 70th Thiruppathigam, second verse; fourth Thirumurai, 49th Thiruppathigam, fourth verse; fourth Thirumurai, 62nd Thiruppathigam, ninth verse; fourth Thirumurai, 65th Thiruppathigam, third verse; sixth Thirumurai, 20th Thiruppathigam, fifth verse; sixth Thirumurai, 23rd Thiruppathigam, third verse; sixth Thirumurai, 65th Thiruppathigam, sixth verse; sixth Thirumurai, 74th Thiruppathigam, eighth verse; sixth Thirumurai, 75th Thiruppathigam, eighth verse; and sixth Thirumurai, 83rd Thiruppathigam, sixth verse. Our Lord Sundaramoorthy Swamigal has sung in the seventh Thirumurai, 39th Thiruppathigam, 11th verse (Thiruthondathogai); seventh Thirumurai, 55th Thiruppathigam, fourth verse; seventh Thirumurai, 65th Thiruppathigam, first verse; seventh Thirumurai, 66th Thiruppathigam, second verse; seventh Thirumurai, 98th Thiruppathigam, 10th and 11th verses. In the ninth Thirumurai, 23rd Thiruppathigam, third verse, Kochengat Chozha Nayanar is praised. In the eleventh Thirumurai, Nakkirar Deva Nayanar's Gopaprasadam and Nambiandar Nambigal's Thiruthondar Thiruvandathi have praised him. This sacred Puranam is found in Periyapuranam. As mentioned above, Thirumangai Azhvar has also praised this Nayanar. Thus, Kochengat Chozha Nayanar, who performed such great sacred service, resides in the shade of the sacred feet of Lord Thillai Koothaperuman. This sacred Puranam concludes with the eighteenth and final verse. "Karuneela midattrar seyya kazhaladi nizhal sera varuneermai udaya sengat chozhar tham malar thal vazhththi tharuneermai isaikkul yazhin thalaivaray ulagametthum thiruneelakanda panar thiram ini seppalutren." The divine Sekkizhar Peruman states that, having praised the lotus-like feet of Kochengat Chozha Nayanar, who was destined to attain the shade of the sacred feet adorned with anklets of the blue-throated Lord, and with its support, he will now proceed to narrate the greatness of Thiruneelakanda Yazhpanar Nayanar, who was the chief of the Yazh (lute) among those who offered music to the world. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman concludes this sacred history, which he has narrated according to his tradition, and now sets the stage for the next sacred Puranam. Let us also praise the sacred feet of our Lord Kochengat Chozha Nayanar, and with its support, continue to contemplate the sacred Puranam of Thiruneelakanda Yazhpanar Nayanar. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of our Lord Kochengat Chozha Nayanar. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of our Lord Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of Nambiandar Nambigal Peruman. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the commentator Aiyya. Thiruchitrambalam.
