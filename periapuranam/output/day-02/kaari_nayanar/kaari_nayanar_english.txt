Chapter 01 — Kaari Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 982, authored by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Karaikkandan Sarukkam, Kaari Nayanar Puranam. In the Periya Puranam authored by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, the Puranam of Kaari Nayanar is the 49th Puranam, following the Puranam of Kanampulla Nayanar. Firstly, in the anthology Thiruthondathogai, this Nayanar is praised by Sundaramoorthy Swamigal as "Kaarikkum Adiyen" (I am a devotee to Kaari). Next, in the classified work Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi, authored by Nambiandar Nambigal Peruman, the 59th Thiru Paadal praises Kaari Nayanar. That Thiru Paadal is as follows: "Pullana aagaa vagai ulagathu punarndhanavum sollainavum nayamaakki sudar porkuvadu thani villanai vaazhthi vilangum kayilai pukkaan enbaraal kallina maamadil suuzh kadavurinil kaariyaiye." The meaning of this Thiru Paadal is that Kaari Nayanar, also known as Kaariyar, who was born in the sacred place of Thirukkadavoor, was a very great Tamil poet. He composed a Tamil work using very excellent and pleasing words. However, its inner meaning and ultimate purpose were the supreme bliss and Lord Shiva. It is said that Kaari Nayanar, having composed it thus, praised the Lord and attained the sacred Kailash of the Lord. Kaari Nayanar was born in Thirukkadavoor. He composed a Tamil work called Kaarikkovaiyar. He created this work with very refined words, showing it as the fruit of the branches of Tamil, and arranged its meaning to be very hidden and subtle. Having composed this, he went to the three Tamil kings, explained it to them, and gained their friendship. He explained the meaning of this work to them. With the great wealth given by the three kings, he built many temples for Lord Shiva. He spoke words of joy that pleased everyone's heart. He bestowed great wealth upon the devotees of Lord Shiva. Kaari Nayanar, who lived thus, resides eternally with the Lord in His sacred Kailash, never forgetting Him. He received Shiva's grace and resides in the sacred northern Kailash mountain. This is the sacred history of Kaari Nayanar. This is what this Thiru Paadal praises. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman explains this to us in five Thiru Paadals as the Puranam of Kaari Nayanar. Therefore, in the detailed work Periya Puranam, the Puranam of Kaari Nayanar is the second Nayanar among the five Nayanars praised in the ninth chapter, Karaikkandan Sarukkam. This sacred Puranam is structured as a section that narrates the history and character of the Nayanar named Kaariyar. In this, the first Thiru Paadal is: "Maraiyalar Thirukkadavoor vandhu udhithu vandamizhin thuraiyaana payan therindhu sol vilangi porul maraiyaatha thamizh kovai tham peyaraal kulavum vagaiyaale muraiyaale thoguthu amaithu moovendhar paal payilvaar." Kaari Nayanar was born in the sacred place of Thirukkadavoor, where many Vedic scholars lived. Kaari Nayanar composed a Kovai work in Tamil. That work is called Kaarikkovai by his own name. He composed this work by selecting the fruit of the rich and sweet branches of Tamil, making its words clear and its inner meaning subtly hidden. Thus, it is understood that Kaarikkovai is the name of the work composed by the Nayanar. This was composed by him as an Akapporul (inner meaning) Kovai work. However, due to our lack of merit, we do not know any more details about this work. According to the divine words of Sekkizhar Peruman, it is understood that this work was structured to excel in the classification of words and meanings. Furthermore, it is understood that he composed this work in such a way that the words are clear and their inner meaning leads to the attainment of liberation (mukti). That is why the divine Sekkizhar Peruman states "thamizhin thuraiyaana payan" (the fruit of the branches of Tamil). The branches of Tamil are Akathurai (inner aspects) and Purathurai (outer aspects). The benefit of these, as arranged in Tamil as Akapporul and Purapporul, is the attainment of liberation, which is beneficial to souls. In Akapporul, the Lord unites with devotees, and in Purapporul, the Lord's victorious knowledge overcomes attachments. Therefore, the benefit and profit for souls from these two branches is the attainment of liberation (mukti). This is the benefit of Tamil literary works. Great scholars like Tholkappiyar have established that the ultimate benefit is liberation. Among these, Kaarikkovai, composed by Kaari Nayanar, is an Akathurai work. It is structured similarly to Thiruchitrambalakkovaiyar, composed by Srimath Manikkavasagar Swamigal. When Srimath Manikkavasagar Swamigal was in Thillai, the Lord Himself came disguised as a Brahmin from the Pandya country and requested Srimath Manikkavasagar Swamigal to recite all of Thiruvachagam again. Srimath Manikkavasagar Swamigal recited it again, and the Lord Himself wrote it down on a palm leaf. Along with that, the Lord requested, "With the same mouth that sang Thiruvachagam, sing Thirukkovaiyar. Praise the Lord of Thiruchitrambalam and compose a Kovai with Him as the protagonist." In accordance with this request, Srimath Manikkavasagar Swamigal composed the divine work Thiruchitrambalakkovaiyar, as we have seen. The commentator for such a Thirukkovaiyar was a scholar named Perasiriyar. Even though Thirukkovaiyar is an Akathurai work, dealing with the intimate matters and Akapporul aspects between a hero and heroine, it is a work that praises the Lord who bestows supreme bliss. In accordance with what Saivite scholars said, Perasiriyar's commentary also begins in that manner. Perasiriyar states in his commentary: "Arivan nool porulum ulaga nool vazhakkum ena iruporul nudhali eduthukondhanar. Aangu avvirandhanul aagama nool vazhiyin nudhaliya gnana yoga nunporulinai unarthatharku ariyadhu." (They took up two meanings: the meaning of the scripture of the wise and the custom of worldly texts. Among these two, it is difficult to convey the subtle meaning of gnana yoga as indicated by the path of Agamic texts.) Perasiriyar mentions this at the beginning of his commentary on Thiruchitrambalakkovaiyar. "Arivan nool porul" refers to the Agamas. "Ulaga nool vazhakku" refers to worldly texts. Both of these are the intended meanings of Thirukkovaiyar. Although Thirukkovaiyar aims to convey both, it profoundly embeds the subtle meaning of gnana yoga according to the Agamic path. Perasiriyar states that it is very difficult to convey all of this. Therefore, it is understood that the inner meanings of Akathurai and Purathurai songs, which have the praise of the Lord as their inner core, are also structured in the same way. Pandit Mu. Kandaiya, who wrote a commentary on this sacred Puranam, states: "Vandamizhin thuraiyaana payan idhu enal" (This is the fruit of the branches of rich Tamil) as mentioned by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Generally, in human life, pleasure is the goal of humans. This pleasure is of two types: Sitrinbam (sensual pleasure) and Perinbam (supreme bliss). Sitrinbam is the state where the soul does not abandon its attachments to mala (impurities), maya (illusion), and karma. In this state, the pleasure derived from the senses and other instruments is Sitrinbam. That is, the pleasure experienced by the inner and outer instruments, which are products of maya, through worldly objects (purapporul), is Sitrinbam. Now, Perinbam is the pleasure experienced in a pure state, having abandoned all these attachments, beyond the senses, faculties, and instruments, with the aid of divine grace, through Shiva-gnana (divine knowledge). This is called Shiva-bhogam, or Perinbam. Thus, there are two types of pleasures. Therefore, the ancient Tamil texts that fully examined life and established its grammar, among which Tholkappiyam is primary, state that in this bound state, when experiencing worldly pleasures and objects, the love that arises in the hearts of a man and a woman, their unity, and the conduct in that state, is called Akathinai. This pleasure, which occurs between a hero and a heroine, can be hindered when they separate due to various reasons. Moreover, this pleasure becomes tiresome as one matures. Furthermore, this Sitrinbam ends with death. Therefore, what does this Sitrinbam teach the soul? It teaches impermanence. Sitrinbam has such great shortcomings. Therefore, Sitrinbam serves to make the soul realize the truth that it should now seek lasting Perinbam. Thus, when the world becomes tiresome, the soul begins to seek true pleasure. It seeks and strives to attain it. Therefore, even though there are Akathinai texts in Tamil that describe inner conduct, it is well understood that their purpose is to move souls towards Perinbam. That is why Nachinarkiniyar, the commentator of Tholkappiyam, states, "Inganam innilaiyamanum piravatranum veettirke kaaranam koorinaar. Inganam koorave ivvaasiriyar peridhum payan tharuvadhor ilakkaname koorinaar aayithu. Idhanaal seidha pulan neri vazhakinai unarnthor immai marumai vazhuvaamal semmai neriyal thurai povar aadhali." (Thus, by this and other means, he stated the cause for liberation. By stating thus, this author has indeed given a very beneficial grammar. Therefore, those who understand the path of the senses will proceed on the righteous path without faltering in this life or the next.) Therefore, the aim of Tamil literature and texts, whether they are Akathurai or Purapporul texts, is to lead to the pleasure of liberation (Perinbam). Thus, it is understood that Akathinai, through Tholkappiyam, conveyed the impermanence of Sitrinbam and its consequence. This becomes the branch of pure Tamil. The ultimate fruit obtained through it, the endless pleasure, the lasting pleasure, the flawless pleasure, that pleasure which is liberation (Veedu), that is the fruit of the branch of pure Tamil, which is beautifully understood. That is why the divine Sekkizhar Peruman has composed it here as "Vandamizhin thuraiyaana payan therindhu sol vilangi porul marai maraiya" (knowing the fruit of the branch of rich Tamil, with words clear and meaning hidden). How do Tamil literary works explaining Akathurai begin? First, the scene of "kaatchi" (sight) occurs. That is, a young hero sees a young heroine. This happens due to destiny. After seeing and then falling in love, when the hero is weakened, his friend asks about his well-being in a teasing manner, "Is the reason for your weakness that you have entered the path of sweet Tamil?" This type of situation in Thirukkovaiyar indicates that the Tamil path, which is Akathinai, indeed points to Perinbam. For the fact that the fruit of Akathurai is liberation (Veedu), we have already seen Nachinarkiniyar's commentary where he states, "This author stated the cause for liberation," which serves as evidence. Therefore, in this way, the initial outcome of Akathinai is Sitrinbam, and its ultimate fruit, the final outcome, is Perinbam. Although these two have significant differences, they also share common characteristics. Whether it is Sitrinbam or Perinbam, they occur individually. They are intimate experiences that others cannot know. They are inexpressible. Those who have experienced them once desire to experience them again. Therefore, the goal of human life encompasses both of these. In such a situation, the Kovai Prabandham is a literary form that explains both Sitrinbam and Perinbam, and unites the place where they are attained. Divine, wise Tamil poets, endowed with subtle insight and clarity of true knowledge, have therefore chosen and handled this type of Prabandham called Kovai. Among them, Kaarikkovaiyar, composed by Kaari Nayanar, is one, and Thiruchitrambalakkovaiyar, composed by the wealthy sage Srimath Manikkavasagar Swamigal, is another. Thiruchitrambalakkovaiyar is available to us. Kaarikkovai is not available to us due to our lack of merit. Therefore, based on Thiruchitrambalakkovaiyar composed by Srimath Manikkavasagar Swamigal, the truth is understood that Kaarikkovaiyar must have been similar. In Thiruchitrambalakkovaiyar composed by Srimath Manikkavasagar Swamigal, although the explanation of sensual love (Sitrinba Kaadhal) appears as the explicit, gross meaning, we see that the explanation of divine love (Perinba Kaadhal) is the main, inner meaning. In a section called "Kalaviyuraithal" in Thiruchitrambalakkovaiyar, there is a verse where the hero, who has experienced divine union, describes the nature of that union's bliss. This is the explicit, gross meaning. However, its Perinbam meaning is explained as expressing wonder at the bliss attained. The Thiru Paadal is: "Sorpaal amudhu ival yaan suvai enna thunindhu ingane narpaal vinai deivam thandhu indru naan ivalaam paguthi porpaar arivaar puliyoor punithan pothiyil verpil karpaaviya varai vaaykkadi thotta kalavagathe." In this Thiru Paadal, Srimath Manikkavasagar Swamigal simultaneously presents worldly love, which is the meaning of worldly texts, and spiritual love, which leads to liberation and is the meaning of Agamic texts. Therefore, there might have been other Kovai works in Tamil that similarly presented both worldly and spiritual love. Among them, the Prabandham called Kaarikkovai, composed by Kaari Nayanar, must have been one. Therefore, Kaarikkovaiyar, composed by Kaari Nayanar, must have shared the same characteristics as Thiruchitrambalakkovaiyar, composed by Srimath Manikkavasagar Swamigal. That is why the divine Sekkizhar Peruman states in his divine words: "Maraiyalar Thirukkadavoor vandhu udhithu vandamizhin thuraiyaana payan therindhu sol vilakki porul maraiya kuraiyaadha thamizh kovai tham peyaraal kulavum vagai muraiyaale thoguthu amaithu." (The Brahmin, born in Thirukkadavoor, knowing the fruit of the branch of rich Tamil, explaining the words and hiding the meaning, composed a flawless Tamil Kovai in his own name, arranging it systematically.) Thus, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman shows us in this verse what the definition of a Kovai Prabandham is. In this Kaarikkovai composed by Kaari Nayanar, even though the literal meaning of the verses is directly understood as an explanation of Sitrinbam, its inner meaning is Perinbam. We can understand that even though the denotative meaning (vaachiyartham) of the words in this work is Sitrinbam, its connotative meaning (lakshiyartham) is Perinbam. Therefore, he composed this work in such a way that it explains worldly love, the common meaning, but within it, he subtly placed Perinbam as the hidden meaning, which would only be revealed upon deep study. Therefore, Kaari Nayanar took this Kovai Prabandham to the three kings and presented it. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman states that wherever the kings could not understand the crucial subtle meanings of the verses on their own, he explained them clearly in his own eloquent style. Now, the second Thiru Paadal: "Angu avar thaam magizhum vagai aduthu urai nayamaakki kongalar thaar mannavarpaal petra nidhikkuvai kondu venkanaraavodu idandhu vilangum ilampirai senni sangaranar inidhu amarum thaanangal pala samaithaar." Thus, making the words pleasing and suitable for the meaning, in a way that pleased the three kings, he took the wealth he received from those kings and built many sacred temples where Lord Shiva, adorned with a white snake and a bright young crescent moon on His head, would reside happily. The third and fourth Thiru Paadals: "Yaavarkkum manamuvakkum inba mozhippayan iyambi devarkkum mudhal devar seeradiyaar ellaarkkum mevutra iru nidhiyam miga alithu vidaiyavartham kaavutra thirukkayilai maravatha karuthinarai yeindha kadal suuzh ulagil engum tham isai niruthi aaindha unarvu idaiyaraa anbinaraay ani gangai thoyndha nedunjadai avartham arul petra thodarppinaal vaaindha manam pol udambum vadakkailai malai serndhaar." Speaking words of joy that pleased everyone's heart, he bestowed great wealth upon all the special devotees of Lord Shiva, who is the prime Deity even for the Devas. He always held in his mind the sacred Kailash of the Lord. Establishing his fame everywhere in this ocean-surrounded world, with profound understanding and ceaseless love, and by the continuous grace of the Lord with long matted hair bathed in the beautiful Ganga, his body, like his mind, reached the northern Kailash mountain.

With ceaseless love, and by the continuous grace of the Lord, he reached that northern Kailash mountain, which he had always contemplated. Our Kaari Nayanar reveled in that liberation bliss from which there is no return. The fifth and concluding Thiru Paadal: "Veriyaar malar kondrai veniyaar adiperum kaariyar kazhal vanangi avar alitha karunaiyinaal variyaar madayaanai vazhudhiyar tham madhi marabil seeriyar nedumaarar thiruthondu seppuvaam." The divine Sekkizhar Peruman, having completed the sacred Puranam of Kaari Nayanar, states, "Worshipping the sacred feet of Kaari Nayanar, who cherished the feet of the Lord adorned with fragrant Konrai flowers, and by the grace he bestowed, we shall now narrate the sacred service of Nedumaarar, the noble one in the lunar lineage of the Pandiyas, who possessed powerful elephants." With this, he begins the sacred Puranam of Nindraseer Nedumaara Nayanar. We too, bowing at the feet of Kaari Nayanar, shall now continue to contemplate the sacred Puranam of Nindraseer Nedumaara Nayanar. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of our Lord Kaari Nayanar! Praise, praise to the sacred feet of our Lord Sundaramoorthy Swamigal! Praise, praise to the sacred feet of Nambiandar Nambigal Peruman! Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the divine Sekkizhar Peruman! Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the commentator Sivakavimani Ayya! Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 02 — Kaari Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 983, authored by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Karaikkandan Sarukkam. Nindraseer Nedumaara Nayanar Puranam. In the Periya Puranam authored by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, the Puranam of Nindraseer Nedumaara Nayanar is the 50th Puranam, following the Puranam of Kaari Nayanar. First, the anthology. In the eighth Thiru Paadal of the Thiruthondathogai Thiruppathikam, authored by our Lord Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, Nindraseer Nedumaara Nayanar is praised. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal praises him as: "Niraikonda sindhaiyaan nelveli vendra nindraseer nedumaaran adiyaarkkum adiyen." (I am a devotee to the devotees of Nindraseer Nedumaaran, who conquered Nelveli with a mind full of integrity.) The meaning of this is that "I am a devotee to the devotees of Nindraseer Nedumaaran, who conquered the battle of Nelveli with a mind filled with integrity." What is meant by "Niraikonda sindhaiyaan"? Niraikonda sindhai (a mind full of integrity) means that the Pandya king Nedumaaran, who won the battle that took place in Nelveli, did so not out of evil intentions like greed for land or anger, but out of a sense of justice to protect his country. This was not a war initiated by Nedumaara Nayanar himself. It was a defensive war fought when kings from the northern regions invaded his country. That was the battle of Nelveli. Our Nayanar was victorious in it. His sacred name is Nedumaaran. He is praised as Nindraseer Nedumaara Nayanar. This name and honor came to him due to the character we observe in his historical narrative. We saw in the Puranam of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman that there was a king named Koon Pandiyan in Madurai, in the Pandya country. Although he had converted to Jainism due to the evil teachings of the Jains, he had as his wife Mangaiyarkkarasi Ammaiyar, a jewel of Saivism, who was the daughter of the Chola king. He had our Lord Kulachirai Nayanar as his minister. This Koon Pandiyan was touched by the sacred hand of our spiritual preceptor Thirugnanasambandha Moorthy Peruman, smeared with sacred ash, received the true teachings of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, had his hunchback straightened, and was named Nedumaaran Nayanar. He is the hero of this Puranam, Nindraseer Nedumaara Nayanar. We have extensively contemplated all the sacred historical events, such as the king's hunchback being straightened, in the Puranam of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman. Thus, the anthology is structured. Now, the classified work. The 60th Thiru Paadal in Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi, authored by our Lord Nambiandar Nambigal Peruman, praises our Nayanar. The Thiru Paadal is: "Kaar than mugil kai kadarkaaLiyar perumaarkku edhiraay aartha amaNar azhindhadhu kandu matru aangu avarai koortha kazhuvin udhi vaitha panjavan endru uraikkum vaarthai adhu pandu nelveliyil vendra maaranukke." The meaning is that Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, who has hands like dark, cool clouds, defeated the Jains who came to oppose Aludaiya Pillaiyar, the Lord of Kadarkaliyar, in a debate and blessed them. The Pandya king impaled the defeated Jains on stakes at that very spot. That Pandya king, Nindraseer Nedumaara Nayanar, was the one who had previously won the battle of Nelveli. Now, in the detailed work Periya Puranam, authored by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, the Puranam of Nindraseer Nedumaara Nayanar is the 50th sacred Puranam, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman in 10 Thiru Paadals. In these 10 Thiru Paadals, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman briefly concludes in the first two Thiru Paadals that Nindraseer Nedumaara Nayanar was initially Koon Pandiyan and under the influence of Jains, and in the second Thiru Paadal, that he became a devout Saivite by the grace of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman. This is because these historical events are extensively sung in the Puranam of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman. Now, in accordance with the praise in the anthology, "Niraikonda sindhaiyaan nelveli vendra nindraseer nedumaaran," the divine Sekkizhar Peruman has described in detail the Nayanar's next historical section, his victory in the battle of Nelveli, in the remaining eight Thiru Paadals. Ninth Sarukkam.

In the glorious Karaikkandan Sarukkam, the third Puranam is that of Nindraseer Nedumaara Nayanar. The first Thiru Paadal: "Thadumaarum neri adhanai thavam endru tham udalai adumaaru seidhu ozhugum amaN valaiyil agappattu vidumaaru thamizh viragar vinai maarum kazhal adaindha nedumaaranar perumai ulagu ezhum nigazhndhadhaal." The path of the Jains is one that causes confusion. Jains hold the belief that torturing the body in various ways is penance. Their doctrine states that lying on hot rocks and standing in the sun is penance. Nedumaara Nayanar, caught in the web of these Jains, was freed from that trap by attaining the feet of Aludaiya Pillaiyar, the master of Tamil. The feet of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman are those that change karma and cut off rebirth. The greatness of Nindraseer Nedumaara Nayanar, who attained such feet of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, spread throughout the seven worlds. The second Thiru Paadal: "AnnaaLil aLudaip piLLaiyaar aruLaale thennaadu sivam peruga sengol uyththu aRam aLiththu sol naama neri pottri surar nagar kon thannaik konda ponnaaram aNi maarbil puravalanar poligindraar." In those days, by the grace of Aludaiya Pillaiyar, Nedumaara Nayanar shone as a protector who ruled with a just scepter, upheld righteousness, and protected the path of Saivism, which is the path of the sacred five-letter mantra of Shiva's name, thereby increasing Saivism in the southern country. He wore a golden necklace on his chest. That golden necklace was received from Devendra. This story appears in the Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam. The Pandya kings were those whose chests were adorned with golden necklaces received from Devendra. Nindraseer Nedumaara Nayanar ruled in their lineage. The golden necklace received from Devendra was passed down from that Pandya king to successive Pandya kings. In that way, Nindraseer Nedumaara Nayanar was one whose chest was adorned with the golden necklace received from Devendra. The story of the Pandya king receiving the golden necklace from Devendra is explained in the Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam. Indra once refused to bring rain to Tamil Nadu. Following the advice of Agathiya Muni, Uggirakumara Pandiyan observed the Somavara Vrata. Along with that, Uggirakumara Pandiyan took the Chera and Chola kings with him and reached Indra's court. Devendra offered seats to the Chera and Chola kings in that court. However, Uggirakumara Pandiyan sat on a seat equal to Indra's. Devendra became jealous that Uggirakumara Pandiyan was seated equally with him. Therefore, as if giving a gift to King Uggirakumara Pandiyan, he gave him a very heavy golden necklace, which was extremely difficult to bear. Uggirakumara Pandiyan carried it as easily as a garland of flowers. This is the story we find in the Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam. Just as Uggirakumara Pandiyan performed that rare feat, the Pandya kings who came after him also bore this golden necklace, which is why the divine Sekkizhar Peruman describes it thus here. Now, the third Thiru Paadal: "Aaya arasu alippaar paal amar vendi vandhu etra seya pulathu evvar edhir nelveli serukkaluthu paaya padaikkadal mudugum parimaavin peruvellam kaayum madakkalittrin nirai parappi amar nadakindraar." While Nindraseer Nedumaara Nayanar was ruling thus, enemies from the northern regions invaded the Pandya country. He confronted the enemy kings in the battlefield of Nelveli with his army, a flood of horses, and a multitude of enraged war elephants. This war was not one desired by the Nayanar. It was a war initiated by the northern king, who came with a desire for land. Therefore, our Nayanar had to engage in battle and defeat him. This battle of Nelveli is also discussed in history. Therefore, the Pandya king undertook this war for self-defense. That is why he was praised by Sundaramoorthy Swamigal as "Niraikonda sindhaiyaan nelveli vendra" (He who conquered Nelveli with a mind full of integrity). The continuous Thiru Paadals four, five, six, and seven explain the prowess of the Pandya king in the battle of Nelveli. "Eduthodandra munai gnaatpin irupadail porupadainar padutha nedungari thuniyum paaymaavin aruguraiyum aduthu amar sei vayavar karunthalaiyum malaiyum alai sennir madutha kadal meelavum thaam vadivel vaangida peruga vayappariyin kalippoliyum maravar padaikkala oliyum kayapporuppin muzhakkoliyum kalandhu ezhu palliya oliyum viyakku mugak kadainaalin mega muzhakku ena meelach sayathodar valliyum indru thaam vidukkumpadi thayanga theeyumizhum padai vazhangum serukkaluthu murukkum udal thoyum nedunguruthi madukkulithu niNam thuythu aadi poya paruvam panikkul bhoothangalai anri peyum arum paNi seiya uNavalithathu ena piranga inaiya kadum samar vilaiya igaluzhandha paranthalaiyil panai nedungai madayaanai panjavanar padaikkudaindhu munai azhindha vadapulathu mudhal mannar padaisariya punaiyum narum thodai vaagai poozhiyar vempudan punaindhu" – these verses describe the battle. In the battlefield of Nelveli, the soldiers, elephant armies, and cavalry of both sides clashed fiercely. A great roar was heard on the battlefield. The sound of horses, the clang of warriors' weapons, the trumpeting of elephants, and the sound of various musical instruments were all heard. The battlefield appeared as if it were providing food for ghosts. Thus, the armies of the Pandya king clashed, and the invading armies of the northern king were defeated, shattered, and fled. The Pandya king wore the Vahai garland, a symbol of victory, along with the neem flower garland, which is characteristic of the Pandya kings. The eighth Thiru Paadal: "Valavar piraan thirumagaLaar mangaiyarkku arasiyaar kalaba maNi mulaith thiLaikkum thadamaarbil gauriyanar iLavar veNbiraiaNiNdhaarkku etra thiruththoNdellaam alavil pugazh pera viLakki aruL peruga arasaliththaar." Nindraseer Nedumaara Nayanar, having married Mangaiyarkkarasi Ammaiyar, the sacred daughter of the great Chola king, performed all the sacred services befitting Lord Shiva, making them shine with immeasurable fame, and ruled so that Shiva's grace might abound. The ninth Thiru Paadal: "Thirai sei kadal ulagin kaN thiruneetrin neri viLanga urai sei perum pugazh viLakki ongku nedumaaranar arasu urimai nedungaalam aLiththu iraivar aruLaale parisu perum sivalogaththil inbutru paNindhu irundhaar." In this world surrounded by wavy seas, the king performed sacred service so that the path of sacred ash, which is Saivism, might shine. He ruled for a very long time. Such Nindraseer Nedumaara Nayanar, by the grace of Lord Shiva, attained the great Shiva Loka, praised by all, and remained there in bliss, always devoted. The tenth and concluding Thiru Paadal: "Pon madhil suuzh pugalik kaavalar adikkeezh punitharaam thenmadurai maaranar sengamalak kazhal vaNangi panmaNigaL thirai odham parappu nedungadal padappai thol mayilai vaayilaar thiruththoNdin nilai thozhuvaam." The divine Sekkizhar Peruman, having completed the sacred Puranam of Nindraseer Nedumaara Nayanar, states, "Worshipping the lotus-like feet of Nedumaara Nayanar, the pure king of Southern Madurai, who was purified by the feet of the Lord of Pugali (Sirkazhi), we shall now praise and narrate the nature of the sacred service of Vaayilar Nayanar, who lived in the ancient Mylai (Thirumylai), a vast coastal region where waves spread many gems." With this, he begins to sing the Puranam of Vaayilar Nayanar. We too, bowing at the feet of Nindraseer Nedumaara Nayanar, shall continue to contemplate the sacred Puranam of Vaayilar Nayanar. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of our Lord Nindraseer Nedumaara Nayanar! Praise, praise to the sacred feet of our Lord Sundaramoorthy Swamigal! Praise, praise to the sacred feet of Nambiandar Nambigal Peruman! Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the divine Sekkizhar Peruman! Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the commentator Ayya!

Thiruchitrambalam.
