அவர்களே பத்தராய் பணிவார்கள். அவர்களது பண்பு இவ்வாறு தெய்வ சேக்கிழார் பெருமானால் விளக்கி காட்டப்படுகின்றது. அவர்கள் அடியவர்களைக் கண்டால் பசுவின் பின் கன்று போல சேருவார்கள். சிவபூஜை செய்பவர்களைக் கண்டால் உவந்து பயன் பெறுவார்கள். அடியவர்களையும் சிவபெருமானையும் வழிபடுபவர்கள் தாம் செய்யும் எத்தொழிலும் சிவனுக்கே ஆவன என்றே செய்வார்கள். சிவன் அடியார் சரித்திரங்களை விரும்பி கேட்பார்கள். எப்பொழுதும் சிவபெருமானை மறக்காத இருப்பார்கள். உலகு விளங்க தவம் காட்டி பயன் பெறுவார்கள். பத்தராய் பணியும் படியினை உலகில் விளக்கம் செய்வார்கள். அவரே சித்தம் பொங்கிய எழுச்சியுடன் பத்தராய் பணிவார்கள் ஆவார்கள் என்று போற்றி போற்றி. திருச்சிற்றம்பலம்.

Chapter 01 — Bakthraaip Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. The Periya Puranam authored by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, Part 993. The chapter of Pattharai Paniwaar, the Puranam of Pattharai Paniwaar. The 11th chapter in the Periya Puranam authored by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman is titled "Pattharai Paniwaar Sarukkam". The Thiruththondaththogai, composed by our Lord Sundaramurthy Swamigal, praises the devotees of Lord Shiva and the collective devotees. Individual devotees are called Nayanmars. The Puranams of 63 individual Nayanmars are found in the Periya Puranam. Similarly, nine collective devotees are praised in the Thiruththondaththogai. Collective devotees are those who are praised as a group. Sundaramurthy Swamigal has praised these nine groups of devotees in the Thiruththondaththogai Thiruppathigam. Among them, the Puranam of the collective devotees known as Thillai Vazh Andhanargal was the first Puranam. Similarly, we have already reflected on the Puranam of the collective devotees known as Poyyadimai Illadha Pulavargal, titled "Poyyadimai Illadha Pulavar Puranam". Thus, out of the nine collective devotees, the Puranams of two collective devotees have already been reflected upon. This 11th chapter is the Pattharai Paniwaar Sarukkam. This chapter corresponds to the 10th Thiruppadal of the Thiruththondaththogai. In the 10th Thiruppadal of the Thiruththondaththogai, our Lord Sundaramurthy Swamigal praises the remaining seven collective devotees and sings "Adiyen" (I am a servant) to all of them. The 10th Thiruppadal of that Thiruththondaththogai Thiruppathigam is as follows: Pattharai paniwargal ellaarkkum adiyen Paramanaiye paaduvaar adiyaarkkum adiyen Siththatthai Sivan paale vaiththaarkkum adiyen Thiruvaroor pirandhaargal ellaarkkum adiyen Muppodhum thirumeni theenduvaarkku adiyen Muzhuneeru poosiya munivarkkum adiyen Appaalum adichchaarndha adiyaarkkum adiyen Arooran Arooril Ammanukku aale. This is the Thiruppathigam. As the 10th Thiruppadal is thus, the 11th chapter corresponding to the 10th Thiruppadal is titled "Pattharai Paniwaar Sarukkam". As we all know, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman has named the chapter after the first part of that Thiruththondaththogai Thiruppadal. In that manner, since this Thiruppadal begins with "Pattharai paniwargal ellaarkkum adiyen", the divine Sekkizhar Peruman has bestowed the name "Pattharai Paniwaar Sarukkam" upon the chapter that narrates all the Puranams of the collective devotees praised in this Thiruppadal. The collective devotees praised in this Thiruppadal are: Pattharai paniwargal, Paramanaiye paaduvaargal, Siththatthai Sivan paale vaiththaargal, Thiruvaroor pirandhaargal, Muppodhum thirumeni theenduvaargal, Muzhuneeru poosiya munivargal, and Appaalum adichchaarndha adiyaargal – thus, seven collective devotees are praised. Among them, the first part is "Pattharai paniwargal ellaarkkum adiyen". Accordingly, the first Puranam in this chapter is the Pattharai Paniwaar Puranam. First, in the collection of works, Sundaramurthy Swamigal praises "Pattharai paniwargal ellaarkkum adiyen". Its meaning is "I am a servant to all those who are devotees of Lord Shiva and serve Lord Shiva and His devotees." "Ellaarkkum" (to all) means "I will be a servant to all Shiva devotees who exist at any time and in any place." Now, the descriptive work is Thiruththondar Thiruvandhadhi. In it, Nambiandar Nambigal Peruman has praised the collective devotees of Pattharai Paniwaar in the 70th Thiruppadal. That Thiruppadal is as follows: Arasinai Aroor Amarar Piranai adipanindhittu urai seytha vaai thadumaari uroma pulagam vandhu kara saranaadhi avayavam kambiththu kann aruvi soridharum angaththinar Patthar endru thoguththavare. This is the Thiruppadal. Those who worship the feet of Thiyagarasa Peruman, the Lord of the Devas residing in Thiruvaroor. Those who worship and praise the feet of Thiyagarasa Peruman, whose speech falters, whose hair stands on end, whose limbs like hands and feet tremble, and whose eyes shed tears like a waterfall – such individuals with these characteristics are categorized as Pattharai Paniwaar. This is its meaning. Thus, the descriptive work Thiruththondar Thiruvandhadhi compiles and describes the characteristics of the collective devotees called Pattharai Paniwaar. This Puranam is structured as a Puranam that states the definition of a Shiva devotee. Due to the great fortune of past good deeds, a soul, through the vision of true knowledge, realizes that it is not the primal being forever, and that there is a primal being who guides it in its inner and outer life. It realizes that this primal being, being the life within its own life, makes it aware of things, and thus it also comes to know. It also understands that this soul cannot do anything without that supreme being, Lord Shiva. Such a person, having attained this clarity, sees the supremely exalted greatness of Lord Shiva, the primal being, and in contrast, their own extreme lowliness and insignificance, and thus becomes humble. Due to the deep sense of gratitude that makes them yearn, thinking that there is nothing they can do in return for the immense, causeless grace that Lord Shiva has showered upon them throughout countless births, Shiva devotees are those whose hearts melt and overflow with love, constantly meditating on Lord Shiva. In return for that melting love, Lord Shiva bestows incomparable bliss. That is the bliss described as Shivanandam and Shivabhogam in Agamas, and Brahmanandam in Upanishads. Having tasted that bliss and been captivated by it, Shiva devotees are true lovers who constantly serve Lord Shiva as their duty, solely for the sweet love that Lord Shiva bestows. They are the Pattharai Paniwaar. Their thoughts, speech, and words are always filled with devotion, and all their actions are always dedicated to serving Lord Shiva. Therefore, they are praised as collective devotees called Pattharai Paniwaar. The special qualities of these devotees, which are beyond the ordinary nature of common people, are capable of inspiring devotion and joy in those who witness or hear about them. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman has presented such special qualities of Pattharai Paniwaar in the extensive Periya Puranam, in the section titled "Pattharai Paniwaar Puranam". In the Periya Puranam authored by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, the "Pattharai Paniwaar Puranam" is the 58th Puranam. It is the first Puranam in the "Pattharai Paniwaar Sarukkam". In eight Thiruppadals, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman has explained the characteristics and nature of the collective devotees called Pattharai Paniwaar. These are not individual Nayanmars. They are a group of collective devotees. Therefore, it is not appropriate to speak about their history here. If they were individual Nayanmars, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman would have sung about their sacred birth, their sacred country, city, wealth, and the services they rendered. Here, those mentioned are collective devotees. Therefore, in this Puranam, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman has presented their nature and characteristics in eight Thiruppadals for our benefit. The first Thiruppadal is: Isarukke anbanar yaavaraiyum thaam kandaal koosi miga kuthukuthuththu kondaadi manam magizhvuRRu aasaiyinaal aavin pin kandru anaindhaal pol anaindhu pesuvana panindha mozhi iniyanaave pesuvaar. This is the first Thiruppadal. Those who are Shiva devotees, who are devoted only to Lord Shiva, if they see anyone, they will be shy, extremely delighted, and their hearts will be filled with joy. With desire, like a calf approaching its mother cow, they will approach and speak only sweet and humble words to the devotees of Shiva. They will be shy when they see Shiva devotees. They will be afraid, observing the immeasurable greatness of true devotees of Shiva and their own insignificance. As Thiruvachagam says, "Vinnorgal ethutharkkum koosum malarpaadham" (the lotus feet that even celestials hesitate to praise), they will be shy in that manner. Then they will be delighted, due to great love and great desire, in that exalted state of love, they will go and join the devotees like a calf following a cow. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman intends to state that Pattharai Paniwaar are the definition and nature of Shiva devotees. To convey that these Pattharai Paniwaar are deeply involved in devotion to Shiva devotees even more than devotion to Shiva, this first Thiruppadal, encompassing devotion to devotees, thus explains and shows us their nature. Let us continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 02 — Bakthraaip Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. The Periya Puranam authored by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, Part 994. The chapter of Pattharai Paniwaar. The Puranam of Pattharai Paniwaar. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman sings about the special characteristics of the collective devotees known as Pattharai Paniwaar. The second and third Thiruppadals are: Thaavariya anpinaal Sambuvinai evvidaththum yaavargalum archikkumpadi kandaal inidhu uvandhu paavanaiyaal nokkinaal palarkana payanperuvaar mevariya anpinaan melavarkku melaanaar. Angananai adiyaarai aaraadha kaadhallinaal pongivarum uvakaiyudan thaam virumbi poosippaar pangaya maamalar melaan paampanaiyaan endru ivargal thangalukkum saarvariya saran saarum thavam udaiyaar. With unfailing love, if they see Sambhu (Lord Shiva) being worshipped anywhere by anyone, they will be sweetly pleased and delighted. By their pure contemplation and gracious gaze, many will benefit. They are superior to all superiors due to their rare love. With unceasing love, they will eagerly worship the Lord and His devotees with overflowing joy. Even Brahma, who resides on the great lotus flower, and Vishnu, who reclines on the serpent bed, find it difficult to approach the Lord's feet. These collective devotees, Pattharai Paniwaar, possess the penance to attain those feet. Therefore, it is understood that only those who have performed prior penance become devotees. Thus, devotees will show equal devotion to Lord Shiva, the God, and to the devotees of Lord Shiva. The fourth Thiruppadal is: Yaadhaanum ivvudampaal seivinagal eru uyarththaar paadhaara vindhaththin paalaaga enum parivaal kaadhaar ven kuzhai avarkkaam pani seivaar karukkuzhiyil podhaargal avar pugazhkku puvanam ellaam podhaavaal. Whatever actions these collective devotees, Pattharai Paniwaar, perform with this body, they do so with the love that these actions may be worthy of reaching the lotus feet of the Lord who bears the bull emblem. They will perform service with the love that all their actions are acts of service to Lord Shiva. For those who do so, there will be no more births. They will no longer enter the womb. Whatever services they perform with love, these are actions acceptable to Lord Shiva. These also serve as means to attain liberation. The fifth and sixth Thiruppadals are: Sangaranai saarndha kadhaithaan ketkum thanmaiyaraai Angananai miga virumbi ayal ariyaa anpinaal Gangai nadhi madhi idhazhi kaadhalikkum thirumudiyaar Sengamala malarppaadham servadhanukku uriyaargal. Isanaiye panindhu urugi inbam miga kalippu eydhi pesina vaai thazhathazhuppa kannirin perundhaarai maasilaa neer aliththu angu karuvi thara mayir silirppa koosiyee udal kambiththiduvaar mey gunamikkaar. They are of the nature to listen only to stories related to Shankara (Lord Shiva). They love the Lord very much with a love that others cannot comprehend. They are worthy of reaching the lotus-like feet of the Lord whose sacred crown lovingly bears the Ganges river, the crescent moon, and the Konrai flower. Worshipping the Lord, their hearts melt, they attain great joy, their speech falters, a great stream of tears flows from their eyes, washing away the sacred ash on their chest, their hair stands on end, and their bodies tremble with shyness. These devotees are full of true qualities. Shankara is a sacred name for Lord Shiva. Shankara means "one who does only good." These collective devotees, Pattharai Paniwaar, are of the nature to listen only to stories related to Shankara, Lord Shiva. These collective devotees desire to hear the histories of devotees who loved and were devoted to the Lord. Their love is a love that others cannot comprehend. Their love is not performed for others to see and praise. Their skill lies in loving the Lord very much in a way that others do not know. With that skill, they are worthy of reaching the lotus-like feet of the Lord whose sacred crown lovingly bears the Ganges river, the crescent moon, and the Konrai flower.

Their love is a love that others cannot comprehend, because the love they have for the Lord is like the love a heroine has for her beloved hero, and therefore it cannot be understood by others. Furthermore, the people of the world cannot in any way comprehend the depth of their love. Regardless of how the people of the world think or perceive, they disregard all of it and love in such a way that only Lord Shiva knows the truth. They love for the sake of love. Didn't we reflect in the Puranam of Karaikkal Ammaiyar: "Andar Nayaganar ennai arivarel ariyaa vaaymai en dhishai maakkalukku yaan evvuruvayan endru" (If the Lord of the celestials knows me, then what does it matter what form I appear to the ignorant masses of the eight directions who do not know the truth)? When Ammaiyar, having desired and received the incomparable form of a ghoul, saw everyone astonished and terrified, fleeing from her, her sacred words were: "Andar Nayaganar ennai arivarel ariyaa vaaymai en dhishai maakkalukku yaan evvuruvayan en enbaar." Therefore, Ammaiyar says, "Lord Shiva, the Lord of all celestials, knows me. So, if I am seen in any form by the ignorant masses who are in a state of doubt and do not know the truth, it does not matter to me." Therefore, these collective devotees, Pattharai Paniwaar, have immense love for Lord Shiva, and whatever state they are in, they disregard what the people of the world say about that state and love in such a way that only Lord Shiva knows. They possess that quality. These devotees, these Shiva devotees, worship the Lord, their hearts melt, and as the inner joy overflows, they become delighted, their speech falters, and a great stream of tears flows from their eyes, washing away the sacred ash on their chest like a waterfall, their hair stands on end, and their bodies tremble with shyness. This is their excellent quality. Joy is an emotional feeling that occurs within the mind and intellect. As it intensifies, their speech falters and trembles. Tears flow profusely from their eyes, reaching their chest and dissolving the sacred ash applied there. We have seen the sacred form of Thirunavukkarasar Peruman, described as "meni kanneer venneetru vandalaada" (the body bathed in tears and sacred ash) and "maarbaara pozhi kanneer mazhai vaarum thiruvuruvum" (the sacred form with tears flowing profusely down the chest like rain). That image now appears before our eyes. The seventh Thiruppadal is: Nindralum irundhalum kidandhalum nadandhalum mendralum thuyindralum vizhiththalum imaiththalum mandraadum malarpaadham orukaalum maravaamai kundraadha unarvudaiyaar thondaraam gunamikkaar. Whether standing or sitting, lying or walking, eating or sleeping, waking or blinking, they never forget the lotus-like feet that dance in the sacred hall. These Shiva devotees are endowed with unwavering consciousness and are called Thiruththondargal (sacred devotees) because of their excellent qualities. Standing, walking, lying, sitting, eating, waking, and sleeping – these are all states of existence in the worldly life, the various conditions that humans experience. These are the various states of existence for souls in the sagala state, arising from the activation, reduction, combination, and separation of the 96 tattvas. However, in whatever state they are, wherever they are, devotees keep their intellect focused on Shiva. They keep it only on Shiva. They are capable of keeping their intellect and love focused on Shiva and never forgetting the feet of Lord Shiva. They are the ones who possess the quality of being called Pattharai Paniwaar. In these seven Thiruppadals, the eight essential qualities of Shiva devotees and servants are described. Srimath Umapathi Sivachariyar Swamigal has compiled these in the Thiruththondar Purana Saram. The Thiruppadal states: "Thondar adi thozhal poosai thozhil magizhdhal azhagaara thulangiya archchanai puridhal thoguthi niyamangal konda pani thiruvadikkE koduththal Isan gunam aruvum arungadhaiyai kulavi kettum mandi vizhi thozhumba mayir silumbaal unnal maruvu thiruppani kaatti varubava vaangi undi kolaadhu ozhidhal ena ivai or ettum udaiyar avar patthar ena uraitthullaare." Now, the eighth and concluding Thiruppadal is: Sangaranukku aalaana thavam kaatti thaam adhanaal pangamara payindru uyppaar padi vilakkum perumaiyinaar Angananai Thiruvaroor aazhvaanai adi vanangi pongiyezhum siththamudan pattharaai potruvaar. They are those who, by demonstrating the penance of being subservient to Shankara (Lord Shiva), flawlessly practice it and make it known to the world, thereby attaining great renown. They are those who worship the feet of the Lord who rules Thiruvaroor, the gracious one, with an overflowing mind, and praise Him as devotees. They are those who undertake the penance of being a servant to Shankara, Lord Shiva, and explain it to the world, thereby flawlessly benefiting from it. Penance means the sacred attire of Shiva and Shiva worship. They possess these, and therefore they make known the love that occurs within. This penance not only yields benefits by making it known to the people of the world, but they themselves also experience the benefits of that penance. Therefore, it is understood from this that those who merely assume the outward appearance, for various reasons such as wanting the people of the world to be amazed by them, but do not have love in their hearts, do not belong to this category. In this very Puranam, we see Muththanathan and Adhisuran. They too wore this sacred attire. But they did not have love for Lord Shiva and His devotees in their hearts. Therefore, they are excluded here. The true nature of genuine devotees is clearly shown here. These collective devotees are those who worship the feet of Lord Shiva, the gracious one, the Lord who rules Thiruvaroor, with an overflowing mind, and praise Him as devotees. They are the Pattharai Paniwaar. Their qualities are thus explained by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. When they see devotees, they approach them like a calf approaches its mother cow. When they see those who perform Shiva worship, they are delighted and benefit. Those who worship devotees and Lord Shiva perform all their actions as if they are for Shiva alone. They eagerly listen to the histories of Shiva devotees. They never forget Lord Shiva. They perform penance for the welfare of the world and benefit from it. They explain to the world the way of serving as devotees. They are the Pattharai Paniwaar, with an overflowing mind. Thus concludes the Pattharai Paniwaar Puranam. Now, let us continue to reflect on the next collective devotee, the Puranam of Paramanaiye Paaduvaar. Praise, praise to the feet of all Pattharai Paniwaar. Praise, praise to the feet of our Lord Sundaramurthy Swamigal. Praise, praise to the feet of Nambiandar Nambigal Peruman. Praise, praise to the feet of the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Praise, praise to the feet of the commentator Sivakavimani Ayya. Thiruchitrambalam.
