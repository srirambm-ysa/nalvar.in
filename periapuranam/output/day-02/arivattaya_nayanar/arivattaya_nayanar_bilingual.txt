பிடிக்கப்பட்டதும் அதனால் தமது கழுத்து அரியும் செயல் தடுக்கப்பட்டதும் உணர்ந்ததால் உண்டான அந்த அற்புதம் உணர்ச்சியின் மெய்ப்பாடாக நாயனார் தன்னுடைய இரண்டு கரங்களையும் குவித்து இறைவனைத் துதிக்கின்றார். நாயனார் என்று நாம் கூறுகின்றோம். இது தூய நாமம் அறிவாட்டாயர் ஆயினார் என்று சேக்கிழார் பெருமான் பாடுகின்றார். தூய்மை தரத்தக்க நாமமாக அறிவாட்டாயர் எனும் நாமம் திகழ்கின்றது. எனவேதான் திருத்தொண்டர் தொகையில் சுந்தரமூர்த்தி சுவாமிகளும் எஞ்சாத வாட்டாயன் அடியார்க்கும் அடியேன் என்று துதித்து நிற்கின்றார். இந்த நாமம் தூய்மையான நாமம். அடியார்களின் திருநாமங்கள் தூய்மை செய்பவன. அந்த நாமங்கள் தூய்மையாக # English

Chapter 01 — Arivattaya Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 170, Ilai Malinda Sarukkam, Arivattaya Nayanar Puranam. Having completed the Puranam of Manakkanjara Nayanar, we now step into the Puranam of Arivattaya Nayanar. In the Ilai Malinda Sarukkam, in sequential order, after the Puranams of Eripattar Nayanar, Enathinatha Nayanar, Kannappa Nayanar, Kungiliya Kalaya Nayanar, and Manakkanjara Nayanar, we are now going to look at the Puranam of Arivattaya Nayanar, who is revered as Arivattaya Nayanar. First, let us look at a summary of the Nayanar's story. In the Chola country, there is a town called Kanamangalam. It is renowned for its abundance of water, land, and crops. In that town, a Nayanar named Tayanar was born into a wealthy Vellalar family. He lived a life engaged in household duties, constantly clinging to the sacred feet of Lord Shiva. Tayanar Nayanar would daily bring the appropriate red rice (chennel arisi), red greens (chenkeerai), and unripe mangoes (ma vadu) for Lord Shiva's sacred food (Thiru Amudu) and offer them at the temple. To show the world that he would continue this sacred service even in times of poverty, and to uplift the world, Lord Shiva concealed his wealth. He brought poverty upon him. Even when his wealth diminished and poverty struck, Tayanar continued his sacred service to Lord Shiva. When all his own lands were gone, he went as a wage laborer to others' fields, harvesting red rice from the red rice fields. The red rice he received as wages from this harvest, he offered for the Lord's sacred food. The black rice (kaar nel arisi) he harvested and received as wages, he used for his own food. This was because red rice was considered suitable for the Lord's sacred food. Thus, he offered the red rice income from the harvest work to the Lord and used the black rice from the black rice harvest for his own sustenance, continuing this sacred service to the Lord. Lord Shiva performed another divine play. He made only red rice grow throughout that town. Therefore, the Nayanar had only the opportunity to harvest red rice. With no black rice available, the Nayanar found himself in a situation where he could not harvest black rice and use the black rice he received as wages to cook and eat for himself. Nevertheless, he joyfully continued his sacred service, thinking, "We are getting red rice entirely, aren't we? We can offer all of this to the Lord." He did not get rice for himself. Black rice was not available as wages. So, Tayanar Nayanar's wife used the greens from their backyard to feed the Nayanar. As days passed, the greens in their backyard also ran out. Now, neither the Nayanar nor his wife had rice. Black rice was not available, and the greens in their backyard were also exhausted. So, they survived by drinking only water. Despite this, they continued to bring red rice, red greens, and unripe mangoes daily to the temple for the Lord's sacred food, as usual, having received red rice as wages. One day, as Tayanar was carrying the pure red rice, unripe mangoes, and tender greens in a basket to offer as sacred food to the Lord, his wife followed him, carrying a pot of Panchagavya for the Lord. The Nayanar had not eaten for many days. He had been living on water alone. Therefore, as he walked to the temple, his body was emaciated. His legs faltered. As a result, he stumbled and fell. At that moment, his wife, holding the pot with one hand, tried to support the Nayanar with the other. However, the red rice, greens, and unripe mangoes that the Nayanar was carrying in the basket for the Lord's sacred food spilled onto the fields. It was the season when the harvest was over, and the fields were cracked. Thus, all the items intended for the Lord's sacred food spilled onto the cracked earth. Seeing this, Tayanar Nayanar lamented, "How can I go to the temple now? The Lord must partake of these spilled offerings right here. Otherwise, I will not live. I will cut off my head," he prayed to the Lord. The Lord then delayed partaking of the sacred food. Seeing this, Tayanar Nayanar thought, "I have committed a great sin. I am not blessed to have the Lord partake of the food." As he had vowed earlier, he began to cut his neck with the Arival (sickle) he held in his hand, which was used for harvesting rice. At that very moment, from the cracks in the field, the Lord's divine hand emerged. The sound of the Lord biting the unripe mango, "Videl Videl," also arose there. As the Lord's divine hand caught the Nayanar's hand that was cutting his neck, the Nayanar's wound disappeared. Tayanar Nayanar then stood with folded hands and praised the Lord, saying, "O Lord Shiva, who, seeing my foolishness and desiring my servitude, has come from the cracks in the earth to partake of the food here, praise be to You!" The Lord appeared on His bull mount (Rishabaroodar) and said, "You have performed a good sacred service. You and your wife shall live forever in Our world." Thus, He blessed them. Because the Nayanar cut his neck with the Arival (sickle) when he thought he was not blessed to have the Lord partake of the food, he received the pure name Arival Tayanar Nayanar. Tayanar is the Nayanar's given name. We call him Arivattaya Nayanar by adding Arival Tayanar. This is a brief summary of the Nayanar's life. Now, let us look at the Nayanar's Puranam in detail. The town where the Nayanar was born and lived is called Kanamangalam. This is in the Chola country, north of the town of Thiruturaipoondi, on the way to Thiruvarur, near the Thevaram-sung shrine of Thiruthandalai Neelneri, located on the south bank of the Kaveri. This town is not found today. The place where the town once stood is now known as Kanamangalam Thidal (Kanamangalam mound). No other evidence for this town has been found. Tayanar Nayanar used to daily bring red rice, fresh greens, and unripe mangoes for the Lord's sacred food to the temple of Thiruthandalai Neelneri, a Thevaram-sung shrine that was near this town. Sekkizhar has compiled and sung the Nayanar's Puranam under the title "Arivattaya Nayanar Puranam" in the Periya Puranam. This Puranam describes the life history and character of the Nayanar named Arival Tayar. In sequential order, Arivattaya Nayanar Puranam is the sixth in the Ilai Malinda Sarukkam. First, in the Thiruthondathogai, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal sang about the Nayanar in the line: "Malai Malinda Thol Vallal Manakkanjaran Enjatha Vattayan Adiyarkkum Adiyen." In this line, he sings "Enjatha Vattayan Adiyarkkum Adiyen," which is the line Sundaramoorthy Swamigal sang about Arivattaya Nayanar. In the second verse of the Thiruthondathogai, in sequential order, he sings: "Ilai Malinda Vel Nambi Eripattarkku Adiyen, Enathinathan Than Adiyarkkum Adiyen, Kalai Malinda Seer Nambi Kannapparkku Adiyen, Kadavooril Kalayan Than Adiyarkkum Adiyen, Malai Malinda Thol Vallal Manakkanjaran Enjatha Vattayan Adiyarkkum Adiyen." The meaning of the line "Enjatha Vattayan Adiyarkkum Adiyen" is: when the divine offerings for the Lord's sacred food spilled onto the ground, he resolved to end his life, so that none of his attachments in this world, such as "I" and "mine," would remain. He cut his neck with the Arival (sickle) that prevents any decline in Shiva's service. The Arival that does not leave anything behind, the Arival that cuts rice – the servitude of Tayanar Nayanar who possessed that Arival, the devotees of Tayanar Nayanar, to them I am a servant. This is the meaning of this line. Now, in the Thirutthondar Thiruvandhadhi, a genre of literature, Nambiandar Nambi has composed the following verse: "Vallal pirarkku amudhu endhi varuvon ugallum inge vellach sadaiya amudhu seyyavidil en thalaiyai thallath thagum endru vaal poottiya thadangaiyinan kaan allar pazhanak kanamangalathu arivattayane." This is the verse about the Nayanar in the Thirutthondar Thiruvandhadhi. Now, let us look at the Arivattaya Nayanar Puranam in the extensive work, Periya Puranam. Sekkizhar Swamigal has sung the Arivattaya Nayanar Puranam in 23 verses. Following the tradition of Periya Puranam, Sekkizhar begins by describing the prosperity of the Nayanar's town and country. In the fertile Chola country, watered by the Kaveri, there is a town called Kanamangalam. Here, sugarcane fields are abundantly cultivated, with bees and wasps singing. Throughout the town, there are fields full of red rice crops. In those fields, lotus flowers have grown as weeds. In those lotus flowers, there are conches. From those conches, pearls have emerged. As the farmers weed out these lotus flowers, which have grown as weeds in the red rice fields, they hold lotus flowers and the conches within them in their hands. Sekkizhar Peruman describes and sings that in this scene, the farmers of that town resemble Kuberas, who hold the two treasures of Shankhanidhi and Padmanidhi. Then, Sekkizhar Swamigal also describes and sings about the women of the farming community who work in the fields there. In such a prosperous town of Kanamangalam, a Nayanar named Tayanar was born into a Vellalar family. The name Tayanar means one who showed motherly love to all living beings. If we separate it as "Thai Anar," it means one who loved like a mother. The Nayanar would always contemplate the sacred feet of Lord Shiva. Every day, as a rule, he would bring red rice, red greens, and fresh bunches of unripe mangoes for Lord Shiva and offer them to the Lord for His sacred food. Red rice is suitable for the Lord's sacred food. Black rice is not suitable for the Lord's sacred food. Red rice, as its name suggests, is reddish and whitish. Black rice is black. Red rice is superior in quality and suitable for the Lord's food because it grows during the period of intense sunlight and heat. However, black rice (kaar nel) is, on the contrary, a type of rice that grows during the season when sunlight is dim. It is not suitable for the food of the exalted or for the Lord's sacred food. Therefore, Tayanar Nayanar offered only red rice for the Lord's sacred food. Similarly, along with red rice, he offered greens and unripe mangoes for the Lord's sacred food. Red greens (chenkeerai) are a type of curry that provides similar taste and nutritional benefits as red rice. Similarly, he offered unripe mangoes that remained fresh and tender for the Lord's sacred food. Here, Sekkizhar Swamigal refers to them as "Mannu Painthunar Ma Vadu." "Mannu" implies that they were preserved after being soaked for many days, indicating that they were processed unripe mangoes. "Painthunar Ma Vadu" indicates that they were very fresh and tender unripe mangoes. The elders believe that unripe mangoes aid digestion. Very tender unripe mangoes, in bunches, should be used in this way. Very tender, slightly sour unripe mangoes are of very good quality. Such red rice, red greens, and unripe mangoes were offered daily as a rule for the Lord's sacred food. Here, Sekkizhar Swamigal says, "Amudhu Seivippar" (he would cause the food to be prepared). Therefore, we get the indication here that he would give all these items, the necessary ingredients for the sacred food, to the Shiva priests, and have the Shiva priests offer the food to the Lord. Let us continue to contemplate the Arivattaya Nayanar Puranam. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 02 — Arivattaya Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 171, Ilai Malinda Sarukkam, Arivattaya Nayanar Puranam. Lord Shiva willed to show the world that Arivattaya Nayanar would continue his sacred service of offering red rice, fresh greens, and unripe mangoes for the Lord's sacred food, even when he faced poverty and suffering. Although Arivattaya Nayanar was a true devotee of Shiva, and thus there was no possibility of him facing hardship, even if he were to face suffering for other reasons, he would not forget his love for Lord Shiva and would continue his sacred service, keeping Lord Shiva always in his thoughts. This is what the Lord intended to demonstrate. The sacred words state that Shiva's devotees always experience joy and never sorrow, and they lack nothing. This signifies that devotees never experience sorrow. "Idarinum thalarinum ena thurnoi thodarinum una kazhal thozhuthu ezhuven" (Even in distress, even in weakness, even if a terrible disease afflicts me, I will worship your feet and rise) is from the Thiruvavaduthurai Pathigam by Aludaiya Pillaiyar. Therefore, to make the world understand that our Nayanar, even when faced with hardship, would not dwell on it but would act with Lord Shiva always in his thoughts, the Lord changed Arivattaya Nayanar's wealth. He made his wealth disappear in such a way that no one could know how it was lost. The wealth that Arivattaya Nayanar possessed vanished like a vilanga fruit eaten by an elephant. "Kaliru un vilangani" refers to a vilanga fruit afflicted by the "elephant disease." When an elephant eats a vilanga fruit, the fruit, though appearing whole from the outside, is empty inside, having nothing. Over time, such a vilanga fruit perishes. In this manner, his wealth disappeared like a vilanga fruit eaten by an elephant. Even in such poverty, Arivattaya Nayanar's intensity did not diminish. This is because the sacred service performed by Arivattaya Nayanar was a service performed according to rules, without any fault in material, action, or mind. His very name was Tayanar, meaning one who loves like a mother. He fed the Lord, who has no mother, like a mother, and he did so daily without fail. Even when all his wealth disappeared, the Nayanar went to harvest rice as a wage laborer. He received rice as wages for his labor. Because there was abundant love in the Nayanar's heart, as a result of that enduring love, the Nayanar engaged in physical effort and offered the red rice obtained through his strenuous labor as sacred food to the Lord. Generally, it is said that when a person experiences poverty, all their good qualities such as truthfulness, charity, and penance leave them due to that poverty. However, even when Tayanar faced poverty, his good qualities and actions did not abandon him. He toiled in others' fields as a wage laborer, enduring physical hardship, received red rice as wages, and offered it as sacred food to the Lord. The saying from Thirukkural, "Deivaththaan aagath eninum muyarchi than mei varuttha kooli tharum" (Even if by divine will it does not happen, effort will yield its reward for the body's toil), applies here perfectly. Arivattaya Nayanar offered the red rice he received as wages for the Lord's sacred food and lived by consuming the black rice he received for himself. However, the Lord willed to change even that. That is, the inner meaning is that Lord Shiva, through His Tirodhana Shakti (power of concealment), had so far concealed the soul's Chith Shakti (power of consciousness), caused karmas to be experienced and exhausted, ripened the soul, brought about the balance of good and bad deeds (iruvinai oppu) and the ripening of impurities (mala paripagam), and then willed to change that act of concealment (Tirodhana Shakti) and then manifest His grace. The Lord now caused only red rice to grow in all the fields. Therefore, only red rice was given to the Nayanar as wages. Thinking that this red rice belonged only to the Lord, the Nayanar daily offered it for the Lord's sacred food. He did not receive black rice as wages. Therefore, he lived without eating any food. Arivattaya Nayanar did this with great joy. Thinking, "This is the sacred merit I have earned for Shiva," he offered all the red rice he received entirely to the Lord. The opportunity to perform Shiva's service is obtained only through great merit accumulated in previous births. Therefore, the Nayanar rejoiced, thinking that the blessing of having all his wages used for Shiva's sacred food was due to the merit he had accumulated. "Enna punniyam seidhanai nenjame irungadal vaiyathu munnai nee puri nalvinai payanadai" (What merit have you done, O heart, in this vast ocean-girt world, that you enjoy the fruit of good deeds done before?) is from the Thiruvizhanchuzhi Thevaram by Aludaiya Pillaiyar. Arivattaya Nayanar, without the slightest thought of not having rice for his own food, rejoiced that all the fields yielded red rice for the Lord's sacred food, didn't he? This is the state where the jiva-bodham (individual consciousness) as a pasu (bound soul) is lost and Shiva-bodham (divine consciousness) is attained. The Nayanar not only offered red rice as sacred food but also sacrificed his own self-consciousness (tatpodham). The inner meaning is that he sacrificed his jiva-bodham. Now, the Lord joyfully partook of the food as the Nayanar desired. Therefore, the Lord accepting the Nayanar's red rice means that the Lord accepted the Nayanar's jiva-bodham, which was his tatpodham, and bestowed His grace. The Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam, in the praise of Siddhi Vinayagar, also states that the Lord consumes our jiva-bodham and bestows His grace, which is Shiva-bodham. "Ullam enum koodaththil ookkam enum thari niruvi urudhiyaaga thallariya anbu ennum thodar pootti idaippaduththi tharugat paasak kallavinai pasupodhak kavalalida kaliththundu karunai ennum vella madham pozhi siddhi vezhaththai ninainthu varuvinaigal theerppam" (In the hall of the heart, establishing the pillar of zeal, firmly binding it with the unbreakable chain of love, and offering the deceitful karmas of the bound soul as a mouthful to the joyous Siddhi elephant that pours forth the flood of grace, let us remember that elephant and remove all our karmas). This is the praise of Lord Siddhi Vinayagar in the Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam. The elephant, Lord Vinayagar, resides in the hall of our heart. If we establish the pillar of our mental resolve, firmly bind it with the chain of love, capture that elephant, Lord Vinayagar, and offer our pasu-bodham as a mouthful, then that elephant, Siddhi Vinayagar, will joyfully consume our pasu-bodham and pour forth the flood of grace, which is compassion. Remembering such an elephant, Lord Siddhi Vinayagar, we shall remove all our karmas. This is the meaning of this verse. Now, as black rice was not available for their food, the Nayanar's wife used the leafy greens from their backyard to prepare food and feed the Nayanar. The sacred food offered to the Lord, the red rice, becomes Shiva Nirmalyam (offerings that have been consecrated to Shiva). Therefore, according to the rule that it should not be consumed, they could not partake of that food and prasadam. Even during such times, the wife gathered greens and prepared food for the Nayanar. It is common in the world for love to diminish when poverty strikes. However, the Nayanar's wife remained undiminished in her love, even in poverty. Both the Nayanar and his wife, united in thought, supported each other in the sacred service of Lord Shiva. They engaged in this sacred service without the slightest worry about their condition. They rejoiced in the honor of feeding the Lord and did not even think about their own lack of food. The Nayanar's wife, who was like Arundhati in her chastity, thus fed her husband with greens for some time. As days passed, those leafy greens also ran out. Then, the Nayanar's wife poured water, and Tayanar Nayanar consumed it as sacred food. The couple not only considered water as something to quench thirst but also as a food item to satisfy hunger. "Thupparkku thuppaaya thuppaayathum mazhai" (Rain is food to those who eat, and itself is food) is a Thirukkural, isn't it? The meaning of this couplet is that rain not only causes food items to grow but also that rainwater itself serves as a food item for people. In accordance with this, the Nayanar and his wife lived by consuming only water. They offered the red rice, red greens, unripe mangoes, and Panchagavya (the five products of the cow), which they had earned through their hard labor, to the Lord. Sekkizhar Peruman, at this point, sings in his own voice, "I have been blessed to speak of the sacred service of those who received such great fortune." Remembering, speaking, and listening to the sacred histories of great Shiva devotees are acts of Shiva's merit. Furthermore, Sekkizhar reminds us through this that Lord Shiva's grace is about to manifest. It is the tradition of our teacher to thus highlight places where divine grace manifests, warning and guiding the listeners. As usual, the Nayanar and his wife, just as their love was pure, carried red rice, unripe mangoes, and tender red greens in a basket on his head to offer to the Lord. There is nothing in this world to compare with the rice and other offerings that Tayanar offered to the Lord with love. Therefore, his love is compared here to the red rice. Furthermore, the Nayanar daily brought the red rice, greens, and unripe mangoes needed for the Lord's sacred food to the temple and offered them there. It is prescribed in the Agamas that the items needed for the Lord's sacred food should be given to the sacred kitchen (Thirumadaippalli), and the sacred food prepared there should be offered to the Lord. Accordingly, the Nayanar also went to the temple and gave the necessary items for the preparation of sacred food. As the Nayanar carried the items needed for the Lord's sacred food on his head, his wife also accompanied him. They walked together, united in love. Tayanar walked ahead, and his wife followed him. His wife carried a pot of Panchagavya for the Lord. Rice and other items would be heavy. Therefore, Tayanar Nayanar himself carried them in the basket, while his wife carried the Panchagavya in an earthen pot. Here, we understand the rule that husband and wife should perform the Lord's puja together. It is seen in the Thevarams of Thirugnanasambandar Peruman that even animals followed this custom. Even today, in the sacred place of Thirukazhukundram, there is a tradition of two eagles coming to worship. Thus, the Nayanar and his wife walked as husband and wife. What the Nayanar's wife carried was Panchagavya, also known as Aanainthu (the five products of the cow), for the Lord. Thirunavukkarasu Peruman says, "Poovinnukku arungalam pongu thamara aavinukku arungalam aran anjaaduthal" (The lotus is the ornament of flowers, the five products of the cow are the ornament of the cow). Does not Thirunavukkarasar say that the benefit of the cow is to provide Panchagavya for the Lord? Accordingly, the Nayanar's wife carried Panchagavya. Panchagavya consists of milk, curd, ghee, cow urine, and cow dung obtained from a single cow. The rules for Panchagavya are described in the Shiva Agamas. In the word Panchagavya, "Pa" signifies the removal of sins, "Cha" signifies the purification of the soul, "Ka" signifies the removal of rebirth, and "Vyam" signifies liberation (moksha). Therefore, it is called Panchagavya because it removes impurities, purifies the soul, eradicates rebirth, and grants liberation. Without Panchagavya, the deities will not be present in the rituals performed. Panchagavya consists of milk, curd, ghee, cow urine (gojalam), and cow dung (gomayam), prepared according to rules. Lord Shiva commanded five cows – Sumana (red) near Rishabadeva in Shiva Loka, Surabhi (white), Nandha (brown), Susheela (smoke-colored), and Subhadra (black) – to be born on earth for the salvation of souls. They originated from the ocean of milk for the purpose of being used in Shiva puja and other rituals, and to remove the suffering of souls. From them came milk, curd, ghee, cow urine, and cow dung. This is stated in the Sivadharmohtthiram. The milk in Panchagavya is consecrated by establishing nine divisions, and in the central division, on a place formed by the Shiva Tattva, the milk in a vessel called Suprathishtai is worshipped once with the Ishana mantra and consecrated. The quality of grace, Ishana, will then reside in it. This will remove enemies. Its presiding deity is Shukra. Next, the curd in the eastern division, on a place formed by the Sadashiva Tattva, in a vessel called Sushantham, is worshipped twice with the Tatpurusha mantra and consecrated. This is for the quality of Tatpurusha to reside in it. This will grant progeny. Its presiding deity is Chandra. Thirdly, the ghee in the southern division, on a place formed by the Vidya Tattva, in a Tejo Vath vessel, is worshipped three times with the Aghora mantra and consecrated. The quality of Aghora will then reside in it. This grants liberation to all. Its presiding deity is Rudra. Fourthly, the cow urine (gojalam) in the northern direction, on a place formed by the Purusha Tattva, in a Ratnodhaka vessel, is worshipped four times with the Vamadeva mantra and consecrated. The quality of Vamadeva will then reside in it. This grants wealth. Its presiding deity is Ganga. Fifthly, the cow dung (gomayam) in the western direction, on a place formed by the Kala Tattva, in an Amirthathmaka vessel, is placed and worshipped five times with the Sadyojata mantra and consecrated. The quality of grace, Sadyojata, will then reside in it. This removes diseases. Its presiding deity is Surya. From cow dung, the Bilva tree, with its desire, knowledge, and action-shaped leaves, emerged. Goddess Mahalakshmi, the goddess of wealth, desired that Bilva and resides in it. Therefore, it received the name Sriphalam and gained the greatness of being always wearable for Lord Shiva and removing the sins of those who worship with it. Later, from cow dung, blue lotus tubers and lotus tubers emerged. If all the Panchagavya ingredients are not available, one can substitute any one of milk or curd, and any one of cow dung or cow urine, for the other. If any Panchagavya ingredient is missing, ghee can be used as a substitute. These rules for Panchagavya are extensively described in the Amsuman Agama. Such Panchagavya is called Aanainthu in Tamil. The Nayanar's wife carries such Aanainthu in an earthen pot and walks behind the Nayanar. Let us continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 03 — Arivattaya Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 172, Ilai Malinda Sarukkam, Arivattaya Nayanar Puranam. Arivattaya Nayanar and his wife, as they did daily, set out towards the Lord's temple, carrying red rice, red greens, unripe mangoes, and Panchagavya. Their condition was such that they had been living on water alone for many days. Therefore, the Nayanar's body was very emaciated. As he walked, his legs could not support his body. His legs faltered. His body became weak. As a result, he stumbled. The Nayanar was about to fall. At that moment, his wife, holding the earthen pot containing Panchagavya in one hand, tried to support the Nayanar with her other hand. The Nayanar's wife carried the Panchagavya in an earthen pot, holding it in her left hand, and covering the mouth of the pot tightly with her right hand. This was to prevent any impurity from mixing with the Panchagavya. Now, as the Nayanar was about to fall, she embraced her husband with her right hand, which had been covering the pot, to prevent him from falling. The wife's desire was to prevent her husband from falling, but even more so, if her husband fell, the offerings in the basket he carried for the Lord would spill onto the ground. Therefore, her desire to prevent those from falling was even stronger. Despite all this, even as she embraced him, the red rice, unripe mangoes, and greens on the Nayanar's head fell onto the ground. This was a time when the ground was cracked. That is, it was known to be the season when crops had been harvested from the fields, and the land was full of cracks. Arivattaya Nayanar attained liberation on the Thiruvathirai star day in the month of Thai. Therefore, it is known that the month of Thai is the period after the harvest. So, there were cracks in the ground then. All those items fell there. The Nayanar was carrying these offerings from his hometown of Kanamangalam to the nearby temple of Thiruthandalai Neelneri. Between these two places was a field. Even today, Sivakavimani writes that the same situation can be seen there. Good red greens, red rice, and fresh unripe mangoes spilled onto the ground. "Good red greens" means greens without wormholes, without wilting, without being overripe, and without any defects like yellowing. Such tender greens were carefully chosen and brought. Similarly, the unripe mangoes were also fresh, tender, and slightly sour. The rice was also red rice. All these spilled onto the ground. Therefore, the Nayanar was deeply saddened, thinking that he was not blessed to have Lord Shiva, who has the power to remove all the sufferings of living beings and take them under His grace, partake of these items. He lamented that he had become such a great sinner. Seeing the rice and other items spilled in the cracks of the earth, the Nayanar thought, "There is no point in going before the Lord now. There is no point in going to see the Lord without these items." So, the Nayanar then prayed to the Lord, "O Lord, You are all-pervading. This earth is also Your divine form. Therefore, You must partake of these items here. If You do not partake of them, I will cut off my head, which has failed in its duty. I will cut it off myself." Tayanar Nayanar, who made this prayer, did not see any sign that Lord Shiva had partaken of the food as he had requested. Even after the Nayanar prayed that the Lord should accept these spilled items as sacred food, and that if He did not, he would cut off his own neck and end his life, Lord Shiva delayed for a while. This delay caused the Nayanar to suffer even more, thinking that the Lord had not yet partaken of the food. Every day, the Nayanar would bring red rice, unripe mangoes, and greens to the temple. There, they would be offered as sacred food to the Lord. At that time, the Nayanar would daily hear the sound "Videl Videl" as the Lord bit the fresh unripe mangoes. But today, the rice, unripe mangoes, and greens had spilled. So, he prayed, "You must partake of these." However, even after praying, he did not hear the sound of the Lord eating the unripe mangoes, which he heard daily. Therefore, heartbroken that the Lord was not accepting the sacred food, he began to cut his neck. At that moment, the sound of the Lord biting the unripe mango, "Videl Videl," reached the Nayanar's ears. "Maasaru sinthai anbar kazhuthu ariya vaal patru maasil van kaiyai maatra ambalaththadum aiyar veesiya seyya kaiyum ma vadu videl videl en osaiyum kamarininru okkave ezhuntha andre" (As the pure-minded devotee began to cut his neck with the sickle, the Lord of the dance hall, whose hand was pure, reached out and caught his hand. At the same time, the sound of the Lord biting the unripe mango, "Videl Videl," arose from the cracks in the earth) is the next verse composed by Sekkizhar Peruman. The Nayanar began to cut his neck with the Arival (sickle). At that moment, from the cracks in the field, the Lord's divine hand emerged. The Lord's divine hand caught the Nayanar's hand, which was holding the Arival, and stopped it. It stopped the Nayanar's action. At the same time, the sound "Videl Videl" of the Lord eating the unripe mango resonated in the Nayanar's ears. Why did the Lord's divine hand catch the Nayanar's hand? Because the Nayanar had begun to cut his own neck with that hand. Therefore, to prevent that, the Lord's divine hand caught the Nayanar's hand. What kind of hand was the Nayanar's hand? It was "maasil van kai" (a pure, strong hand). "Maasu" means fault. The Nayanar's hand was faultless. What is the fault of a hand? It is not doing what should be done with the hands, and doing what should not be done. These are the faults of the hands. These are the faults committed by the hands. What is the duty of the hands? It is to worship the Lord with folded hands. "Kai kaal kooppi thozheer. Kadi malar thoovi nindru paivai paambarai aartha paramanai kai kaal kooppi thozheer" (Worship with folded hands and feet. Stand and scatter fresh flowers. Worship with folded hands and feet the Supreme Being who wears a serpent with a hood around His waist) is from the Thiruangamalai Thiruppathigam by Thirunavukkarasu Peruman. Therefore, the worship of the Lord and the Sariya and Kriya (preliminary and ritualistic practices) associated with it are the duties and actions to be performed by the hands. In the Thirukurippuththonda Nayanar Puranam, Sekkizhar Peruman describes Ambika performing Shiva puja, singing, "Karam tharum payan ithu ena unarnthu kambam meviya umbar nayagar paal nirandha kaathal sei ullaththal aagi needu nanmaigal yaavaiyum peruga varam tharum porulaam malaivalli maarilaa vagai malarntha per anbaal siram paninthu ezhu poojai naal thorum thiruvullam kola perugiyathandre" (Realizing that this is the benefit of the hands, with a heart filled with unwavering love for the Lord of the heavens who resides in Kamba, so that all enduring good things may flourish, the mountain goddess, who is the giver of boons, with boundless love, bowed her head and performed puja daily, which the Lord graciously accepted). Sekkizhar Peruman sings, "Karam tharum payan ithu ena unarnthu" (Realizing that this is the benefit of the hands). The meaning here is that Ambika performed puja in Kanchi, realizing that the benefit of the hands is to worship the Lord. Therefore, one should perform Sariya and Kriya with the hands. One should perform the Lord's puja. One should worship the Lord with folded hands. This is the rule established by our ancestors. Here, Arivattaya Nayanar, even when he had no food, continued to perform the Lord's service without fail by working as a wage laborer, harvesting rice in the fields. It was the Nayanar's sacred hands that performed this service without fail. Therefore, the Lord holds and grasps those sacred hands, which performed their duties perfectly and were without any fault. The Lord who holds and grasps such sacred hands is the Lord who dances in the Ambalam (dance hall). When the Lord dances in the Ambalam, His hand is a swinging hand. That swinging hand is the Lord's left side, the left hand, which is the divine hand of the Mother, who is grace. Therefore, that divine hand is full of grace. Furthermore, in the form of Nataraja Peruman, which is the form of the Panchakshara, the swinging hand of Ambika represents the letter "Va." In the Thirupanchaksharam, "Va" is Ambika, isn't it? It is the Lord's grace, isn't it? Therefore, Ambika's hand, that divine hand, catches the Nayanar's hand, which had begun to cut his neck. Similarly, in the Thirukurippuththonda Nayanar Puranam, when he tried to strike his head against a rock, the hand that caught him was "valaiyal anintha malarchenkai" (a flower-like red hand adorned with bangles), which Sekkizhar Peruman states in the Periya Puranam was Ambika's hand. In the Puranam of Kannappa Deva Nayanar, which we have already seen, when Kannappa Deva Nayanar finally tried to gouge out his left eye, it was Ambika's divine hand that caught his hand. Ambika herself is the embodiment of the Lord's grace, isn't she? There, the Lord's left hand, adorned with bangles, Ambika's divine hand, emerged from the Shiva Lingam and stopped Kannappa Nayanar's hand. The voice "Kannappa nirkka" (Kannappa, stop) was heard there. Similarly, here, Ambika's divine hand caught and stopped the Nayanar's hand, which had begun to cut his neck. At that moment, the sound "Videl Videl" of the Lord biting the unripe mango reached the Nayanar's ears. From the cracked earth, the Lord's divine hand emerged. The truth taught by sacred texts is that the Lord, who is present everywhere, manifests like ghee in curd within the temple, in the form of the Shiva Lingam, and in the guise of devotees, but remains hidden like ghee in milk elsewhere. Therefore, the all-pervading Lord extends His divine hand even from the cracks in the earth and bestows His grace upon the Nayanar. In accordance with the Thevaram verse, "Viragil theeyinan paalil padu nei pol maraiya nindrulan mamani chothiyan" (He is like the fire in wood, like the ghee in milk, hidden yet standing, the great jewel of light), the all-pervading yet hidden Lord will manifest to devotees with intense devotion and ripened spiritual maturity at the place they desire, but will not manifest clearly to others. This is the truth taught by sacred texts. As soon as the Lord's divine hand caught the Nayanar's hand holding the Arival, the wound on the Nayanar's neck disappeared. Not only did the wound disappear, but the Nayanar's karmas also left him. The Nayanar, surprised by his hand being caught suddenly and his act of cutting his neck being prevented, expressed his profound emotion by folding his two hands and praising the Lord. The Nayanar's karmas left him. "Vevvinai" means a part of the root karma that causes rebirth. The Nayanar's hand was caught by the Lord's divine hand. As a result, his physical suffering was removed. As a result, his illusory body was removed, and he attained a Shiva body. Having thus been freed from bondage, the Nayanar now attained Shiva's grace. The Nayanar, hearing the sound "Videl Videl" of the Lord biting the unripe mango, realized that the Lord had partaken of the food. He understood that his daily sacred service was fulfilled and that the Lord had accepted the red rice, unripe mangoes, and greens. Immediately, he praised the Lord, saying, "Adiyen arivin illamai kandu men adimai vendi padimisai kammaril vandhu ingu amudhu sei parane potri thudiyidai paagamana thuyana natjothi potri podiyani pavalameni purisadai purana potri" (Seeing my foolishness and desiring my servitude, O Supreme Being who has come here to the earth to partake of the food, praise be to You! O pure, good light who has the slender-waisted one as a part, praise be to You! O ancient one with matted locks, whose coral-like body is adorned with sacred ash, praise be to You!). First, the Nayanar praised the Lord as "Parane Potri" (Praise be to the Supreme Being) who came here to partake of the food. Then he praised the Mother as "Thudiyidai paagamana thuyana natjothi potri" (Praise be to the pure, good light who has the slender-waisted one as a part). Then he praised the ancient Lord with matted locks, whose coral-like body is adorned with sacred ash, as "Podiyani pavalameni purisadai purana potri." The Lord's divine body is like crystal, untouched by differences in color. In Sanskrit, it is praised as "Shuddha Spatika Sangasam." However, the scriptures also praise the Lord as having five colors. Nevertheless, it is our tradition that the Lord's color is coral. "Pavalam pol meniyil paal venniru" (Milk-white sacred ash on a coral-like body) is from Thevaram. He praises the Lord as the ancient one with matted locks, whose coral-like body is adorned with sacred ash, as his third praise. At that moment, the Lord appeared on His bull mount (Rishabaroodar). He praised the Nayanar's extraordinary deed, saying, "What you did is good, a good act." He blessed the Nayanar and his wife with the great life in Shiva Loka, saying, "You and your wife shall live forever in Our world." The Nayanar, whose name was Tayanar, was saddened that the Lord had not partaken of the food. Out of love for the Lord, he placed the Arival (sickle) on his neck and cut it. Thus, he received the pure name Arival Tayanar Nayanar. We call Arival Tayanar Nayanar as Arivattaya Nayanar. Sekkizhar Peruman sings, "Thooya naamam Arivattayar aayinar" (He became known by the pure name Arivattayar). The name Arivattayar shines as a name that grants purity. That is why Sundaramoorthy Swamigal also praises him in the Thiruthondathogai, saying, "Enjatha Vattayan Adiyarkkum Adiyen" (I am a servant to the devotees of Vattayan, who does not leave anything behind). This name is a pure name. The sacred names of devotees are purifiers. Those names, being pure, purify the world. Those who contemplate the sacred names of the Lord's devotees, always meditate on the sacred feet of devotees, and praise the sacred names of the Lord's devotees as mantras, those who shiver with devotion, melt with emotion, and praise with true love, will be freed from all sufferings, from the disease of rebirth, and will live eternally in the non-dual, blissful, undivided, perfect fullness. This is the truth stated by Emadhavar Sivagnana Swamigal in the Thiruththondar Thirunama Kovai. Thus, having praised and worshipped the sacred feet of Arival Tayanar Nayanar, also known as Arivattaya Nayanar, the devotee who heard the sound of the Lord biting the unripe mango, when the red rice and other items he carried for the Lord spilled, and the Lord partook of them, and with its support, Sekkizhar Swamigal concludes the Puranam of Arivattaya Nayanar and begins the Puranam of Anaya Nayanar. We too shall conclude this Puranam of Arivattaya Nayanar and continue to contemplate the Puranam of Anaya Nayanar. Praise be to the sacred feet of Arivattaya Nayanar. Praise be to the sacred feet of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Praise be to the sacred feet of Sekkizhar Peruman. Praise be to the sacred feet of Sivakavimani Ayya. Thiruchitrambalam.
