Chapter 01 — PerumizhalaiKurumba Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 481, Thirunindra Sarukkam, Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar Puranam. In the Periya Puranam, which is the Puranam of the holy devotees, the Puranam of Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar follows the Puranam of Kulachchirai Nayanar. Therefore, in the concluding verse of Kulachchirai Nayanar's Puranam, Sekkizhar Peruman introduces the upcoming Puranam of Perumizhalai Kurumbar Nayanar. He states in the 11th and final verse of Kulachchirai Nayanar's Puranam, "I will now praise the feet of Veda Neethi Mizhalai Kurumbar." Here, he refers to Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar as Veda Neethi Mizhalai Kurumbar. Mizhalai is the name of a place. It is Veda Neethi Mizhalai, a place where the rules prescribed in the Vedas are followed. Kurumbar means a minor chieftain or a petty king, who was the lord of such a Mizhalai. Therefore, in the concluding verse of Kulachchirai Nayanar's Puranam, Sekkizhar Peruman introduces the next Puranam by saying, "I now praise and adore the feet of Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar, who is Veda Neethi Mizhalai Kurumbar." There are some similarities between the Puranam of Kulachchirai Nayanar, which we just saw, and the Puranam of Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar, which we are about to see. Both Puranams consist of 11 verses. Both Kulachchirai Nayanar and Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar excelled in guru worship, standing at the forefront of it. Kulachchirai Nayanar adorned the lotus feet of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman. Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar, on the other hand, held the feet of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal on his head and worshipped them. Therefore, the gurus of these two Nayanmars are two of the great Acharyas of Saivism. Furthermore, the sacred histories of Kulachchirai Nayanar and Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar are intertwined with the sacred histories of their respective great Acharyas, Thirugnanasambandha Peruman and Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Thus, there are many similarities between these two Puranams. With such an introduction, Sekkizhar Peruman begins the Puranam of Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar. This section describes the character and historical narrative of Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar. In the order of this Sarukkam, that is, Thirunindra Sarukkam, the Puranam of Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar is the third Puranam. Generally, the Puranam of Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar is the 23rd Puranam in the Periya Puranam. Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar is the 22nd Nayanar in chronological order. Earlier, we contemplated the Puranam of Ilaiyankudi Mara Nayanar. In that, Ilaiyankudi was the name of the village, and Maran was the name of that Nayanar's lineage. Similarly, here, Perumizhalai is the name of the village, and Kurumbar is the name of his lineage. Therefore, the given name of Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar is not known to us. Now, let us contemplate the Puranam of Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar according to the method of Thogai (summary), Vagai (category), and Viri (elaboration). First, in the Thogai Nool (summary text), Thiruthondath Thogai, in the fourth verse, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal sings, "I am a devotee of Perumizhalai Kurumbar and Peyar." The verse continues, "I am a devotee of Thirunavukkarayan, who held righteousness as the ultimate righteousness. I am a devotee of the great Kulachchirai. I am a devotee of Perumizhalai Kurumbar and Peyar." Here, in half a line, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal praises two devotees together: "Perumizhalai Kurumbar and Peyar." "Perumizhalai Kurumbar" refers to Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar, and "Peyar" refers to Karaikkal Ammaiyar. Thus, in this half-line, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal praises two Nayanmars. This is the second instance where two devotees are praised in half a line. We have already seen in the Mummaiyal Ulakanda Sarukkam, where Sundaramoorthy Swamigal praised Muruga Nayanar and Uruthira Pasupathi Nayanar together in half a line, saying, "I am a devotee of Murugan and Uruthira Pasupathi." Similarly, in this Sarukkam, by praising "Perumizhalai Kurumbar and Peyar," Sundaramoorthy Swamigal praises these two devotees, Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar and Karaikkal Ammaiyar, in half a line. The third such instance is "Naattamigu Thandikkum Moorkkarkkum Adiyen," which appears in the fifth verse of Thiruthondar Thogai. Thus, in the Thogai Nool, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal praises the Nayanar by saying, "I am a devotee of Perumizhalai Kurumbar and Peyar." Now, in the Vagai Nool (category text), Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi, Nambiyandar Nambigal dedicates a verse to Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar. It is the 27th verse in Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi. The verse is as follows: "The Lord of Thirunavalur, whose good waters are dammed, will reach the good anklets of the Lord of the rich Kailash tomorrow. To praise the great grace of such Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, I, the Nambi named Mizhalai Kurumban, who has a good crescent moon on his head, will reach the feet of the Lord today itself," saying this, he separated from his body. The meaning of the verse in this Vagai Nool, Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi, is as follows: Just as the water held back by a dam rushes to merge with the sea when the dam breaks, so too, Aludaiya Nambigal, the Lord of Thirunavalur, is to reach the good anklets of Lord Shiva, the Lord of the rich Kailash, tomorrow. Realizing this, Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar, in order to praise the great grace of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, resolved to reach the feet of Lord Shiva today itself and separated from his body. Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar considered Sundaramoorthy Swamigal as his guru and daily contemplated Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's holy feet in his mind, praised them with his words, and worshipped them with devotion. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal decided to go from Thiru Anjaikkalam to Mount Kailash. Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar came to know of this decision the day before, that is, the day before Sundaramoorthy Swamigal was to depart from Thiru Anjaikkalam to Kailash. Knowing this, Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar thought that if he wanted to praise Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's feet, he would have to go to Mount Kailash. Therefore, the day before Sundaramoorthy Swamigal was to go from Anjaikkalam to Kailash, Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar, through the path of Yoga, left his body here and reached the feet of Lord Shiva on Mount Kailash. This is what Nambiyandar Nambigal has sung very concisely, subtly, and deeply in this verse in Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi. He explains Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's journey from this world to Kailash with a beautiful simile. Just as water held back by a dam rushes rapidly to merge with the sea when the dam breaks, so too, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal reaches Kailash from Thiru Anjaikkalam. Nambiyandar Nambigal states this as "Sirai nanpunal Thirunavalur Aali Sezhungailaikku Irai nankazhal naalai eydhum." The same simile is used by Meykandar Devanayanar in the eighth Sutra, fourth Adikarana, of Sivagnana Botham, in the illustrative Venba. This Adikarana states that when the instruments (of the body) cease, the soul reaches the feet of the Primal Lord. The illustrative Venba for this is as follows: "Just as the dammed rich water, after being released from its confinement, goes and merges with the vast ocean without returning, so too, the soul, without wavering, reaches the feet of the Lord." Here, we can compare and enjoy the statement in Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi, "Sirai nanpunal Thirunavalur Aali Sezhungailaikku Irai nankazhal naalai eydhum," with the line in the illustrative Venba, "Sirai seyy nindra sezhumpunalinul an." Here, Nambiyandar Nambigal sings about Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's ascent to Kailash from Thiruvenjaikkalam using this simile. According to the eighth verse of the Thiruvenjaikkalam Thiruppathigam, "Veruthen Manai Vazhkaiyai Vittolinthen," Sundaramoorthy Swamigal makes a plea for liberation to the Lord. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal is about to leave this earthly birth and attain the path of no return, the anklets of Lord Shiva, the Lord of Kailash. Knowing that Sundaramoorthy Swamigal is about to leave this world for Mount Kailash tomorrow, Perumizhalai Kurumbanar decides that he must go to Mount Kailash today itself. Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar was always devoted to Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's feet, worshipping them with his hands, praising them with his mouth, and contemplating them in his mind. Therefore, when Sundaramoorthy Swamigal was to ascend to Mount Kailash tomorrow, in order to praise his grace there, Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar resolved to reach the feet of Lord Shiva, leaving his body here, and thus reached Mount Kailash the day before Sundaramoorthy Swamigal went there. Nambiyandar Nambigal sings all these historical events in this one verse. Now, let us contemplate the Puranam of Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar through the elaborate text, Periya Puranam. Perumizhalai is a very ancient village in the Pandya kingdom. It is a village in the Mizhalai Nadu. This Perumizhalai is currently located in Pudukkottai district, a few miles southwest of Pudukkottai town, near Peraiyur, on the south bank of the Vellar River. It is very close to a village called Thillaivilagam. West of Thirumeyyam, a holy place in Pudukkottai district, and a little further west from Peraiyur, there is a hill called Devamalai. There is a cave temple there. Inside, in front of the Shiva Lingam, there is an image of a sage with matted hair seated in a yogic posture. This is believed to be the image of Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar. Perumizhalai is the name of the village where the Nayanar was born. It belongs to a country called Mizhalai Nadu. There is a holy place called Vizhimizhalai in Chola Nadu. Therefore, to distinguish it, Sekkizhar Swamigal refers to the Nayanar's birthplace and country as Mizhalai Nadu's Mizhalai, Mizhalai Nadu's Perumizhalai. Thus, Perumizhalai, a village in the ancient Mizhalai Nadu in the Pandya kingdom, is the birthplace of our Nayanar. Therefore, in this first verse, Sekkizhar Peruman describes the glory of the country and the lineage. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 02 — PerumizhalaiKurumba Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 482, Thirunindra Sarukkam, Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar Puranam. The village of Perumizhalai, where the Nayanar was born, was a very prosperous village. It was surrounded by mango, coconut, jackfruit, and areca nut trees, and had fertile fields in its surroundings. It was a prosperous place. In every street there, the radiance of sacred ash would abound. The streets of this village were filled with people who lived in the path illuminated by the radiance of sacred ash. The people of Perumizhalai lived with unwavering adherence to righteousness, knowing the rules prescribed in the Vedas and acting according to them, and knowing the exceptions and refraining from acting contrary to them, doing what is enjoined and avoiding what is forbidden. Perumizhalai is a village filled with such virtuous citizens. This glory has prevailed there for a long time without diminishing. It possesses such fame. As we have seen before, Perumizhalai was the capital of the country called Mizhalai, and Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar was the lord of that village of Perumizhalai. We have reason to believe that he was a minor chieftain. Kurumbars were chieftains of minor kingdoms. Therefore, Perumizhalai Kurumbar means a minor chieftain of the village of Perumizhalai. This is not the Nayanar's personal name. His given name is not known to us. Such a Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar would anticipate the needs of the devotees of the Lord and perform all the necessary services for them, even before the Shiva devotees could express what services they needed and how they should be performed. He would receive them as guests, understand their intentions, and carry out their wishes. "If you approach the devotees of Aran with love, make their work your own, speak with humility, and stand in the sign of grace, you will worship, dance, and wander," is the second verse pertaining to the 12th Sutra of Sivagnana Siddhiyar. Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar lived as an embodiment of that principle. Thus, Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar, by honoring Shiva devotees, attained the benefit of mature wisdom. Even those who do not perform such services for devotees with understanding may acquire knowledge. But they will not attain the benefit of that knowledge. Only those who serve devotees truly attain the benefit of the knowledge they possess. Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar was one who attained such benefit. Many devotees would gather there, and even as they ate, Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar would serve them food that never diminished. Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar would conduct himself humbly before Shiva devotees. He would also provide the wealth that devotees needed. Even after serving inexhaustible food and giving away wealth, his mind was not content or full; he continued to perform these services for devotees. Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar possessed the distinction of holding the red lotus feet of Lord Shiva, the husband of Umaiyammaiyar with bee-laden tresses, in the lotus of his heart and worshipping them. Sekkizhar Peruman states this as "Vandu maruvum kuzhal Umaiyal kelvan seyyathaal ennum pundareegam akamalaril vaithu potrum porpinar." "Pundareegam akamalaril vaithu potrum porpu" refers to the nature of contemplating and worshipping the Lord's feet daily in the lotus of one's heart. Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, in the first Thirumurai, 132nd Thiruppathigam, sixth verse, of Thiruveezhimizhalai Thevaram, says, "The Lord resides in the lotus of the hearts of those who are filled with inner love, who have conquered the six inner enemies, controlled the five senses, and are renowned for their wisdom." Just as a gem is set in gold, Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar placed the lotus feet of the Lord, which are His sacred feet, in the lotus of his heart. "Akamalar" is mentioned here. "Akam" means heart. Therefore, "akamalar" is referred to as Hrudaya Kamalam (heart lotus). Hrudayam is the place of worship. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman mentions "akamalaril vaithu potrum porpu." "Worshipping Aran with the five letters, performing homam in the Kundalini with the five letters in the heart, if the Kodandam is broken, then the Lord is there," is an illustrative Venba in the third Adikarana of the ninth Sutra of Sivagnanabotham. Madhavach Sivagnana Yogigal, who wrote the Mahabhashyam for this, explains this passage as follows: "According to the method of external gnana puja, in the lotus of the heart, with the form made of the five letters, worship the Lord with the eight flowers of love, non-violence, control of the five senses, patience, grace, knowledge, truthfulness, and austerity, using that mantra." Sivakavimani Ayya explains "Pundareegam akamalaril vaithu potrum porpu" as follows: The Agamas prescribe the heart, navel, and eyebrow center as the places for puja, homam, and samadhi respectively in inner worship. In this context, Sekkizhar Peruman first sings about the services Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar rendered to Shiva devotees, and then mentions his worship of Lord Shiva's feet in the lotus of his heart. Therefore, by mentioning devotee service first, he indicates that it should precede the service to the Lord. This also becomes a historical note about Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar. Sekkizhar Peruman continues to sing: "In those days, when he lived in this manner, he bowed to Nambi, who sang the Thiruthondath Thogai, which revealed the true nature of endless holy service to the world, and attained the grace of the Eternal Lord, and stood firm in the discipline of contemplating His feet." This is the next verse composed by Sekkizhar Peruman. In those days, when he lived with all the qualities mentioned above, Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar bowed to and worshipped the holy feet of Aludaiya Nambigal, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Because he had attained the grace of Lord Shiva, the Eternal Lord, Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar became bound by the duty of daily contemplating and praising the feet of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Why did he excel in the discipline of contemplating Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's feet in this manner? Because Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, through his divine grace, sang the Thiruthondath Thogai, revealing the true nature of holy service to the world. This act of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal singing the Thiruthondath Thogai became the reason for Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar to bow to and worship Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's holy feet. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, by singing the Thiruthondath Thogai, demonstrated the true nature of holy service to the world. Holy service alone is the special and enduring means of salvation for the soul. Therefore, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, out of great compassion, sang this hymn, bowing down, so that the world might understand the nature of Shiva's holy service and attain salvation. In the Thiruvarur temple, in the Devasiriya Mandapam, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, contemplating when he would become a servant to the Shiva devotees seated within, worshipped Lord Thyagaraja. Lord Thyagaraja appeared before Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, showed him His feet, and when Sundaramoorthy Swamigal worshipped those feet, the Lord, to make him understand holy service, granted him grace and said, "You shall attain these (devotees)." That is, Lord Thyagaraja commanded him, "Attain the Shiva devotees inside the Devasiriya Mandapam." Hearing this, Nambigal worshipped and praised, saying, "I have attained the path free from defects." Lord Thyagaraja then commanded, "Sing about these devotees." Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, pleading with Lord Thyagaraja, said, "What do I know to sing about?" Then, Lord Thyagaraja, giving the first line, "I am a servant to the devotees of Thillai," disappeared. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, bowing many times from a distance to the assembly of devotees, approached them and stood there, singing and worshipping with the Thiruthondath Thogai. All these events are extensively discussed in the Thaduthatkonda Puranam. We have contemplated them from verse 335 to 348 of the Thaduthatkonda Puranam. Here, Sekkizhar Peruman briefly and subtly indicates Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's singing of the Thiruthondath Thogai as "sang by bowing according to destiny." He conveys it with subtlety. Furthermore, the specialty of this Thiruthondath Thogai is that it is a hymn that true devotees always cherish in their hearts. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman emphasizes to us that those who wish to attain salvation through true devotion to Lord Shiva should always keep this in their hearts and never forget it. That is why there is a tradition that the Thiruthondath Thogai is one of the daily hymns recited by Shiva devotees. In the Thiruthondath Thiruthogai Thiruppathigam, the names of the devotees and Nayanmars are praised. Therefore, Shiva devotees revere this Thiruppathigam as a daily recitation. During the ten days of the Thiruvembavai festival, when there was a rule that only Thiruvembavai should be sung, sealing off other Thirumurais, Shiva devotees appealed to Madhavach Sivagnana Yogigal, expressing concern that the Thiruthondath Thogai might not be sung. Out of compassion, Madhavach Sivagnana Swamigal composed a hymn called Thiruthondar Thirunamakkovai. This shows that Sekkizhar Swamigal emphasizes the importance of daily, unfailingly, and continuously chanting and praising the holy names of Shiva devotees. Therefore, Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar had the duty of daily contemplating and worshipping the feet of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, who sang the Thiruthondath Thogai. Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar adopted both bowing to Nambi, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, and contemplating the feet of the devotees as his discipline. The devotees mentioned earlier, whom Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar worshipped, were those he directly saw and worshipped. Here, "true devotees" refers to all devotees who constantly contemplate the Thiruthondath Thogai. Thus, by bowing to Nambi, Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar attained the grace of the Eternal Lord. Therefore, he was blessed with the great fortune of regularly contemplating His feet. If the reason for Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar's worship of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal was the Thiruthondath Thogai Thiruppathigam, then a question might arise: is Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar also one of the devotees mentioned in the Thiruthondath Thogai Thiruppathigam? This is the nature of divine grace, which reveals the future when Sundaramoorthy Swamigal stands in the grace of Lord Shiva. This is the reason why Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar was also included as one of the devotees in the Thiruthondath Thogai. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 03 — PerumizhalaiKurumba Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 483, Thirunindra Sarukkam, Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar Puranam. Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar worshipped the lotus-like feet of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal by touching them with his hands, praising them with his mouth, and contemplating them in his mind. Sekkizhar Peruman describes this as "Kaimaal thozhuthu vaai vaazhththi manaththaal ninaikkum kadappaadu," meaning "the duty of worshipping with hands, praising with mouth, and contemplating with mind the lotus feet of the husband of Paravaiyar with dark, wide eyes." Although Sundaramoorthy Swamigal is referred to here as "Paravaiyar Manavaalan" (husband of Paravaiyar), even though Paravaiyar is mentioned primarily, we should also include Sangili Nachiyar by association. Furthermore, Sekkizhar Peruman here describes the method of worship performed by Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar for Sundaramoorthy Swamigal using all three instruments (mind, speech, and body). He mentions "thozhuthu vaazhththi ninaikkum" (worshipping, praising, contemplating). However, to emphasize that these instruments were given only for this purpose, he explicitly states "kaiaal thozhuthu vaai vaazhththi manaththaal ninaikkum" (worshipping with hands, praising with mouth, contemplating with mind), mentioning both the instruments and the actions performed by them. Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar undertook this worship as an unfailing duty. The red, golden feet of Lord Shiva, which even Vishnu, the husband of Lakshmi, and Brahma, his son, could not comprehend, are the feet of Lord Shiva. The way to attain such feet is this: that is, Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar understood that worshipping the feet of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal was the way to attain the feet of Lord Shiva, and with love, he continued to praise Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Here, let us contemplate the sequence established by Sekkizhar Peruman. First, Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar considered serving devotees as the fruit of knowledge. Then, he worshipped the feet of Lord Shiva in the lotus of his heart. After that, he bowed to Sundaramoorthy Swamigal and regularly contemplated his feet. He continued to do this with love, considering this regular contemplation of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's holy feet as the right way to reach the feet of Lord Shiva. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman describes these as the means by which Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar's path of love grew, one leading to another. Because he regularly worshipped the feet of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal daily, Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar attained the eight great siddhis, such as Anima. If we consider worshipping the feet of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal and, through that, worshipping the feet of Lord Shiva as the means, and attaining these eight siddhis as the fruit, then Sekkizhar Peruman further clarifies in the Puranam of Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar that these eight siddhis are only an intermediate benefit, not the ultimate one. The next verse composed by Sekkizhar Peruman states: "By the goodness of daily chanting Nambi Arurar's name, after attaining the siddhis like Anima, he, with increasing love, became firmly established in the five letters of the Lord's name, which are the essence of hearing and meaning." Here, he sings that by the goodness of daily praising Nambi Arurar's name, Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar attained the eight types of siddhis, such as Anima. "Naalum Nambi Arurar naamam navirntha nalaththaale" (by the goodness of daily chanting Nambi Arurar's name) is mentioned here. "Navirntha" means "uttered." "Naakkai kondu Aran naamam navittrilaar" (those who did not utter the name of Aran with their tongue) is a Thevaram saying. We should always utter the holy names of our great Acharyas, Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, Thirunavukkarasu Swamigal, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, and Manikkavasaga Peruman. Our tongue should utter those names, for they are the protective mantras for those who utter them. "Gnanasambandhar enum naama mandhiramum solla" (to utter the mantra of Gnanasambandhar's name) is a verse from the Puranam of Thirugnanasambandhar, verse 721. Just as Sri Panchaksharam shines as five letters, so too, the names Gnanasambandhar, Navukkarasar, Nambiyarurar, and Manikkavasagar are also the great mantra of the five letters. If we remove the single letters from the names Gnanasambandhar, Navukkarasar, Nambiyarurar, and Manikkavasagar, these names will contain five letters. Therefore, they are called five letters. We have seen the history of Koon Pandiyar, who attained salvation by receiving the mantra initiation of Gnanasambandhar and became Nindra Seer Nedumaran Nayanar, and the history of Appoodhi Adigal Nayanar, who attained salvation by meditating on the name Navukkarasar. Similarly, we see that Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar attained a great life by daily practicing the name Nambiyarurar of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Through this, we can understand that the holy names of our great Acharyas shine as five-letter mantras. The name Nambiyarurar itself is a Shiva name. As mentioned before, if we remove the single letters, it shines as five letters. The Thiruvaindhezuthu (five letters) of Nambiyarurar shines as an excellent means of bringing about devotion to the guru and various other benefits. That is why it easily brought about the eight types of siddhis, such as Anima. Not only that, but it also brought about a strong attachment to the Thiruvaindhezuthu, which grants liberation. We saw that these Anima and other siddhis came to Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar. These are the eight types of siddhis: Anima, Mahima, Laghima, Garima, Prapti, Prakamya, Ishatva, and Vasitva. In the Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam, in the chapter on the instruction of the eight great siddhis, Paranjothi Munivar has sung extensively about these eight types of siddhis. Anima means making very large objects as small as an atom. Mahima means making very small objects large. Laghima means making very heavy objects light. Garima means making very light objects heavy. Prapti means attaining whatever one desires. Prakamya means enjoying them as one wishes. Ishatva means the power to rule, to be a ruler. Vasitva means the power to bring everything under one's control. Of these, Anima, Mahima, and Laghima are attained through the body, and the others are attained through the mind and other instruments. Furthermore, Thirumoola Deva Nayanar, in his Thirumandhiram, third Thanthiram, from verse 92 to 141, has sung extensively about these eight great siddhis. All these siddhis come to Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar. These siddhis act according to the command of Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar. If it were others, they would have fallen and perished, considering the attainment of these siddhis as the ultimate goal. But Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar uses these siddhis to reach a higher state. He used these siddhis as tools to reach a higher state. For others, these eight great siddhis might have been great benefits among the benefits derived from chanting Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's name. But the point here is that these siddhis should be considered as being of a lower status, and they should be used as tools to reach another higher state. In the Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam, in the chapter on the instruction of the eight great siddhis, we saw that Lord Shiva explains all about these eight great siddhis. After explaining all this, Lord Shiva further states, "Yogis who have realized Me do not desire these." Therefore, these siddhis serve as aids to Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar's divine journey. We will see them in detail later. Thus, these yogic siddhis were instrumental in Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar's divine journey to Mount Kailash. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman mentions them here. Even so, Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar continued to practice the five letters of Lord Shiva's name alone as his worldly, vital, and bodily support. How does the five-letter mantra alone become the bodily, worldly, and vital support? The five-letter mantra alone is the true nature that becomes these three supports and bestows benefits upon a soul in this world, the next world, and in liberation, which is the ultimate state, and remains with it everywhere. That is the Sri Panchakshara mantra, the five-letter name of the Lord. It is the holy name of Lord Shiva. "It is the name of the five letters that tastes sweet like the juice of sugarcane," says Thirunavukkarasu Peruman in Thiruthandagam. "Nandi naamam Namashivaya" is a Thevaram saying of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman. The goodness of chanting Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's name first brought about the Anima and other eight great siddhis to Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar. Furthermore, that worship, by chanting Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's name, caused love to grow and made the five-letter name of the Lord the ultimate certainty. While Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar was living and conducting himself in this manner, at the same time, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal reached the city of Kodungallur in the mountainous region. Sekkizhar Peruman sings: "As he lived in this manner, the Lord with the bull flag, whose golden anklets touched the earth, came and argued, showed the original palm leaf before the assembly, and enslaved Vanthondar, who then reached Kodungallur, where the moon-kissed mansions abound." Sundaramoorthy Swamigal was known by the name Vanthondar. Lord Shiva, with His sweet golden feet touching this earth, came here, argued, showed the original palm leaf before the assembly, disrupted Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's mind, and enslaved him. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal was enslaved by Lord Shiva. All these events have been discussed in the Thaduthatkonda Puranam, from verse 173 to 219. This event, where the palm leaf was shown and he was enslaved, was the first event that revealed Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's greatness to the world through divine grace. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, thus enslaved, at this time, reached Kodungallur, the capital of Cheraman Perumal Nayanar's mountainous country. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's friend was Cheraman Perumal Nayanar, also known as Kalaritrarivar Nayanar. Therefore, at this time, he went to the mountainous country with a desire to meet his friend Cheraman Perumal Nayanar. He went to the mountainous country and reached Kodungallur. This was the second time Sundaramoorthy Swamigal visited the mountainous country. We can refer to the third, 28th, and 29th verses in the Vellana Sarukkam to know this history. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 04 — PerumizhalaiKurumba Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 484, Thirunindra Sarukkam, Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar Puranam. The city of Kodungallur, as it is called, is currently the town of Kodungallur near Thrissur city in Kerala state. Very close to it is the holy Shiva temple of Thiru Anjaikkalam. It is currently known as Thiruvanchikulam. While staying at the palace of Cheraman Perumal Nayanar in Kodungallur, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal had the divine will, the thought, the desire to go to Kailash. There, he praised and worshipped the Lord of Thiru Anjaikkalam, sang the Tamil garland of verses beginning with "Thalaikku Thalaimalai," and made his plea to the Lord. As he circumambulated the temple, the Lord sent a white elephant and deities from Kailash, and Sundaramoorthy Swamigal ascended the white elephant and went to Kailash. We will see this history in the concluding section of the Periya Puranam, the Vellana Sarukkam. Here, since this section is included with Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar, Sekkizhar Peruman briefly presents that history to us. When the same events appear in multiple places in the Periya Puranam, Sekkizhar Peruman's unique specialty is to present them concisely where needed and elaborately where appropriate. This is one such instance. Sekkizhar Peruman sings: "The Kurumbanar of Mizhalai, who lived long, understood in his heart that he would attain the life of reaching the northern Kailash, shining in the clouds, by the grace of the Lord, as he praised the nectar-swallowing Lord of Anjaikkalam and offered garlands of beautiful Tamil verses." Vanthondar, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, who went to the city of Kodungallur, worshipped the Lord residing in Thiru Anjaikkalam there. While staying in Kodungallur and daily worshipping the Lord of Thiru Anjaikkalam, one day, while seated in the palace of Cheraman Perumal Nayanar, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal had the divine will to go to Kailash. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal clearly understood in his heart the life of reaching the northern Kailash mountain. After thus understanding, the next day, he worshipped the Lord of Thiru Anjaikkalam and sang garlands of beautiful Tamil verses. This Thiruppathigam consists of verses from Thiru Anjaikkalam, beginning with "Thalaikku Thalaimalai." Each verse of this pathigam was considered a garland. That is why Sekkizhar Peruman refers to them as "Senchol Tamil Malaigal" (garlands of beautiful Tamil verses). In the Vellana Sarukkam, in the 29th verse, Sekkizhar Peruman later expresses the same idea as "edutha Tamil malai" (the Tamil garland that was taken). In this Thiruppathigam, sung about the Lord of Thiru Anjaikkalam, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal makes a plea for liberation to the Lord, saying, "Veruthen manai vazhkaiyai vittolinthen" (I have renounced and abandoned household life). As a result of this plea, as he reached the entrance of the Thiru Anjaikkalam temple, the Lord's command and a white elephant arrived there. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal ascended that white elephant and went to Mount Kailash. This event is the concluding event in the divine history of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's acts of grace. Thus, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's heart had the divine will to go to Kailash and attain that life in Kailash. Therefore, the clarity that he should go to Mount Kailash the next day arose in Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's heart. Therefore, on the first day, he grasped that idea in his mind. Accordingly, the next day, when he went to the Thiru Anjaikkalam temple, he sang the pathigam "Thalaikku Thalaimalai" with this idea in mind. Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar understood and realized this thought that Sundaramoorthy Swamigal had in his heart the day before, in Cheraman Perumal Nayanar's palace, that he should go to Kailash the next day. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal was currently residing in the city of Kodungallur, near Thrissur in Kerala state, in Cheraman Perumal Nayanar's palace. At the same time, Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar was living in the village of Perumizhalai, near Thirumayam in Pudukkottai district, in the Pandya country. Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar's village is on the east coast. Kodungallur, where Sundaramoorthy Swamigal was residing, is on the west coast, in the mountainous region. Thus, Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar, while in Perumizhalai, understood the thought that Sundaramoorthy Swamigal had in his heart there. How is this possible? This can be understood through yogic vision, by the nature of yogic vision. We have already seen that Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar had attained the eight great siddhis. Therefore, Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar possessed yogic vision. Through this yogic vision, one can transcend the boundaries of time and space and understand what has happened and what is about to happen. Sivagnana Siddhiyar, in its Alavaiyiyal (Epistemology), describes the nature of this yogic vision. "The knowledge that arises from the samadhi, which burns away the impurities, and perceives all objects, transcending the nature of space and time, is the good yogic vision," says verse 12 of Sivagnana Siddhiyar Alavaiyiyal, describing this yogic vision. Generally, direct perception, called Pratyaksha, is of four types: Indriya Katchi (sensory perception), Manasa Katchi (mental perception), Thanvedanai Katchi (self-perception), and Yoga Katchi (yogic perception). The fourth type is called yogic perception. The knowledge of the soul is pervasive. The soul is an all-pervading entity. Therefore, its quality of knowledge is also pervasive knowledge. But this knowledge is obstructed by the power of mala (impurity). These mala powers can be somewhat destroyed by the Ashtanga Yoga Samadhi, which includes Yama, Niyama, etc. Thus, when the mala powers that obstructed the soul's knowledge are somewhat destroyed by Ashtanga Yoga Samadhi, and one is in samadhi at a particular place and time, the perception of objects in all three places (past, present, future) and all three times (earth, sky, underworld) is called yogic perception. The Sivayoga mentioned in our Dasakaryam is different from the Ashtanga Yoga mentioned here. This Ashtanga Yoga has eight limbs: Yama, Niyama, etc. Yama consists of five principles: non-violence, truthfulness, non-stealing, non-covetousness of others' possessions, and control of the senses. Niyama consists of five principles: contentment with what one has, austerity, purification of the body with water and the mind with truth, study of philosophical texts, and worship of deities. Asana refers to various postures like Padra, Gomukha, Pankaya, Kesari, Swastika, Sukhasana, etc. Pranayama is the control of the breath through Rechaka, Puraka, and Kumbhaka. Pratyahara is withdrawing all senses from objects and turning them inwards. Dharana is the continuous focus of the inner instrument, like a stream of oil. Dhyana is the continuous flow of the inner instrument towards God without interruption. The eighth is Samadhi. When the latent impressions in the soul are removed and the inner instrument is focused, it is called Samadhi. In this Samadhi, from the place where one is in Samadhi, at the time of Samadhi, one can perceive objects in all three persons (first, second, third), all three realms (earth, sky, underworld), and all three times (past, present, future). This ability is attained in Samadhi because in Samadhi, the soul's knowledge merges with God. Therefore, the soul's pervasive knowledge becomes manifest without any obstruction. When thus merged with God's knowledge, everything, including the future, is determined by God. Therefore, in that state of Samadhi, when merged with God, the soul's pervasive knowledge becomes manifest. In that state, future events also become visible to those in Samadhi. Therefore, even though Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar and Sundaramoorthy Swamigal were in different places, Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar had attained all the yogic powers. Therefore, the thought that Sundaramoorthy Swamigal had there was conveyed to him here. Thus, Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar realized that Sundaramoorthy Swamigal was about to go to Kailash tomorrow, at the very moment Sundaramoorthy Swamigal thought of it. The Anima and other siddhis attained by Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar were capable of producing such power. This is the first way. Now, we can understand this in a second way. Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar was one who daily chanted the name of Nambiyarurar. Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar always worshipped Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, keeping him in his mind without separation, and his power was the power of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Therefore, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's mental event was revealed to him here. If we see that even in ordinary worldly life, two deeply loving individuals, even when separated for long periods, experience similar mental events, then it is very easy to understand that this is possible for our saints who have risen above this worldly state. It is not surprising that Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar, who was deeply immersed in the thoughts of Thirunavukkarasu Swamigal, realized the thought that Sundaramoorthy Swamigal had and clarified in his mind. In Thirukkural, "Punarchi pazhaguthal venda" (no need for physical intimacy), Parimelazhagar illustrates how King Kopperuncholan and Pisiranthaiyar were united in feeling. Therefore, the thought that Sundaramoorthy Swamigal had of going to Kailash tomorrow was conveyed to Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar. Therefore, Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar thought that he should go before Sundaramoorthy Swamigal went to Kailash. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 05 — PerumizhalaiKurumba Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 485, Thiruneetru Sarukkam, Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar Puranam. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal intends to go to Kailash tomorrow. This intention, while he was staying in the Kodungallur palace, was perceived and conveyed to Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar. Sekkizhar Peruman continues to sing: "The Vanthondar who came from Thirunavalur, shining on earth, wishes to reach the inaccessible holy Kailash tomorrow. I will not live as if a precious jewel has left my eye. Thinking this, he resolved, 'I will reach Shiva's feet today itself through yoga.'" This is the verse composed by Sekkizhar Peruman. The meaning of this verse is: The Vanthondar, who appeared in the venerable Thirunavalur on this earth, wishes to reach the inaccessible holy Kailash tomorrow. Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar, thinking, "Will I live in this world, separated from Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, like someone whose precious jewel has left their eye? I will not," resolves to reach the feet of Lord Shiva, who resides in Kailash, today itself through the power of yoga. "Maniyil thigazhum Thirunavaluril vantha Vanthondar" is the phrase here that refers to Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. "Man" means this earthly world. The holy place of Thirunavalur shines in this earthly world. Why? Because it is glorious with many distinctions, including being the birthplace of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, one of the great Acharyas of Saivism. Therefore, that Thirunavalur becomes "Maniyil thigazhum Thirunavalur." "Nadhanukku oor namakku oor Narasinga Munaiyaraiyan aathariththu eesanukku aat seyyum oor aninavalur" (The Lord's village, our village, the village where Narasinga Munaiyaraiyan lovingly serves the Lord, is beautiful Thirunavalur) is a Thevaram verse by Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, describing the glory of Thirunavalur. Thus, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal is the Vanthondar who came from Thirunavalur, shining on earth. This can be interpreted as "Thirunavalur shining on earth" and "Vanthondar who came to earth." "Coming to earth" refers to Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's descent from Mount Kailash to this earthly world. He was born on this earth by the command of the Lord, as mentioned in "thenpuvi meethu thondri." He wishes to go to the inaccessible holy Kailash mountain tomorrow. Mount Kailash is an inaccessible mountain. It is a mountain that cannot be directly approached by others. In the Puranam of Thirunavukkarasu Nayanar, in the 1630th verse, we saw that Mount Kailash is a mountain that cannot be reached by those with human nature, and it is difficult even for the gods with sharp spears to approach. Therefore, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal will reach such an inaccessible holy Kailash mountain tomorrow. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal resolved in his heart to reach Kailash the very next day. Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar, realizing this, decides, "I will not live separated. I will go today and reach Shiva's feet through yoga." Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar considered Sundaramoorthy Swamigal as the pupil of his eye and worshipped him daily. If there is an eye but no pupil, sight cannot occur. Therefore, Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar thought that if Sundaramoorthy Swamigal went to Kailash, he would not be able to see the Lord even if he existed without Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. The implication here is that the grace of the Guru is indispensable for attaining the Lord and experiencing bliss. Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar, who daily chanted the holy name of Nambiyarurar, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, and thereby attained the Anima and other siddhis, and then adopted the five letters of Lord Shiva as his bodily, worldly, and vital support, was in a state of spiritual experience. Therefore, he thought, "If Sundaramoorthy Swamigal leaves this earthly world, I will be like an eye without a pupil." Therefore, Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar resolves, "To worship the feet of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, I will reach the holy feet of Lord Shiva today itself." "Kanne kannin karumaniye" (O eye, pupil of the eye) is a Thevaram saying. The fact that Sundaramoorthy Swamigal says, "I will go to Kailash today itself, before Sundaramoorthy Swamigal goes tomorrow," is an expression of boundless devotion to the Guru. This is to go to Mount Kailash today, the first day, to meet Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, and to attain the benefit of his grace. Although attaining Lord Shiva's holy feet is the ultimate goal, the point here is that Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar attempted to attain Lord Shiva's holy feet as a means to attain the blessing of directly meeting Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Therefore, the state of unsurpassed bliss of attaining Lord Shiva was not Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar's aim. Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar's aim was to attain Lord Shiva's holy feet and praise Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's holy feet. Therefore, to meet and praise Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, the Nayanar adopted attaining Lord Shiva's holy feet as a means. Therefore, he thinks and resolves, "I will attain Shiva's feet through yoga." The yoga mentioned here refers to the practice of Sivayoga. Sivayoga is completely different from Patanjali's yoga. Sekkizhar Peruman continues to sing about how Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar reached Mount Kailash through yoga. "The four inner instruments became one, he embraced pure knowledge, directed his consciousness through the Brahma Nadi, and when the previously practiced path opened the secret root in the middle of the skull, he reached the feet of the Primal Lord and arrived at Kailash." This is how Sekkizhar Peruman sings about how Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar reached Mount Kailash through yoga. First, the inner instruments, such as the mind, all became one. Then, embracing pure knowledge, the consciousness directed the life-breath, the Prana Vayu, through the Brahma Nadi, also called Sushumna. When the Prana Vayu settled in the middle of the skull through the Brahmarandhra, the opening at the crown of the head, the Pranava mantra, which he had practiced before, opened that Brahmarandhra gateway. Through that path, the Brahmarandhra opened, and through that, Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar reached the feet of Lord Shiva and arrived at Mount Kailash. This is the meaning of this verse. Sekkizhar Peruman first sings that the four inner instruments became one. The four inner instruments are the Antahkaranas: Manas (mind), Buddhi (intellect), Chitta (consciousness), and Ahamkara (ego). These four Antahkaranas become one. That is, they unite in thought. "Alapparun Karanangal naangum sinthaiye aaga" (The immeasurable four instruments becoming one thought) is a verse composed by Sekkizhar Swamigal when Sundaramoorthy Swamigal worshipped Lord Nataraja in Thillai. Sekkizhar Peruman refers to this here as "Naalu karanangalum ondraai." The Antahkaranas of mind, intellect, and ego, which are distinct from thought, abandon their functions and become solely the function of thought, and these four Antahkaranas stand as one. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman first sings that the four instruments are one. Generally, these four instruments do not unite so easily. But these instruments, which are by nature not easily united, now unite. Therefore, he then composes the next phrase, "nallarivu merkondu" (embracing pure knowledge). "Nallarivu" means pure knowledge. That is, it means excellent knowledge, pure knowledge, unmixed with the functions of the external and internal instruments. Thus, embracing pure knowledge, the consciousness directs the Prana Vayu through the Sushumna Nadi. Sekkizhar Peruman refers to Sushumna Nadi as Brahma Nadi in this verse. It is a rule that when the Prana Vayu is directed through the Brahma Nadi, the Brahmarandhra opens. "Anganainthu poy mela eri alar mathi mandalathin muka maarka amuthu udal mutta thekki" (Having reached there, ascended upwards, and filled the body with the nectar from the moon's orb at the forehead) is the 21st verse of the eighth Sutra of Sivagnana Siddhiyar. In this, Arunandi Peruman explains how to perform inner worship through the path of yoga. He explains how to perform inner worship in Sivayoga: by controlling the two breaths, Puraka and Rechaka, and stabilizing the Prana Vayu in the Sushumna path; by withdrawing the mind from sensory objects; by meditating on the letters that shine in the six chakras like Muladhara and Swadhisthana, which are triangular and square in shape, respectively; by meditating on the presiding deities of those chakras; by understanding how the Asaba Shakti stands as Shiva's form in those six chakras; then, starting from Muladhara, ascending with the Asaba Shakti up to the Brahmarandhra, associating with deities like Vinayagar; then, making the lotus bud in the Brahmarandhra blossom; then, drawing the lunar orb in its pericarp with the Mulagni; then, filling the entire body with the nectar that flows through that path; then, meditating on the full effulgence. We can recall this explanation here. Sivagnana Siddhiyar also states that those who diligently practice this inner worship, worshipping the sixty-three forms, will attain the Apara Mukti Sthana, which is Sivaroopa. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman's statement in this verse, "directing the Prana Vayu, that consciousness, through the Brahma Nadi, the Sushumna Nadi," can be considered in conjunction with that Sivagnana Siddhiyar verse. Furthermore, in the Thiruvathavuradigal Puranam, it is sung, "He raised the Moolavayu, blocked the two paths firmly, opened one path, and entered and immersed himself with the sound of the dancing anklets." We can also recall that here. Thus, when the consciousness directs the Prana Vayu, the life-force, through the Sushumna, Sekkizhar Peruman sings the next phrase, "marai moolam kapala nadu thirappa" (the secret root in the middle of the skull opens). That is, the Pranava mantra, uttered correctly and appropriately, which is also the Thiruvaindhezuthu, as we can recall here. That Pranava mantra, through the previously practiced method, opens the Brahmarandhra gateway in the middle of the skull. This is a locked gateway at the crown of the skull. The Pranava mantra causes this locked gateway to open, through which the life-force passes. This opening of the locked gateway in the middle of the skull, through which the life-force passes, is called Kapala Moksha. Yogis say that this is the method by which they intentionally expel their life-force. Yogis open the Brahmarandhra and expel their life-force through it. Therefore, even today, as a symbol of this, the practice of breaking coconuts on the heads of yogis, gnanis, and ascetics before placing their bodies in samadhi after their life-force has ceased, is still prevalent. The top part of the skull, which was initially in two halves, later becomes strong and united like a six-spoked wheel as days pass. Then, when it breaks, that is, when the Brahmarandhra, the gateway of the skull, opens, it will appear broken into two halves, just as it was initially in two halves and then united. Yogis are capable of opening this gateway, the Brahmarandhra, by themselves, without external effort. This is one of the yogic achievements. Therefore, only yogis have the power to open the Brahmarandhra and expel their life-force through that opening. For others, the paths through which the life-force leaves the body are the other orifices in the body. Therefore, here, Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar's life-force reaches the Brahmarandhra gateway, and the Pranava opens that gateway. Through that, Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar leaves that body and reaches Mount Kailash. Here, although the Pranava mantra is mentioned, the Thiruvaindhezuthu is also equivalent to the Pranava mantra. Therefore, the Sthula, Sukshma, Karana, etc., Panchaksharas, at the time the life-force leaves the body, stand with the life-force and cause this process of the life-force leaving to occur according to the karma of each soul. This is stated in the gnana shastras. "Thunjum podhu nin naamaththu thiru ezhuththu anjum thondra arulum aiyarrarae" (O Lord, who grants the appearance of the five holy letters of your name at the time of death) is a Thevaram saying. "Saam andru uraikka tharuthi kandaai engal Sangarane" (You will grant us to utter it on the day of death, O our Shankara) is also a Thevaram saying of Appar Swamigal. Therefore, in any case, at the moment the life-force leaves the body, it is the Thiruvaindhezuthu that protects the soul and causes this to happen according to the karma performed by each soul. This is the truth stated in the gnana shastras. The ninth Thanthiram, fifth section, Panchaksharam, Sthula Panchaksharam, fifth verse of Thirumooladeva Nayanar's Thirumandhiram is as follows: "When the seven rays glow and fire blazes, and the life-force rises, leaving the instruments, and when death comes and changes the life-force, it is the five letters that unite with the fortress." Therefore, its meaning is that whether death comes naturally or suddenly due to great evil deeds performed by the soul, it is always the Thiruvaindhezuthu mantra that provides protection to the soul. In the same section, the seventh verse, Thirumooladeva Nayanar also states: "For those who stand in the qualities taught by the Guru, and are able to cut off the account of birth, it is the five letters that show the path of grace." Its meaning is that for those who stand in the path taught by the Shiva Guru, and are able to abandon the religious texts that speak only of the path leading to birth, and are able to destroy the path of birth, it is the Thiruvaindhezuthu mantra that shows the path of divine grace and bestows that power. Therefore, the truth stated in the gnana shastras is that the Thiruvaindhezuthu stands with the soul at the time the life-force leaves the body and causes this to happen according to the karma of each soul. Therefore, the reasons for Sekkizhar Peruman's previous verses, such as Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar practicing Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's holy name, thereby attaining the Ashtama Siddhis, thereby mastering yoga, and then adopting the Sri Panchaksharam of the Lord as his certainty, all help us here. Sekkizhar Peruman composes all the previous verses as an explanation for this verse, which describes how Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar reached Kailash through yogic practice. Thus, even before Sundaramoorthy Swamigal went to Mount Kailash, one day before, our Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar, leaving his body through the Brahmarandhra, reached Kailash. Such is the greatness of Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar. Sekkizhar Peruman thus sings the history of Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar. Now, as the concluding 11th verse, Sekkizhar Peruman composes the following: "Having worshipped the feet of Mizhalai Kurumbar, who, through practiced and intense yoga, attained the feet of the Lord of Kailash, the husband of Paravai, we will now describe the greatness of Karaikkal Ammaiyar, whose soft nature surpasses that of peacocks, and who is like a cuckoo, along with the old woman." This is the concluding verse. Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar, who, through yogic practice of inhaling, retaining, and exhaling the life-breath, attained the feet of Lord Shiva, who resides in Kailash, to be inseparably united with the feet of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, the husband of Paravai. Having worshipped the feet of such Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar, Sekkizhar Peruman concludes this Puranam and introduces the Puranam of Karaikkal Ammaiyar, saying, "I will now begin to narrate the history of Karaikkal Ammaiyar, whose soft nature surpasses that of peacocks, and who is like a cuckoo, along with the old woman." We too, worshipping the holy feet of Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar, will now contemplate the Puranam that narrates the incomparable greatness of Karaikkal Ammaiyar, as told by Sekkizhar Peruman.

Let us begin. Praise, praise to the holy feet of Perumizhalai Kurumba Nayanar. Praise, praise to the holy feet of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Praise, praise to the holy feet of Nambiyandar Nambigal. Praise, praise to the holy feet of Sekkizhar Peruman. Praise, praise to the holy feet of Sivakavimani Ayya, who wrote the commentary on the Periya Puranam. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.
