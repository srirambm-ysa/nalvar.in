Chapter 01 — Muruga Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 191. Mummayaal Ulagaanda Sarukkam, Muruga Nayanar Puranam. Following the Puranam of Moorthi Nayanar in the Mummayaal Ulagaanda Sarukkam, the Puranam of Muruga Nayanar is presented. First, let us contemplate a summary of Muruga Nayanar's Puranam, and then his sacred history in detail. Thirupugalur is a beautiful and excellent city surrounded by flower gardens in the Chola country, where the Kaveri river flows. The sacred ash on the bodies of the Saivite elders living there shines with a white light, radiating both inside and out. In the groves, birds called Poovai sing sacred hymns (Thirupathigams) from what they have heard and learned. Hearing this, devotees' hearts melt, and tears well up in their eyes. In such a prosperous Thirupugalur, Muruganar was born into the lineage of Brahmins (Maraiyavar). He excelled in Shiva Gnanam (divine knowledge of Shiva). His heart melted with abundant love for the sacred feet of Lord Shiva. Due to the true penance he had performed earlier, Muruga Nayanar undertook the sacred service of plucking and offering Thirupallithamam (flowers for worship) to Lord Shiva. He would rise before dawn, complete his daily duties, immerse himself in pure water, enter flower gardens and ponds, and gather flowers at the right time for blooming, arranging them in different sacred flower baskets. From the four types of flowers—Kottuppoo (tree flowers), Kodi-poo (creeper flowers), Nila-poo (ground flowers), and Neer-poo (water flowers)—he would select those suitable for offering to Lord Shiva. He would then sit in a specially designated place and arrange these flowers into Kovai, Thaamam, Maalai, Kannni, Pinaiyal, Thodaiyal, etc., according to the appropriate time. At the right moments, he would lovingly offer them to the Lord, understanding and delighting in His divine will. He performed regular archana (worship) with sandalwood paste, incense, and other offerings, his love overflowing. He continuously recited the sacred five-letter mantra (Thiru Ainthu Ezhuthu), which is the essence of Lord Shiva's Thirupathigams, with his tongue. He understood and followed the rules and exceptions. He attained the great honor of being a friend to Thirugnanasambandar, the child saint who was blessed to partake of the milk from the Universal Mother's breast, mixed with Shiva Gnanam. In this manner, Muruganar performed his regular worship daily without fail for many days. As a result of his previous worship, he participated in the wedding of Thirugnanasambandar and attained the great eternal life of dwelling in the shade of Lord Shiva's sacred feet. This is the brief summary of Muruga Nayanar's history. Sivakavimani Aiyya mentions the following special features of Thirupugalur, where Muruga Nayanar was born and lived: Thirupugalur is a place where Agni Devan (the Fire God) worshipped. Hence, it is called Agneesam. This sacred place offers refuge to souls fearful of rebirth. Therefore, it is called Pugalur (place of refuge). It is also called Punnai Vanam (Punnai forest) because the Sthala Viruksham (sacred tree) of this place is the Punnai tree. Since the Lord's idol is reclining, it is called Konesam. The Agni Theertham (sacred pond of Agni) surrounds the temple like a moat. Thirunavukkarasu Peruman performed Uzhavara pani (manual service) here and attained mukti (liberation) on the Chithirai Sadhayam day. Here are located the Bhootha, Bhavishya, and Varthamana Lingams, representing the past, future, and present times respectively, each holding its own significance. It is a place where all three of the Saivite Paramacharya Moorthigal (great spiritual teachers) visited, worshipped, and sang sacred hymns. This place holds the special distinction that countless devotees, including Thirugnanasambandar, Thirunavukkarasar, Thiruneelanakkar, and Siruthondar, resided for a long time in Muruga Nayanar's matham (monastery). This Sthalam is unique in having two temples, Thirupugalur and Thirupugalur Varthamaneswaram, located in the same place. To the north, next to the main shrine of Varthamaneswaram, is the Varthamana Lingam. Muruga Nayanar worshipped this Lord daily as his Atmaartha Moorthy (personal deity) and attained great blessings. Let us consider some more special features of this Sthalam. It is located near the town of Nannilam in the Thanjavur region, on the northern bank of the Mudikondan River. The temple is situated in the center of the town, facing east. A beautiful five-tiered gopuram (tower) about 90 feet high adorns the entrance of the temple. Other names for this sacred place, Thirupugalur, include Punnagavanam, Saranyapuram, Saranyapuri, and Rakthaaranyam. Inside this temple, there are two main sanctums. The Swayambhu (self-manifested) Lord is known by names such as Pugalur Nathar, Agneeswarar, Agnipureeswarar, Saranyapureeswarar, Punnaganathar, Pratyaksha Varadar, and Konapiran. The Goddess is known as Karunthaal Kuzhali, Karunthaar Kuzhali, and Soolikaambal. The Lord in the other main sanctum is called Varthamaneswarar, and the Goddess is called Manonmani. In this shrine, there is an idol of Muruga Nayanar. There is a Thevara Thirupathigam specifically for Varthamaneswaram, composed by Sambandapperuman. The names Konapiran and Karunthaal Kuzhali are found in Thevaram, and Thirupugalur Mahadevar, Kailasamuḍaiya Nayanaar, and Konar Peruman are found in inscriptions. The Chandrasekarar shrine in this temple is particularly special. Inside the inner prakaram (circumambulatory path) of the temple, there are idols of Bhootheswarar, Bhavisheswarar, Appar Swamigal, Vathapi Ganapathi, and others. In the outer prakaram, in the northeast, there is the shrine of the Goddess. On the southwest bank of the sacred tank surrounding the temple, there is a Siddhar temple. It is said that 18 Siddhars worshipped the Punnian (virtuous one) who resides in Pugalur. In the southern matham complex, there is a Gnana Vinayagar temple worshipped by Nerkondravanar. These are all the special features of the Sthalam. Now, information about the Theerthams (sacred waters) and Sthala Virukshams (sacred trees). The sacred tank surrounding the temple is called Agni Theertham and Bana Theertham. It is called Agni Theertham because Agni Bhagavan dug a four-cornered tank and performed penance in its center, and the Lord appeared to him. It is called Bana Theertham because Banasuran, for the sake of his mother, tried to move the Swayambhu Lingam in this place, but when he couldn't, he dug around it on all four sides, thus creating this tank. Appar Swamigal sang, "Theerthame Thigazhum Poigai Thirupugalur" (Thirupugalur, the pond shining with sacred water), Sambandapperuman sang, "Poigai Soozhndha Pugalur, Pongu Than Punal Soozhndhu Pothani Pozhir Pugalur" (Pugalur surrounded by ponds, Pugalur with cool water and flower-laden groves), and Sekkizhar Peruman sang, "Poigai Soozhndha Poom Pugalur, Poovil Poli Poigai Soozh Pugalur" (Pugalur with beautiful ponds, Pugalur with beautiful ponds adorned with flowers). The Sthala Viruksham is the Punnai tree. It is located in the northern part of the inner prakaram of the temple. In the Thevara Pathigams of this Sthalam, it is mentioned as "Punnai Pozhir Pugalur" (Pugalur with Punnai groves) and "Punnai Malarthalai Vandurangu Pugalur" (Pugalur where bees sleep on Punnai flowers). The Periya Puranam states, "Punnaga Manam Kamazhum Poom Pugalur" (Beautiful Pugalur fragrant with Punnai). It was in this sacred place that the Lord, for Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, transformed bricks into precious gold. It was in this Sthalam that Thirunavukkarasu Peruman attained liberation on the Sadhayam star in the month of Chithirai. Therefore, the Appar Swamigal festival is celebrated here with great splendor, with the Sadhayam star in Chithirai as the tenth day. His entire history is celebrated during this festival. On the first day of the festival, the history of Appar curing his colic by reciting the Thirupathigam "Kootraayina Vaaru" to Veeraattaneswarar of Thiruvathigai is celebrated. On the second day, the history of Appar being placed in a lime kiln by the king's order and sitting there singing the Pathigam "Maasil Veenaiyum" is celebrated. On the third day of the festival, the history of Appar eating poisoned rice and stopping an elephant sent to kill him by singing a Pathigam is celebrated. On the fourth day of the festival, the history of Appar being tied to a stone and thrown into the sea, but reciting the Pathigam "Sotrunai Vedhiyan" and using the stone as a raft to reach the shore at Thiruppathirippuliyur is celebrated. On the fifth day, the history of Appar singing the Thirupathigam "Ponnar Thiruvadikku" at Thiruppennagadam and receiving the marks of the trident and bull on his shoulders is celebrated. On the sixth day, the history of Appar receiving the Shiva Deekshai (initiation) at Thirunallur as desired at Thiruchakthimuttram is celebrated. On the seventh day of the festival, the history of Appar reviving the son of Appoothi Adigal, who died from a snakebite at Thingalur, by singing the Pathigam "Ondru Kolam" is celebrated.

That night, the history of visiting Thiruppazhanam is celebrated. On the eighth day of the festival, the history of receiving coins to alleviate famine at Thiruvizhimizhalai is celebrated. That night, the history of opening the temple doors at Thirumaraikkadu by singing the Pathigam "Panniner Mozhiyaal" and the history of witnessing the Rishabharoodar (Lord on the bull) at Thiruvaaimoor are celebrated. On the ninth day morning, the history of praising the hidden Sivalingam at Thiruppazhaiyarai Vadathali by singing a Pathigam is celebrated. That night, the history of Appar immersing himself in a pond in Kailash and emerging from a pond in Thiruvaiaaru, thereby witnessing the Kailash vision, is celebrated. On the tenth day morning, the history of partaking of the Kattamuḍu (food offered by the Lord) given by Lord Shiva, who appeared as a Brahmin on the way to Thiruppainjeeli, is celebrated. In the evening, the history of throwing gold and precious gems, which appeared wherever he performed Uzhavara pani, into the pond as if they were mere stones, is celebrated. Subsequently, the history of the Apsaras (celestial dancers) performing dance and song before him, and him singing a Pathigam that made their minds waver, is celebrated. Finally, that night, the history of merging with the sacred feet of the Lord by singing the Pathigam "Ennukken En Solli Ennukkeno" and the Pushpa Vimana Kaatchi (vision of the floral chariot) are celebrated. Thus, the Appar Swamigal festival is celebrated here for ten days. In the month of Thai, Brahmotsavam is celebrated for the Panchamoorthigal (five deities). Commemorating the history of Pugalur Nathar appearing as Chandrasekarar and granting darshan to Agni Bhagavan, the fifth day of this Utsavam (festival) features Vasanthotsavam, the ninth day features Maha Rathotsavam (great chariot festival), and the tenth day features Bhaktha Kaatchi Utsavam (devotee darshan festival) for Chandrasekara Swamy. Other festivals that should be celebrated in Shiva temples in other months are also held without fail. Six daily pujas are performed meticulously. A Sthala Puranam (history of the place) in Tamil is not available for Thirupugalur. In the Saraswathi Mahal Library of the Thanjavur Palace, there is a palm-leaf manuscript in Sanskrit called "Saranyapura Mahatmyam." This is said to be narrated by Brahma Devar to Narada in nine chapters, from 180 to 188, in the Sthana Vaibhava Kandam of the Agneya Mahapurana. Agni Bhagavan, to dispel the blame of consuming everything, reached this Pugalur, which is Punnagavanam, dug a Theertham on all four sides, and performed penance in the center, whereupon the Lord granted him darshan. The Devas (celestial beings) resided in this Pugalur until the demons Vathapi and Vilvalan were destroyed by Agathiyar, unable to bear their cruelty, hence the name Saranyapuram. The demon Rakthan performed Shiva Puja here until he was killed by another demon, Kesi, hence the name Rakthaaranyam. It is said that Banasuran, for the sake of his mother, tried to move the Swayambhu Lingam in this place, and when he couldn't, he grieved. In response to Banasuran's request, the Swayambhu Moorthy slightly tilted his head to the north. It is also mentioned that Kashyapar, Jamadagni, Vyasar, and Bharadwajar installed and worshipped Lingams here. The Rajarishi Pravarar, having committed the sin of destroying Vishnu idols, was destined for hell. However, his descendant, Veerasenan, worshipped Punnaganathar, and as a result, Pravarar attained Swargam (heaven). The greatness of Panchaksharam, Vibhuti, and Rudraksham, and the grandeur of festivals like the chariot festival and divine weddings, are all clearly and beautifully described in this Sanskrit text, the Saranyapura Mahatmyam. Nerkundrakizhar has composed a work called "Thirupugalur Andhadhi." The works that have sung about this Thirupugalur Sthalam include three Pathigams by Sambandapperuman, five Pathigams by Appar Swamigal, and one Pathigam by Sundarar Peruman. In the 11th Thirumurai, Paranadeva Nayanar has sung about this Pugalur in his "Sivaperuman Thiruvandhadhi." Pattinathupillaiyar has sung about Pugalur in his "Thiruvekambamudaiyar Thiruvandhadhi." Nambiyandar Nambigal has also sung about it. In the Periya Puranam, Sekkizhar Peruman has sung about Thirupugalur in many verses in the histories of Muruga Nayanar, Thirunavukkarasu Peruman, Thirugnanasambandapperuman, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, and Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar. On the way from Thirupugalur to Thiruchenkattangudi, there is a temple called Ramanatheswaram. It is located near Thirukkannapuram. This temple is subordinate to the Thirupugalur temple. Ramanathi

The Thirucharam temple has two large prakarams. The main sanctum houses a large Mahalingam. It is said that Rama Peruman worshipped here. There is also a Pathigam sung by Sambandar Peruman for this Sthalam. These are all the special features of Thirupugalur, where Muruga Nayanar was born and lived. Let us continue to contemplate the Puranam of Muruga Nayanar in detail through Sekkizhar Peruman's Periya Puranam. Thirucharam.

Chapter 02 — Muruga Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 192. Mummayaal Ulagaanda Sarukkam, Muruga Nayanar Puranam. Sekkizhar Swamigal has composed and sung the Puranam of Muruga Nayanar in 14 sacred verses. Firstly, in the anthology Thiruthondath Thogai, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal sang about Muruga Nayanar with the phrase "Muruganukkum Uruthira Pasupathikkum Adiyen" (I am a devotee of Murugan and Rudra Pasupathi). When interpreting this, it should be understood as "I am a devotee of Murugan" and "I am a devotee of Rudra Pasupathi" separately. The meaning is, "I am a devotee of the Shiva devotees named Murugar." Next, in the category of works, Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi, Nambiyandar Nambigal sang about Muruga Nayanar as follows: "Pathigan thigazh tharu panchakkaram payil naavinan seer mathiyan sadaiyal kalar thottanipavan yaan magizhndhu thuthiyan kalar sanbai naatharkku thozhan van thonda nam pon athigam perum pugalur murugan enum andhanane." This is the verse about Muruga Nayanar in Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi. Now, let us contemplate the Puranam of Muruga Nayanar through Sekkizhar's Periya Puranam. The section that describes the history and character of the Nayanar named Murugar is the Muruga Nayanar Puranam. It is placed second in the order of presentation, after the Moorthi Nayanar Puranam, in the Mummayaal Ulagaanda Sarukkam. First, an explanation of the Nayanar's name: The Nayanar's sacred name is Muruga Nayanar. Murugan is the name of the ever-fragrant, eternally youthful, divine Lord with the Vel (spear). It was a good custom in our country from ancient times for people to name their children after the deities they worshipped. There is reason to believe that the ancestors of this Nayanar, having great love for Lord Muruga, gave him this name. It was also a tradition of our ancestors to name children not only after deities but also after devotees. Naming children after deities and devotees is believed to be beneficial because it causes us to frequently remember and utter those sacred names, thereby bringing good fortune. In contrast, naming children after meaningless objects like trees, stones, plants, creepers, or flowers is a new and useless custom that has emerged in recent times. Now, let us contemplate the Puranam of Muruga Nayanar through Sekkizhar Peruman's sacred verses. The sacred place where Muruga Nayanar was born is Thirupugalur. It is a sacred place in the Chola country, surrounded by groves and ponds. This Thirupugalur is the sacred abode where Lord Shiva, whose body is embraced by the Goddess with her tender hands, and who has matted locks adorned with the Ganga, lovingly resides. This Pugalur is also called Poompugalur (beautiful Pugalur). This is because Pugalur is surrounded by ponds and groves. By stating that it is surrounded by groves, it is understood that three types of flowers—Kottuppoo (tree flowers), Nila-poo (ground flowers), and Kodi-poo (creeper flowers)—among the four types of flowers, are found around this town. By stating that it is surrounded by ponds, Neer-poo (water flowers) are also indicated here. Our Muruga Nayanar performed sacred service to the Lord using these four types of flowers. Therefore, the description is structured to signify this. The special feature of this place is not only that the Lord accepts and blesses the devotees who gather and string these four types of flowers according to the rules, but also that this sacred verse contains references to the Nayanar's history, including the country and city of his birth. Furthermore, Sekkizhar Peruman has composed the Muruga Nayanar Puranam in a six-syllable Virutham (meter). In this first sacred verse, the word "soozhum" (surrounding) is used six times as a figure of speech (sol porul pinvaru nilai ani). It also implicitly suggests the special quality of Muruga Nayanar, a Brahmin who performs six duties, who arranged six types of flower garlands (six types of flowers) and offered them at all six times, according to the appropriate times. Sekkizhar Peruman calls this Pugalur "Poompugalur." Its meaning is "beautiful Pugalur." It also implies "Pugalur like a lotus." Since it is surrounded by ponds, it can also be interpreted that this Sthalam shines like another lotus blooming amidst the lotus flowers in all the ponds. Pugalur means a town where souls fearful of rebirth seek refuge in the Lord, i.e., a town where they surrender. It means a town worthy of refuge. Therefore, we saw that it also has the name Saranyapuri. Thus, in this single sacred verse, Sekkizhar Peruman has beautifully and gracefully woven together the special features of Muruga Nayanar's country, city, river, government, and deity, which is highly commendable. Sekkizhar Peruman continues to describe this Pugalur in subsequent verses. This Pugalur Sthalam is an ancient and glorious sacred place. Good Shiva devotees live here. Just as their minds shine with whiteness, so does the sacred ash on their bodies shine. That sacred ash shines so brightly that it illuminates the darkness of the night. The sacred ash shining on the bodies of devotees is sacred ash that has the power to protect those who wear it. "Manthiramavathu Neeru, Senan Tharuvathu Neeru, Ela Udambu Idar Theerkkum Inbam Tharuvathu Neeru" (Ash is the mantra, ash bestows wealth, ash cures bodily ailments and brings joy) are sacred Tamil verses about sacred ash. This is why we traditionally call it "Thiruneetru Kaappu" (protection of sacred ash). "Other protections are superfluous, they do not desire them. They established and filled their foreheads with sacred ash" is from the Puranam of Thirugnanasambandapperuman. Thus, it is our tradition to call sacred ash "Thiruneetru Kaappu" and "Ratchai" (protection) because it has the power to protect those who wear it. Vasudeva applied sacred ash to Krishna is mentioned in the Vishnu Puranam, as stated by Sivakavimani Aiyya. The flowers in the groves and ponds of Pugalur are blooming. Therefore, the humming sound of bees sitting on them is heard. Just as house doors are opened to give alms to those who sing for food, so too do the flowers open their petal-doors to the bees, which wander singing in search of nectar, and offer the honey within them to the bees. This refers to the sweet honey that the flowers yield. This sweet honey from the flowers only provides the pleasure of taste, which is a sensory pleasure. However, there is a greater honey than this. That is the honey of Thirupathigam. That is the rich honey. This rich honey, which consists of the sacred hymns composed by our Samayacharyas (spiritual teachers) in praise of the Lord, provides not only the sensory pleasure of hearing but also the pleasure of love, which is essential for the soul.

Such sacred hymns are sung daily without fail by all the devotees in Pugalur. Hearing these, the birds called Poovai, having become accustomed to listening, also sing these sacred hymns in Pugalur. Pugalur is a sacred place where a large gathering of devotees, including Thirunavukkarasu Peruman, Thirugnanasambandapperuman, Siruthondar Nayanar, and Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar, resided together for a long time in the matham of our Muruga Nayanar. Furthermore, Thirunavukkarasu Swamigal lived here alone for a long time, composed countless sacred hymns, and attained the sacred feet of the Lord here. Thirugnanasambandapperuman also composed sacred hymns about the Lord here. In those days, people must have learned and recited the Thevara Thirupathigams composed by these two great personalities. The verse "Pillaiyar amarnda thunga mamadan thannida thondar tham kuzhangal engum oothiya thirupathiga thisai edutha pongu peroli" (The great sound of the sacred hymns recited everywhere by the groups of devotees in the great matham where Pillaiyar resided) is from the Puranam of Thirugnanasambandapperuman. "Thirupathiga senthamizhin thiran potri" (Praise the power of the sacred Tamil hymns) is from the Puranam of Thirunavukkarasu Peruman. From these, we understand that the devotees in the assembly in those days learned and recited the sacred hymns. Because people learned and practiced such glorious sacred hymns, the Poovai birds also learned them. People also trained these bird species to sing the sacred hymns. It is about this special feature that Nambiyandar Nambigal sang about Muruga Nayanar in the category of works, Thiruthondar Thiru Andhadhi, as "Pathigam thigazh tharu panchakkaram payil naavinan" (He whose tongue practices the Panchaksharam, shining with hymns). Sekkizhar Peruman sings that the lotus flowers in the tanks, due to the humming of the bees surrounding them, are shedding drops of water, which is like tears welling up in the lotus-like faces of the devotees who listen to the Thevara Thirupathigams. Bees hum around the budding flowers. Knowing the blooming season, the bees sing with the desire to make them bloom and drink their honey. Since it is the blooming season, the flowers will also open their buds and bloom. As a result, fresh honey will drip there. Sekkizhar Peruman metaphorically describes this event as the bees singing, the flowers' faces blooming from that song, and shedding tears of joy. It is traditional to compare lotus flowers to faces. Therefore, when the devotees' lotus-like faces repeatedly hear the nectar of the melodic music of the Thevara Thirupathigams, love overflows within them, creating joy, and as a result of this abundance of love, tears well up in drops from their eyes. "Thadam moondrudaiyan thanai unni kan muthu arumba kalar sevadi kai thozhuvargal un muthu arumba uvagai tharuvana" is from Thiruvengadu Thevaram. "Kadhalagi kasindhu kannir malki" is from Thirugnanasambandapperuman Thevaram. Listening to the nectar of the sacred verse with overflowing ears is like consuming the nectar of that sacred verse by turning the ears into a mouth. In the previous verse, it was mentioned that the Poovai birds sang the nectar of the Thevara Pathigam music. In this verse, the state of the devotees who, upon hearing the sacred praises of the Lord in the Thevara Thiruppattukkal, are overcome with emotion, their hearts melting, and tears flowing, is described. Tears flowing upon hearing the Lord's praise is one of the characteristics of love. "Vellanthal virisadaiyai vidaiyai vinnor perumane ena kettu vetta nenjai pallan thaluru punaleer keezh melaga pathaithu urugum avar" is from Thiruvachagam. In these three sacred verses, our teacher Sekkizhar Peruman connects the historical references of Muruga Nayanar's sacred service, which is his daily ritual, with the natural setting of the Sthalam. In the first verse, the description of bees settling on flowers at the time when the darkness of the night gives way to dawn implicitly conveys to us the reason why flowers appear distinct in the twilight for Muruga Nayanar, who rises before dawn, bathes, applies sacred ash, and goes to pluck flowers, and also presents that scene. The sight of flowers in the shining grove, with bees humming around them, opening their mouths and dripping honey, and the Poovai birds singing the honey of the Pathigam, all refer to the events when Muruga Nayanar enters the grove and gathers Kottuppoo, Kodi-poo, and Nila-poo at their blooming stage. Now, in the third verse, the events of bees singing, lotuses blooming and yielding honey, and the faces of devotees blooming and shedding tears upon hearing the nectar of the sacred verse, implicitly convey the events when Muruga Nayanar plucks water flowers, his heart overflowing with love, and tears welling up when he hears the music of the Pathigam. By mentioning "devotees who heard the Poovai Pathigam," it is indicated that the devotees, while plucking flowers, were unable to perform other services of speech, such as singing. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman's divinely fragrant poetic skill lies in describing the natural scenery as if it were the history of the Nayanars, and in presenting the inner meaning as a Shiva-vision, a true essence that can be seen with the eyes. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 03 — Muruga Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 193. Mummayaal Ulagaanda Sarukkam, Muruga Nayanar Puranam. In that glorious place called Pugalur, Muruga Nayanar was born into a noble Brahmin lineage. What kind of glory does this Pugalur possess? It is a cool Pugalur, for it is the Pugalur where all souls, fearing the heat of rebirth, seek refuge. Therefore, this Pugalur has the quality of cooling and transforming the heat of rebirth for souls. The glory of this Pugalur is that all three, Thirugnanasambandapperuman (the child saint), Thirunavukkarasu Peruman (the king of saints), and Sundaramoorthy Swamigal (the beloved saint), visited it four times. This Pugalur is a sacred place where many devotees, including Thirugnanasambandapperuman, Thirunavukkarasu Peruman, Thiruneelanakkar Nayanar, and Siruthondar Nayanar, resided for a long time in Muruga Nayanar's matham, singing and praising the Lord. To demonstrate to the world the greatness of the devotion of Lord Shiva's devotees, the Lord, during the Panguni Uthiram festival, transformed bricks into precious gold for Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, who needed gold to feed the devotees. Such is the glory of this sacred place called Pugalur. In this sacred place, Muruga Nayanar was born into a Brahmin family. Sekkizhar Peruman sang, "Manamaraiyor kula marabin vandhar munthai marai mudhalvar" (He came from the lineage of esteemed Brahmins, the foremost of ancient Vedic scholars). By stating "came from the lineage of Brahmins," it is understood that he was born into a Brahmin family. By referring to him as "foremost of ancient Vedic scholars," it is conveyed that he not only had the glory of being born into that lineage but also excelled in the Vedic conduct inherent to the Brahmin lineage, thereby shining as a leader in Vedic discipline. Now, Sekkizhar Peruman sings about Muruga Nayanar, "Gnana varambin thalai nindrar" (He stood at the pinnacle of knowledge). The meaning of this is that, in addition to being skilled in the Vedas, he was a practitioner of Shivagamas (Shaiva scriptures), having attained the supreme Shiva Gnanam (divine knowledge of Shiva) mentioned in the Shivagamas, and he strictly followed the rules in those fields. Here, the service performed by the Nayanar encompasses the sacred duties of the four paths: Chariya (external service), Kriya (internal worship), Yoga (meditation), and Gnanam (divine knowledge), which is the implicit meaning contained in the following seven sacred verses. Here, "Marai" refers to the Vedas, and "Gnanam" refers to the Shivagamas. "Thalai nitral" means excelling in that discipline. Next, Sekkizhar Peruman describes Muruga Nayanar as "Nagam punaiwar sevadi keezh oonamindri nirai anbaal urugum manathar Muruganar" (Muruganar, whose heart melted with complete and faultless love for the sacred feet of Lord Shiva, who adorns Himself with serpents). The meaning of this is that Muruga Nayanar had an uninterrupted and abundant love for the sacred, reddish feet of Lord Shiva, who wears serpents as ornaments, and his heart melted with that love. What does it mean for the heart to melt with love for the Lord's feet? It means that the Lord is the greatest of all great things. Souls are small. When one contemplates the greatness of the immense grace that the Lord, who is the greatest of all great things, always bestows upon souls, disregarding their smallness, and feels affection, that softening of the heart is what is meant by the heart melting with love. "Aththa un adiyenai anbaal aarthai pizhaithanaigal aththanaiyum poruthai emperuman thirukkarunai irundhavare" is from Thiruthandagam. "Naichuvigai yetruvittha ammai" is from Thiruvachagam. "Nayanai ennai porutpaduthi nangu aruli thayanayanai arulum thambiran" is from Koyil Nanmanimalai, 11th Thirumurai, and other sacred sayings that we can contemplate here. Therefore, our Muruga Nayanar

was a highly distinguished person, excelling in the Vedas. By virtue of that excellence, he became proficient in the Shiva Agamas. Consequently, he followed the path of Gnanam. As a result, his heart melted with love for Lord Shiva's sacred feet, and he recited Lord Shiva's sacred name. Thus, understanding Shiva Puja as the ultimate truth, he adorned and worshipped Lord Shiva with flowers three times a day, delighting in His beauty. As he excelled in sacred knowledge, he also excelled in the service of devotees who sought refuge at the Lord's feet. Consequently, he attained the grace of Shiva's feet. In this verse, it is stated that these qualities and methods led to the subsequent historical events. "Vedach chirapporulal migath thelindhum sendrar Saiva thiraththadaivar. Idhil Chariya Kriya Yogam seluthiya pin Gnanaththal Sivanadiyai seravar" (Those who clearly understand the essence of the Vedas will attain the Saiva path. After practicing Chariya, Kriya, and Yoga, they will reach Shiva's feet through Gnanam) is a sacred saying from Sivagnana Siddhiyar. Our Muruga Nayanar, through the true penance of becoming a servant of Lord Shiva, performs the sacred service of plucking and offering Thirupallithamam to the Lord. Sekkizhar Peruman mentions here that the Nayanar possessed true penance, having become a servant of Lord Shiva. There are many types of penance. Among them, the penance of becoming a servant of Lord Shiva is the true penance. Thirunavukkarasu Peruman has even classified Tamil into two types: "Aaluru Tamil" (sublime Tamil) and "Aalura Tamil" (inferior Tamil). "Aaluru Tamil" refers to Tamil songs that implicitly contain Lord Shiva, which elevate souls and lead them to salvation. Therefore, Thirunavukkarasu Peruman named such Tamil "Aaluru Tamil." "Aalura Tamil" refers to Tamil songs that do not elevate souls and and cause harm to them, hence such Tamil songs are referred to as "Aalura Tamil" by Thirunavukkarasu Peruman. It is noteworthy that in Tamil literature, besides Saivism, there are also Jain and Buddhist literatures. Therefore, Thirunavukkarasu Peruman refers to the literatures of external religions that show false paths instead of the path of penance, which is the path of Shiva, and Tamil literatures that do not implicitly contain Lord Shiva, as "Aalura Tamil." Therefore, Muruga Nayanar, who possessed the true penance of being a servant only to Lord Shiva, performs the sacred service of offering Thirupallithamam to the Lord. Even for performing Chariya and Kriya, such as offering Thirupallithamam, one must have performed penance in the past. Did we not see in the Puranam of Kannappa Nayanar, "Munbu sey thavathin eettam" (the accumulation of past penance)? Lord Shiva is the goal of all types of penance. But all those mature and yield results over time. The penance performed as a servant of Shiva is true penance, which yields results in this life and leads one to the sacred feet of the Lord. It is a Saiva tradition to refer to flowers, leaves, roots, etc., used for the Lord's worship as Thirupallithamam. Therefore, Muruga Nayanar is one who performs the sacred service of plucking and offering such Thirupallithamams. We have seen in detail the methods of collecting Thirupallithamams in the Puranam of Eripatha Nayanar, where Sivagami Andar collected Thirupallithamams. We have seen all those methods mentioned in the book "Pushpa Pasanam Pushpa Vidhiyum," which is based on the Shivagamas. We should contemplate and remember them here. Similarly, Muruga Nayanar here wakes up before dawn, bathes in holy water, and goes to the Nandavanam (flower garden). There, the flowers are in their blooming stage. All those flowers rejoice, thinking that they have been blessed to adorn the sacred body of Lord Shiva.

Muruga Nayanar collects such flowers, which are smiling in their blooming stage as a symbol, and arranges them in the sacred flower baskets he brought. Various types of flowers should be collected and arranged in separate baskets at the time of plucking, based on their species and qualities such as hardness and softness. If not done so, if flowers are not plucked and arranged separately according to their nature, there will be a delay when stringing them later, and damage may also occur. Therefore, according to this rule, Muruga Nayanar collects flowers in different ways, understanding their maturity and nature, in sacred flower baskets. Our ancestors divided flowers into four types: Kottuppoo (tree flowers), Nila-poo (ground flowers), Kodi-poo (creeper flowers), and Neer-poo (water flowers). Among these, the Pushpa Vidhis (rules for flowers) prescribe which flowers are suitable for offering to which deities. Muruga Nayanar plucked and brought those flowers that were suitable for offering to Lord Shiva according to these rules. When selecting flowers, one should consider their species, blooming time, plucking time, and day. Furthermore, one should select flowers that are free from worm bites, damage, etc. The time for plucking flowers is early morning. Therefore, it is not possible to see them completely with the eyes. This can only be done through long practice of plucking with the hands. We saw four types of flowers, and among them, Kottumalar, or Kottuppoo, comes first. "Kodu" means branch. Therefore, our ancestors classified flowers that bloom directly on tree branches as Kottuppoo. In the book "Pushpa Vidhi" composed by Thiruvarur Gnanaprakasa Pattaragar, these types are sung about. According to it, Kottuppoo are as follows: Vanni, Pala, Elumichai, Naraththai, Kongu, Mandharai, Oomaththai, Mavilangai, Nochi, Agil, Sandhanam, Mathulai, Ma, Asogu, Pathiri, Vellerukku, Ilanthai, Palasu, Naravam, Punnai, Vila, Maruthu, Konrai, Nelli, Kura, Serunthi, Kiluvai, Kurunthu, Vilvam, Alari, Vetci, Pavalamalligai, Sanbagam, Nyazhal. These all belong to the Kottuppoo category. Next is a type called Nila-poo. These are flowers that bloom on plants. Flowers that bloom on plants on the ground belong to the Nila-poo or Nilamalar category. These Nila-poo types are as follows: Patti, Nayuruvi, Poolai, Pachchai, Sevvanthi, Thumbai, Vettiver, Marukuzhundhu, Karanthai, Vishnukanthi, Thulasi, Sengkirei, Maththai, Semparaththai, Vangam, Kokkiragu, Anicham, Valampuri. These all belong to the Nila-poo, or plant, category. Now, thirdly, the Kodi-poo type. These are flowers that bloom on creepers. The Kodi-poo types are as follows: Kanthal, Kaattumalligai, Mullai, Mavval, Vetrilai, Karumugai, Karungaa Konrai, Venkaa Konrai, Kurukkaththi, Iruvaatchi, Kogudi, Picchi. These all belong to the Kodi-poo, or creeper, category. Next is the Neer-poo type. This is the fourth type. These are flowers that bloom in water. These are as follows: Lotus and lotus-type flowers, Neythal, Neerotpalalm, etc., all flowers that bloom in water bodies in the Neythal land. Thus, Muruga Nayanar collects these four types of flowers—Kottuppoo, Kodi-poo, Nila-poo, and Neer-poo—according to the rules, for offering Thirupallithamam to the Lord. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 04 — Muruga Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 194. Mummayaal Ulagaanda Sarukkam, Muruga Nayanar Puranam. We saw that Muruga Nayanar collected the four types of flowers—Kottuppoo, Kodi-poo, Nila-poo, and Neer-poo—which are Thirupallithamams suitable for offering to the Lord, and arranged them separately in their respective baskets. He then brought all these collected flowers to the Thiruppoo Mandapam (sacred flower pavilion). The Thiruppoo Mandapam is a clean, cool, and secluded place specifically designed for stringing garlands. This is because the place where flowers are strung must be free from impurities, protected from sun and wind, and kept cool. Therefore, it is our tradition to construct separate Thiruppoo Mandapams for stringing flowers. Using the four types of flowers he brought, he strung them in six different ways. The method of stringing flowers in various ways is found in our Tamil tradition. However, in our Saiva tradition, six methods are considered important for offering to the Lord. These six types are as follows: Kovai, Indai, Kanni, Pinaiyal, Maalai, Thodaiyal. These are the six types. First, Kovai. It is called Kovai because flowers are strung together. It is also a long arrangement. Next, the second type is Indai. This is a circular arrangement. Garlands strung in a circle, mostly for wearing on the head, are called Indai. Next, the third is Kanni. This is a garland arranged for adorning the hair bun. Kanni is a hair garland made by arranging flowers along a large fiber and tying them in a curve with a small thread. It is specifically called Kanni. Next, a type called Maalai. If flowers are strung very long and joined together, it is called Maalai. Next, the fifth type is Pinaiyal. A garland made by placing the stems of two flowers opposite each other, one on top of the other, and tying them, is called Pinaiyal. Next, Thodaiyal. That which is strung is called Thodaiyal. Thus, stringing garlands in these six ways is the Saiva tradition. Our Muruga Nayanar, born into a Brahmin lineage, collected the four types of flowers—Kottuppoo, Kodi-poo, Nila-poo, and Neer-poo—and strung them into six types of garlands, performing the service of Thirupallithamam. Muruga Nayanar came from the lineage of Brahmins who recited the Vedas. Therefore, the four types of flowers he collected are likened to the four Mahavakyas (great sayings) mentioned in the four Vedas: "Prajnanam Brahma," "Aham Brahmasmi," "Tat Tvam Asi," and "Ayam Atma Brahma." He chose these four Mahavakyas as the four types of flowers. Similarly, the meanings of these Vedas can be understood through the six Angas (limbs). The six Angas, called Shad Angas of the Vedas, explain the meaning of the Vedas. Therefore, it is implied that he transformed these four types of flowers into six types of garlands, representing the six Angas, and offered them to the Lord during the six periods of Shadathva Sodhanai (purification of the six paths). The six periods are Ushathkalam (pre-dawn), Udhayakalam (dawn), Uchchikalam (noon), Saayaratchai (dusk), Maalai Sandhi (evening), and Arthajamam (midnight). Among these, Ushathkalam is the period when souls are brought out of darkness by Tirodhana (concealment). Kaalai Sandhi is the period when souls are shown the two types of actions (good and bad) and are involved in them. Uchchikalam is the period when souls experience those two types of actions. Pradosham, or Saayaratchai, is the period when unexperienced actions are stored, calculating the abundance of experiences. Saayankalam, or Maalai Sandhi, is the period when those stored actions are removed. Arthajamam is the period when one is under His control and inactive. Thus, Alala Sundaram Pillai offers a special commentary here, stating that Muruga Nayanar, who came from a Brahmin family, chose the four Mahavakyas mentioned in the Vedas as the four types of flowers, and the six types of garlands represent the six Angas, and the six periods correspond to the six periods of Shadathva Sodhanai. Sivakavimani Aiyya cites this. Thus, Muruga Nayanar would bring these Thirupallithamams, string them appropriately, carry the flowers, go to the temple, and offer them to the Lord. We can recall here the sacred sayings "Pothodu neer sumanthu ethi puguvargal" (They enter, carrying flowers and water, praising) and "Poovodu neer sumakkum nin adiyar" (Your devotees who carry flowers and water). Muruga Nayanar offers all these flowers with love. It is a rule that actions such as plucking flowers, stringing them, and offering them should all be done with a heart full of love. Muruganar, with love, offered garlands to the Lord, delighted, understood His will, and melted with emotion. Thirugnanasambandapperuman has praised Muruga Nayanar's sacred service in the Pugalur Thevaram: "Thondar than kayam moozhgi thunaiyalum santhamum pugaiyum kondu kondadi paravi kuripari Murugan sey kolam kandu kandu kan kulira kali parandhu oli malku kallar vandu pan seyyum pugalur varthamanichcharaththare" (The devotees, bathing in the cool tank, carrying garlands, sandalwood paste, and incense, praising and worshipping, seeing the form made by Murugan who understands the Lord's will, their eyes cool, their joy overflowing, their light abundant, the bees humming in Pugalur, the Lord of Varthamanichcharam). Furthermore, Thirugnanasambandapperuman has sung about Muruga Nayanar's sacred service and Muruga Nayanar in the Thevarams as "Pann vannaththaragi padalodu adal aratha vinn vannaththaraya viri pugaloorar" (The people of vast Pugalur, who are of the color of music, never ceasing their songs and dances, like celestial beings), and "Moosu vandarai konrai Murugan muppodhum sey mudimel vasama malarudaiyar varthamanichcharaththare" (Murugan, whose fragrant flowers are on the head of the Lord of Varthamanichcharam, worshipped by humming bees three times a day), and "Thensol vinjamar vadasol thisai mozhi ezhinarambaduththu thunju nenjiru neenga thozhuthu ezhu thol pugaloorar" (The people of ancient Pugalur, who rise and worship to dispel the darkness of their sleeping hearts, adorned with the beautiful languages of Tamil and Sanskrit). Here, Sekkizhar Peruman mentions that he offered flowers with love and performed archana appropriately. He states that he offered flowers and performed archana. Therefore, this indicates that Muruga Nayanar performed various types of worship, including bathing, applying sandalwood paste and other sacred unguents, adorning with flowers, offering lamps and incense, praising, and worshipping with flowers, in addition to offering flowers. This word "archana" indicates this. These too are to be performed differently at different times. It is also implied here that they were performed according to the rules. We can recall here the third sacred verse of the Pugalur Varthamanichcharam Thevaram we saw above. Muruganar worshipped the Lord of Varthamanichcharam as his Atmaartha Moorthy (personal deity). Therefore, he performed this sacred service with a heart melting with love, in the prescribed manner, with all the components of Shiva Puja. Furthermore, Sekkizhar Peruman sings about Muruga Nayanar: "Paramar pathigappattrana ongi sirandha anjezhuththu meva navin unarvinar" (He whose tongue possessed the understanding of the exalted and excellent five-letter mantra, which is the essence of Lord Shiva's sacred hymns). The meaning of this is that Muruga Nayanar possessed the understanding of the exalted and excellent sacred five-letter mantra, which is the essence of Lord Shiva's sacred hymns, and he continuously recited it. The Sri Panchaksharam (sacred five-letter mantra) is the inner meaning of the four Vedas. Similarly, the Sri Panchaksharam, the sacred five-letter mantra, is also the inner meaning of the Thevara Thirupathigam, which is the Tamil Veda. Muruga Nayanar always practiced this sacred five-letter mantra both by uttering it with his tongue and by contemplating it in his mind. Muruga Nayanar, who excelled in Vedic discipline and Shivagama principles, having understood and performed the ethical conduct explained by the Dharma Shastras and wise men, attained the honor of being a friend of Thirugnanasambandapperuman. Thirugnanasambandapperuman is the great one who partook of the milk from the Universal Mother's breast, mixed with the Shiva Gnanam (divine knowledge of Shiva) that was first expounded in the clear Vedas, scooped up in a golden bowl, and who protects all worlds. Muruga Nayanar had the honor of being a friend to such a great personality. Such a Muruga Nayanar worshipped Lord Shiva in the Varthamaneswaram temple, which is located in a separate shrine to the northeast, next to the main temple of the Lord in Thirupugalur, as his Atmaartha Moorthy. Here, an idol of Muruga Nayanar, in the posture of performing the sacred service of carrying a flower basket and garland, has been installed in the sanctum. Thus, he performs puja daily without fail. He practices the five-letter mantra. He practices puja daily. He bows down. He praises. Similarly, he practices the sacred five-letter mantra. He bows down. He praises. Practicing the sacred five-letter mantra is an act of the mind. Praising the sacred five-letter mantra is an act of speech. Bowing down to the sacred five-letter mantra is an act of the body. Thus, he practices, praises, and bows down to the sacred five-letter mantra with his mind, speech, and body. Such was Muruga Nayanar, who, by the merit of the puja he had performed earlier, entered the divinely auspicious wedding of Thirugnanasambandapperuman, the child saint born in Sirkazhi, and attained the eternal state of dwelling in the shade of the Lord's sacred feet, which is the supreme grace of Lord Shiva. Muruga Nayanar joins the wedding of Thirugnanasambandapperuman. The reason for his participation in Thirugnanasambandapperuman's wedding was Shiva Puja. Only those who had performed Shiva Puja, which is penance, participated in that wedding. This is because all those who participated in that wedding merged with the Shiva Jyothi (divine light of Shiva) that appeared there and attained liberation. The conclusion of the Gnana Shastras is that only those who perform penance will attain Shiva's feet. Therefore, our Muruga Nayanar also participated in Thirugnanasambandapperuman's wedding due to the puja he had performed earlier. By participating in that wedding, he merged with the Shiva Jyothi that appeared there and attained liberation. That liberation is Paramukti (supreme liberation), which is eternal union with the Lord. The truth that one attains the sacred feet through Gnanam is indicated here in Muruga Nayanar's history, where the Nayanar attained those sacred feet with the help of Gnanasambandar. The implicit meaning here is that Muruga Nayanar, having performed Chariya (external service) by plucking and offering flowers with delight, Kriya (internal worship) by worshipping and performing archana on the Sivalingam with sandalwood paste, incense, etc., and Yoga (meditation) by practicing the five-letter mantra as internal worship, entered into Gnanam (divine knowledge) as a result of these. According to Saiva Siddhanta, a soul associated with this birth, realizing the higher association it needs to attain, and living a life that destroys its worldly attachments through Shiva Gnanam (divine knowledge of Shiva), attains the permanent association of Shiva's feet. Muruga Nayanar attained such a permanent association. This history is explained in the 1251st verse of Thirugnanasambanda Nayanar Puranam, which begins with "Seerperugu Neelanakkar Thirumurugar mudhal thondar." Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman, praising the greatness of the true sacred service of Pugalur Muruga Nayanar, who worshipped Lord Shiva and thereby attained His sacred feet, now states that he will proceed to narrate the glory of Rudra Pasupathi Nayanar, who worshipped Shiva by contemplating Him in his mind and praising Him with Sri Rudram, and thereby concludes the sacred Puranam of Muruga Nayanar and begins the Puranam of Rudra Pasupathi Nayanar. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.
