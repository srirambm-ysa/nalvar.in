Chapter 01 — Muzhu Neeru Poosiya Munivar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam Part 1005, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Paththarai Paniwaar Sarukkam. Muzhu Neeru Poosiya Munivar Puranam. The next devotees among the Thogaiyadiyargal are the Muzhu Neeru Poosiya Munivargal. Muzhu Neeru refers to the sacred ash (Thiruneeru) prescribed in the Sivagamas. Those who prepare it according to the Sivagamas and apply it according to the rules mentioned in the Sivagamas are called Muzhu Neeru Poosiya Munivargal. Vibhuti is one of the most important Saivite symbols. It is also called Saiva Bhushanam. Vibhuti and Rudraksha are known as Saiva Bhushanam and Saiva symbols. Both of these originated from the sacred body of the Lord. Those who wear both of these are the sacred devotees who bear the Saivite form. The greatness of Vibhuti is immense. The compassionate nature that Lord Shiva, the Supreme Being, has for souls is called Parasakti. Shiva, being under the sway of this compassionate power, is called Pathi. That Pathi performs the five functions for the sake of souls. When He took the form of Pathi, the ash that naturally bloomed on that sacred body is called Vibhuti, and the beads that originated from the eyes of that sacred body are called Rudraksha. Innumerable texts, starting from the Vedas and Agamas, expound the greatness of these two. Vibhuti and Rudraksha are Shiva-related symbols. Since both originated from the Lord, they are said to be pure, supreme, inner spiritual tools and forms of Shiva's power, imbued with divine grace. Since they serve as ornaments, they are also called Saiva Bhushanam. Just as a chaste woman cherishes the sacred thirumangalyam given by her husband with utmost importance, Saivite devotees wear the Shiva symbols of Vibhuti and Rudraksha. A chaste woman, even if she has to discard all other ornaments, will not agree to discard the thirumangalyam. Similarly, even if one does not wear any other ornaments, Saivite devotees consider it essential to lovingly wear only the sacred ash and Rudraksha, which are Shiva symbols given by Lord Shiva and are called Saiva Bhushanam, without discarding them. Therefore, Vibhuti and Rudraksha are shown as supreme spiritual tools. Wearing Vibhuti and Rudraksha is considered a sacred form for all devotees of Shiva. Vibhuti has names such as Vibhuti, Bhasitam, Bhasmam, Ksharam, Rakshai. Vibhuti means supreme wealth. Bhuti means wealth. Vibhuti means supreme wealth. Therefore, it is called Vibhuti because it bestows upon those who wear it the supreme wealth of the imperishable kingdom of liberation, which is eternal. The wealth mentioned here is the supreme liberation that the mind and speech cannot reach. Just as Vinayakan means one who has no lord above him, the term Vibhuti also means that there is no wealth superior to it. Furthermore, another meaning for this is that 'Vi' means Shiva, 'Bhu' means contemplation, and 'Ti' means attainment, thus signifying its nature to illuminate the contemplation of Shivathvam. All these are emphasized in Agamas, including the Vathula Agama. Vibhuti also has the name Bhuti. It is also called Bhasmam. It is called Neeru or Bhasmam because it incinerates all the sins of souls. It is called Bhasitam because it removes ignorance and bestows Shiva Gnanam, which is true knowledge. It is called Ksharam because it washes away the bonds of Anava and other impurities. It is called Rakshai or Kaappu because it protects from troubles caused by ghosts, spirits, demons, witchcraft, and sorcery. Vibhuti is Veda-prescribed. Among the 108 Upanishads that explain the essence of the Gnana Kanda of the Vedas, 15 Upanishads specifically praise Vibhuti. Among them, Bhasma Jabalam, Kalagni Rudram, and Jabali Upanishads explain the glory of Bhasma with its rules of application. The Brihat Jabalam not only elaborates on the proper method of wearing sacred ash as an integral part of Brahma Vidya but also describes the method of preparing sacred ash according to Vedic injunctions. The tradition of wearing Vibhuti has long existed in the practices of Vedic followers through this. In this Upanishad, wearing sacred ash is established as an important part of all karmas and as a part of spiritual knowledge. The glory of this sacred ash is illuminated in the Isha Vasya and Brihadaranyaka Upanishads, which are the primary and concluding Upanishads among the Dasa Upanishads, the essence of the 108 Upanishads, and also in the Taittiriya Upanishad. Not only that, but the greatness of sacred ash is extensively described in the 28 Sivagamas, from Kamika to Vathula, and their sub-agamas, in Smritis like Parasara, Gautama, and Manu, in Puranas like Saivam, Kurmam, Lingam, and Skandam, and in epics like Bharatam, Ramayanam, and Sivaragasyam. Furthermore, Thirugnanasambandamoorthy Swamigal, one of the great Acharyas, who partook of the divine milk of Ambikai's grace, recited a Thiruneetru Pathigam (hymn on sacred ash) in his Tamil Veda, performed miraculous deeds, and demonstrated its glory in such a way that even followers of other religions understood it. We have extensively contemplated this in the Puranam of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman. The primary and secondary texts that speak generally and specifically about sacred ash, which shines as both Vedic and a Shiva symbol, are countless. Therefore, it is clearly understood, like a mountain of silver, that this is Vedic. That sacred ash is Vedic is a supreme doctrine that cannot be denied even by great deities like Mal and Ayan. Vibhuti Dharanam is a daily ritual. One should not consume food or water, or perform any other karma, without wearing Vibhuti. He who performs karma without wearing Bhasma does not attain liberation from bondage. Bhasma is the supreme virility of Shiva Murthy, who is Agni. Therefore, one who is adorned with Bhasma is always virile. The sin committed by one adorned with Bhasma is consumed by the fire of Bhasma. The Brihat Jabala Upanishad and the Taittiriya Upanishad emphasize and guide us on the right path by stating: "Burn the forehead that is without sacred ash, burn the village that is without a Shiva temple, set fire to the life that is without Shiva worship, and destroy the knowledge that is without Shiva's refuge." Avvai Pirattiyar, the mother of wisdom, also said, "A forehead without ash is barren," when she came to guide people on the right path. The proverb, "Who was ruined by wearing sacred ash? Who was saved by forgetting Shiva?" shines forth, indicating that no one was ruined by wearing Vibhuti. Furthermore, if the Karma Kandas and Gnana Kandas declare that one who desires liberation should embrace this to cut off their birth, and that Vibhuti is the cause of the destruction of sins and the supreme kingdom of liberation, then it is clear, like a nail driven into a green tree, that there is no other superior daily ritual. This Bhasma Dharanam is the external observance of the Vedic Pashupata Vrata. Therefore, it is determined that everyone should wear white ash, and that all benefits will accrue from it. Moreover, if we, as souls, wish to remove the impurities that bind us, it is essential to burn and wear the dung of a cow, in which all the gods reside in each limb. We are called "pasu" (bound souls) because we are bound by pasam (bondage). Just like us, there is an animal called "pasu" among other creatures. It is called "pasu" because it is tied by cowherds. As a symbol of burning our impurities and attaining Shivam, it has been ordained by Shiva that we should burn the dung of that cow and wear it on our bodies to perform Shiva Aradhana. Therefore, this sacred ash daily reminds us of the truth that we are bound by impurities, and that to remove and burn those impurities, we must perform Shiva Aradhana. This tradition of wearing sacred ash is not only prevalent in the Saivite religion but is also worshipped by other religions, spreading throughout the world and shining ever brighter. Nammazhvar, the Prabandha Janakoodasthas who were blessed with wisdom free from delusion, stated the glory of Vibhuti in his Thiruvaimozhi, saying, "Kavalakkaliru Attapiran Thirunamaththal Thavalappodi Kondu Neerittidum Enru Aniyume," (The Lord who slew the elephant demon, wears white ash by His sacred name) and "Neeru Sevve Idakkaannin Nedumal Adiyar Enrodum" (If one sees sacred ash properly applied, they will run to the devotees of Nedumal). The Agamas describe how to wear Vibhuti. Vibhuti should be worn as Uththoolanam and Thripundram. Uththoolanam means taking it as powder and spreading it on the forehead. Thripundram means applying it in three lines. The places where Thripundram can be worn are generally said to be 16: head, forehead, chest, navel, two knees, two shoulders, two elbows, two wrists, two sides, back, and neck. However, there is also a method of omitting the two sides and including the two ears. It is also common to omit the elbows and wrists and consider 12 places. The reason for wearing Vibhuti in these 16 places is that the four paths of Chariyai, Kiriyai, Yogam, and Gnanam each have four types, making 16 in total. Therefore, if one wears it in those respective places, they will attain the supreme wealth of liberation. Additionally, the states of Jagrat, Svapnam, Sushupti, and Turiyam are also 16 in number (four times four). Therefore, the meaning of this is that Vibhuti is also important for transcending these states. There are also those who wear Vibhuti in 12 places. The significance of this is that by applying three lines in 12 places, it becomes 36. Therefore, the meaning is that by dissolving the 36 Tattvas (Atma Tattvam, Vidya Tattvam, Shiva Tattvam) with "Nethi Nethi" (not this, not this) and wearing it, one attains liberation. When applying Thripundram, it should be applied on the forehead up to the eyebrows. On the chest and shoulders, it should be six inches long. In other places, it should be one inch long. At no time should one line touch another. The gap between the three lines should be one inch. Everyone who has received Shiva Diksha should apply it with water only three times a day: morning, noon, and evening. The Saiva Nithya Anushtanam describes the mantras to be chanted while applying it. At other times, it should be applied as dry powder (Uththoolanam) without water. Men and women who have not received Shiva Diksha, Vaidika Sanyasis, Brahmacharis, Vanaprasthas, and unmarried women must necessarily wear sacred ash. The white ash, Vibhuti, which bestows all wealth, should be worn. Furthermore, Vibhuti given by those who have not received Shiva Diksha, Vibhuti bought for money, Vibhuti received with one hand, and Vibhuti given by those who consume alcohol and meat should never be worn. Vibhuti should be kept in a silk pouch or a small box. Vibhuti should be applied while facing north or east, using the middle three fingers of the right hand. It should not be applied with one finger or one hand. When applying it on the forehead, one should slightly tilt the head upwards to prevent it from falling to the ground, meditate on Lord Shiva and the Guru, and chant "Shiva Shiva" while applying it. One should never apply it while shaking the head, bowing down, looking up, or with an open mouth. If, by chance, Vibhuti falls to the ground, it should be removed, and the spot should be purified. It is especially important to apply Vibhuti before going to sleep, after waking up, after defecation and purification, after cleaning the teeth, at sunrise and sunset, immediately after bathing, before eating, and after eating. If a Guru or a Shiva devotee offers Vibhuti, one should prostrate before them, extend both hands to receive it, and apply it with devotion. After applying it, one should again prostrate before them. If one has to apply Vibhuti in front of the Lord, the Guru, or Shiva devotees, one should turn their face away and apply it. One should always leave the house after wearing Vibhuti. The face of those who do not wear Vibhuti is equal to a cremation ground. The elders have said, "The mind of those who do not wear ash is a cremation ground." Vibhuti is not only an item worn on the body but also possesses the excellence of being consumed as medicine. If consumed with faith and meditating on Lord Shiva, any incurable disease will be cured. Therefore, Ayurveda also praises sacred ash as a wonderful medicine to be applied and consumed. The Shankara Samhita proclaims that by consuming Vibhuti, all sins are destroyed, and the Anava Malam, which is the root of all evil deeds, is also eradicated. Those who consume it are said to have consumed the nectar of immortality. Let us continue to contemplate the glory of Vibhuti. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 02 — Muzhu Neeru Poosiya Munivar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam Part 1006, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Paththarai Paniwaar Sarukkam. Muzhu Neeru Poosiya Munivar Puranam. We are contemplating the glory of Vibhuti. Vibhuti is an eternal substance. Vibhuti is of two types: Guru Bhasma and Laghu Bhasma. Among these, Guru Bhasma is Lord Shiva Himself. Laghu Bhasma is the sacred ash that bloomed on His divine body. Tamil Veda praises Him as "Neeru Pootha Meniyane" (He whose body is covered with ash). "Neeru Pootha Neruppu" (fire covered with ash) is a common proverb in the world. Lord Shiva is in the form of fire, has fiery eyes, bears fire, and is the Lord of fire. Therefore, the Vedas proclaim Him as Agni. Moreover, the Vedas themselves praise Lord Subramanya, the younger son, as Agni Bhugu. Just as Lord Shiva, who is in the form of fire, is an eternal substance, so too is the sacred white ash that bloomed on His divine body an eternal substance that never perishes. There is nothing in this world that cannot be reduced to ash. As it is said, "Sarvam Anthamavathu Neeru" (everything ends in ash), everything must eventually become ash. No matter how many times Bhasma is purified, its quantity will not decrease, its color will not change, and it will remain imperishable. Then, need we speak of the sacred ash that bloomed on the divine body of Lord Shiva? The saying is, "Sunnaththil Irukkirathu Sootchumam" (the subtlety lies in the ash). Sunnam is ash. To convey this truth to the world, the Dravida Veda speaks of "Sathiyamavathu Neeru, Thathuvamavathu Neeru" (Truth is ash, principle is ash). Furthermore, the Kurma Purana states that when Brahma Devar was creating, he created the forehead crosswise specifically for wearing the eternal Bhasma Mahatmyam, which he described. That is why he did not create the forehead upwards or round. Even though Brahma created it that way, some ignorant people do not wear Thirupundram on their foreheads, as the good counsel states. Therefore, the Kurma Purana teaches that Brahma created the forehead for the purpose of wearing sacred ash. The Mahabharata, in the Shanti Parva, states that those who desire longevity, wealth, and liberation, these twice-born (Dvijas), should wear Bhasma daily. The characteristics of Vibhuti are described in the Sivagamas. Vibhuti is the sacred ash obtained by taking cow dung of good quality and characteristics, as prescribed, from a Yaga Agni or Shiva Agni, rolling it with mantras, and burning it. This is of two types: Vaidika Vibhuti and Saiva Vibhuti. Among these, Vaidika Vibhuti is the ash obtained from Yagas performed according to Vedic injunctions. This bestows wisdom. Saiva Vibhuti is the ash obtained from fire consecrated according to Sivagama rules, after Shiva Diksha. This bestows both wisdom and liberation. This Vibhuti is of three types: Karpam, Anu Karpam, and Upakarpam. Karpam refers to the excellent cows that originated from the Thirupparkadal (Ocean of Milk) by Shiva's command, through the five cows Nanthai, Pathirai, Surabhi, Susilai, and Sumanai, who reside with Rishabha Devar on Sri Kailasam, and spread throughout this world. From the dung of such cows, which are excellent in characteristics, excluding those that have given birth within ten days, barren heifers, diseased cows, cows whose calves have died, old cows, sterile cows, cows that eat dung, pregnant cows, and those with broken tails, ears, or horns, Vibhuti should be prepared. The dung of cows that are excellent in characteristics, without the aforementioned defects, and that have grazed on paddy straw (pasaanam) in the month of Panguni, should be collected on a lotus leaf on the Tithis of Ashtami, Amavasya, Pournami, and Chaturdasi, while chanting the Sadyojata Mantra. The upper impurities should be removed, Panchagavyam should be added while chanting the Vamadeva Mantra, it should be kneaded with the Aghora Mantra, and then rolled into balls with the Tatpurusha Mantra. These balls, either wet or dry, should be placed in the Homa Agni with paddy husks and burnt with the Ishana Mantra. The resulting ash should be filtered through a new cloth, filled into a new vessel, and placed on the ground while chanting the Vibhuti Gayatri Mantra. Flowers should be offered, and the mouth of the new vessel should be tied with a white cloth and safely stored. This is the method of collecting Vibhuti called Karpam. Now, Anu Karpam is the second type. In the month of Chithirai, dried cow dung found in the forest is collected, powdered, mixed with cow urine, kneaded with the aforementioned mantras, rolled into balls, and then produced in the Shiva Agni according to the method described for Karpam Vibhuti. This is Anu Karpam. Now, Upakarpam is the Vibhuti produced by taking ash from forest fires, mixing it with Panchagavyam, kneading it with the aforementioned mantras, rolling it into balls, and then producing it in the Shiva Agni. Those who, without understanding the greatness of such a glorious Vibhuti, shamelessly condemned it as mere cow dung ash, are ignorant and have committed sins. The glory of wearing Vibhuti is immeasurable. Just as it is impossible to understand Lord Shiva, the beginningless and endless Supreme Being, without His grace, no matter how long one tries, similarly, the four Vedas declare that the nature of the milk-white sacred ash, which bestows Dharma, Artha, Kama, and Moksha, is immeasurable. Who else but those steadfast in Shiva-Gnanam can describe its glory? Sacred ash is essential even for Avadhutas who have transcended Varnasrama. If the Vedas decree that it cannot be rejected even by them, then can those in other ashramas like Brahmacharya, Grihastha, Vanaprastha, and Sanyasa reject sacred ash even for a moment? Can they remain without wearing it? The 26th Upanishad, Brihat Jabalam, in its sixth Brahmana, states that Narayana and other deities, by wearing Vibhuti on their bodies and consuming it like a mantra, overcame their delusion and attained great effulgence. What does this mean? It means that during the marriage of Sage Gautama, all the deities were afflicted with lust upon seeing Ahalya. Being thus afflicted, they lost their wisdom and approached Sage Durvasa, praying to him to remove their दोष (dosha - fault/sin). Immediately, Durvasa said, "I have previously given Bhasma consecrated with the Satarudriya Mantra to many and removed sins like Brahmahatya." Saying this, he consecrated Bhasma and gave it to them. They, by that Bhasma, were immediately freed from their fault and regained their former effulgence and wisdom. Having removed their sins by smearing their entire bodies with Bhasma consecrated with the Satarudriya Mantra, they informed the sage, "We are freed from our fault. The power of Bhasma is astonishing." Again, Durvasa told those deities, "Listen to another miracle of this Bhasma. This is what bestowed knowledge upon Vishnu and Gunarudra. It bestows great wealth." Lord Shiva, taking Bhasma from His chest with His nail, consecrated it with Pranava, Gayatri, and Panchakshara, and applied it on Vishnu's head and other limbs, saying, "From now on, meditate on Ishvara in your heart." Vishnu meditated and exclaimed with joy, "I have seen Shiva! I have seen Shiva!" Then Lord Shiva commanded him to place Bhasma on his tongue. Vishnu first smeared his entire body with Bhasma and then consumed it. Upon consuming it, Vishnu attained an astonishing radiance, like a reflection of Paramashiva, and became pure white like a pearl. After attaining this, he called out to Lord Shiva, "Oh Vibhu! By the knowledge of Bhasma, Your glory cannot be described. Salutations to You, salutations to You. I have taken refuge in You. O Shambhu! May I always have devotion for Your lotus feet. May my devotee be adorned with Bhasma." If the Vedas themselves declare this, then the nature of sacred ash cannot be measured or conceived. Once upon a time, when this great earth was submerged in the deluge, Brahma and other great deities residing in the lotus flower also dissolved. At that time, in that terrible deluge, Lord Vishnu, who possesses an unattainable power, reclined in a deep sleep on a banyan leaf. Then, Lord Shiva, adorned with the crescent moon, appeared before him and stood in such a way that the terrible deluge spread to the measure of His sacred foot, astonishing Vishnu. Seeing Him, Lord Vishnu, who was blue-hued, directly beheld Lord Shiva but, due to his ignorance, asked, "Who are you?" Seeing this, Lord Shiva, the ocean of supreme compassion, realized that Vishnu was deluded by Maya within him. He then gave the pure sacred ash to Vishnu and removed the Maya from Vishnu's mind. The Maha Skandam states that Vishnu, freed from Maya, gained wisdom, worshipped the lotus feet of Lord Shiva, and received the boons he desired. Since Shiva symbols are called Shiva, those who condemn them are condemning Lord Shiva. There is no atonement for those who condemn Shiva symbols, which can remove all great sins like Brahmahatya, Gōhatya, Śiśuhatya, and Strīhatya. The Jabala Upanishad states that those who do not have faith in the Shiva symbols of Vibhuti and Rudraksha do not have the right to perform Vedic rituals like Garbhadhana. Among Mani (gems), Mantra (incantations), and Aushadham (medicine), sacred ash is the Aushadham, and therefore it simultaneously cuts off the diseases of the soul, the body, and the cycle of rebirth. When Uma Devi, the embodiment of divine grace, appeared as His consort, Lord Shiva, the Vedic scholar who removes the Anava Malam (impurity of ego) of souls, performed a Yaga with the great deluge as water, innumerable universes as mandapams, the earth surrounded by oceans as the altar, Hari, Brahma, Indra, and other deities as the Havis (offerings), and other living beings as the Pasus (sacrificial animals). He kindled the fire from His third eye, which is the kundam (fire pit) on His forehead. From that Yaga, the white ash appeared, and He arranged for the three types of souls – Vignanakalar, Pralayakalar, and Sakalar – to wear it on their bodies so that their inner and outer attachments (Ahangara Mamakaram) would be destroyed, as stated in the Vedas and Agamas. The Supreme Shiva, who is older than the oldest, is dissolved by the five deities – Brahma, Vishnu, Rudra, Anantesvara, and Sadashiva Paramaptar – respectively, into the five Kalas: Nivritti Kala, Pratishtha Kala, Vidya Kala, Shanti Kala, and Shantyatita Kala. During the five types of dissolution – Brahmanda Pralayam, Prakrita Pralayam, Madhyama Pralayam, Mahapralayam, and Prathama Mahapralayam – which are the dissolutions of the Tattva Bhuvanas up to the end of Shuddha Maya Bhuvana, He burns all the bodies of the Anusadakiyas, who are at the very end, with a spark of fire from His third eye and smears their ashes on His divine body. Therefore, He ordained that souls should also wear the sacred ash made from the pulverized dung of cows, in which all those deities reside as individual limbs, as a symbol of this. Bhujabalan, the king of Vanga Desam, who scorned sacred ash, lost his kingdom and wife, became a beggar, and later, by the grace of a great Brahmin, received sacred ash, defeated his enemies, and regained his wife and kingdom. Sudharman, a Brahmin from Kashmir Desam, and his younger brother, who spoke ill of Brahma and Vishnu, were cursed to become mute and lame. Sage Dadhichi removed their curse with Vibhuti and helped them attain a good state. When Brahma Devar was grieving inconsolably over the separation from Saraswati Devi, Lord Shiva appeared in the form of Bharathi, performed the Pandarangak Koothu (a divine dance), and applied Vibhuti on Brahma Devar's forehead, thereby removing his delusion. Three Asuras named Sarpan, Panchameshti, and Agni killed all the Devas. Veerabhadra Deva went there, sprinkled Vibhuti, and revived the Devas. A Brahma Rakshasa who had caught Sage Vamadeva to kill him, upon touching the body of Sage Vamadeva, which was smeared with sacred ash, attained good knowledge and a divine body. A Brahmin who died a bad death due to adultery and was immersed in hell attained heaven when Vibhuti touched his corpse. Later, he also attained Shiva Loka Mukti. Thirugnanasambandha Moorthy Nayanar, who shone as the lamp of the Kauniya clan, a foremost ascetic, and a spiritual teacher, cured the fever of Koon Pandiyan with sacred ash. We will see in the Periya Puranam that countless Munivars who smeared their entire bodies with sacred ash attained Shiva Sayujya Mukti. Let us continue to contemplate the glory of Vibhuti. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 03 — Muzhu Neeru Poosiya Munivar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam Part 1007, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Paththarai Paniwaar Sarukkam, Muzhu Neeru Poosiya Munivar Puranam. We have been contemplating the glories of Vibhuti. We saw that Vibhuti should be applied in 16 places. Doing so will remove all sins. How? If sacred ash is applied on the crown of the head, sins committed above the neck will be removed. If applied on the forehead, the evil effects of bad karma written by Brahma will be removed. If applied on the throat, the sin of eating forbidden food will be removed. If applied on the chest, sins committed by the mind will be removed. If applied on the navel, the sin of sensual indulgence committed by the male organ will be removed. If applied on the knees, sins committed by the legs will be removed. If applied on the three joints below the back, sins committed by the anus will be removed. If applied on the shoulders, the sin of embracing other women will be removed, as stated in the Skanda Purana. Furthermore, the otherworldly benefits of wearing sacred ash are also mentioned. If sacred ash is applied on the crown of the head with the respective mantras, one Chandra Kala will be attained. If applied on the forehead, Netram (divine vision) will be attained. If applied on the throat, an identity will be attained. If applied on the chest, Sarvagnyatvam (omniscience) will be attained. If applied on the shoulders, divine women will be obtained. If applied on the hands, divine weapons like the deer and axe will be obtained. If applied on the navel and three joints, Rishabha Vahanam (bull mount), animal skin garments, and residence in Kailasam will be attained, as stated in the Brahma Kaivartha Purana. Moreover, when describing the benefits for one who applies Vibhuti in the 16 places starting from the head, it is said that applying pure Vibhuti on the head bestows crowns and diadems. Applying it on the forehead makes one a ruler. Applying it on the neck, chest, shoulders, and hand joints bestows divine ornaments like pearl necklaces and golden chains. Additionally, silk garments, divine waistbands, and divine enjoyments with five adornments will be obtained. Applying it on the hands bestows divine ornaments like armlets. Applying it on the knees bestows divine vehicles like chariots, elephants, horses, and palanquins. Many other benefits will also be obtained. Vibhuti Dharanam bestows great enjoyments in this world and liberation in the next. Now, the method of meditating on the presiding deity of Vibhuti is described. The method of meditating on the Vibhuti deity is as follows: those who meditate on Him with three faces, nine eyes, three hands, three legs, bestowing boons, dispelling fear, in the posture of divine dance, with a reddish divine body, a fierce form, and smeared with Vibhuti Uththoolanam, will attain their desired accomplishments, as stated in the Makudagama. Wearing black Vibhuti causes disease. Wearing red Vibhuti destroys fame. Wearing golden Vibhuti removes wealth. Therefore, only white Vibhuti should be worn. The Kamikagama states that wearing white Vibhuti bestows both enjoyment and liberation. Wherever one who wears Vibhuti and Rudraksha partakes of food, Paramashiva, along with Parvati, also partakes of food there. The Sutha Samhita states that those who follow one who wears Shiva symbols all over their body, even if they are great sinners, become purified. The Manava Samhita states that true devotees who wear Vibhuti three times a day – morning, noon, and evening – regardless of their caste, should be regarded as Lord Shiva Himself. Lord Shiva will never leave true devotees who wear sacred ash with mantras and devotion. Serpents, planets like the sun, stars, directional deities, Yama, Kala, Yama's messengers, fire, terrible diseases, demons, lightning, ghosts, and troubles from fierce creatures like lions, tigers, and bears will not approach those who wear Vibhuti with true devotion and mantras. They will conquer their two karmas, attain Shiva Gnanam, and surely achieve liberation. The Pashupata Vrata is described. The Pashupata Vrata is the continuous wearing of Vibhuti and Rudraksha after receiving Shiva Diksha from a Guru of good character. It is also called Shiro Vrata. This is a vrata of wearing Vibhuti and Rudraksha, which are the forms of Pashupati. Therefore, it is called Pashupata Vrata and is superior to all other Shiva vratas. Hence, it is also called Shiro Vrata. This vrata removes sin, disease, fear, and the increase of sorrow. Those who observe this vrata are called Pashupatas and Maha Vratas. The Kurma Purana, Vayu Samhita, Shambhu Bharatam, and Kali Khandam state that Lord Krishna observed this and attained great blessings. How? Krishna married nine women – Rukmini, Satyabhama, Kali, Mitravinda, Sakthi, Bhadra, Jambavati, Lakshana, and Nappinnai – and had many children with all of them except Jambavati. Jambavati, being greatly distressed and weeping over her childlessness, was consoled by Krishna. He went to Sage Upamanyu to seek a solution. Sage Upamanyu initiated Krishna into Shiva Diksha and instructed him to perform penance as a Pashupata Vrati. By doing so, Jambavati gave birth to ten sons, including Sambavan, as stated in the aforementioned texts. Texts state that children are afflicted by various deities from the tenth day after birth until the age of 16. It is a common experience that children suffer various troubles, diseases, and deformities from these deities. To avoid these, it is customary to tie protective threads and wear five weapons. However, wearing Vibhuti and Rudraksha serves as the best protection. Dakini, Shakini, ghosts, spirits, and Brahma Rakshasas will leave the child. The afflictions from the Bala Arishta deities such as Thanthai, Peetani, Kalikai, Bheemaroopi, Ankushini, Boothini, Muththakesi, Akasayini, Arikkerathini, Shakuni, Bhoomayi, Uththami, Pingalai, Gokarni, Pangai, Seemuki, Sheni, Kumbakarni, Thabakai, Kalargi, Nandini, Krodhani, Thanuni, Shakini, Panchalai, Kamini, Medini, Nalini, Maha Kumbini, Arani, Akasakarani, Vayu Vichingni, Suganthini, Vanadevi, and Kundali will all vanish like dew before the sun. Therefore, Bhasma serves as protection for children. Since these Shiva symbols are declared as Shiva by the Shastras, those who condemn Shiva symbols are condemning Lord Shiva. There is no atonement for those who condemn Shiva symbols, which can remove all great sins like Brahmahatya, Gōhatya, Śiśuhatya, and Strīhatya. The Skanda Purana states that even if one is a Brahmin or an Atiyashrami, if they do not wear the sacred ash and Rudraksha prescribed by the Vedas, they become fallen. Those who wear Vibhuti and Rudraksha, which are Saiva Bhushanas, should learn certain mantras from a Guru. These mantras are of five types: Shuddha Prasadam, Moola Prasadam, Tattva Prasadam, Adi Prasadam, and Atma Prasadam, which are Shiva mantras representing creation. There are also five types of Sadasiva mantras representing sustenance: Mokshadam, Abhivrutthi, Kamiyam, Shantikam, and Paushtikam. There are three types of Maheswara mantras representing dissolution. There are five types of Pranava mantras: Shakhalvya Pranavam, Saukhya Pranavam, Shambava Pranavam, Sarva Shiva Pranavam, and Sayujya Pranavam. There are 14 types of Panchakshara mantras: Moola Panchaksharam, Sthoola Panchaksharam, Sookshma Panchaksharam, Karana Panchaksharam, Misra Panchaksharam, Dhara Panchaksharam, Mukti Panchaksharam, Mayakhya Panchaksharam, Prasada Panchaksharam, Nirmala Jagrat Panchaksharam, Nirmala Svapna Panchaksharam, Nirmala Sushupti Panchaksharam, Nirmala Turiya Panchaksharam, and Nirmala Turiyatita Panchaksharam. There are also divine Moola Mantras like Shadaksharam, Ashtaksharam, Navakshari, Dasakshari, and Panchadasakshari. There are five types of Astra Mantras: Shivastram, Suryakastram, Pashupatastram, Vibhomastram, and Aghorastram. There are also the Pancha Brahma Mantras starting with Ishanam. Gayatri, Mrityunjayam, Dakshina Krishnamoorthy Mantra, Sharabha, Vigneshwara, Bhairava, Veerabhadra, Subramanya, Vidyashwara, Dwarapalaka, Brahma, Vishnu, Surya Devadi Mantras, and Maha Shakti Mantras like Durga, Lakshmi, Saraswati, Bhuvaneshwari, Ashwaroodi, Tripurambikai, and Siddha Mantras like Siddha Shavarar, Satti Shavarar, Meru Tantrar, Shakini Tantrar, Dakini Tantrar, Koormandar, Salliyar, and other Kriya Mantras. These should be learned from a Guru through initiation. Having learned the above-mentioned mantras, and understanding their Vachakam (meaning), Vachyam (object), Bhavakam (feeling), Lakshyam (aim), Lakshanam (characteristics), Dhyanam (meditation), Niram (color), Chandas (meter), Adhidevata (presiding deity), Nyasam (placement), Beejam (seed syllable), Vyahruti (utterance), and Enn (number) according to time, place, and action, and applying the six Pallavas (endings) like Namas according to the order of the six Anga Mantras, and the Netra Pallavam according to the five Anga Mantras, and improvising Angas for mantras without them, and knowing how to chant the Hridaya Pallavam, Shiro Pallavam, Shikha Pallavam, Kavacha Pallavam, Netra Pallavam, and Astra Pallavam for various actions, and applying them mentally, verbally, and softly, as per the rules. These rules are extensively described in texts like Vathulagamam, Sharada Tilakam, Mantra Devata Prakashikai, Prapancha Mantra Saram, Mantra Sahasrottam, and in the Atharva Veda, Dhanur Veda (Upaveda), and Chandas (Vedanga). These are of ten types: Jananam, Jeevanam, Pudevadanam, Bodhanam, Abhishekam, Vimalikaranam, Apyayanam, Tarpanam, Deepanam, and Guttam. In Sanskrit, these are called Dasa Samskaram. Although mantras are described in this way, the mantras specifically mentioned in the Vedas, Agamas, Upanishads, Smritis, and Puranas are of five types. They are Pancha Brahma, Triyambakam, Triyayushmanastokam, and Agniriti. The Bhasma Jabala Upanishad speaks of Pancha Brahma, the Siddha Karana and other Shivagamas speak of Triyambakam and others, the Shangayana Guhyam, Madhyadina Guhyam, and Brihat Jabala Upanishad speak of Triyayushadi Mantras, and the Shvetashvatara Upanishad specifically mentions the Manastokade Mantra. Furthermore, the Jabala Upanishad, Atharva Shiro Upanishad, Matsya Purana, and Sutha Samhita proclaim the seven mantras starting with Agniriti as special. Now, Vibhuti Snanam. Vibhuti Snanam is also called Agneya Snanam. The Veda proclaims, "Agneyam Bhasma Snanam." Agneya Snanam means bathing in the sacred ash related to fire. This bath is primarily performed when it is not possible to immerse oneself in water, or when there is a fault due to not bathing completely with water. This bath yields a benefit a crore times greater than bathing in sacred rivers like the Ganges. The Agama shloka states, "Bhasma Snanam Jala Snanath Kodi Punnya Gunambhavet." Those who perform Vibhuti Snanam should purify their bodies, take water, sprinkle it on the ground to purify the earth, sit in Padmasana facing north, perform Achamana and Pranayama three times, make a Sankalpa (vow), take sacred ash with the Kuranga Mudra while chanting the Pancha Brahma Mantra, cover it with the right hand, place it on the right thigh, chant the seven mantras starting with Agniriti seven times, and then sprinkle it all over the body with the Manastokade Mantra. The Shastras describe this Vibhuti Snanam in even more detail. The Vibhuti Upanishad, also known as Bhasma Jabala Upanishad, expounds all the glories of Vibhuti. The Bhasma Jabala Upanishad is the 87th Upanishad among the 108 Upanishads. It is part of the Atharva Veda, the fourth Veda. Since the Muktikopanishad places it before the Yoga Kundali Upanishad, it is understood that sacred ash cannot be rejected even by Yogis. Now, the characteristics of the Vibhuti pouch are also described. After preparing Vibhuti as described in the Agamas, the collected Vibhuti should be filtered through a pure new cloth, placed in a new vessel, and kept in a clean container while chanting the Vibhuti Gayatri Mantra. Inside it, fragrant flowers like jasmine, wild jasmine, patiri, and small champak should be placed with the Sadyojata Mantra. The mouth of the vessel should be tied with a new cloth, and it should be kept as a great treasure. A small portion should be taken from it for wearing and placed in a pouch before applying it. Vibhuti should be kept in a silk pouch, a small box, or a pouch made of bilva fruit, bottle gourd, deerskin, or tiger skin. Among these, pouches made of skin are suitable for Naishthika Brahmacharis and Sanyasis. When making the pouch, it should be eight inches wide and twelve inches high, with a circular opening. Vibhuti should not be inverted from containers other than these fruit shells. If inverted, one will fall into terrible hells like Maharauravam and suffer. The sacred white ash, which burned brightly in the Yaga Shala of Lord Shiva, Uma Devi, Ganesha, Skanda, Bhairava, and Veerabhadra during festivals and other occasions, is suitable for Brahmins, Kshatriyas, and Vaishyas. Good deeds performed by those who do not wear Vibhuti will be fruitless. The intelligence, conduct, truthfulness, penance, and other qualities of those who do not wear Vibhuti will be destroyed. One should fear to look at the face of those who do not wear Vibhuti. Manikkavasagar Swamigal, who says, "I fear not even if all diseases come," fears those who do not wear white ash, saying, "Amma, we fear those who do not wear sacred ash," as stated in the Thiruvachakam. The three lines of the Thripundram, which we apply as three marks, are the forms of sacred ash. These three represent the truths of Pathi, Pasu, Pasam; the individual Pranavas A, U, M; the three-letter mantra forms of Pathi, Parai, Atma; the three forms of Arūvam, Aruvuruvam, Uruvam; the deities Shiva, Sadashiva, Mahesan who possess them; the Somaskanda Murthy formed by the union of Shiva, Shakti, and Skanda; the three types of divine limbs: Pratyangam, Sangam, Upangam; the Adhva Murtham, Mantra Murtham, Pancha Brahma Murtham; the Sahopadhanam of Bindu, Man, Yogini; the states of Layam, Bhogam, Adhikaram; and the underlying support of Satthan, Udyuktan, Pravirutthan; the three functions of creation, sustenance, and dissolution; the three powers of Iccha, Kriya, Gnanam; the Tantra Kala, Mantra Kala, Upadesa Kala; the three proofs of Pratyaksham, Anumanam, Agamam; the tools of Guru, Lingam, Jangamam; the three luminaries of Sun, Moon, Fire; the three types of souls: Vignanakalar, Pralayakalar, Sakalar; the three genders: He, She, It; the Mani, Mantra, Aushadham; the Tattvas of Bhogam, Bhogyam, Prerakam; the Mantra, Bhavana, Kriya; the Sagunopasana, Nirgunopasana, Atiyashrama Bhavana. The Agneya Purana states that Brahmins should consider the Thripundram as their Upaveetham, Shikha, and Vedam. The Manapurana states that it is the form of Shiva. The Brihat Jabala Upanishad states that the upper line of the Thripundram is the form of the Sama Veda, the middle line is the form of the Yajur Veda, and the bottom line is the form of the Rig Veda. Similarly, the Vedas declare it to be the cause of liberation, knowledge, and intellect. The Vashishta Linga Purana mentions Virasakni, Aupasanaagni, and Pasakni. The Kali Khandam states that it is in the form of the three fires – Garhapatyam, Ahavaniyam, Dakshinagniyam – the three Murtis, the three Gunas starting with Sattva, the three Varnas, and the three worlds. It is understood that whoever wears the sacred ash, which is the Shivathva Peru (attainment of Shivahood) resulting from burning the three impurities of Anava, Karma, and Maya with the fire of Shiva Gnanam, attains liberation. Furthermore, the Vibhuti applied as three lines in Thripundram destroys the three faults of Kama (lust), Veguli (anger), Mayakkam (delusion), and the three Gunas of Sattva, Rajas, Tamas, and shines like three elevated flags of victorious knowledge. It removes the three Eshanas (desires) – Dhana Eshana (desire for wealth), Putra Eshana (desire for children), Loka Eshana (desire for worldly fame); the three Karmas – Prarabdham, Sanchitam, Agamiyam; the three types of grossness – Sthoola Tamam, Sthoola Taram, Sthoolam; the three types of actions – good deeds, bad deeds, and deeds that do not bind; the three genders – male, female, neuter; the three Tattvas – Atma Tattvam, Vidya Tattvam, Shiva Tattvam; the three causes – Anava Karyam, Maya Karyam, Karma Mala Karyam; the three objects of knowledge – Gnyatru (knower), Gnyanam (knowledge), Gneyam (knowable); the three types of suffering – Adhidaivikam, Adhyatmikam, Adhibhautikam; the three divisions – Bhedam (difference), Abhedam (non-difference), Bhedabhedam (difference and non-difference); the three sensory perceptions – Pori (sense organs), Pulam (objects of senses), Dharanam (perception); the three types of intellect – Sandeha (doubt), Vipareeta (misconception), Mayakkam (delusion); the three bodies – Sthoola (gross), Sookshma (subtle), Karana (causal); the three spiritual practices – Chariyai, Kiriyai, Yogam; the three worlds – upper, lower, middle; the three times – past, present, future; the three persons – first, second, third; the three obstacles to liberation – Agnyanam (ignorance), Samsayam (doubt), Vipareetam (misconception); the three types of imagination – Nama Kalpanai (name), Vyavahara Karpitam (transactional), Uruva Kalpanai (form); the three types of objects – Preeti Vishayam (liked), Dvesha Vishayam (disliked), Upeksha Vishayam (indifferent); the three types of Upadhis (limiting adjuncts) – Avachinnopadhi, Pratibimbopadhi, Adhatyashopadhi; the three types of conduct – Sadacharam, Nithacharam, Ghanacharam; the three states of consciousness – Jagrat (waking), Svapnam (dream), Sushupti (deep sleep); the three realms – Brahma Loka, Vishnu Loka, Rudra Loka; the three limitations – Kala (time), Desa (space), Vastthu (object); the three practices of good and bad deeds – doing, causing to do, consenting; the three humors – Vatham, Pitham, Sletthumam; the three types of differences – Sajatiya, Vijatiya, Svagatam; and the three instruments – Manam (mind), Vakku (speech), Kayam (body); and the three aspects of knowledge – Uddesham (aim), Lakshanam (definition), Pareekshai (examination); and the three births – this birth, the next birth, and the birth after that; and thus, the three lines of the Thripundram clearly signify the attainment of the supreme state beyond these. Therefore, the glories of sacred ash continue to expand. Thus, Bhasma Dharanam is explained as being praised by all Vedas, bestowing the fruits of all karmas, being an intimate means for devotion to God, being a form of daily protection, being the cause of human pursuits, being the root of mental purity, being important for Shiva's grace, being the direct form of Shiva, and being the cause of the incomparable bliss called eternal liberation. Therefore, knowing that Lord Shiva alone is the bestower of liberation, and that Vibhuti and Rudraksha, imbued with His divine power, are the Saiva Bhushanam, and that Vibhuti is supreme wealth, and that it has five sacred names, and that the Lord Himself arranged for the three types of souls to wear it on their bodies to destroy inner and outer attachments, and that it leads to miraculous events like the curing of Koon Pandiyan's fever, and the parts of the body where Vibhuti should be worn, and the glory of Vibhuti, and how Vibhuti is mentioned along with Rudraksha as a Saiva Bhushanam, and that Vibhuti is a suitable direct means to remove the three impurities of Anava, Karma, and Maya that bind souls and to attain the liberation called Moksha – all these are extensively explained and established in the Shastras. Let us continue to contemplate the Puranam of Muzhu Neeru Poosiya Munivar. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 04 — Muzhu Neeru Poosiya Munivar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam Part 1008, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Paththarai Paniwaar Sarukkam, Muzhu Neeru Poosiya Munivar Puranam. In the tenth verse of the Thiruththondaththogai, a compilation of hymns, our Lord Sundaramoorthy Swamigal has sung, "I am a devotee of the Munivars who are fully smeared with sacred ash." The divine Sekkizhar Peruman will explain in the Periya Puranam who these Munivars, fully smeared with sacred ash, are. Our Lord Sundaramoorthy Swamigal has sung that he is a devotee of such Munivars. Now, in the Thiruththondar Thiruvandhadhi, a comprehensive work, Nambiyandar Nambigal Peruman has praised the Munivars fully smeared with sacred ash in the 75th verse. "Even if the world is disturbed, even if the eons change, those good, wise ones who possess an unwavering law, will never deviate. They are the lords who adorn their bodies with the radiant white ash, shining beneath the feet of the Lord of Thiruvarur, who possesses immeasurable great qualities." The Lord of Thiruvarur is a great Lord with immeasurable great qualities. These are our leaders who adorn their bodies with the sacred white ash that shines beneath the feet of the Lord of Thiruvarur. They are wise ones who remain steadfast, their minds never disturbed, even if all the worlds are in turmoil or if the eons change. This is its meaning. Now, in the comprehensive work, the Periya Puranam, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, the 63rd Puranam, "Muzhu Neeru Poosiya Munivar Puranam," has been composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman in six verses. The first verse describes the types of sacred ash. "Aadharamai Anaiththumagi Ninra Anganan Emperuman Neeranintha Venikadhar Ven Thirukkuzhaiyan Aruli Cheitha Karpam Anukarpam Upakarpam Thanam Agathu Enru Angu Uraiththa Agarpam Neekki Yam Enru Munmozhintha Moonru Betha Mogathi Kutrangal Arukkum Neetrai Mozhiguthu Nam Iruvinai Kazhivathaga." Our Lord, the beautiful-eyed one, who is the support of all and is all things, wears sacred ash on His braided hair. Lord Shiva, who wears a white earring in His ear, has, in the Shivagamas, graciously described Karpam, Anu Karpam, and Upakarpam as the acceptable types of Vibhuti, excluding the type called Akarpam. We shall speak of this sacred ash, which removes faults like lust, so that our two karmas may be removed. Lust, anger, greed, delusion, pride, and envy are the six evil qualities. Sacred ash removes them. There are three types of sacred ash: Karpam, Anu Karpam, and Upakarpam. These are for Saivites who are eligible for initiation. Akarpam is sacred ash that does not fall into these three categories. It is for those who are not eligible for initiation. This is called Asaiva Vibhuti. The three types of sacred ash, Karpam and others, are also of two types: Vaidika and Laukika. Among these, they belong to the Laukika category. Vaidika Vibhuti is of two types: Purodhani, which arises from Brahma's Homa Kundam, and Sadyojata, which arises from the Homa Kundam of Brahmins. The details of these are discussed in texts like Shivagamas and Saiva Samaya Neri. Now, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman explains Karpaneeru (Karpam ash) in the second verse. "Ambalaththē Ulaku Uyya Aadum Annal Uvanthu Aadum Anjinaiyum Aliththa Aakkal Imparmisai Anamayamai Iruntha Pōthil Eentru Aniya Kōmaya Manthiraththinal Eetru Umpar Thozha Ezhum Siva Manthira Hōmaththal Urpaviththa Sivakkini Thanil Unarvukku Ettā Emperuman Kazhal Ninainthu Anga Itta Thūneeru Ithu Karpam Enreduththu Ingu Ēththal Ākum." The Lord who dances in the sacred hall for the salvation of the world, joyfully performs the sacred bath with the Panchagavyam given by the cows. When these cows are free from disease in this world, and have given birth more than ten days ago, their dung is collected with the Sadyojata Mantra. Then, in the Shiva Agni, which arises from the Shiva mantras worshipped by the Devas, the pure ash obtained by placing it there, meditating on the feet of the Lord Shiva who is beyond comprehension, is called Karpam. In Thirukailayam, the five cows Nanthai, Pathirai, Surabhi, Susilai, and Sumanai, who live with Rishabha Devar, originated from the Thirupparkadal (Ocean of Milk) by the command of Lord Shiva and are the lineage of cows in this world. Among these cows, excluding those that have given birth within ten days, barren heifers, diseased cows, cows whose calves have died, old cows, sterile cows, cows that eat dung, and pregnant cows, and those with broken tails, ears, or horns, the dung of excellent cows that have grazed on hay in the months of Panguni and Thai should be collected as previously described. On the days of Ashtami, Amavasya, Pournami, and Chaturdasi, it should be collected with the Sadyojata Mantra, and the upper impurities should be removed. Panchagavyam should be added with the Vamadeva Mantra, kneaded with the Aghora Mantra, and rolled into balls with the Tatpurusha Mantra. These balls, either wet or dry, should be placed in the Homa fire with the Ishana Mantra and burnt. The resulting ash is the type of sacred ash called Karpam. Now, in the next verse, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman describes Anu Karpam ash. "Araniyaththu Ulantha Kōmayaththai Kaikkondu Azhagura Nunpodiyakki Āvin Suththa Neer Aniviththu Thanthira Manthiraththinale Nisaiyamura Pidiththu Ōma Neruppil Ittu Seeraniyumpadi Ventukonda Selvat Thiruneerām Anukarpam Thillai Manthril Vāraniyūm Mulai Umaiyāl Kāna Aadum Mānikkūththar Mozhi Vāymaiyāle." According to the rules of the Shivagamas, as spoken by Manikkooththar Peruman, who performs the sacred dance in Thillai Ambalam for Umaiyamaiyar to witness, dried cow dung from the forest is collected, finely powdered, mixed with pure cow urine, kneaded firmly with Astra mantras, rolled into balls, and then placed in the Homa fire. The sacred ash obtained after it is well burnt and acquired with glory is called Anu Karpam. Now, in the fourth verse, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman sings about Upakarpam ash. "Adavipadu Angiyinal Ventha Neerum Ānilaihal Anal Thodakka Ventha Neerum Idavagaihal Erikkoluva Ventha Neerum Ittigaihal Sutta Eripata Neerum Udan Anri Verē Āvin Neerāl Urai Thigazhum Manthiram Kondu Undaiyākki Madamathanil Polinthiruntha Siva Angi Thannāl Ventathu Matru Upakarpam Marapin Āhum." The ash burnt by forest fires caused by trees rubbing against each other, the ash burnt when cow sheds catch fire, the ash burnt when various other places catch fire, and the ash produced by the fire used to bake bricks – these are not mixed together. Instead, they are separately mixed with cow urine, kneaded into balls with the appropriate mantras prescribed in the Shivagamas, and then burnt in the Shiva Agni that shines in the sacred monasteries. This is the third type of Vibhuti called Upakarpam. Now, in the fifth verse, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman sings about the manner of wearing sacred ash. "Intha Vagaiyāl Amaiththa Neeru Kondu Iruththiramum Suththi Vara Theliththa Pinnar Anthamillā Aran Angi Āru Meymai Ariviththa Guru Nanmai Allā Bhoomi Muntha Ethir Aniyāthē Aniyumpōthu Muzhuvathum Meypundaram Santhiranil Pāthi Nanthi Eri Theepam Nigazh Vattamāga Nāthar Adiyār Anivar Nanmaiyāle." Having prepared and produced the sacred ash in this manner, after sprinkling it to purify both the inner and outer realms, and then sprinkling a little in the southwest direction (Niruthi Moolai) with the appropriate mantra, devotees of Shiva, by their goodness, apply this sacred ash in the form of a full body smear (Meypundaram), a crescent moon, a lamp flame (triangle), or a shining circle, without applying it beforehand in places like the shrine of the endless Shiva, the fire, the Guru who taught the true path, or in inauspicious places, but only when applying it according to the rules. Now, in the sixth verse, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman describes the qualities of the Munivars who are fully smeared with sacred ash. "Sathiyinil Thalaiyāna Dharma Seelar Thathuvaththin Neri Unarnthōr Thangal Kolgai Neethiyinil Pizhaiyāthu Neriyil Nirpōr Niththa Niyamaththu Nigazh Angi Thannil Boothiyinai Puthiya Āsanaththu Kondu Puli Adhalin Udaiyānai Pōtri Neetrai Āthi Varum Mūmmalamum Aruththa Vāymai Arumunivar Muzhuvathum Mey Anivar Anrē." These rare Munivars, who are foremost in their caste in righteous conduct, who have understood the path of Tattva, who stand firm in their principles without deviating from justice, and who have truly cut off the three primordial impurities, take the sacred ash produced in the daily fire of their unwavering observances, place it in a new vessel, worship the Lord who wears tiger skin, and then smear that sacred ash all over their bodies. These are called Muzhu Neeru Poosiya Munivars. Thus concludes the Puranam of Muzhu Neeru Poosiya Munivar. Let us continue to contemplate the Puranam of Appalum Adisarnthar.
