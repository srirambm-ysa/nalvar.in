Chapter 01 — Chithaththaich Sivan Pale Vaiththar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam Part 996, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. The Puranam of Chithaththaich Sivan Pale Vaiththar, from the chapter "Paththarai Paniwaar Sarukkam". This Puranam, the 60th in the divine Periya Puranam composed by Sekkizhar Peruman, is a Togaiyadiyar Puranam (a Puranam about a group of devotees) titled "Chithaththaich Sivan Pale Vaiththar Puranam". It is the third Puranam in the 11th chapter, "Paththarai Paniwaar Sarukkam". Firstly, in the 10th verse of "Thiruththondaththogai", a collective work composed by our Lord Sundaramurthy Swamigal, Sundaramurthy Swamigal has sung, "I am a servant to those who placed their minds solely on Shiva." The word "Chiththam" means mind or intellect. They placed it solely on Shiva. This means they placed their minds only on Lord Shiva and not on other authoritative deities. Therefore, those referred to as "Chithaththaich Sivan Pale Vaiththar" are those who enshrined Lord Shiva within their minds. It means they did not place anyone else in their minds except Lord Shiva. "To those who can think of Him, excluding all else," is a sacred saying from Thevaram. The meaning here is that "I am a servant to such people who have placed their minds solely on Shiva." Now, the 72nd verse of "Thiruththondar Thiruvandhadhi", a categorized work composed by Nambiyandar Nambigal Peruman, praises those who placed their minds solely on Shiva and describes their nature. It is as follows: "Having cast aside dharma, artha, and kama from the supreme realm, and having taken a unique path to the realm of wisdom, they are said to have placed their minds at the lotus feet of the Lord who wears the Datura flower on His head, and these are the wealthy ones who have attained liberation." The meaning of this verse is that these individuals, through the abundance of supreme Shiva-punya (merit), have cast aside the forest of dharma, artha, and kama. They have transcended the five abodes of wisdom, such as the heart, and beyond that, they have opened the Sushumna nadi and traveled through it, establishing their minds at the sacred feet of the Lord who wears the Datura flower on His head. These are the wealthy ones who have attained liberation (moksha) in this very life, in this very world. The "uththama dhanam" (supreme realm) refers to Shiva-punya. Through the abundance of that supreme Shiva-punya, they have transcended dharma, artha, and kama. Dharma, artha, and kama are used to establish souls in the worldly path. Only by abandoning these can one attain liberation (moksha). That is why Avvaiyar says, "The state of remembering the Supreme is liberation." She, who defined these three: "Giving is dharma, earning wealth by avoiding evil is artha, and the union of two hearts in love is inba." Next, she speaks of liberation (moksha). She says, "Abandoning these three and remembering the Supreme is the great bliss of liberation." Thus, all actions performed by remembering Shiva, the Supreme, are acts of Shiva-punya. Therefore, they are called "uththama sthanam" (supreme abodes). Thus, these are the ones who, by reason of abundant Shiva-punya, have placed their minds at the feet of the Lord, abandoning the three states of dharma, etc. How did they do this? The five centers of knowledge are revered as the heart, throat, base of the tongue, the space between the eyebrows, and the Brahmarandhra. These are the abodes of wisdom. The states of these centers are described as five lotus flowers. The path that goes above these abodes is a unique path, the Sushumna nadi. Opening that path and traveling through it. By doing so, one attains Shiva in the Nadhanta. Having attained Shiva, they placed their minds at the feet of Lord Shiva. They are called Jivanmuktas (liberated souls) in this very life. They are the ones who placed their minds solely on Shiva. They are the wealthy ones who attained liberation (moksha) by doing so. Thus, the Thiruththondar Thiruvandhadhi describes the nature of the Togaiyadiyars who placed their minds solely on Shiva. Now, in the detailed work, Periya Puranam, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, in the chapter "Paththarai Paniwaar Sarukkam", in the Puranam of Chithaththaich Sivan Pale Vaiththar, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman has explained the characteristics of these Togaiyadiyars with a single verse. That verse is as follows: "Having transcended the abodes of the five causal deities, the minds that attained Shiva through the concentration on the full, true, supreme light shining in the Nadhanta, reached the path of service at the feet of the Lord of the unique assembly, the cause of the Vedas, the Dancer." This is a verse in which the divine Sekkizhar Peruman describes the nature of those who placed their minds solely on Shiva. Its meaning is as follows: Those who are praised as "Chithaththaich Sivan Pale Vaiththar" are those who, having transcended the abodes of the five causal deities, such as Brahma, which are associated with five lotus flowers, and having gone beyond, established their minds in the Nadhanta, where the full, true, self-luminous Shiva shines with the light of knowledge, thereby establishing their minds in Shiva. They are said to have reached the Lord, the cause of the Vedas, the Dancer, who shines in the unique sacred assembly, by following the path of service at His feet. This is the meaning of this verse. Now, for its detailed explanation. This verse begins with "Karanapangayam aindhin kadavular." The "Karanapangayam aindhin kadavular" are the five authoritative deities, such as Brahma, who perform the five actions of creation, etc. Their seats are five types of lotus seats. These are the positions and statuses of these causal deities. These five, who are praised as the causal deities of the five actions, perform these actions according to the command of Lord Shiva. The causal deities are those who have attained maturity and Shiva-jnana (divine knowledge) in the category of souls called Vijnanakalar. Saiva Siddhanta divides souls into three categories: Sakalar, Pralayakalar, and Vijnanakalar. This division is based on the malabandha (bondage of impurities) they possess. Among them, the highest category is the Vijnanakalar. These souls have overcome the two impurities of karma and maya, out of the three impurities of anava, karma, and maya, and only possess the subtle remnants of anava. These Vijnanakalar, having attained maturity and Shiva-jnana, are the lords of the worlds that arise from pure maya. Although they are free from impurities, they still have a slight desire for authority and enjoyment due to the remnants of impurities. They perform actions such as creation, etc., for the souls within their sphere of authority, having received the command of Lord Shiva. All these beings have attained these positions due to the abundance of their merits. Although they bear the name of deities, they too belong to the flock of souls. The truth stated by the scriptures of knowledge is that it is Lord Shiva Himself who stands within them, empowering them to perform these actions. Therefore, in reality, Lord Shiva is the one who does everything. Even in their state as causal deities, these causal deities are detached from everything. Therefore, they desire nothing. For the jnanis (wise ones) who have experienced Shiva-bhoga (divine enjoyment), worldly pleasures taste bitter and become unpalatable. These are the Shiva-bhedas (forms of Shiva). Lord Shiva Himself, in order to perform the five actions of creation, etc., assumes these forms and resides in these positions, in those respective lotuses. We also see in the scriptures of knowledge that Brahma and others who follow will perform the duties of creation, etc., assigned to them. One must transcend the abodes of these five causal deities. Beyond these five abodes of the authoritative deities, there is the place where Lord Shiva resides in the Nadhanta. Transcending the abodes of these five causal deities means first meditating on Brahma, the deity of the heart center, associated with the square, golden color, and the letter "La" of the Prithvi tattva. Then, moving upwards, one meditates on Vishnu, the deity of the throat center, associated with the two-angled shape (some call it a lotus), white color, and the letter "Va" of the Appu tattva. Then, moving further upwards, one meditates on Rudramurthy, the deity of the base of the tongue center, associated with the three-angled shape, red color, and the letter "Ra" of the Tejas (Agni) tattva. Then, moving further upwards, one meditates on Maheswaramurthy, the deity of the Ajna chakra (between the eyebrows), associated with the hexagonal shape, dark color, and the letter "Ya" of the Vayu tattva. And finally, moving further upwards, one meditates on Sadashivamurthy, the deity of the Brahmarandhra center, associated with the circular shape, smoky color, and the letter "Ha" of the Akasha tattva. To perceive these deities in their respective places and remain unwavering in that meditation, and then to reach the Nadhanta, the sixth stage where the door to liberation opens. This is the meaning of transcending the abodes of the five causal deities. One must transcend them through Dharana (concentration) and attain Shiva. There are eight limbs in Yoga. Patanjali's Ashtanga Yoga is different. The Shiva Yoga described in the Shiva Agamas is very different. In Shiva Yoga, one must stabilize the mind in Shiva through the yogic path of Dharana. This involves awakening the Kundalini Shakti in the Kundalini center of the body with Pranava and raising it upwards through the Sushumna. By doing so, one transcends the abodes of these five causal deities, transcends the Brahmarandhra, and reaches the Nadhanta when the door to liberation opens. That Nadhanta is the abode of the Lord. One must stabilize the mind in that Nadhanta state. Nadhanta refers to the supreme liberation, Shiva Sayujya, where even the pure tattvas like Bindu and Nada are transcended. Those who have stabilized their minds in the Nadhanta state are those who have placed their minds solely on Shiva. In the Nadhanta, which is the sixth support, beyond the Brahmarandhra, in the Ajna (command) state, Shiva shines as the complete, supreme light. Those who meditate on Him there, perceive Him, and stabilize their minds there, are those who have placed their minds solely on Shiva. In the Puranam of Thirukannappadeva Nayanar, we saw that "those who ascend the great staircase of tattvas and reach Shiva, who is the command, are those who embrace Him." That command refers to Shiva's compassion, Shiva's power, Shiva's grace, Shiva's mercy. Shiva is the consciousness that stands as pure knowledge, without looking at external objects. Since Shiva removes the animality of souls and reveals their Shivahood, doing so out of mercy, "Shiva who is the command" refers to Shiva's grace, mercy, and compassion. That compassion is Ambika, the power of Shiva, whom we praise. In that command, in the Nadhanta, in that supreme space, in that supreme ether, Lord Shiva shines as the complete, supreme light, in His own form.

Beyond the realms of creation, sustenance, dissolution, obscuration, and grace, without focusing on external objects, the consciousness that stands as pure knowledge, pervading everywhere in that Nadhanta, in the supreme ether, as existence, self-luminous, and a mass of knowledge, is called Nishkala Shiva. His abode is the thousand-petalled, pure, supportless lotus called Amala Niradhara Kamala. By establishing their minds in that Nadhanta, these great souls, having their minds fixed on Shiva, are called "Chithaththaich Sivan Pale Vaiththar" (those who placed their minds solely on Shiva). They are the ones who have attained the supreme liberation of Shiva Sayujya. This state of realizing Shiva, merging with Him, and forgetting oneself, as described, is called Shiva Yoga Samadhi. This is what Srimat Umapathi Sivacharyar Peruman states in the Thiruththondar Purana Saram: "Are they not the ones who rule us, who have placed their minds solely on Shiva, having cast aside the senses and internal organs that wander, and having focused on the Nada that pervades the central nadi, and having transcended the lives of the five, including the causal Brahma, and having seen the effulgence of the full, true, supreme light above, and having merged with the experience of Shiva-anubhava?" The details of this Shiva Yoga Samadhi are extensively described in Saiva Siddhanta scriptures, Kashikandam, Vayu Samhita, Thiruchendur Agaval, and in Shiva Agamas like Thirumandiram. Let us continue to reflect on all of them. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 02 — Chithaththaich Sivan Pale Vaiththar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam Part 997, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. The Puranam of Chithaththaich Sivan Pale Vaiththar, from the chapter "Paththarai Paniwaar Sarukkam". Those who placed their minds solely on Shiva are those whose minds have attained Shiva. The state of realizing Shiva, merging with Him, and forgetting oneself through Shiva Yoga Samadhi is called Shiva Yoga Samadhi. This is extensively discussed in texts like Kashikandam, Vayu Samhita, Thiruchendur Agaval, and in Shiva Agama scriptures. Those who have placed their minds solely on Shiva are Jivanmuktas (liberated souls) who have attained liberation in this very life. The yoga described here is praised as Shiva Yoga. In the Shiva Agamas, four paths are discussed: Charya, Kriya, Yoga, and Jnana. The Yoga mentioned here is Shiva Yoga. This is different from Patanjali's Ashtanga Yoga, which is generally known. Patanjali's Ashtanga Yoga focuses on Manolaya (dissolution of the mind) as its ultimate goal, where all external and internal instruments and faculties merge into the soul. However, the Shiva Yoga praised here is a progressive, step-by-step method (sopana murai) of Shiva Charya, Shiva Kriya, and Shiva Yoga, which serves as a means to attain Shiva Jnana and thereby achieve a state of inseparable union with Shiva. In the Puranam of Thiruvathavooradigal, in the Thirupperundurai Sarukkam, Shiva Yoga is discussed. It describes how Shiva Yogis attain Shiva Sarupya (attainment of Shiva's form) by suppressing the three gunas (Sattva, Rajas, Tamas) and the five senses (sound, touch, form, taste, smell), raising the Mulavayu (prana vayu from Muladhara), completely blocking the Ida and Pingala nadis, opening the Sushumna nadi (the central nadi), and perceiving with the eye of wisdom the nature of the five letters becoming one letter amidst the ceaseless sound of anklets of the divine dance subtly performed in the Ajna chakra (between the eyebrows), the seat of meditation, and entering and merging into the supreme space where divine grace flows. Suppressing the three gunas with the five senses means preventing them from following the mind's impulses and stabilizing them through Dharana. The statement "blocking the two paths, Ida and Pingala" implies that they were previously open. The phrase "opening one path" indicates that the Sushumna was previously blocked and is now open. Thus, meditating by perceiving the five letters as one, as taught by the Jnana Guru (spiritual master), and immersing oneself in the supreme ether means that the meditator (soul) and the meditation both disappear, and only Shiva, the object of meditation, shines forth. Thiruchitrambala Nadigal's Thiruchendur Agaval explains this in great detail. The "true path" is praised as Charya, Kriya, Yoga, and Jnana. Charya is the external activity of the body and senses, a form of worship performed according to Shiva Agama rules, focusing on the physical form of Lord Shiva. The eyes see the divine form, the ears hear the glory of the Lord's qualities, the mouth praises, the feet circumambulate the temple, the hands fold in prayer to the Lord, and other senses and faculties remain in their respective places through this devotion. For those who practice Charya worship, Lord Shiva, like ghee hidden in curd, remains hidden in the divine form and bestows grace. Shiva Charya, as an indirect benefit, grants Saloka Mukti (liberation of residing in Shiva's world) in the Shiva Loka of the Bhuvanapatis below Shrikanta Bhuvana and Suddha Vidya. It also serves as a means to awaken Jnana (knowledge) and thereby leads to supreme liberation through that Jnana. Next is Shiva Kriya, which is the worship of the formless-form (aruvuruva thirumeni) through external and internal activities by those who have attained Manda Shakti Nipatha (a mild descent of divine power), received special initiation, somewhat controlled their minds, and largely directed their senses. Internally, worship is performed in the heart through the mind, meditation in the Ajna chakra (between the eyebrows), and homa (fire ritual) in the navel. Externally, worship is performed on the Linga form and through homa in the fire with the senses. Through this, the internal organs and senses become focused and participate in the worship. This Kriya path, as an indirect benefit, grants Shiva Samipya Mukti (liberation of proximity to Shiva). It also serves as a means to generate true knowledge and thereby leads to supreme liberation through that true knowledge. Beyond these, the third stage is Shiva Yoga. This is what is extensively and subtly discussed in the Puranam of Chithaththaich Sivan Pale Vaiththar. Shiva Yoga is a formless worship. Generally, Yoga is defined as restraining the mind that wanders through the senses, bringing it under control through Rechaka, Puraka, and Kumbhaka (breathing exercises), and focusing it on a single point. Worshipping the formless Lord through knowledge is the best method. This Yoga path serves as an initial step towards that knowledge-based worship. The Dhyana (meditation) that comes in this Jnana Yoga serves as a means for it. Those who practice Yoga must necessarily follow Yamas and Niyamas. They must always adopt the means to remove impurities. They must always wear sacred ash (Thiruneeru). They must meditate on the seven crore mantra forms. They must perform temple worship. They must reduce food, avoid meat, salt, sour, and bitter tastes. They must eat when hungry. They must not sleep during the day. All these are certainly required practices. Avoiding killing and other such actions is called Samhara Yoga. This is also a great vow. These are the general dharmas that a Yogi must strictly adhere to for inner purity. The original impurity, Anava Mala, and the hostile forces like Kama (desire), Krodha (anger), etc., which are enemies of the soul, are called the Asuras (demons) of Manda Kkol. To drive away these demonic qualities, one meditates on and recites the Gayatri Mantra during Sandhyavandanam (twilight worship) daily, takes the mantra-infused water in hand, and sprinkles it like an arrow upon them. Kama, Krodha, etc., these six enemies, are summarized as three in ethical texts: Kama (desire), Veguli (anger), and Mayakkam (delusion). There is also Pranava Yoga. When the impurities in the soul are removed, knowledge arises. However, due to habitual tendencies, if the bondage attacks, the arisen knowledge is obstructed. Therefore, to prevent knowledge from being obstructed and to remove the remnants of bondage, the appropriate means is Pranava Yoga. This is extensively discussed in the Upanishads. The Kaivalya Upanishad of the Krishna Yajurveda states that by taking the Atman as the lower Arani (fire-stick) and Pranava as the upper Arani, and by repeatedly practicing the churning of meditation, a wise person will remove bondage and attain liberation. The abode of Lord Shiva, who is Pranava, is the Dwadasanta in the body. One must ascend through the path of Pranava Yoga, guided by the Guru's teachings, using Pranava as the chariot. After that, one needs Kevala Bhavanatita (transcendence of mere imagination) and Rudropasana (worship of Rudra) in the Sushupti state. When that too slips away without slipping, the blissful state of consciousness, where one becomes Him and He becomes one, will be attained. That is the state of "being still." That is the state of "sleeping without sleeping and attaining bliss." The Rig Veda Nirmana Upanishad refers to this as Shiva Yoga Nidra. This Pranava Yoga is the supreme means to attain liberation. Thirunavukkarasu Peruman has also sung, "If you churn with the staff of relationship and the rope of awareness, He will appear before you." The Svetasvatara and other Upanishads state that one can attain supreme bliss and liberation through the experience of the Pranava Mahamantra. All these described are Yoga in Charya. Similarly, there are four stages: Yoga in Kriya, Yoga in Yoga, and Yoga in Jnana. The second part, Yoga in Kriya, is explained as follows. The state of meditating on the Lord's feet with a mind that has dissolved into its origin in the Ajna chakra (between the eyebrows), the seat of the mind, and with the Prana Vayu held motionless, dispelling the darkness of ignorance with the luminous knowledge, is called Kriya Yoga. When breathing, the Prana Vayu travels 12 inches outwards. When inhaling, it returns 8 inches. The remaining 4 inches do not return and dissipate. Due to this dissipation, the allotted lifespan in a birth decreases, and the body weakens and perishes. Therefore, means are described to protect the body from perishing. "Prana" refers to Prana Vayu. The Prana Vayu, originating from the base of the Omkara within the upward-pointing triangle above the Muladhara, at the center of the base of the triangle, flows directly into the Sushumna. However, since the Sushumna is blocked, it does not flow through the Sushumna. Instead, it flows sometimes through the Ida nadi and sometimes through the Pingala nadi, getting obstructed at all the knots, scattering, and rising upwards to circulate the skull and exit through the nostrils. These three—Ida, Pingala, and Sushumna—are the nadis associated with Prana Vayu. We saw that the Prana Vayu exits 12 inches through the nostrils, and only 8 inches return, with 4 inches dissipating. Vasi Yoga is performed to compensate for this loss of 4 inches that dissipates outwards. The total number of breaths in a day is 21,600. Of these, 14,400 breaths return, and 7,200 breaths dissipate without returning. This daily dissipation of breaths shortens lifespan. Yoga texts describe how to compensate for this loss of breath. When one performs Rechaka for 32 matras, Puraka for 16 matras, and Kumbhaka for 64 matras, this balance can be achieved. This Pranayama Yoga, which involves drawing back all the breath that went out without dissipating the four inches, is called "preventing decay." Now, there are 72,000 nadis. Among these, 10 are prominent. Texts describe how to purify them and how to stop the ten types of vayu flowing within these 10 nadis. Nadis are like tubes. There is space within them. There are 72,000 nadis, and 72,000 types of vayu flow through them. Among them, the 10 prominent nadis are Ida, Pingala, Sushumna, Gandhari, Hasti, Sihguvai, Alambudai, Purudan, Sankini, and Kuhutham. The 10 types of vayu flowing through these 10 nadis are Prana, Apana, Vyana, Udana, Samana, Nagan, Kurman, Krikaran, Devadattan, and Dhananjayan. Stopping the vayu in these 10 nadis is Pranayama Yoga, which involves drawing in all 10 nadis simultaneously. Among these 10 nadis, the Sushumna nadi is the central nadi, located between the Vina Danda (spinal column), the body, and the other nadis. On either side of the Sushumna nadi are the Ida and Pingala nadis. The Sushumna nadi is the Sambhavi Shakti, the best of all nadis. This nadi, called Parai, is said to dissolve into the Supreme, be free from impurities, and stand as the form of Brahman, hence it is called Brahma Nadi. The Sushumna, originating from the Muladhara, within the upward-pointing triangle in the square, between the two lower corners of the triangle, holding the base of the Atri Sona, curving like Omkara to the right, descending, folding, splitting in the middle of Omkara, rising directly with a triangular head, passing through the spinal column called Brahma Danda, and reaching the skull. In the middle of the Sushumna, in the navel, there is a spherical nadi called Vilambini. From it, 72,000 nerves arise and spread upwards, downwards, and sideways like a net. The base of the Sushumna nadi is like a split bamboo flute. Just as one tube is inserted into another, the Chitra nadi is inside the Sushumna nadi, and the Vajra nadi is inside the Chitra nadi. The Kundalini Shakti follows this Sushumna nadi. The body of the Sushumna nadi is of three types: Sthula (gross), Sukshma (subtle), and Para (supreme). The gross form of the Sushumna nadi is the virya (essence) in the Muladhara. Its subtle form is the Bindu in the Ajna chakra. Its supreme form is the Amrita (nectar) in the Chandra Mandala. The Sushumna nadi shines as the form of the sun, continuously flowing between Ida and Pingala, from Muladhara to Brahmarandhra and from Brahmarandhra to Muladhara. Now, the Ida and Pingala nadis. In the Muladhara, at the lower left corner of the upward-pointing triangle, the Ida nadi arises, and at the lower right corner, the Pingala nadi arises. These two nadis, desiring the Sushumna that flows upwards, continuously twist and turn, forming a curve at the level of the triangular head, another curve where they are threaded into the Vilambini nadi in the navel, another curve in the heart, another in the throat, and another in the forehead, and then they leave the Sushumna nadi and reside at the tip of the nose. These curves are called Panchagranthi (five knots). We saw these curves in five places, and they are called Panchagranthi. The Ida nadi is associated with the left nostril. Its presiding deity is the moon. The air inside the Ida nadi is cool. The Pingala nadi is associated with the right nostril. Its presiding deity is the sun. The air inside the Pingala nadi is hot. Therefore, among the 72,000 nadis, the best are Ida, Pingala, and Sushumna. Pranayama Yoga involves controlling the Prana Vayu separately in each of these, and then separating it. Now, the movements of vayu in the Agni Mandala, Surya Mandala, Chandra Mandala, and through the path of Prasada Kala, and directly at the tip of the nose, as it ascends, descends, and circulates, are described. Agni, Surya, and Chandra refer to their respective mandalas. The Agni Mandala pervades the Muladhara and Swadhisthana chakras. The Surya Mandala pervades the Manipura and Anahata chakras. The Chandra Mandala pervades the Vishuddhi and Ajna chakras, and the top part above them. In these mandalas and through the path of Prasada Kala, there are 16 Prasada Kalas. They are Medha Kala, Arkisa Kala, Visha Kala, Bindu Kala, Ardha Chandra Kala, Nirodhi Kala, Nada Kala, Nadhanta Kala, Shakti Kala, Vyapini Kala, Vyamarupa Kala, Anantai Kala, Anadhai Kala, Anasirutai Kala, Samanai Kala, Unmanai Kala, from Medha to Unmana, these 16 are Prasada Kalas. The word Prasada means "that which grants the state of Shiva Jnana." Directing the vayu in the three mandalas mentioned above is Pranayama Yoga called "Mandalathu Aakkal." Directing the vayu in the Prasada path is Pranayama Yoga called "Kala Vazhiyil Niruththal." The phrase "Muccaramum Nere Pukka Idam" (the place where all three breaths directly enter) refers to "Saram" meaning breath. "Muccaram" refers to the movement of breath in the left nadi (Ida), the right nadi (Pingala), and the central nadi (Sushumna). These three breath movements. Ida, Pingala, and Sushumna, starting from the Muladhara, penetrate all six chakras and three mandalas, and then Ida and Pingala join with Sushumna and circulate up and down. Now, the Kundalini, like a snake, with its tail upwards and head downwards, coiled three and a half times, breathing and sleeping. This Kundalini rises from the Muladhara, ascends through the Sushumna nadi, awakens Shiva Jnana, and bestows liberation through that knowledge. This is called Kundala Yoga. Kundali is a power in the form of a snake with one head and one tail. It is in the Muladhara. It is almost like the spear in Murugan's hand. Following the tradition of worshipping Murugan's spear itself as Murugan, Kundalini Shakti is referred to as Murugan. We can recall Devaraya Swamigal's statement in Kanda Sashti Kavasam, "Kundaliyām Shiva Gugan dinam varuga" (May Shiva Gugan, who is Kundali, come daily). This Kundalini will be coiled in three and a half circles. The sounds of the inner and outer breath of Kundalini Shakti are the Ajapa Mantra. Vinayagar Agaval states, "The Ajapa that is united with Kundali is the mantra that arises." Among Yogis who understand the yogic tradition, this Mulakanal (primordial fire) affects the Kundalini Shakti. When one practices the method of firmly holding and moving the air within the body, the Mulakanal, which rises with light, affects the Kundalini Shakti. That Kundalini is raised upwards. From the Muladhara lotus, like a delicate thread shining within the lotus stem, subtly like a flash of lightning, it rises and travels upwards through the spinal column (Vina Danda) along with air and fire. This Kundalini Shakti breaks through the Brahma Granthi in the Sushumna, ascends further, breaks through the Vishnu Granthi, ascends even further, breaks through the Rudra Granthi, and reaches its destination. Beyond that, it moves the Bindu Sthana in the Ajna chakra. At that moment, the union of the sun and moon occurs. Thus, when the Kundalini Shakti, having risen upwards through the Sushumna nadi in the Vina Danda, reaches the thousand-petalled lotus in the Jnana Chandra Mandala in the supreme ether, knowledge arises. Through knowledge, the destruction of bondage and the enjoyment of Shiva-bhoga occur. This supreme ether is the Dwadasanta region, two inches above the forehead, at the end of the upper part of the skull. Therefore, the principle explained here is that the Kundalini Shakti is moved and raised upwards, connecting the three mandalas—Agni Mandala, Surya Mandala, and Chandra Mandala—respectively with the base, middle, and top, and passing through the inner channel of the Vina Danda, which extends from the Muladhara to the Brahmarandhra. Vinayagar Agaval states this as "Mundiya thoonin naandril paambin navil unarthi" (awakening the snake in the tongue of the pillar that stands in the three mandalas). It is also said that Kundalini can be awakened through the path of Upasana. Let us continue to reflect on Shiva Yoga. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 03 — Chithaththaich Sivan Pale Vaiththar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam Part 998, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. The Puranam of Chithaththaich Sivan Pale Vaiththar, from the chapter "Paththarai Paniwaar Sarukkam". The "three-coiled flame" of the Muladhara is located between the anus and the genitals. The Muladhara is square-shaped. Within this square, there is an upward-pointing triangle. Within this triangle, there are three locations of Agni Bija (seed of fire). When Prana and Apana unite, the Mulakanal (primordial fire) is born. This is called the "three-coiled flame." In Vinayagar Agaval, Avvai Perumatti states, "Knowing the art of raising the three-lettered flame of the Muladhara with the feet." Now, the chakras above this are Swadhisthana and others. One meditates on the luminous Shakti with the fire of the Muladhara triangle and pushes the Vayu upwards in Kumbhaka. One restrains the Prana Vayu from flowing out through the Ida and Pingala nadis by blocking those paths, opens the Sushumna, and makes it flow through the Sushumna nadi. The Sushumna, located below the navel, has roots pointing upwards and branches pointing downwards, and it spreads throughout the body as a pathway for Vayus. That is, if a tree is placed upside down, the roots would be upwards and the branches downwards, similar to that. The pathways for Vayus are 72,000 nadis. All these nadis have openings everywhere. Through yogic practice, if one blocks all these openings with the flame that arises in the Muladhara, the Prana Vayu will flow upwards through the Sushumna, which is in the middle of the Vina Danda. This is called the path to liberation. If the openings of the other nadis are not blocked, it leads to rebirth. That is called the path of conception. The Yoga Shikha Upanishad discusses this in detail. That is why it is said that what is directly opposite to Swadhisthana is the path of conception, and if it is not blocked, the fire of desire will increase, and the yogic effort will be ruined. Therefore, one must block the path of conception and all other nadis and raise the Vayu upwards through the Sushumna. The six chakras are arranged in the form of lotus petals. One must direct the Prana Vayu in six different ways in the six chakras, starting from Muladhara. Meditating by identifying oneself with the divine form of Shakti in each of the six chakras is called Adhara Yoga. Now, according to the creation sequence of the Prasada Kalas, one must establish the divine power in the Ajna chakra (between the eyebrows), which is the culmination of the 12 Kalas, in the Sushumna Sthana, and understand through meditation the unified state where the Prana Vayu does not flow through the Ida and Pingala nadis but remains stable in the Sushumna. As soon as the Prana becomes stable in the Sushumna, the moon and sun, the presiding deities of Ida and Pingala nadis, merge into the Sushumna, and the Prana merges into the Sushumna, and the mind merges into the Sushumna. At that time, the experience of blissful rasa arises. The Yoga Shikha Upanishad refers to the mind as the moon and Prana as the sun. Therefore, the Ajna chakra (between the eyebrows), which is above the six chakras, three mandalas, and three granthis, is the culmination of the 12. Thus, the Ajna chakra, which is the culmination of the 12, can be calculated in this way. Vayu, along with the mind and consciousness, all merge here. The union of the sun and moon is called Hatha Yoga. Thus, Kriya Yoga is explained. Now, the third path, Yoga in Yoga. This third path, Yoga in Yoga, is called Niradhara Yoga. Meditating as instructed by the Guru, following the methods of Prasada Panchakshara, etc., is called Niradhara Yoga. Ajapa Yoga also has this characteristic. A tortoise must protect its five limbs from external attacks. When external attacks occur, the tortoise, called Kapadam, withdraws its five limbs inside and remains still. Similarly, without worldly desires, without being controlled by them, by suppressing the power of the jnanendriyas (organs of knowledge) and karmendriyas (organs of action), with the mind and consciousness still, one melts the lower part of the Chandra Mandala with the three-rising flame in the Muladhara, drinks the milk-nectar that drips through the inner tongue, and fills the entire body. These actions, these karmas, bind the mind with the merits and demerits they produce. Therefore, births occur. So, the Yoga Shikha Upanishad states that if you restrain this mind and practice silence, merits and demerits will cease to arise, and consequently, births will cease. This is also called Laya Yoga. This section serves as an explanation of that. The Shiva Agamas refer to this as "Shiva Aham Shivoham" in Siddhanta. Siddhanta, which clarifies Vedanta, states that both these visualizations are equivalent. Srimat Madhava Sivajnana Munivar Peruman, in his Sivajnana Bodha Mahabhashyam, has explained about Ajapa. He states that the subtle Panchakshara, transforming into Ajapa, controls and releases the Prana Vayu, thereby causing alternating experiences of thought and forgetfulness, which are Sakala and Kevala states. Therefore, Vinayagar Agaval also states, "The state of the goad in the six chakras is the great dance." Avvai Perumatti says this. The Ajapa that travels through the Sushumna is the Nada form, the form of Kundalini Shakti. Vinayagar Agaval previously stated, "The Ajapa that is united with Kundali." Therefore, this yogic practice of manifesting Lord Shiva in each chakra is called the method by which Ajapa Shakti stands as the form of Shiva. Thus, Yoga in Yoga is explained. Therefore, Shiva Siddhanta scriptures state that Panchakshara Yoga is an excellent means for removing the vasana mala (impurity of habitual tendencies). In this Sri Panchakshara, the method of the three entities—Pati (Lord), Pasu (soul), and Pasam (bondage)—is described. The subtle Panchakshara consists of Sikaram, Vakaram, Yakaram, Nakaram, and Makaram. Here, Sikaram is Shiva, Vakaram is Arul Shakti (graceful power), Yakaram is the soul, Nakaram is the Tirodhana Shakti (obscuring power) that unites with the impurity to bring about the soul's maturity, and Makaram is the dark Anava Mala (primordial impurity) that acts like darkness, being associated with that Tirodhana. Therefore, in this Sri Panchakshara, the aspects of Pati, namely Shiva, grace, and Tirodhana Shakti, reside in Sikaram, Vakaram, and Nakaram. The soul, Pasu, resides in Yakaram. The impurity, Pasam, resides in Makaram. This is how the three entities—Pati, Pasu, and Pasam—reside in this Sri Panchakshara. In the traditional, oral lineage, they say: Porul (substance), Arul (grace), Therul (clarity), Marul (delusion), Irul (darkness). 'Si' is Shiva, which is Porul. 'Va' is Arul. 'Ya' is Therul. 'Na' is Marul. 'Ma' is Irul. Sri Panchakshara is of five types: Sthula Panchakshara (Namashivaya), Sukshma Panchakshara (Shivaya Nama), Karana Panchakshara (Shivaya Shiva), Mahakarana Panchakshara (Shiva), and Mahamanu (Si). Among these, excluding the Sthula Panchakshara, the remaining four are Mukti Panchakshara. In the Sthula Panchakshara, which begins with Nakaram, Yakaram is at the end. This is taught by both the teaching Guru and the practicing Guru. It bestows benefits in this world and the next. This Sthula Panchakshara is auspicious for all castes and those who are initiated. Here, the Nakaram and Makaram at the beginning represent Pasam (bondage), the Sikaram and Vakaram in the middle represent Pati (Lord), and the Yakaram at the end represents Pasu (soul). This implies that Pati is the one who activates Pasam, which is insentient, and Pasu, which is sentient. Next comes the Sukshma Panchakshara, which is the path to Jnana. Chanting this Sri Panchakshara, which begins with Sikaram, gradually brings about the maturation of impurities. Those who desire liberation should chant the Sukshma Panchakshara. In Tamil, this is called "Vasi." In Sanskrit, it stands as "Vasi." The Mahamanu is "Si." At that time, the soul, without being "you" and "I," stands as pure consciousness, pervading everywhere. This is the great secret of this Mahamanu. This is also the state of perfect fullness. This is realized by Shiva Jnanis through experience. This Mahamanu is also called "Iraiezhuthu" (divine letter), "Peruvezhuthu" (great letter), "Pesaa Ezhuthu" (unspoken letter), and "Brahma Bija" (seed of Brahman). Thus, the Prana Vayu, moving upwards with the chanting of "So'ham," will stabilize in each chakra from Muladhara to Brahmarandhra. When the Vayu stabilizes, the mind dissolves. In Manolaya, the vision of the respective chakra deity occurs. This practice leads to becoming one with that deity. The Vedanta Upanishad refers to this as "Sa Aham So'ham." The Shiva Agamas refer to this as "Shiva Aham Shivoham" in Siddhanta. Siddhanta, which clarifies Vedanta, states that both these visualizations are equivalent. Srimat Madhava Sivajnana Munivar Peruman, in his Sivajnana Bodha Mahabhashyam, has explained about Ajapa. He states that the subtle Panchakshara, transforming into Ajapa, controls and releases the Prana Vayu, thereby causing alternating experiences of thought and forgetfulness, which are Sakala and Kevala states. Therefore, Vinayagar Agaval also states, "The state of the goad in the six chakras is the great dance." Avvai Perumatti says this. The Ajapa that travels through the Sushumna is the Nada form, the form of Kundalini Shakti. Vinayagar Agaval previously stated, "The Ajapa that is united with Kundali." Therefore, this yogic practice of manifesting Lord Shiva in each chakra is called the method by which Ajapa Shakti stands as the form of Shiva. Thus, Yoga in Yoga is explained. Now, let us continue to reflect on the fourth path, Yoga in Jnana. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 05 — Chithaththaich Sivan Pale Vaiththar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam Part 1000, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. The Puranam of Chithaththaich Sivan Pale Vaiththar, from the chapter "Paththarai Paniwaar Sarukkam". We have seen Shiva Yoga, which is union with Shiva, and then Shiva Bhoga, which is immersion in Shiva. The four stages of Saiva Siddhanta scriptures—Charya, Kriya, Yoga, and Jnana—include the true knowledge, Nishta, which consists of ten stages called Dasakaryam: Tattva Rupam, Tattva Darshanam, Tattva Suddhi, Atma Rupam, Atma Darshanam, Atma Suddhi, Shiva Rupam, Shiva Darshanam, Shiva Yogam, and Shiva Bhogam. Firstly, Tattva Rupam signifies the soul's method of performing Tattva research. Rupam means characteristic, so knowing the characteristics of the Tattvas is called Tattva Rupam. This involves knowing the characteristics of the 36 Tattvas and their derivatives. In the Anadi Kevala state, the soul, unable to know the Lord who is eternally with it due to the obstruction of Anava Mala, suffers under its bondage. Out of compassion for this suffering, the Lord, like giving a staff to a blind person, bestows a body upon the soul for the removal of impurities. From this point, the Lord bestows Tanu (body), Karana (instruments), Bhuvana (worlds), and Bhoga (experiences) upon the soul, intending to bring about its maturity. Knowing the characteristics of these 36 Tattvas and their derivatives is Tattva Rupam. Next is Tattva Darshanam, the second stage. The soul, having performed Tattva research, realizes that these Tattvas are not itself. This is Tattva Darshanam. The five Shiva Tattvas, seven Vidya Tattvas, and twenty-four Atma Tattvas—all these function only when united with the soul; they do not function independently. Moreover, they are insentient. When the soul perceives that all these are known by it and are not itself, that is Tattva Darshanam. Thirdly, Tattva Suddhi. The soul, having attained Tattva Darshanam, realizes the method of removing them. This is Tattva Suddhi. As mentioned above, the soul, having realized through intellectual inquiry that the 36 Tattvas are insentient and are known by it, yet unable to detach itself from them through experiential knowledge, approaches a Jnana Satguru, requesting the way to remove them. By the grace of the Jnana Acharya, when these are revealed, just as one who, having gone thirsty to a mirage, upon inquiry realizes it is not real water and cannot quench thirst, no longer desires it, similarly, the delusion of identifying with the body, instruments, etc., formed by these Tattva groups as "I" and "mine" is removed. With the removal of delusion, the consciousness that follows them ceases. When it ceases, the soul realizes that the Tattvas appeared as intelligent entities due to its own ignorance, and that in reality, these Tattva groups are insentient. Thus, this soul attains liberation from them. With this liberation, the soul realizes that it is a conscious entity, that the Tattvas are impermanent while it is eternal, and that it is distinct from these Tattvas, thus realizing its pervasive nature. That is Tattva Suddhi. After this, fourthly, Atma Rupam. This signifies the soul's method of realizing its own nature. By the Guru's grace, the soul realizes that the Tattvas are insentient and that it is a conscious entity. Through this realization, the soul understands its true nature, that it has no independence, as it remembers and forgets these Tattvas. Therefore, it realizes that there is a Lord who is independent, and He, in accordance with our Karma, stands as the knowledge within our knowledge, revealing the truth. This is Atma Rupam. Fifthly, Atma Darshanam. The soul, having realized its own nature, through contemplation, realizes that it has no action apart from Lord Shiva. This is Atma Darshanam. Realizing that it has no ability to know unless the Lord reveals it, and that it is the divine grace that makes it know, makes it act, and makes it experience, and then transcending even that realization, is Atma Darshanam. Sixthly, Atma Suddhi. The soul, having realized itself, realizes the method of being absorbed in the pervasiveness of Lord Shiva. This is Atma Suddhi. The soul, unable to know or act on its own without Shiva's grace, must remain absorbed in the divine grace, realizing that all its actions are actions of grace. When, through Pati Jnana, the divine vision arises where Shiva is oneself, and oneself is Shiva, pervading without separation, that is Atma Suddhi. Seventhly, Shiva Rupam. This signifies the method of having the vision of the divine Guru, who is the embodied form of Lord Shiva. The soul, having attained Mala Paripakam (maturation of impurities) and Shakti Nipatham (descent of divine power), will have the Supreme Being, who has been revealing from within, appear before it as the Guru, taking on a human form. He will explain the method of being in a non-dual state through instruction. When explained, the soul will realize without doubt or misconception in its heart that the one explaining is Lord Shiva Himself. Thus, knowing that Shiva has taken a divine form is Shiva Rupam. Furthermore, perceiving all sentient and insentient objects as Shiva, because the Lord pervades them as the indwelling spirit, is also Shiva Rupam. Eighthly, Shiva Darshanam. The soul, having had the vision of the Jnana Guru, realizes the method of having the vision of Lord Shiva's graceful form. This is Shiva Darshanam. It is the experience of perceiving the full bliss of the Lord everywhere, without any worldly pleasures appearing anywhere. Ninthly, Shiva Yogam. The soul, having attained Shiva Darshanam, realizes the method of uniting inseparably with Shiva. This is Shiva Yogam. Yoga means union. The union of the soul with Shiva is Shiva Yogam. Those who have attained this are Shiva Yogis. They are the ones praised in the Puranam of Chithaththaich Sivan Pale Vaiththar. How are they united with Shiva? What is the method of that union? When any of the seven objects of senses come to them in accordance with their karma, they will confront that object and detach themselves, realizing it is a product of Maya. They will realize that the pleasant and unpleasant experiences arising from objects are due to karma, and thus detach themselves from the objects. Even though the soul is eternal, perfect, and conscious, it does not have the independent ability to know. Therefore, insentient objects also do not come to it. Thus, perceiving the divine grace within the soul as the Lord's grace that brings us objects, makes us know them, and makes us experience them, and that it is the divine grace that drives and makes us experience them, is Shiva Yogam. Realizing that the Lord, who is inseparable from divine grace like fire from heat, out of His great compassion for us, came as the Guru in human form and taught us, and then standing as the Lord Himself, who is omnipresent within grace, is Shiva Yogam. When the soul stands in such a unified state, even when the effects of past karma (prarabdha vasana) strike, it perceives them in the Lord's grace, realizing that it is His grace that causes both good and bad actions, and that the knowledge of these good and bad things and their enjoyment are also His grace, and that it has no action whatsoever apart from His grace, and thus remains in the action of grace. This is Shiva Yogam. Therefore, to summarize Shiva Yogam, it is the state of perceiving the soul within the object, grace within the soul, and the Lord within grace, and realizing that there is nothing apart from the Lord, and becoming one with that Lord. Finally, the tenth stage is Shiva Bhogam, which is supreme liberation. The soul united with Shiva. The method by which the soul united with Shiva experiences Shiva Ananda is Shiva Bhogam. Experiencing Shiva Ananda non-dually with Shiva Jnana, while united with Shiva, is Shiva Bhogam. Remembering the great compassion of the Lord who nurtured it from the Kevala state, immense love for the Supreme Being Shiva arises, and the Lord continuously bestows supreme bliss using that immense love as the cause. That is Shiva Bhogam. Just as the eye, united with the sun's radiance, knows the sun, the soul, united with Parasakti, experiences Shiva Ananda non-dually. That is Shiva Bhogam. Here, the only object of knowledge (jneyam) is Shiva. Shiva, who is the embodiment of knowledge, makes the knowledge of the soul, which is the knower (jnana svarupi), merge into His own knowledge, becoming one with it. Yet, by nature, He is distinct from the soul, and out of compassion, He takes the soul as His form and remains with it. Therefore, in Nishta, the soul experiences Shiva Ananda. In that state of union, the soul does not stand apart from Shiva. That bliss cannot be communicated to others. For those who experience this Shiva Bhogam, the impurities of Mala, Maya, and Karma, which cause limited knowledge, distorted knowledge, and attachment/aversion, are completely removed. Even when they are not in Nishta, they will only perceive the Supreme through supreme knowledge; they will not perceive other objects. Thus, the soul that has experienced Shiva will, as long as it inhabits this body, perform actions driven by the karma that sustains this body. However, these actions will never appear as the soul's actions, but rather as Shiva's actions, because the soul remains in the pervasiveness of grace. When the soul remains in this state, the Lord, taking all the soul's perfect knowledge as His own form, and being the life of all that perfect knowledge, reveals all the soul's knowledge, action, and experiences as His own perfect bliss, and remains non-dual with the soul, immersing it in His own blissful fulfillment. This overflow of Shiva Ananda is the method of Shiva Bhogam. Srimat Thayumanavar Swamigal states, "The boundless flood of Shiva Bhogam, which is omnipresent, full, and one, is overflowing." Thus, the soul that serves the Lord as one with Him, without forgetting the Lord's divine compassion, will find its immense love for the Lord continuously growing and overflowing. When that love overflows, the soul experiences the supreme bliss of Shiva Ananda, which manifests as love itself. The Lord makes the soul experience this. This state is the Turiyatita Paramukti state; there is no state higher than this. The words of Srimat Manikkavasagar Swamigal in Thiruvachagam, "Ammaiye Appa Oppilla Maniye Anbinil Vilaitha Aramuthe" (Mother, Father, peerless Jewel, rare nectar born of love), convey that Shiva manifests in the maturation of ever-growing love. We can understand from the words of Srimat Manikkavasagar Swamigal, "The boundless joy that transcends all incomparable states is my love," that love and Shiva stand non-dually. This is what the 11th Sutra of Sivajnana Bodham states: "The feet of the Lord are attained by unwavering love." "Ayaramai" (unwavering) refers to not forgetting the Lord's divine compassion, which is an activity of Jnana (knowledge). The love that grows due to this unwavering remembrance is an activity of Iccha (desire). The resulting experience of Shiva Ananda, Shiva Bhogam, is an activity of Kriya (action). This Sutra explains how the soul's Iccha, Jnana, and Kriya manifest and function in Shiva Bhogam. Therefore, in liberation, the soul experiences supreme bliss, Shiva Ananda. Those who experience this supreme bliss, Shiva Ananda, while still in the body, are called Jivanmuktas. For such Jivanmuktas, their Iccha, Kriya, and Jnana do not wander to other places even if they sometimes perceive this world. If, by chance, the world appears, the Jivanmuktas, who have reached the feet of the Lord through unwavering love, will cleanse the three impurities, which are like dirt that prevents one from reaching the Lord's lotus-like feet that expand and support everything, with the water of knowledge. Due to unwavering love, they cleanse the impurities and unite with the true Jnanis who stand unblemished. They will worship the divine form of the devotees and the Shiva temples as Lord Parameswara Himself. If it is appropriate for true Jnanis who have attained liberation (Paramukti) to see Shiva everywhere without separation, then why are only the divine form of devotees and Shiva temples mentioned for worship? Because Lord Shiva, like ghee in milk, remains hidden and unmanifest in other places, but in these places, that is, in the divine form of devotees and in Shiva temples, He manifests clearly like ghee in curd. Therefore, Jivanmuktas, who have cleansed the dirt of impurities with the water of knowledge and united with devotees, will worship the divine form of the devotees and the Shiva temples as Lord Parameswara Himself. This is the essence of the twelfth Sutra of Sivajnana Bodham. The Periya Puranam, which is the Thiruththondar Puranam, serves as an illustration of this. That is why in this Puranam, union with devotees, worshipping the divine form as Shiva, visiting temples, etc., are emphasized and shown. All the Nayanmars were in this state of supreme liberation. Yet, we see in the Periya Puranam that they unite with Shiva's devotees, worship Shiva's devotees, praise the divine form, and worship temples. Therefore, we see that they did all these things and also served as guides to the world. Thus, those who stood in Shiva Yoga would attain Shiva Jnana, experience Shiva Bhogam, and be liberated souls. They are the ones whom the Thiruththondar Thiruvandhadhi praises as "the wealthy ones who have attained liberation, who placed their minds at the lotus feet of the Lord." The divine Sekkizhar Peruman has explained this in the Puranam of Chithaththaich Sivan Pale Vaiththar in the Periya Puranam as "The minds that attained Shiva reached the path of service at the feet of the Lord of the unique assembly, the cause of the Vedas, the Dancer." Thus, the Puranam of Chithaththaich Sivan Pale Vaiththar concludes. We will continue to reflect on the Puranam of Thiruvallur Piranthar. Glory, glory to the sacred feet of those who placed their minds solely on Shiva! Glory, glory to the sacred feet of our Lord Sundaramurthy Swamigal! Glory, glory to the sacred feet of Nambiyandar Nambigal Peruman! Glory, glory to the sacred feet of the divine Sekkizhar Peruman! Glory, glory to the sacred feet of the commentator, Ayya!
