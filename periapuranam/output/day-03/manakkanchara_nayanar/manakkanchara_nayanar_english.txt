Chapter 01 — Manakkanchara Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam Part 164. Ilaimalinta Sarukkam. Manakkanchara Nayanar Puranam. We have contemplated the Puranam of Kungiliya Kalaya Nayanar. In the second verse of the Thiruththondaththogai, composed by Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, the next Nayanar in sequence is Manakkanchara Nayanar. "Malai malinta thol vallal Manakkancharan" (Manakkancharan, the generous one with mountain-like shoulders) is the line sung by Sundaramoorthy Swamigal about Manakkanchara Nayanar. According to our custom, we will first contemplate a summary of Manakkanchara Nayanar's Puranam and then extensively discuss his Puranam through the Periya Puranam. In the Chola kingdom, there is a town called Kancharur. It shines as one of the best towns in the Marutha land (agricultural region). Fields and ponds glitter in that town. Due to its abundance, paddy crops grow as high as areca palms. There, mansions are surrounded by walls and adorned with festive flags and full pots. Agricultural families, excelling in farming for generations, thrived there. The streets always resonated with the sounds of festivals. In that town, Manakkanchara Nayanar, who understood the true essence, was born into the Velalan (agricultural) community. He served as a commander for the king. The Nayanar understood that devotees of Aran (Siva) were the owners of himself and his possessions, and he dedicated himself to serving Shiva's devotees, anticipating their needs and providing them even before they expressed them. For some time, the Nayanar had no children. So, he prayed to Lord Shiva for offspring. By the grace of Lord Shiva, a baby girl was born to Manakkanchara Nayanar. This female child was given to the Nayanar by the grace of God, as a path to escape the cruel cycle of birth. The Nayanar was overjoyed at having a daughter, bestowed great gifts upon everyone, and raised the girl with great care. The Nayanar's daughter reached the age of marriage. The elders arranged for her to be married to Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar. Both Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar and Manakkanchara Nayanar were very pleased and happy. Astrologers fixed an auspicious day for the wedding, suitable for both parties. Preparations for the wedding festival were underway in Manakkanchara's house and throughout the town. The groom, Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar, was also expected to arrive in Kancharur for the wedding. We will see the later history of Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar separately in his own Puranam. Manakkanchara Nayanar, one of the 63 Nayanmars, arranged for his daughter to be married to another Nayanar, Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar. Before Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar's procession reached Kancharur, Manakkanchara Nayanar's town, the Lord decided to enact a divine play. Lord Shiva assumed the guise of a Mahavrata Muni (a great ascetic). These Mahavratas, called Mahavratas in Sanskrit and Mavirathas in Tamil, belong to one of the sub-sects of Saivism. Those belonging to this Maviratha sect meditate on Shiva as a deity adorned with a garland of bones. They observe practices such as wearing bones after receiving initiation, as prescribed in their scriptures. They wear a wide cord made of hair as their sacred thread.

The Lord also comes in that Maviratha guise. Where does he come? He comes to the sacred home of Manakkanchara Nayanar, where the wedding is to take place. How does he come? His forehead shines with three lines of sacred ash. His hair is tied in a topknot, and at its tip, a bone bead is strung. In his ears are earrings made of white bones. A long chain of bone beads hangs on his chest. A yogapattai (ascetic's band) is on his shoulder. A sacred thread made of hair rope shines on his shoulder. In one hand, he holds a pouch of sacred ash, and on his forearm, a string of bone beads. He wears a loincloth around his waist, with another garment flowing over it. His sacred feet touch the ground, and the five sacred marks (Panchamudra) are visible on them. His entire body, glowing with sacred ash like fire hidden by powder, entered the home of Manakkanchara Nayanar, a devotee whose mind sought the Lord's feet. Seeing this, Manakkanchara Nayanar went forward to greet him and humbly bowed with love, saying, "I am saved by your presence here, O Lord." Then, Lord Shiva, who had come as a Mahavrati, asked, "What special event is happening here?" To this, Manakkanchara Nayanar replied, "My daughter's wedding is to take place here." Immediately, Lord Shiva, who had come as a Mahavrati, blessed him, saying, "May you have auspiciousness." Manakkanchara Nayanar then bowed to him, went inside the house, brought his daughter, who was adorned for the wedding, and made her prostrate at the feet of the Mahavrati. As the bride, Manakkanchara Nayanar's daughter, prostrated at his feet, the Mahavrati looked at her hair, which was long and dark like a cloud. Then, he looked at Manakkanchara Nayanar and said, "Her hair will serve as a Panchavadi (sacred thread made of hair) for me." Hearing this, Manakkanchara Nayanar thought, "I have been blessed to hear the Mahavrati ask for my daughter's hair." He drew his sword, cut off his daughter's hair, and offered it to the Mahavrati. Lord Shiva, who had come in the guise of a Mahavrati, stood as if to receive it, then disappeared from that spot and revealed his Rishabharooda (mounted on the bull) form to Manakkanchara Nayanar. He appeared in the sky with Goddess Parvati, mounted on the bull, to Manakkanchara Nayanar. Manakkanchara Nayanar fell prostrate, rose, and, forgetting himself, praised the Lord. Then, Lord Shiva bestowed his grace upon Manakkanchara Nayanar, saying, "We have made your love for us known to the whole world." Then, Lord Shiva granted Manakkanchara Nayanar the great blessing of always praising Him, a path from which there is no return. The Lord, surrounded by celestials praising Him, then disappeared. At the same time, the groom, Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar, arrived at the house. He heard about the event that had taken place, an act unimaginable even in thought. He remembered Shiva's grace and praised Shiva. Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar felt disheartened that he did not have the blessing of witnessing the vision of the Lord, who had first come as a Mahavrati and then appeared as Rishabharooda. Then, hearing the power of the divine words spoken by the Lord, his despondency vanished. Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar married Manakkanchara Nayanar's daughter and returned to his town with his relatives. This is a summary of Manakkanchara Nayanar's history. Now, we will continue to contemplate Manakkanchara Nayanar's Puranam in detail through the verses of Sekkizhar's Periya Puranam. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 02 — Manakkanchara Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam Part 165. Ilaimalinta Sarukkam. Manakkanchara Nayanar Puranam. In the second verse of Thiruththondaththogai, composed by Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, he sings, "I am a servant to Ilaimalinta Vel Nambi Eribaththar; I am a servant to the devotees of Enathi Nathan; I am a servant to Kalai Malinta Seer Nambi Kannappar; I am a servant to Kalayan of Kadavur." Following this, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal sings in the next line, "Malai malinta thol vallal Manakkancharan, Enjatha Vaattayan adiyarkkum adiyen" (Manakkancharan, the generous one with mountain-like shoulders, and Enjatha Vaattayan, I am a servant to their devotees). In this line, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal sings about two Nayanmars. In the first part, "Malai malinta thol vallal Manakkancharan," he refers to Manakkanchara Nayanar, and in the second part, "Enjatha Vaattayan," he refers to Vaazhththaaya Nayanar, and then sings "adiyarkku adiyen" (I am a servant to their devotees). Therefore, in the line "Malai malinta thol vallal Manakkancharan Enjatha Vaattayan adiyarkkum adiyen," the first part, "Malai malinta thol vallal Manakkancharan," refers to Manakkanchara Nayanar. We should interpret this as "I am a servant to Manakkancharan, the generous one with mountain-like shoulders," and "I am a servant to Enjatha Vaattayan." Thus, following this method, we will now look at the Puranam of Manakkanchara Nayanar, who is praised as "Malai malinta thol vallal Manakkancharan." Sekkizhar Peruman has sung the Puranam of Manakkanchara Nayanar in 37 verses. The meaning of Manakkanchara Nayanar Puranam is the section that describes the history and character of the great person who lived in the glorious town of Kancharur. In the sequential order, this is the fifth Puranam in the Ilaimalinta Sarukkam. Firstly, in the anthology Thiruththondaththogai, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal has sung, "Malai malinta thol vallal Manakkancharan adiyarkkum adiyen." Its meaning is, "I am a servant to the devotees of Manakkanchara Nayanar, the generous one whose shoulders are like mountains." Here, Manakkanchara Nayanar is described with the epithet "generous one whose shoulders are like mountains." The nature of a mountain is that it is tall, strong, glorious, unmoving in its position, and yields prosperity even when it comes (as rain), possessing qualities of generosity. Similarly, Manakkanchara Nayanar's shoulders are lofty, strong, glorious, and generous. This is because Manakkanchara Nayanar served as a commander for the king, and thus possessed high, strong, and large shoulders befitting a commander. Such a generous person, with shoulders like mountains, is Manakkanchara Nayanar. "Vallal" means one who gives without limits. This will be clearly understood through the Nayanar's history. This is the meaning of the phrase "Malai malinta thol vallal Manakkancharan" found in the primary text, Thiruththondaththogai. In the primary text, Thiruththondaththogai, it is a common practice to praise each Nayanar in half a line and say "adiyen" (I am a servant). In this instance, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal has praised two Nayanmars in one line of this verse. Similarly, he has praised and glorified two Nayanmars in the line "Karaikkandan kazhal adiye kaappukkondu iruntha Kanampulla Nambikkum Karikkum adiyen" (I am a servant to Kanampulla Nambi and Kari, who held the ankleted feet of the blue-throated one as their protection). Likewise, in the line "Madal soozhntha thar Nambi Idangazhikkum Thanjai Mannavan am Seruththunai than adiyarkkum adiyen" (I am a servant to Idangazhi Nambi, adorned with garlands, and Seruththunai, the king of Thanjai), he has praised two Nayanmars. In some places, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal has praised three Nayanmars in one line. In the line "Kaithadinta varisilaiyan Kalikkamban Kaliyan Kalarchaththi Varinjaiyarkkum

adiyarkkum adiyen" (I am a servant to Kalikkamban, Kaliyan, Kalarchaththi, and Varinjaiyar, who wielded the powerful bow), he has praised three Nayanmars. Then, in the line "Manniya seer Marai Navan Ninra Oor Poosal Varivalaiyan Maanikkum Nesanum adiyen" (I am a servant to Marai Navan of Ninra Oor, Poosal, Varivalaiyan Maanik, and Nesan), he has also praised three Nayanmars. Similarly, in some places, he has praised two Nayanmars in half a line. In "Muruganukkum Ruththira Pasupathikkum adiyen" (I am a servant to Murugan and Ruththira Pasupathi), and in "Perumizhalai Kurumbarkkum Peyarkkum adiyen" (I am a servant to Kurumbar of Perumizhalai and Peyar), and in "Naattamigu Thandikkum Moorkkarkkum adiyen" (I am a servant to Thandi and Moorkkar), in these three places, two Nayanmars are praised in half a line of a verse. In Thiruththondaththogai, there are also six places where one Nayanar is praised per line. In the line "Thiruninra Semmaye Semmayaik konda Thirunavukkaraiyan than adiyarkkum adiyen" (I am a servant to Thirunavukkarasar, who embraced righteousness as righteousness), he praises Thirunavukkarasu Peruman. In the line "Vaarkonda vanamulaiyal Umaipangan kazhale maravathu kallerinta Sakkiyarkkum adiyen" (I am a servant to Sakkiyar, who never forgot the ankleted feet of Umaipangan, whose breasts are adorned with a girdle, and threw stones), he praises Sakkiya Nayanar. In the line "Niraikonda sinthaiyan Nelveli venra Ninraseer Nedumaran adiyarkkum adiyen" (I am a servant to Ninraseer Nedumaran, who had a full mind and conquered Nelveli), he praises Ninraseer Nedumara Nayanar. In the line "Thuraikkonda sempavalam irulagattrum jothi Thonmayilai Vayilan adiyarkkum adiyen" (I am a servant to Vayilar of Thonmayilai, whose red coral dispels darkness like a light), he praises Vayilar Nayanar. In the line "Kadal soozhntha ulagellam kaakkindra Peruman Kadavarkon Kazharsingan adiyarkkum adiyen" (I am a servant to Kazharsingan, the Lord who protects the entire world surrounded by the sea, and the king of Kadavar), he praises Kazharsinga Nayanar. In the line "Pudai soozhntha puliyathal mel aravaada aadi ponnadiyke manam vaiththa Pugazhthunaikkum adiyen" (I am a servant to Pugazhthunai, who danced with a snake on his tiger skin, and whose mind was fixed on the golden feet), he praises Pugazhthunai Nayanar. Thus, in these six places, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal has sung about one Nayanar per line. Now, in two places, he has sung about Nayanmars in one and a half lines. In the one and a half lines "Vampara varivandu manam naara malarum mathumalar narkondraiyan adiyalal pena Embiran Sambandan adiyarkkum adiyen" (I am a servant to the devotees of our Lord Sambandan, who does not cherish anything but the feet of the good Kondrai flower, which blooms with fragrant honey, attracting buzzing bees), he praises Thirugnanasambandha Peruman. In the one and a half lines "Meimmaiye thirumeni vazhipada nirka vegundezhuntha thathai thal mazhuvinal erintha Ammaiyan adichchandip Perumanukku adiyen" (I am a servant to Chandeesa Peruman, who, when his father stood to worship the true form, angrily struck his father's feet with an axe), Sundaramoorthy Swamigal praises Chandeesa Nayanar. These are notes on how Sundaramoorthy Swamigal has praised Nayanmars in one and a half lines, one line, and half a line in the primary anthology, Thiruththondaththogai. We have seen the meaning of the line about Manakkanchara Nayanar in Thiruththondaththogai, composed by Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Now, the verse about the Nayanar in the classified work, Thiruththondar Thiruvandathi, is as follows: "Kalasamulai kanni kadhal puthalvi kamazh kuzhalai nal sey thavaththavan panchavadikku ivai nalka enalum alasum enak karuthathaval koonthal arinthu aliththan malai sey mathil Kanchai Manakkancharan enum Vallale." Now, let us contemplate the Puranam of Manakkanchara Nayanar through Sekkizhar Peruman's extensive work, Periya Puranam. The town where the Nayanar lived is called Kancharur. It is currently known as Ananda Thandavapuram. Ananda Thandavapuram is colloquially known as Anathandavapuram. This was called Kancharur in ancient times. The deity residing in the temple at this sacred place, Thirukkancharur, is named Panchavadeeswarar. It is very noteworthy that this name, Panchavadeeswarar, is related to the Nayanar's history. Furthermore, the Lord is also known by the names Parijathavaneswarar, Bharathvajeeswarar, and Anantha Muniswarar. This sacred place, Thirukkancharur, is located about a quarter nazhigai (a unit of time/distance) from the Anathandavapuram railway station. This Shiva temple is situated there. To the east of this place, about four nazhigai away, is the town of Kanjanagaram, colloquially known as Kanjanapuram, and to the north, about six nazhigai away, is a town known as Vadakkancharu. These confirm that this place is the Nayanar's town. About 45 years ago, some very ancient copper idols were unearthed from the ground near this place, as stated by Sivakavimani Ayya. The period when Sivakavimani Ayya states this, i.e., the period when he was writing the commentary for the second part of Periya Puranam, was 1940. So, he states that ancient copper idols were found there 45 years prior to that. Among those copper idols were those of Vinayagar, Somaskandar, Umaiyamaiyar, Chandeeswarar, Bikshatanar, Jatadhar (the Mahavirathi), Manakkancharanar, Appar Swamigal, Thirugnanasambandha Swamigal, and Kalyanasundarar (known as Kaatchinathar). All of them are worshipped in this temple. The idols of Mahavirathi and Nayanar depict all the characteristics mentioned in this Puranam. It is known that since the day the idols of Mahavirathi and Nayanar were found and worshipped, this town has been flourishing with prosperity and wealth. The lands in the upper part of this town, known as Vallathu Vazhi or Vellathu Vazhi, still yield very fertile sugarcane crops. This place is praised in the Kshetra Kovai Thiruththandagam as "Kanjanur, Kancharu, Panchakkaiyum Kailayanathanai kaanalaame" (One can see Kailayanathan in Kanjanur, Kancharu, and Panchakkai). Therefore, this place is a Thevara Vaippuththalam (a place mentioned in Thevaram hymns). This explains the antiquity and greatness of this place. There is a Sanskrit Sthala Puranam (local legend) for this place. It is elaborated in the Kshetra Kandam, Uparibhagam, chapters 61 to 69, of the Kandama Puranam. Sivakavimani Ayya writes that all these are the glories of the sacred place called Kancharur. Sekkizhar Swamigal begins the Puranam of Manakkanchara Nayanar by describing and singing about this glorious Kancharur. Kancharur is a sacred place where Lord Shiva delights to reside. This town has tall trees. There is a lot of honey in the branches of these trees. Therefore, honey flows from the beehives through the branches. The juice of ripe, luscious fruits also flows from these trees. This town has abundant sugarcane. Therefore, the juice of sugarcane flows like a river in the channels of the fields. The honey flowing from the trees, the juice of the fruits, and the sugarcane juice flowing in the river all mix together and flow like a river. This town, thus, is glorious, praised and sung by great scholars who have understood things properly. This is because great personalities like Sundaramoorthy Swamigal have sung about this town. As we have already seen, Appar Swamigal has sung about this town in the Kshetra Kovai Thiruththandagam, saying, "Kanjanur, Kancharu, Panchakkaiyum Kailayanathanai kaanalaame" (One can see Kailayanathan in Kanjanur, Kancharu, and Panchakkai). Furthermore, our teacher Sekkizhar Peruman, who is among the foremost of those who truly understand the scriptures, has also sung about the glory of this town. In this town, the juice of sugarcane flows like a river. Sekkizhar Peruman sings that the canals, carrying the honey and fruit juice mentioned above, join this river, making it fragrant with the scent of honey and fruits. Here, the honey flowing from the high branches refers to Manakkanchara Nayanar's daughter. The fruit juice mixing with the honey refers to Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar, who comes to marry her. The river of sugarcane juice flowing in the fields specifically refers to Manakkanchara Nayanar. The commentary suggests this interpretation. The natural truth of small canals joining rivers and enriching them can also be seen here. Thus, this country is part of the fertile Marutha land, rich in rivers. It is fertile with groves, fruits, and sugarcane, which are the elements of the Marutha region. And by this means of describing all its riches, Sekkizhar Peruman sings about the many glories of this country's river, country, land, city, wealth, and people in this first verse. This town had many paddy fields. During the weeding season in those paddy fields, agricultural women plucked the weeds that grew among the paddy plants. Due to the abundance of water, water-lilies, blue flowers, lotus flowers, and others also grew in these fields. So, the women plucked these, along with the weeds, from among the paddy plants and discarded them. However, some of these red water-lily vines, when plucked, bent and moved to the edges of the paddy field bunds. They escaped and took refuge there. These red water-lily vines, which had been set aside during weeding, grew along with the paddy and later bloomed with their red flowers. At the same time, the paddy also grew tall, bore ears, ripened, and stood bent. Thus, the paddy in the fields grew very tall, bore ears, ripened, and stood bent. On the bunds, red water-lilies shone in red. Sekkizhar Peruman describes this as the paddy bowing its head in reverence to the red water-lilies. Approximately two months after the weeding season, the paddy ears ripen and begin to bend. It is noteworthy that the red water-lily vines also took root and bloomed within that period. This scene, where red water-lilies bloom on the edges of the bunds and the paddy ears bow their heads as if in reverence, conveys a profound spiritual meaning. The red water-lilies symbolize the garland of sacred marks worn by an Acharya (spiritual teacher) who takes souls under his care and saves them. The red flowers of these water-lilies signify the compassionate gaze of the Acharya, filled with grace. The paddy ears bowing their heads to the red water-lilies signify that when a soul receives the gracious gaze of the Lord, its ego, which had previously stood tall, bows down in humility to the Lord. Similarly, the paddy plants, which had stood tall before ripening, now bend their heads upon ripening, as if bowing to the red water-lilies. This same description, in the context of the Nayanar's history, subtly indicates Manakkanchara Nayanar as the bowing paddy plant and the Lord, who bestowed His gracious gaze upon him and took him under His care, as the red water-lilies. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 03 — Manakkanchara Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam Part 166. Ilaimalinta Sarukkam. Manakkanchara Nayanar Puranam. Sekkizhar Peruman continues to sing about the riches of Kancharur. In the fields of Kancharur, paddy ears ripen and shine. To harvest them, groups of women go and cultivate. There are many ponds in those farms. It is the season when paddy ripens, so there is no need for irrigation there. The act of drawing water is not taking place there. Therefore, water is stagnant and abundant in the ponds. There, the carp fish are plump and leaping. The women are engaged in the harvest work of cutting paddy ears. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman sings about the agricultural prosperity of the town of Kancharur. This is because the Nayanar in this story is a farmer. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman specifically describes the aspects of agriculture in connection with this. In the fields of this town, paddy ears have grown very tall. The paddy ears have grown as high as the areca nut trees on the banks near those fields. Even today, in regions with abundant water from large rivers like the Godavari, paddy plants can be seen growing eight to nine feet tall. Furthermore, some paddy plants are said to grow up to 20 to 30 feet in height. Thus, the paddy fields outside the town are rich. There are tall mansions in that town. Flags adorn every house, and full pots are placed at the entrances of the houses, with beautiful festoons above. Festivals are always taking place in this town. Kancharur is a town full of festivals. It is a town filled with farmers engaged in agriculture. In such a town, filled with agricultural families, lived Manakkancharar, a Nayanmar who shone like a treasure to that agricultural lineage. He was the commander of the king. The profession of being a commander for the king is also suitable for agriculturalists. This profession of commander has been a hereditary occupation for some families among the agricultural communities. Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar, who came to marry Manakkanchara Nayanar's daughter, was also born into a commander's family. Similarly, in the Puranam of Thirunavukkarasu Peruman, Kalippakaiyar, who was proposed for marriage to Thilakavathi Ammaiyar, also served as a commander for the king and engaged in warfare. Thus, Manakkancharanar, who lived and was born into this lineage, shone brightly. Manakkanchara Nayanar is the sacred name of the Nayanar. Manakkancharanar is the sacred name. The word "Maanam" in it refers to the Nayanar's greatness and glory. "Kancharan" or "Kancharanar" refers to the town where the Nayanar lived. Therefore, the Nayanar's given name is not known. We do not know the Nayanar's given name. So, Sekkizhar Peruman has sung about him as Manakkancharanar, combining his greatness and the town he lived in. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal has also sung about him as Manakkancharanar. The Nayanar understood and realized the true essence. The true essence refers to the sacred form of Shiva, adorned with sacred ash and matted hair, which is spoken of as the true ascetic's guise. This is the explanation we found for "Meipporul" (true essence) in the Puranam of Meipporul Nayanar. Here, Sekkizhar Peruman states that Manakkanchara Nayanar also understood and realized the true essence. He says "arinthu unarnthar" (understood and realized). "Arinthu" (understood) implies hearing and contemplating. "Unarnthu" (realized) implies clarity and attaining samadhi (deep meditation). The true essence is a reality that cannot be known by the knowledge of the senses (gnanendriya arivu), the inner faculties (anthakarana arivu), or the knowledge of the soul (pasu arivu), but only by the true realization of Shiva-gnanam (divine knowledge). The meaning is that he understood and realized such a true essence through the four stages of knowledge: sravanam (hearing), mananam (contemplation), nidhidhyasanam (clarity), and nishtai (attaining samadhi), and stood at the pinnacle of knowledge. Sekkizhar further sings about Manakkanchara Nayanar: "Panivudaiya vadivudaiyar paniyinoḍum panimathiyin aṇivudaiya sadaimudiyarkku āḷākum patham petra thanivil perum pēruḍaiyār tham perumān kazhal sārntha thuṇivuḍaiya thoṇḍarkkē ēval seyyum thozhil pūṇḍār." Manakkanchara Nayanar was the embodiment of humility. He was the embodiment of bowing. The very appearance of Shiva's devotees, in their sacred form, manifested humility in the Nayanar's heart. Humility is the virtue of behaving as a very small person before great ones. "Thazhvenum thanmaiyodum Saivamam samayam saarum oozh peral arithu" (It is difficult to attain the fortune of embracing Saivism with the quality of humility) says Sivagnana Siddhiyar. "Methakaiyar avar munbu miga siriyarai adainthar" (He approached the great ones as a very small person) is from Siruththondar Nayanar Puranam. To stand before great Saivite elders, considering our own smallness and lowliness, is the foundation of Saivism. In the Puranam of Thaduththaatkondu, we saw how Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, in the Devasiriyar Mandapam of Thiruvarur temple, where countless devotees of Shiva were gathered, yearned, "When will I become a servant to all these great Saivite elders?" and then entered the temple. Furthermore, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal bowed and sang, "I will become a separate servant to each of them." We should recall and apply that conduct here. Here, Manakkanchara Nayanar, upon seeing the form of Shiva, the sacred form of Shiva's devotees, immediately felt humility in his heart. It is natural that such humility arising in the heart would manifest in speech and body. Such Manakkanchara Nayanar was blessed with the great fortune of becoming a servant to Shiva's devotees. He possessed that great blessing which never diminishes at any time. Those who love the sacred feet of Lord Shiva will be courageous devotees. It is a courage born from having attained the feet of the Lord, which are worthy of being attained. It is the courage that Thirunavukkarasu Peruman sang, "Anjuvadhu yathonrum illai, anja varuvathum illai" (There is nothing to fear, and nothing fearful will come). "En ulame puguntha adanal Gnayiru, Thingal, Sevvai, Pudhan, Viyazhan, Velli, Sani, Pambu irandum udane aasaru nalla nalla avai nalla nalla adiyar avarukku migave" (Since He entered my heart, Sunday, Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday, and the two serpents (Rahu and Ketu) are all good, good, good for His devotees) sings Gnanasambandha Peruman. He sings that for the devotees of the Lord, there is no suffering or fault from days or planets. That courage is the courage mentioned here. We should note here that Gnanasambandha Peruman sings, "Idarinum thalarinum enathu oru noi thodarinum una kazhal thozhuthu ezhuven" (Even if I suffer, falter, or if a disease afflicts me, I will worship your feet and rise). We should also remember the sacred words, "Sevadi keezh senru angu irumaanthu iruppan kolo" (Will I go and stay proudly at His sacred feet?). The courage spoken of in all these sacred words is the courage of Shiva's devotees. Manakkanchara Nayanar was dedicated to serving these devotees. This service to devotees, unlike other occupations that yield only bodily benefits and then cease, is a profession that continues to bind the soul. Such Manakkanchara Nayanar had the nature of anticipating the needs of Shiva's devotees even before they expressed them and quickly providing them. The devotees of the Lord are those who have received the Lord's sacred feet as their greatest wealth. Therefore, Shiva's devotees are those who are capable of bestowing liberation. Such devotees are the receptacles for bestowing eternal benefits upon impermanent things. Therefore, they are the true owners. Sekkizhar Peruman praises them here as "Eeril perum thiru udaiyar udaiyar" (They are owners of boundless great wealth). Unlike other forms of wealth, the wealth of liberation is imperishable. The Lord is the one who bestows that wealth of liberation. The devotees are the ones who possess that Lord, who is the great wealth. Therefore, all other forms of wealth become the possessions of the devotees. Thus, Shiva's devotees are the owners of all wealth. Therefore, Manakkanchara Nayanar, believing that his own wealth also belonged to them, served Shiva's devotees. Thus, Manakkanchara Nayanar, who was born into the Velalan community, served as a commander in the king's army, possessed great wealth, and dedicated himself to serving devotees and giving them everything, had no children for some time. Therefore, by the divine will, the Nayanar prayed to the Lord in his mind for offspring. In household life, the cause of happiness is having children. That is why Thiruvalluvar, in the chapter on household life in Thirukkural, after listing "Illaram" (household life) and "Vazhkai Thuna Nalam" (excellence of spouse), placed "Puthalvarai Peruthal" (having children) as the next chapter, speaking of having good children. This is because among the five debts mentioned for householders (Grihasthas), the debt to Rishis (sages) is repaid by studying the scriptures composed by them. The debt to Devas (gods) is repaid by performing yagnas (sacrifices). But the debt to Pitrus (ancestors) is repaid only by having children. Therefore, Parimelazhagar, in his commentary on the chapter, writes that having good children to repay the debt to ancestors is systematically placed after "Illaram" and "Vazhkai Thuna Nalam." In the tenth Kural (verse) of the previous chapter, "Vazhkai Thuna Nalam" (excellence of spouse), which is the concluding verse, Valluvar Peruman states, "Mangalam enba manai maatchi matru athan nankalam nanmakkal perru" (Auspiciousness is the excellence of a wife, and its best ornament is having good children). He places this as an introduction to the next chapter, "Puthalvarai Peruthal" (having children). Its meaning is that for a person, goodness is the good character and good deeds of his wife. The ornament for that goodness is having good children. Thus, the Nayanar prayed to the Lord's feet for offspring, which is the cause of happiness in household life. Having offspring, which is the cause of happiness in household life, is for those in the worldly state. But here, the Nayanar is one who understands and realizes the true essence, Lord Shiva. So, did he desire and wish for children, which is different and superior to that? No, he did not wish for children in that way. But here, the story says that he prayed to the Lord for children. That desire did not come from the Nayanar's own will, but by the divine command of grace. By the divine command of grace, the Nayanar had the desire for offspring. This is because the offspring the Nayanar received greatly supports this history of the Nayanar. Not only that, it also greatly supports the events in the history of Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar. Therefore, it was by the divine command of the Lord that the Nayanar worshipped the Lord, desiring offspring. Manakkanchara Nayanar, who did not know the nature of the Lord's feet, by the grace of the Lord of Dance, whose ears are adorned with swinging earrings, had a baby girl through his wife, who possessed great chastity. The great chastity of women is the chastity that helps bring rain. Thirukkural also states this as "Peyyena peyyum mazhai" (Rain falls when commanded). Our ancestors believed that the chastity of women is also a reason for the rain that should fall three times every month. Such a wife of the Nayanar, who possessed great chastity, gave birth to a beautiful baby girl. That baby girl was born there to help the Nayanar find the path to escape the cruel cycle of birth, which is surrounded by the fruits of karma. "Kuzhaikkalum vadi kaathil kooththanar arulale mazhaikku udavum perum karpin manaikkizhaththiyar thampal izhaikkum vinaippayan soozhntha ippiravi kodum soozhal pizhaikkum neri thamakku udava pen kodiyai petreththaththar" is the verse composed by Sekkizhar Peruman. "Izhaikkum vinai" means the karmas accumulated by souls. "Vinaippayan soozhntha ippiravi" refers to the cruel cycle of birth, surrounded by the fruits of good and bad karma (pleasure and pain). It is the conclusion of scriptures that future births will be according to the karmas one performs. This birth, surrounded by karma, creates confusion, further accumulates karmas, leads to countless births, and souls, not knowing the way out of this cycle of birth, get confused and sink into this forest of birth. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman sings of "piravi kodum soozhal" (the cruel cycle of birth). The conclusion of spiritual texts is that the karmas performed by beings are the cause of these births in which they suffer. When karma is accumulated, it is done through the three ways of mind, speech, and body, with the adhvās (paths) such as mantras as their locus. Such accumulated karma is called sthūla karma (gross karma) and āgāmya (future karma). Then, when this karma ripens, it becomes sūkshma karma (subtle karma), residing in the buddhi tattva (intellectual principle) and in māyā (illusion), and then becomes the cause of the jathi (species), āyul (lifespan), and bhogam (experience) of the births that beings take. This same karma also takes the names apūrva, sanchita, punniya pāvam (merit and demerit), and then when these karmas yield their results, it is called prārabdha. That is, when a soul takes birth with ripened karmas, what it brings with it is called prārabdha. When that karma is experienced, i.e., when that prārabdha karma comes to us, it takes the general name prārabdha and reaches us in three forms: ādidaivikam (divine), ādhyātmikam (self-generated), and ādibhautikam (material). Ādibhautikam refers to pleasures and pains arising from inanimate objects. Ādhyātmikam refers to pleasures and pains arising from other living beings, which are conscious beings associated with matter. Ādidaivikam refers to pleasures and pains arising from divine sources. Thus, the cycle of birth, as a cruel cycle, reaches beings and becomes the cause of their birth and death. That baby girl was born there to help the Nayanar find the way to escape and survive this cycle of birth. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 04 — Manakkanchara Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam Part 167. Ilaimalinta Sarukkam. Manakkanchara Nayanar Puranam. Manakkanchara Nayanar had a daughter to help him find the path to salvation. Here, "path to salvation" refers to the way to escape the cruel cycle of birth, which is indeed the path of Shiva. Manakkanchara Nayanar desired offspring and prayed to the Lord's feet. Manakkanchara Nayanar had attained the state of being a servant to the Lord's devotees and was engaged in serving them. Due to that penance, he was blessed with a child. That child came to lead the Nayanar to the path of Shiva. This is because, after having this daughter, on the day of her wedding, he cuts off his daughter's hair and gives it to a Shiva devotee. By this act, he attains the grace of Shiva. Furthermore, he gives his daughter in marriage to another Nayanar, Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar. Therefore, this child is also the cause of Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar's virtuous history. That is why Sekkizhar praises this daughter as the "daughter who came to help find the path to salvation." It is a rule stated in ethical texts that those who give a daughter in marriage into a good family, thereby elevating that family, will attain a good state. Similarly, it is stated in Smriti and other texts that having male children will protect the parents who bore them from hell. But here, in the history of our Nayanar, Sekkizhar praises this daughter as the "daughter who helps find the path to salvation." This path here refers to the path of Shiva, which transcends the paths mentioned in Smriti and other texts. If one follows the rules stated in ethical texts, they will attain worldly merits (pasu punniyam) and, as a result, enjoyments and pleasures. But this daughter born to Manakkanchara Nayanar helps Manakkanchara Nayanar in his path of Shiva. She also becomes the wife of Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar and is the cause of Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar's Puranam. In the Gnana Sastras (scriptures of wisdom), both male and female are considered equally in the category of living beings due to the influence of karma. Therefore, the daughter born to Manakkanchara Nayanar was born to help the Nayanar in the path of Shiva, which leads to escaping the cycle of birth. This is because a very important event in the Nayanar's history takes place through this daughter. By cutting off this daughter's hair and giving it to a Shiva devotee, the Nayanar receives a vision of Shiva. He attains the state of Shiva. Thus, this daughter, possessing immeasurable glories, was born to the Nayanar. Therefore, the Nayanar, filled with great joy, performs countless generous acts of charity to all devotees who excel in Shiva dharma, to the accompaniment of auspicious musical instruments. Shiva dharma refers to the sacred service, beginning with Chariyai (physical service). Thus, the Nayanar performs what is needed for Shiva devotees who excel in the Shiva discipline of Chariyai, Kiriyai, Yogam, and Gnanam, and expresses great joy there. He performs countless great acts of charity, born of the joy of having a baby girl, all for Shiva devotees. Such acts of Shiva merit performed for Shiva devotees have the power to lead souls on the path of liberation. Other acts of charity and donations made to worldly people are worldly merits (pasu punniyam), which will exhaust themselves by granting pleasures like heaven. But the donations given to Shiva devotees who are established in Chariyai, Kiriyai, Yogam, and Gnanam, also become Shiva merit and will have the power to further lead us on the path of liberation. Here, by stating that the Nayanar gave gifts to those who excelled in the path of Shiva, it is also implied that he gave to other worldly people according to their needs and made them happy. The foster mothers all praise and celebrate the baby girl born to Manakkanchara Nayanar. They raise the baby girl. They adorn the baby with a protective amulet (kaappu) and protect her. This "kaappu" stage occurs in the second month after the child's birth. Children face three types of troubles: adhibhautika, adhyatmika, and adhidaivika. Of these, troubles arising from adhibhautika and adhyatmika are protected by their own intelligence and with the help of elders. Adhidaivika trouble is trouble that comes from divine sources. It is customary to pray to the deities and adorn the child with a protective amulet to prevent such troubles. This is why the "kaappu paruvam" (protective stage) is mentioned first in children's literature (pillaitamil). They adorn the baby girl's curly hair with various flowers. They raise the baby girl beautifully according to each stage of her growth. They adorn her beautifully and rejoice. Earrings and ear ornaments hang together in her ears. They adorn the child with an ornament called "kovai," strung with small bells, around her waist. She wears a small garment around her waist. Small ankle bells (kinkini) jingle on the child's feet. Kinkini is the name of a type of ornament worn on the feet. These make a "kin kin" sound whenever the child takes a step, hence their name. Thus, the child reaches the stage of taking tender steps. In this way, the baby girl grows, passing through many stages. She plays games like building small houses, which are done between two and three years of age. Later, as she grows, she practices games like "kazhangu" and "ammanai." Thus, the girl grows like nectar. Now, the girl has grown and reached the age of marriage. It is our tradition that the age of marriage for girls is 12 years. Similarly, it is our tradition that the age of marriage for boys is 16 years. The girl who has reached the age of marriage is superior to Lakshmi in form, wealth, radiance, and grace. The wealth bestowed by Lakshmi, the goddess of wealth, is perishable wealth. But here, this girl helps her father, Manakkanchara Nayanar, attain the grace of Shiva. Later, as the wife of Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar, she also helps Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar attain the grace of Shiva. Therefore, the spiritual wealth that comes through this girl is imperishable wealth. It is not perishable wealth like the worldly wealth bestowed by the goddess of wealth, but rather imperishable liberation (mukthi) that this girl will bestow upon her father and her husband. That is why Sekkizhar Peruman celebrates this daughter as an incomparable woman, superior to the goddess of wealth. Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar, like Manakkanchara Nayanar, was born into the Velalan community and, like him, served as a commander for the king. Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar was also a devotee of Lord Shiva, who wears the blue throat, and was engaged in Shiva service. Therefore, the elders of Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar's family came to Manakkanchara Nayanar's house to arrange the marriage and asked Manakkanchara Nayanar to give his daughter in marriage to Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar. It was customary in those days to send elders, rich in age, experience, and wisdom, to arrange marriages. This is because they were capable of appropriately explaining what was suitable for both parties and bringing about a harmonious union. Therefore, sending such wise and virtuous elders to arrange marriages was an ancient custom. We saw this in the history of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal in the Puranam of Thaduththaatkondu as well. Manakkanchara Nayanar, in turn, gladly welcomed and honored these wise elders who had come to arrange the marriage. After hearing all they had to say, he was pleased, believing that Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar would be suitable for his lineage, and agreed to give his daughter in marriage. This is because the lineage, profession, and devotion to Shiva of both Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar and Manakkanchara Nayanar made them compatible. Therefore, Manakkanchara Nayanar gladly gave his consent. The elders who had come from the groom's side to arrange the marriage asked for the girl. The parents of the daughter gave their consent. These are the initial stages of the wedding ceremony. These are the beginning of the wedding ceremony. However, in reality, the wise consider this event itself as a completed marriage. When both families have happily agreed and given their consent, it is considered equivalent to the marriage having taken place. This is the custom and character of those who possess high virtue. According to the proverb "Subham Seekiram" (Auspiciousness quickly), as soon as Manakkanchara Nayanar gave his consent, the elders who had come departed and conveyed the news to Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar. Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar also heard it and was very pleased. At this point, astrologers fixed an auspicious day for the wedding, suitable for both parties. They selected an auspicious day that would bring good fortune to both parties, considering all aspects like day compatibility, planetary compatibility, general compatibility, and professional compatibility, as prescribed in astrological texts. This auspicious day brought about the auspicious marriage of Manakkanchara Nayanar's daughter and Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar. The same auspicious day also became an auspicious day that led to Manakkanchara Nayanar's eternal auspiciousness, his great liberation. The truth stated in spiritual texts is that liberation (Sivapperu) alone is the great auspiciousness, and all other auspiciousness are merely conventionally spoken of as auspiciousness. Manakkanchara Nayanar, the generous father of the bride, makes all the necessary arrangements for the wedding. He invites all his relatives, who are filled with great joy, and makes wedding arrangements. He adorns the entire town of Kancharur. They place sprouted pots (mulaipalikai). Thus, all the wedding arrangements proceed well. The wedding day also approaches. On that wedding day, Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar, surrounded by all his relatives and to the sound of auspicious musical instruments, approaches Kancharur, the town of Manakkanchara Nayanar, to marry Manakkanchara Nayanar's virtuous daughter. At that time, Lord Shiva, the Lord of the Devas, decided to enact a divine play and come to Manakkanchara Nayanar's sacred home for the salvation of the entire world. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 05 — Manakkanchara Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam Part 168. Ilaimalinta Sarukkam. Manakkanchara Nayanar Puranam. Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar, the groom, accompanied by his relatives, approaches the town of Kancharur. Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar's relatives are full of integrity. "Anbu, naan, oppuravu, kannottam, vaaimaiyodu ainthu saalpu oonriya thoon" (Love, shame, impartiality, compassion, and truthfulness are the five pillars of integrity) says Thirukkural. Who are those with integrity? Those with integrity will not speak ill of others. Those with integrity are very humble. Those with integrity are capable of embracing everyone and completing any task. Those with integrity do good even to those who have harmed them. Those with integrity remain steadfast in their resolve even if the world comes to an end. Such were the relatives of Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar, endowed with these noble qualities. That is why all these relatives witnessed the grace of Shiva bestowed upon the Nayanar. Later, they had the glory of witnessing Eyarkon Kalikkamanar's wedding, which was celebrated with great splendor throughout the world. That is why Sekkizhar Peruman specifically describes these relatives as "relatives with integrity." These relatives were blessed to witness Manakkanchara Nayanar receiving the grace of God and then God bestowing His grace upon Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar. With such relatives of integrity, Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar, the generous one, arrives near Kancharur in a wedding procession. At that time, Lord Shiva, the Lord of the Devas, decided to enact a divine play and come to Manakkanchara Nayanar's sacred home. Lord Shiva, the all-powerful, comes to Manakkanchara Nayanar's sacred home in the guise of a Mahavrata. Those who belong to a sect that worships Shiva are called Mahavratas. In Sanskrit, they are called Mahavratas and their path is called Mahavrata Marga. In Tamil, they are called Mavirathas, Mavirathis, and Maviratha Marga. Saiva Siddhanta classifies the religions related to it into four divisions. Each division contains six religions. Those four divisions are Purappura Samayangal (outermost religions), Purachchamayangal (outer religions), Akappura Samayangal (inner-outer religions), and Akachchamayangal (inner religions). Saiva Siddhanta thus categorizes six religions in each of these four divisions. The Purappura Samayangal is a division that includes religions that do not accept the Vedas and Agamas. In this division, Saivism places religions like Lokayatam, Sautrantika Buddhism, Yogachara Buddhism, Madhyamika Buddhism, Vaibhashika, and Aruhatam (Jainism). These six religions do not accept the Vedas and Agamas. They reject the Vedas and Agamas. Next, they classify a division called Purachchamayangal (outer religions). Purachchamayangal means religions that accept the Vedas but reject the Agamas. Six religions are also included in this. They are Tarka (Logic), Mimamsa (Exegesis), Ekantmavada (Monism), Sankhya (Enumeration), Yoga (Union), and Pancharatra. Next, the third category consists of six Akappura Samayangal (inner-outer religions). These six Akappura Samayangal are religions that accept both the Vedas and Agamas. However, there are slight differences in their acceptance of the Agamas. These six Akappura Samayangal are Pasupatam, Mahavratam, Kapalam, Vamam, Bhairavam, and Aikkyavada Samayam. One of these Akappura Samayangal is the religion of Maviratam or Mahavratam. It is in the guise of a Mahavrati, belonging to this Mahavrata religion, that Lord Shiva is now about to appear at Manakkanchara Nayanar's sacred home. Now, let us look at the religions in the fourth division, the Akachchamayangal (inner religions). Akachchamayangal are those that accept both the Vedas and Siva Agamas. However, these six Akachchamayangal have differences of opinion regarding the state of the Atman and Shiva in liberation. The six Akachchamayangal are as follows: Pashanvada Saivam, Bhedavada Saivam, Siva Samavada Saivam, Siva Sankrantivada Saivam, Ishvara Avikaravada Saivam, and Sivadvaita Saivam. Thus, Saiva Siddhanta classifies religions into 24 types, grouping them into four divisions: six Purappura Samayangal, six Purachchamayangal, six Akappura Samayangal, and six Akachchamayangal. Saiva Siddhanta elders explain that Saiva Siddhanta itself transcends all these religions, being Samayatitham (beyond religions). Here, the guise in which Lord Shiva appears at Manakkanchara Nayanar's house is that of a Mahavrati, belonging to Maviratam or Mahavratam, one of the six Akappura Samayangal. Mahavratas, just as Lord Shiva adorns himself with a garland of bones and applies sacred ash, also wear garlands of bones and various bone ornaments, apply sacred ash, and adopt the guise of observing the Chariyai (physical service) path. They have a bone bead strung at the end of their hair, wear bone earrings in their ears, bone necklaces around their necks, bone ornaments on their hands, and a sacred thread made of hair on their chest. They also wear a yogapattai (ascetic's band), apply sacred ash all over their body, wear a loincloth around their waist, and another garment over it, coming with a pouch of sacred ash. This is called the Maviratha form. Lord Shiva appears here in this guise. Sekkizhar Swamigal describes and sings about this. Lord Shiva, coming in this manner, sees the beautiful hair of Manakkanchara Nayanar's daughter. He asks for that hair for himself on her wedding day. On his daughter's wedding day, Lord Shiva, enacting a divine play, asks Manakkanchara Nayanar to cut off his daughter's hair and give it to him, intending to make Manakkanchara Nayanar's greatness known to the world. Manakkanchara Nayanar, without even considering that it was his daughter's wedding day, cuts off his daughter's hair and gives it to the Lord, who had come in the guise of a Mahavrati, a Shiva devotee, without a moment's hesitation. The Mahavrata guise is suitable for enacting this act of grace. This is because these Mahavratas tie their hair into a sacred thread and wear it. Therefore, the Mahavrata, seeing Manakkanchara Nayanar's daughter's hair, thinks that it would be good hair for a sacred thread, and the Lord enacts this divine play. Therefore, He comes here in the Mahavrata guise, which is suitable for this. Lord Shiva, who came as a Mahavrati in the Mahavrata guise, had three lines of sacred ash (Thiripundram) shining on his forehead. His hair was shaven (mundiththiruththal). "Mundiththiruththal" means to shave hair. In Sanskrit, it is called "vapanam." While the hair in other parts was shaven, hair was grown only on the crown of the head. There was hair as a topknot there. While other parts of the head were shaven, there was hair only on the topknot. He had it as a topknot, a chikai. At the tip of that topknot, a bead made of bone was strung. Here, in accordance with the Mahavrata Saivite guise assumed by the Lord, he wore earrings made of bone beads in his ears. The sacred thread (Thazhvadam) worn on the chest was a cord made of bone. The Lord always wears a snake as a garland on his shoulder. Now, he has come in the Mahavrata guise. Therefore, in place of the snake, he wears a yogapattai (ascetic's band). Furthermore, he wears a sacred thread made of hair rope on his chest. This is called Panchavadi. Wearing a sacred thread made of hair rope is characteristic of Mahavrata Saivites. Their form is known from Thevarams like "Savam thāngum mayānaththu sāmpal enpu thalaiyōdu mayir kayiru thariththān thannai pavam thāngu pāsupatha vēdaththānai" (He who wears a hair rope with ashes and bones from the cremation ground, bearing the burden of sins, in the guise of a Pasupatha) and other places. "Saivar Pasupathargal vanangum Sanpai nagarāre" (He is the Lord of Sanpai, worshipped by Saivites and Pasupathas) is from Aludaiya Pillaiyar Thevaram. "Pāmbu sadai thirumunivar kapālakkaiyar palaveda saivar" (He is a sage with matted hair and snakes, holding a skull, a Saivite in many guises) is from Thirugnanasambandha Nayanar Puranam. Thus, we can find these Akappura Samaya forms in various places in the Thirumurais. These Akappura Samayaththargal, who wear these Akappura Samaya forms, worship Lord Shiva as a deity adorned with bones. Thus, Lord Shiva, who came in the Mahavrata guise, wore a string of bone beads on one of his two forearms. He wore a loincloth around his waist. The loincloth worn by the Lord is the loincloth of the scriptures. Our Lord wears the Vedas themselves as a loincloth. We have extensively seen this loincloth of the Vedas, called "Mamaraikkovanam" and "Ongu Kovanam," in the Puranam of Amarneethi Nayanar. He wore a sacred garment that swayed over that loincloth. The five sacred marks (Thiru Panchamuththirai) were visible on the sacred feet of the Lord who came in the Mahavrata guise. Panchamuththirai refers to Padma (lotus), Sangam (conch), Makaram (crocodile), Chakram (discus), and Thandam (staff). They are also called Panchayudham (five weapons): Thandu (staff), Vaal (sword), Sanghu (conch), Chakram (discus), and Vil (bow). These Panchamuththirais are seen on the sacred feet of great personalities like Gnānis (sages) and Yogis (ascetics). It is said to be one of the Samudrika Lakshanangal (bodily characteristics) in the physical descriptions of Gnānis and Yogis. The Lord, assuming such a Mahavrata guise, walks with his sacred feet, which are difficult to describe, touching the ground. Although the Lord pervades and is embedded in all things and does not usually walk with his feet touching the ground, now, in accordance with the guise he has assumed, his sacred feet touch the ground as he walks. Sekkizhar Peruman praises the Lord's feet here as "Ezhuthariya Thiruvadi" (feet that cannot be written). "Ezhuthariya Thiruvadi" means feet that cannot be written down or depicted. His feet cannot be copied, visualized, or painted. Therefore, they are praised as "Ezhuthariya Thiruvadi." Thirunavukkarasu Peruman also states that the Lord's feet are "Ezhuthariya Thiruvadi" in the second verse of Thani Thirunerisai in the fourth Thirumurai. "Kovanam uduththavārum kōḷaravu asaiththavārum thīvaṇach chāmbar pūsi thiruvuru irunthavārum pūvaṇak kizhavanārai puli uri araiyanārai ēvaṇach silaiyinārai yāvarē ezhuthuvāre" (Who can depict in writing the Lord who wears a loincloth, has a fierce snake tied around his waist, applies fire-colored ash, has a sacred form, is the Lord of the flowering forest, wears a tiger skin around his waist, and has a beautiful bow for arrows?) is the verse. Thirunavukkarasu Swamigal asks, "Who has the power to depict in a painting the beautiful form of the Lord, who wears a loincloth, ties a fierce snake around his waist, applies ash on his fire-colored body, has a beautiful form, is the Lord of the flowering forest, wears a tiger skin around his waist, and has the beautiful bow Pinaka for arrows?" and implies that no one can depict his beautiful form in a painting. Similarly, in another place, Thirunavukkarasu Swamigal sings the following in the tenth verse of Vinavidai Thiruththandagam in the sixth Thirumurai: "Mai padintha kannāḷum thānum kachchi mayānaththān vār sadaiyān eṇṇinallan oppuraiyan allan ōr uruvan allan ōr ūran allan ōr uvaman illi appaḍiyum aṇṇiṛamum avvaṇṇamum avaṇaruḷē kaṇṇākak kāṇin allāl ippaḍi en niṛaththan ivvaṇṇaththan ivaṇ iṟaivaṇ enṟezhuththik kāṭṭoṇāthē." He sings that the Lord, with his dark-eyed consort, resides in Kachchi Mayanam. He has long matted hair. If one says this, he is not one who can be described as being in such a way. He is not one who accepts anything for his own sake. He is not one among worldly things. He does not belong to any one town. He has no comparison to anything. Therefore, His nature, His color, and His form should be seen with His grace as the eye. Otherwise, like other things in the world, it is impossible to describe Him in words or depict Him in writing as being of this kind, of this color, or of this form. Saivite elders consider the sacred feet as the embodiment of divine grace and Shiva's power. Those who always wish to see an object keep it with them by drawing it. This is a worldly custom. But the Lord's feet can only be seen if He reveals them to us. It is impossible to keep a painting or drawing of the feet or the form of the Lord that has been seen in this way. Therefore, the Lord's feet are feet that cannot be written. Even the Vedas are unwritten because they contain His feet within them. Therefore, in Tamil, the Vedas are called "Ezhuthakkilavi" (unwritten word). That is why our ancestors passed on the Vedas through oral tradition, through instruction, without writing them down. The Lord comes in such a Mahavrata guise. The color of the Lord's sacred body is the color of red fire. It is the color of fire. He wears sacred ash as if there is ash powder on that fire. Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam, in the Man Sumandha Padalam, sings, "Ivvaṇṇam irukkum engal iṟai vaṇṇa vaḍivu antha sevvaṇṇa mēni pūththa thiruvenṇīru" (Such is the beautiful form of our Lord, His red body adorned with sacred ash). Here too, the color of the Lord is described as the color of red fire, and He is described as wearing sacred ash on that color. "Neeru pooththa neruppu" (fire covered with ash) is a proverb. Thus, the Lord comes in the Mahavrata guise with such a radiant appearance. With such a sacred body, He comes seeking the home of Manakkanchara Nayanar, who always remembers His cool sacred feet in his heart. Thirugnanasambandha Peruman explains the nature of never forgetting the sacred feet in the Thiruvavaduthurai Thevaram. "Idarinum thalarinum enathu oru noi thodarinum vālinum sāvinum varunthinum pōy vīzhinum naṇavilum kaṇavilum thummalōdu arunthuyar thōṇṭiṭinum kai athu vīzhinum kazhivuṟiṇum venthuyar thōṇṭi ōr veṟuvuṟiṇum veppoḍu viraviyōr viṇai variṇum pēriṭar perukiyōr piṇi variṇum uṇṇiṇum pasippinum uṟaṅkiṇum pitthoḍu mayañkiyōr piṇi variṇum uṇṇuṭaiya thirukkaḻalkaḷai nāṇ thozhuthu ezhuven" (Even if I suffer, falter, or if a disease afflicts me, even if I live or die, suffer or fall, in waking or dreaming, even if a severe sorrow arises with a sneeze, even if my hand falls or is severed, even if a burning sorrow arises and causes fear, even if a fever or a karmic affliction comes, even if a great danger or disease comes, even if I eat or starve, sleep or am afflicted by madness, I will worship your sacred feet and rise) sings Thirugnanasambandha Peruman in the Thiruvavaduthurai Thevaram. As he sang there, Manakkanchara Nayanar here is one who always meditates on the Lord's feet in his mind. The Lord's feet remained steadfast in the Nayanar's mind, never forgotten, even during times when worldly people would forget. Such feet of the Lord come to Manakkanchara Nayanar's house, touching the ground, in the guise of a Mahavrata. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 06 — Manakkanchara Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam Part 169. Ilaimalinta Sarukkam. Manakkanchara Nayanar Puranam. The Lord, in the guise of a Mahavrata, comes to Manakkanchara Nayanar's house. That day is the wedding day of Manakkanchara Nayanar's daughter. While Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar, the groom, is approaching Kancharur, Lord Shiva comes to Manakkanchara Nayanar's house in the guise of a Mahavrata. Seeing this Mahavrata Shiva devotee, Manakkanchara Nayanar is filled with great joy and humbly welcomes him with love, saying, "Your coming to our place is the result of our penance. Therefore, I am saved." Then, Lord Shiva, who had come in the guise of a Mahavrata, asks Manakkanchara Nayanar, "What auspicious event is happening here?" To this, Manakkanchara Nayanar replies, "Today is my daughter's wedding day. My daughter's wedding is to take place here today." Manakkanchara Nayanar prostrates at the feet of the devotee, who had come in the guise of a Mahavrata, a sage, embodying wisdom. Then, he goes inside his house and brings his daughter, who is adorned for the wedding. He brings his daughter, who is in her wedding attire, and makes her prostrate at the sacred feet of the Shiva devotee who had come concealing his blue throat. As Manakkanchara Nayanar's daughter, the bride, prostrates at his feet, the Mahavrata Shiva devotee looks at her dark hair. Her hair is like a dark cloud. Ordinary clouds bring rain, causing living beings to flourish. The hair of Manakkanchara Nayanar's daughter, which is like a cloud, becomes the cause for the showering of divine grace. The hair of Manakkanchara Nayanar's daughter, the bride, who rose after prostrating at his feet, is long, thick, and black. Therefore, the Mahavrati thinks that this hair would be suitable for his Panchavadi (sacred thread made of hair). Then, addressing Manakkanchara Nayanar, who stood with folded hands, the Mahavrati says, "This girl's hair will serve as my Panchavadi." We have already seen, haven't we, that Mahavrata Saivites, who wear the Mahavrata Saivite guise, wear a sacred thread made of hair? Therefore, here, the Mahavrati thinks that the hair of Manakkanchara Nayanar's daughter would be suitable for his sacred thread, the Panchavadi. Panchavadi is a sacred thread made of hair. "Panjam" means wide, and "vadi" means cord. Therefore, Panchavadi means a wide, braided hair rope, a sacred thread. There are also Thevarams like "Mayirkkayiru thariththan thannai Panchavadi marbinanai" (He who wears a hair rope, who has a Panchavadi on his chest). Hearing what the Mahavrati said, Manakkanchara Nayanar was filled with great joy and thought, "I have been blessed to hear the words that my daughter's hair would be suitable for the Panchavadi." He then cut off his daughter's dark hair with his sword, took it separately, and offered it into the hands of the Mahavrati. He cuts off his daughter's hair and offers it into the lotus-like hands that sever the birth of living beings. "Arul seytha mozhigalo vadachchurigai thanai uruvi porul seytham enap petren enakkondu poonkodi than irul seytha karunkoonthal adiyil arinthu ethirninra marul seytha pirapparuppar malarkkarathin idai neetta" is the verse composed by Sekkizhar Peruman. As if severing attachment completely, without any trace remaining, he cut off his daughter's hair and offered it into the hands of the Mahavrati.

Then, in the next verse, Sekkizhar Peruman sings, "Vanguvar pol ninra marai porulam avar marainthu pangin malai valliyudan pazhaiya mazhavidai yeri ongிய vinmisai vanthar oli visumbu nil nerunga thoongiya pon malar mari thozhumbar thozhuthu ethir vizhunthar." Thus, the Mahavrati, who stood as if to receive his daughter's hair, then disappeared and appeared in the sky with Goddess Parvati, mounted on his bull, granting grace to Manakkanchara Nayanar. Golden flowers rained down from the bright sky, filling the earth. Manakkanchara Nayanar, the devotee, prostrated himself on the ground many times before the Lord. The confusion that bewilders souls, preventing them from reaching Lord Shiva, the leader of souls, and instead binding them, thereby causing them to fall into birth, is called "marul" (delusion). Thirukkural says, "Porul allavatrai porul enru unarum marulala mana pirappu" (The deluded birth of one who considers non-things as things). Sekkizhar Peruman refers to this human life, which is caused by delusion, as "marul seytha pirappu" (deluded birth), where one considers the illusory things like hair as real and suffers through birth and death for them. It is wrong to reject worldly pleasures as not being real, and it is also wrong to consider them as real and suffer for them. The measure of things is only to the extent that they are useful to Shiva and Shiva's devotees. Thus, Lord Shiva is the one who severs this deluded birth. Here, he stands before Manakkanchara Nayanar to sever his birth and grant him liberation, the state of his sacred feet. As known from the Vedas, such as Sri Rudram and Ninra Thiruththandagam, He who pervades everything came here as a Mahavrata Muni and stood before him. Here, he stood as if to receive the hair. Then, he himself disappeared. He was on the bull. He also grants a vision to Manakkanchara Nayanar in the sky of wisdom, on the bull, with Umaiyamai. Seeing this, Manakkanchara Nayanar prostrates on the ground many times and rises. He worships and rises. To Manakkanchara Nayanar, the true devotee who stood thus, forgetting himself, the Lord of Dance bestows his grace as follows: "We have made the nature of your love for us known to these flourishing worlds." Thus, Lord Shiva bestows his grace upon Manakkanchara Nayanar. Therefore, Lord Shiva grants Manakkanchara Nayanar the great blessing of always praising the great compassion of Lord Shiva. Manakkanchara Nayanar, considering the acts of grace as great grace—that the Lord himself, making himself an object, came as a Mahavrati, asked for the hair, disappeared, appeared on the bull, and made his love known to the worlds—praised Him with a melting heart. He folded his hands above his head in worship. His three faculties (mind, speech, body) were all directed towards the Lord. To such Manakkanchara Nayanar, Lord Shiva grants the great compassionate power to always praise Him. This is a great blessing, a rare and great blessing. It is the blessing of liberation, of uniting with the Lord. Saiva Siddhantins say that never forgetting the sacred feet is the characteristic of liberation. Sivagnana Bodham, "Ekamaai Ninre," also explains this. The Lord grants such liberation to Manakkanchara Nayanar. After thus bestowing grace upon Manakkanchara Nayanar, Lord Shiva, whose long matted hair was adorned with garlands, disappeared, praised by all the Devas. At that time, Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar, the groom, arrives at the spot. Then, Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar, a great devotee of Shiva, hears about the event, which is unimaginable even in thought, from those gathered there. He is greatly pleased and praises the grace of the Lord. These events—Manakkanchara Nayanar cutting off his incomparable daughter's hair on her wedding day and offering it to the Mahavrati, the Mahavrati disappearing, and Manakkanchara Nayanar receiving a great blessing—are events beyond thought and imagination. Hearing all this from the relatives, Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar was greatly pleased, remembering the great blessing Manakkanchara Nayanar had received. He praised the divine grace, believing that the Lord's grace was the cause of this event. Then, he felt a little disheartened. This is because he thought that if this event had happened after the wedding, he too would have had the blessing of witnessing this rare event. But then, the divine voice was heard when grace was bestowed upon Manakkanchara Nayanar, "We have made the love you have for us known in these worlds." Hearing the power of that divine word, he then removed his disheartened state of mind and became even happier. Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar felt disheartened that he did not have the blessing of directly witnessing the events of grace. After that, he understood that the Lord enacted this manifestation of grace to make the power of love known to the world, and by hearing the power of the Lord's divine words, he too was enlightened and saved, understanding the power of love and grace. Therefore, he was now happy. Next, by the grace of the Lord, the bride's hair grew back. The marriage of Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar and Manakkanchara Nayanar's daughter took place there. Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar, who came from the royal commander's lineage, went to his town, Thirupperumangalam, with his bride. Thirupperumangalam, the town of Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar, is located north of Thiruppunkur. There, Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar resided with his wife. Can my ability suffice to praise the greatness of Manakkanchara Nayanar, who, on his daughter's wedding day, cut off her hair and offered it to the Mahavrati? No, it cannot. Now, I begin to speak of the history of Arival Thaya Nayanar, says Sekkizhar Peruman, concluding the sacred Puranam of Manakkanchara Nayanar and beginning to sing the Puranam of Arival Thaya Nayanar. Let us also worship Manakkanchara Nayanar and conclude his Puranam, and now begin to contemplate the Puranam of Arival Thaya Nayanar. Glory to the sacred feet of Manakkanchara Nayanar. Glory to the sacred feet of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Glory to the sacred feet of Nambiyandar Nambigal. Glory to the sacred feet of Sekkizhar Peruman. Glory to the sacred feet of Sivakavimani Ayya. Thiruchitrambalam.
