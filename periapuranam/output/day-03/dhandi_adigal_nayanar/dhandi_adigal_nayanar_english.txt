Chapter 01 — Dhandi Adigal Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Volume 908, Vamparaa Varivandu Sarakkam, by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Thirumooladevar Nayanar Puranam. We learn from Thirumooladevar Nayanar's own words in the Thirumandiram that he composed the entire Thirumandiram, one mantra each year, while performing penance under the Bodhi tree in Thiruvavaduthurai. The 79th mantra states: "Serndhirundhen Sivan Avadu Thandurai Serndhirundhen Sivabodhiyin Nezhaliyil Serndhirundhen Sivan Namangal Odhiye" (I was united with Shiva at Avaduthurai, I was united in the shade of the Shiva Bodhi, I was united chanting Shiva's names). Thirumooladevar Nayanar composed these 3000 mantras, one each year. It is then understood that after completing the entire Thirumandiram, he re-instructed it to those who were spiritually mature in the world at that time. Our Lord Manikkavasagar Peruman travels to various holy places and composes the Thiruvachagam hymns. Later, when the Lord himself came as a Brahmin and asked him to recite the Thiruvachagam again so he could write it down, Manikkavasagar Peruman, from his memory, categorized and recited the entire Thiruvachagam, as we see in his sacred history. Therefore, we observe in the Saiva tradition that great saints, out of their love for the Lord, re-recite their sacred compositions to the people of the world so that they may worship and attain salvation. Even Thirunavukkarasu Peruman's hymns, composed when he was in the lime kiln of the Jains, praising the Lord, and the hymns composed when he floated on a stone in the ocean, were later re-recited by him for the clarity and salvation of the people of this world. That is why those hymns were revealed and are now revered by us. Here, Thirumooladevar Nayanar, a Sivayogiyar who received the grace of Nandi Peruman, had his compositions recorded in the Akashic records of knowledge at various times. He had them recorded. Therefore, it is understood that after the completion of the entire Thirumandiram, he retrieved all the recorded mantras from the Akashic records of knowledge and re-instructed them to his disciples and to those who were spiritually mature. Not only that, it is not just the Thirumandiram composed by Thirumooladevar Nayanar that remains in the Akashic records of knowledge. Why? Every thought, word, and action of every soul is recorded in the Akashic records of knowledge as karma at that very moment. This happens by the command of the Lord. That is why the scriptures of knowledge state that the Lord bestows the karmic fruits corresponding to those actions. The Thevaram praises this with the words "Ezhudhum Keezhkanakku Innampar Eesane" (The Lord of Innampar writes the accounts below). Thirumooladevar Nayanar was a yogi, and therefore skilled in yogic vision. What is yogic vision? Srimath Madhava Sivagnana Munivar has stated that yogic vision is the perception of objects in all three places and all three times, from a single place and time, by somehow destroying the mala shaktis (impurities) that obstruct the soul's knowledge, through yogic practices like yama and niyama, and perceiving them with the soul's consciousness. While this is so, we have briefly considered the structure of the first Tantra. Following that, we can now consider the structure and state of the subsequent Tantras. The Sivagnana Siddhiyar, in its eighth sutra, eleventh verse, describes the way a soul comes into the Saiva path, attains knowledge, and achieves salvation. "Purachchamaya nerinindrum agachchamayam pukkum pugal miruthi vazhi uzhandrum pugalum achchirama araththuraiyavai adaindhum arunthavangal purindhum arungalaigal pala therindhum aranangal padiththum sirappudaiya puranangal unarnthum vedach sirapparulai migath thelindhum sendral Saivath thiraththu adaivar. Idhil Sariyai, Kiriyai, Yogam seluthiyapin gnanaththal Sivanadiyai seravar." (By standing in external religions, entering internal religions, wandering in the path of smritis, attaining the paths of ashrama dharma, performing severe penances, learning many rare arts, studying the Vedas, understanding the glorious Puranas, and clearly comprehending the special grace of the Vedas, one will reach the Saiva path. In this, after practicing Chariya, Kriya, and Yoga, they will attain Shiva's feet through gnana.) If we carefully examine this verse, and also consider the Dasakariyam (ten stages) mentioned in Saiva Siddhanta, the structure of the Tantras within the Thirumandiram will become clear. The first Tantra describes all the practices that come under "entering internal religions, wandering in the path of smritis, attaining the paths of ashrama dharma, performing severe penances, and learning many rare arts." Now, in the second Tantra, topics such as Linga Purana, Dakshan's sacrifice, Pralaya, Chakra Peru, Adimudi Thedal (searching for the head and feet of Shiva), Ainthozhil (five divine functions), Garbha Kriyai, Moovagai Uyir (three types of souls), Pathiram, Apathiram, Theertham, Thirukkoil Izhivu, Adhomukha Darisanam, Sivaguru Maheswara Nindhai, Poraiyudaimai, Periyarai Thunai Kodal (seeking the help of elders) are discussed in their respective sections. A sadhaka (spiritual aspirant) who seeks Sivagnana (divine knowledge) must follow the path mentioned above and proceed. Therefore, one who has already studied the Vedas must then understand the true meaning of the Mahapuranas in a systematic way and act accordingly. Among the topics discussed in the second Tantra, the sections from Veeratttam and Linga Purana up to Adimudi Thedal form the essence of the Mahapuranas. The unified conclusion of the Vedic Sivagamas, that Lord Shiva is the Supreme Being, which was taught in a general way in the prologue, is here taught in a specific way. Even though Lord Shiva, as the Supreme Being, does not need any action for himself, out of great compassion for souls like us, he performs the five divine functions, such as creation, as taught in the subsequent five sections on creation. The things that are subject to these creative functions are the body, its external and internal instruments, the worlds where souls live, and the experiences they enjoy. Among these, the state of the body and soul coming together in creation is discussed in the section on Garbha Utpatti (origin of the embryo). The souls that come into creation are classified into three types based on their impurities: Vignanagalar (those with one impurity), Pralayakalar (those with two impurities), and Sakalar (those with three impurities). The 15th section, Moovagai Jeeva Vargam (three types of living beings), instructs the sadhaka that among these, the one who desires liberation falls into the Sakala category, which has three impurities. Next, the wise soul, who comes with three impurities, must purify itself by knowing the worthy recipients (satpathiram) and understanding the nature of the sacred waters (theertham), as it needs to live a virtuous life in the world and progress. Thirumooladevar Nayanar then instructs in the subsequent sections that a good disciple will understand the nature of the temple, realize that the Lord, out of compassion, resides there to remove the sufferings of souls and bestow joy, worship Him, avoid the grave evils of siva nindhai (blasphemy of Shiva), guru nindhai (blasphemy of the guru), and maheswara nindhai (blasphemy of Maheswara), adorn himself with the ornament of compassion, know the elders, and seek their help. Therefore, the second Tantra explains how a soul progresses to the state of "understanding the special Puranas and clearly comprehending the special grace of the Vedas," as seen in the Sivagnana Siddhiyar verse on Sivagnana, which was mentioned first. Through this, the second Tantra describes how a sadhaka makes himself worthy to enter the first path of Chariya, among the four paths of the Saiva righteous path. In accordance with "performing severe penances" in the Sivagnana Siddhiyar verse, the third Tantra describes the methods by which a sadhaka makes himself worthy to enter the path of Kriya. The inner meaning of this section of the third Tantra is Sarira Siddhi Upaya (means of attaining bodily perfection). Only by remaining stable within a body can one receive Shiva's gifts and preserve what has been received to benefit from it. It is for this reason that the Lord gives each soul a body according to its karma, as a dwelling place. Without a body, the soul cannot act independently. The author, who first described the degradation and impermanence of the body in the first Tantra, emphasizes here in the section on Kaya Siddhi (bodily perfection) in this third Tantra that the body must be nurtured to the extent that it helps the soul attain its ultimate goal. "Udambinai munnam izhukku endru irundhen Udambinukku ullae oru porul kanden Udambinul uthaman koyil kondan endru Udambinai yan irundhu ombugindrene" (I once considered the body a defect, but I found a substance within the body. The Supreme One has taken residence within the body, so I remain and cherish the body) is the 725th Thirumandiram. "Udambar azhiyin uyirar azhivar Thidampada meignanm seravum mattar Udambai valarkkum upayam arindhen Udambai valarthen uyir valarthene" (If the body perishes, the soul perishes. They cannot attain firm true knowledge. I knew the means to nourish the body, I nourished the body, I nourished the soul) is stated in the 724th Thirumandiram. Therefore, the sections on Ashtanga Yoga, Ashtama Siddhi, Kalai Nilai, Sarira Siddhi Upaya, and the subsequent yogic states such as Amori Dharanai, all contain this same inner meaning. Now, in the fourth Tantra, the sadhaka who attains Sarira Siddhi as mentioned above, merely attaining Kaya Siddhi is not enough. To enter the section of Sivayoga, which follows Chariya and Kriya, and to remain there and gain further benefit, one must attain the practice of mantras and their power. Therefore, this fourth Tantra extensively instructs on the nature of worshipping the mantra chakras, starting with Thirumbala Chakram. Thirumooladevar Nayanar elaborates on the form, size, lines, letters inscribed within them, their corresponding mantras, methods of worship, their presiding deities, powers, and benefits. Details such as how to write a particular chakra on a particular medium, in a particular place, with particular anointments, offerings of flowers, and how long to worship it to obtain specific worldly benefits, can also be found here. We can see this in the sections describing chakras such as Eroli Chakram, Bhairava Chakram, Sambhavi Chakram, Bhuvanapathi Chakram, Navakshari Chakram. Why are these worldly benefits also mentioned within the Thirumandiram, which came to instruct the path of Sivagnana, the essence of the Agamas? Is it because these are also in the Sivagamas? Not so. A sadhaka who seeks the righteous path must attain it while residing within a body. Therefore, as long as he lives in the world, he will sometimes desire worldly benefits. That is why these are also discussed in the Sivagamas. We also find good worldly conduct, rules of eating, and so on, there. We also see that some Agamas even describe ways to discover hidden treasures. We also observe here that these chakras mostly deal with the different forms of Shakti. Saints say that mantras are embodiments of Shakti, and through them, the Lord manifests and bestows grace through His power. Now, if we look at the fifth Tantra, it is the lifeblood of this book of knowledge called Thirumandiram. The preceding four Tantras are its means. The subsequent four Tantras are its benefits. In this, the four types of Saiva states – Suddham, Asuddham, Margam, Kadunjuddham – the four types of Saiva paths – Chariya, Kriya, Yoga, Gnana – and above them, the states of Sanmargam, Sahamargam, Satputhra Margam, Dasa Margam, and the corresponding attainments and states of Salokam, Samipyam, Sarupyam, Sayujyam for those who adhere to them, are instructed. The states of spiritual maturity (Shakti Nipatha Pakkuva Nilai) of good disciples who follow these four paths – Mandam, Mandataram, Theeviram, Theevirataram – are also explained. Furthermore, the nature of external religions that a sadhaka must understand and avoid is discussed under the heading "Purachchamaya Dhushanam" (Criticism of External Religions). This is discussed here as a warning, so that a disciple who has completed all the practices in the Saiva righteous path and attained the Saiva path does not fall into the deceptive pit of external religions, which teach and establish religions based on partial truths they have perceived, without seeing the full truth. Above this, this Tantra concludes by explaining the states of other internal-external and internal religions through the sections "Niracharam" and "Uchchamayam." After a sadhaka who has followed the aforementioned practices attains the Saiva path, he is then systematically instructed in this Tantra about the four paths that are the essence of Saivism, and their nature, benefits, and so on, through the statements of this Tantra. In this, after practicing Chariya, Kriya, and Yoga, one becomes spiritually mature to attain liberation through Gnana. Thirumooladevar Nayanar beautifully, concisely, and briefly instructs the nature of the four paths of the Saiva religion. Later books of knowledge, such as Sivagnana Siddhiyar, elaborate on these in the same manner. Thirumooladevar Nayanar has systematically classified these four types of paths, their inherent fourfold expansions, the distinctions that arise from the four types of initiations (Samaya, Visesha, Nirvanadeekshai, Acharya Abhishekam). It is good for those who are inclined to disregard the practice of initiation to study these and benefit. The sections on "Purachchamaya Dhushanam" and "Uchchamayam" may appear to criticize external and internal-external religions. But it is not so. Saivism encompasses the principles of all religions, shows them a hierarchical path, and establishes itself as complete, not confined by any one. Therefore, it neither hates nor accepts any religion. All the religious arguments undertaken by Saivism are only to enlighten people about the true state, warn them, and arrange them in a way that suits each individual. Therefore, these sections should be interpreted in this way. All religions are bestowed by the Lord. They are bestowed by the Lord considering the different levels of maturity of souls. Now, the conclusions of the Sivagamas state that there are three entities: Pathi (Lord), Pasu (soul), and Pasam (bondage). Their definitions are as follows. This is the fundamental principle of Saiva Siddhanta Sivagama. Among them, there are many souls (Pasu). The Lord (Pathi) is only one. Souls are bound by mala (impurity) from eternity. To enable their salvation, the Lord, out of compassion, guides souls through births corresponding to their karma, and in this birth, souls perform karmas and continue to wander through more births. The goal of the souls' efforts and actions should be to strive to escape these birth sufferings and attain eternal bliss. For this, there is no other support than the grace of the Satguru and the means such as Sri Panchaksharam and Thiruneeru. If souls follow the Saiva righteous paths with that support, they will be freed from suffering and attain eternal bliss. To ensure that this experience remains constant, one must also have the company of devotees. This is the truth stated by the Sivagamas. Here, Thirumooladevar Nayanar describes the definition of the three eternal entities. "Pathi, Pasu, Pasam ena paghar moondril Pathiyinaip pol pasu pasam anadhi. Pathiyinaich chendru anugaa pasu pasam Pathi anugil pasu pasam nilave" (Among the three spoken of as Pathi, Pasu, and Pasam, like Pathi, Pasu and Pasam are eternal. Pasu and Pasam do not approach Pathi. If Pathi approaches, Pasu and Pasam do not remain) is the mantra. Now, a Thirumandiram about Chariya: "Naadum nagaramum natrirukkoiyilum Thedith thirindhu Sivaperuman endru Paadumin paadip panimin panindhapin Koodiya nenjaththuk koyilaak kolvene." (Search and wander through nations, cities, and good temples, saying "Sivaperuman." Sing and worship. After worshipping, I will take residence in the united heart as a temple.) This is the 1445th mantra. Now, a mantra about Kriya, the 1451st mantra: "Paththu disaiyum paramoru deivam undu Eththikkum ilar illai enbathu inamalar Koththu thiruvadi neezhal saran ena Thaththum vinaikkadal saraadhu kaanume." (In all ten directions, there is one supreme deity. There is no one in any direction who is not. If one takes refuge in the shade of His clustered flower-like feet, the ocean of karma will not approach.) Now, about Yoga, mantras 1457 and 1459: "Neri vazhiye sendru nermaiyul ondri Thari irundhar pol thammai iruthi Sozhiyinum thakkkinum thunnendru unaraa Kuriyari vaalarkkuk koodumaame." (Those who go by the right path, unite in righteousness, and sit like those who are still, not startled by sound or touch, for such knowers of the goal, it will be attained.) "Pooviniyil gandham porundhiyavaaru pol Jeevanukkullae sivamanam pooththathu Oviyam pola unarndhari vaalarkku Naavi anaindha naduththariyaame." (Just as fragrance is inherent in a flower, the divine fragrance blossomed within the soul. For those who understand like a painting, it is the central pillar reached by the navel.) Now, about Gnana, the 1468th mantra: "Saththamum saththamanamum thagumanam Uyththa unarvum unarththum agandhaiyum Siththam endru immoondrum sindhikkum seygaiyum Saththam kadandhavar petra sanmaarkame." (Sound, the mind that hears sound, the mind that is fit, the consciousness that guides, and the ego that makes one conscious – these three, called Chittam, and the act of thinking, are the Sanmargam attained by those who have transcended sound.) About Sanmargam, the 1478th mantra: "Saivap perumai thani nayaga nandi Uyya vaguththa guruneri ondru undu. Theivach sivneri sanmaarkam serndhu Uyya vaiyaththullaarkku vaguththu vaiththane." (There is one unique guru path, the greatness of Saivism, established by Nandi for salvation. He established the divine Saiva path, Sanmargam, for those in the world to attain salvation.) All the above mantras describe the four paths of Saivism. Regarding the necessity of escaping the dangers of external religions, the 1530th mantra, which is the 908th mantra, states: "Aayaththul nindra arusamyangalum Kaayaththul nindra kadavulai kaankilaa Maayak kuzhiyil vizhuvar manai makkal Paasaththutru pathaikkindravaare." (The six religions that exist in the world do not see the God who resides in the body. They fall into the pit of illusion, trembling in the bondage of family and people.) The 1527th mantra, "Iruvinai neroppil innarul sakthi Guruvena vandhu gunam pala neekki Tharum enum gnanaththal than seyalatral Thrimlam theerndhu Sivan avaname," and other mantras, instruct on the equality of two karmas (iruvinai oppu), the maturity of impurities (mala paripakam), the states of Shakti Nipatha, the grace of the Guru, the state of samadhi where one's actions cease due to the knowledge thus obtained, and the benefits of liberation from bondage and attainment of Shiva. The ninth Tantra discusses the states of samadhi, such as Pranava Samadhi and Mona Samadhi, and other similar sections. These are discussed in the eighth to eleventh sutras of Sivagnana Bodham. The Thirumandiram, in the ninth Tantra, in sections like "Anainthor Thanmai" (nature of those who have attained), explains the truth discussed in the twelfth sutra of Sivagnana Bodham, that even if states of gnana samadhi are attained, the company of devotees is needed to prevent one from deviating from those qualities when those states cease. Thus, in the fifth Tantra, we find the ultimate truths of the Saiva path, as concluded in the Sivagamas. As commanded, "after practicing Chariya, Kriya, and Yoga, they will attain Shiva's feet through gnana," a good disciple who has received Saiva initiation in the fifth Tantra is in a state of attaining liberation. For that purpose, the sixth Tantra instructs on the means of attaining gnana, which are the things to be grasped through the further prescribed practices. These topics are discussed in sections such as Sivaguru Darisanam, Thiruvadi Peru, Gnana Gneyam, Thuravu, Thavam, Aruludaimaiyin Gnanam Varuthal, Avvedam, Thavavedam, Thiruneeru, Gnanavedam, Sivavedam, Appakkuvan, Pakkuvan. Would a good disciple who has understood the conclusions of Saiva Siddhanta, as instructed in the fifth Tantra, still need such further means of gnana? Certainly, they would. If one stops at the stage of merely hearing, the benefit will not be realized. One must reflect on what has been heard through one's own experience and clarify it. Having clarified it, one must remain in that path and attain Nishthai (steadfastness). Among the ten favors the Lord bestows upon souls (Dasakariyam), six favors are done concerning Pasam (bondage) and Pasu (soul). We see the truth that after the purification of the soul, which comes at the end of these, the soul attains Sivabhogam (enjoyment of Shiva) and remains inseparable, through the fulfillment of Sivaroopam and the other four stages. These four stages are systematically instructed in this Tantra and in the subsequent seventh, eighth, and ninth Tantras. Here, it is taught that Thiruvadam, Thiruneeru, Rudraksham, and other Shiva practices, Sivaguru Darisanam, the discipline of renunciation, penance, and the realization that gnana comes through Shiva's grace, are all needed. Now, the excellent topics instructed in the seventh Tantra include the six Aadharas (psychic centers), the various types of Shivalingas such as Anda Lingam, Arul Oli, Gurulinga Sangama Pujas, Pindam, Manam, Gnanam, Sivan, and the five types of Adithars (suns) that manifest in them, the method of controlling the five senses, Satguru's instructions, and so on. These explain Sivaroopam and Sivadharisanam among the Dasakariyam. Rules of eating, rules of begging, the path of the perfect cave, samadhi, samadhi kriyai, bindu jayam are also discussed. Worshipping the six types of lingas, such as Anda Lingam, within the six Aadharas, and worshipping Shiva as he manifests brightly in the three places of Guru, Linga, and Jangama, by merging into the light of grace – these are the great topics prescribed in Saiva books of knowledge, to be performed both internally and externally. The method of controlling the five senses, avoiding improper conduct, feeling compassion for those in distress – these are the qualities that a steadfast disciple should adopt and follow. We see the worship of Guru, Linga, and Jangama, and Shiva practices, in many of the histories of the true Nayanmars in the Periyapuranam. At the end of this, the author concludes with an instruction: "Sagindra podhum thalaivanai naadumin Embirana endru neeridavaar adiyaar nigazh thevargal Saarnthavarkku inbam kodukkum thazhal vannan." (Even at the time of death, seek the Lord. The devotees who pour water saying "Our Lord" are the manifest gods. The fiery-hued one bestows joy upon those who approach Him.)

"Ondre kulamum oruvane devanum" (One clan and one God) are excellent gems of instruction. The meaning of "Ondre kulam" here is that all these souls belong to the same clan or class of "pasu" (souls). Now, even though God and souls are both "chit" (consciousness), only the Lord is God. Other souls are His servants. We recall here what Sivagnana Siddhiyar says: "Sivan Jeevan endru irandum chiththu ondram enil Sivan arul siththi avan arulai seram chiththu" (If Shiva and Jeevan are both one consciousness, then Shiva's grace is the perfection, and the consciousness that attains His grace). Therefore, we must reject all the various modern interpretations of "Ondre kulam" that people invent according to their own whims. Now, the various states of Jagrat (waking), Svapna (dream), Sushupti (deep sleep), Turiya (fourth state), Turiyatita (beyond the fourth state), and the states of Kevala, Sakala, Suddha, and the actions of a gnanin such as Arivudayam (dawn of knowledge), Upashantham (tranquility), Arantham (beginning), Mahavakyam (great saying), Vaimai (truthfulness), Ava Aruththal (cutting off desire), Bhakti (devotion), Mukti Udaiyamai (possession of liberation), and other rare and great topics are discussed. In the eighth Tantra, the idea is to instruct on the state of Sivayoga, union with Shiva, after Sivadharisanam (vision of Shiva) among the Dasakariyam. The states of Sivayoga discussed here are to be understood through direct experience by approaching experienced Sivayogis. The section called Mahavakyam contains a very profound meaning. The true meaning of the Siddhanta principle discussed in various Vedas, such as "Tat Tvam Asi" and "Sivadvaitam," is explained here. We see that the last section of this Tantra, called "Sodhanai" (testing), indicates the ultimate state of Sivayoga and serves as an introduction to the beginning of the subsequent state of Sivabhogam. "Pemman perunandhi pechchatra perinpaththu Ammaan adi thandha arutkadal aadinom. Emmaayamum viduththu emmai karandhittu Summaa thirundhidum sodhanaiyaagume" (We bathed in the ocean of grace that gave the feet of the Lord, the great Nandi, the Lord of wordless supreme bliss. Abandoning all illusions, hiding ourselves, remaining still is the test) is the 2635th mantra. Now, the ultimate state of attaining Shiva's feet through gnana is instructed in the ninth Tantra. This is the final stage, the state of completion, called Sivabhogam in Dasakariyam. This is the state where the soul, after uniting with Shiva through yoga, experiences Shiva and remains there. This is Sivasaayujyam. The sections here, such as Gurumada Darisanam, Gnanaguru Darisanam, Thirukkuththu Darisanam, Akasha Peru, Gnanodayam, Satya Gnananandam, Svaroopa Udayam, Sivadharisanam, Sivaroopa Darisanam, indicate states of experience rather than states of practice. Pranava Samadhi, Sthula Sukshma Ati Sukshma Panchaksharams, Mauna Samadhi, describe the nature of remaining in a state of deep experience, where the Panchaksharams are placed in their respective positions in such a way that all things are contained within them, and one becomes that itself. We see here that the practice of Panchaksharam leads to absorption in Sivabhogam. Furthermore, we can find a true explanation of these in Sivagnana books such as Sivaprakasam and Thiruvarutpayan. The sections on Varaiyarai Maatchi (greatness of definition), Anainthor Thanmai (nature of those who have attained), Thothiram (praise), Sarva Vyapi (all-pervading) describe the qualities of great souls immersed in Sivabhogam. We see the qualities of such great souls immersed in Sivabhogam in some of the following mantras. "Malaimillai maasillai maanabimanam Kulamillai kollum gunangalum angu illai Nalmillai nandhiyai gnanaththinale Palamanni anbil pathiththu vaipporkke" (There is no impurity, no blemish, no great ego. There is no caste, no qualities to possess there. There is no good. For those who firmly establish Nandi in love through knowledge) is the 2957th mantra. "Aaraalum ennai amatta ottadhu ini Seeraar piraan vandhu en sindhai pugundhanan Seeraadi ange thirivadallal ini Yaar paadum saara arivarinththene" (No one can now make me wander. The glorious Lord has come and entered my heart. Now, other than wandering there in glory, I have known the knowledge that does not approach anyone else) is the 2960th mantra. Thirumandiram describes Sarva Vyapi as follows: "Unarvum avane uyirum avane Punarvum avane pulanum avane Inarvum avan thannai ennalum aakaan Thunaril malarkkandham thunni nindrane." (He is consciousness, He is life, He is union, He is the senses. He is also the fragrance of the clustered flowers, He cannot be thought of as separate from Himself.) These sections explain the truths of the 10th and 11th sutras of Sivagnana Bodham. The section "Thirukkuththu Darisanam" (Vision of the Sacred Dance) explains very profound gnana topics. In this, "Sivananda Kuththu" (Dance of Shiva's Bliss) is the eternal dance of bliss that gnanis perceive and experience. Thirugnanasambandar Peruman explains this as "Nindra sayangiya aadale ninaippathe niyamame" (To remember the self-luminous dance that stands is the rule). The sacred dance that is eternally performed with the five divine functions for the sake of other souls is different. That is taught in the sections on Sundara Kuththu, Porpathik Kuththu, Portillaik Kuththu, Arputha Kuththu. The nature of these can be understood by hearing from experienced teachers. The mantra for Sundara Kuththu is as follows: "Andangal ezhinukku appuraththu appaal Undendra sakthi sadhasivaththu uchchimel Kandangariyaan karunai thiruvuruk Kondu angu umai kaanak kuththu ugandhane." (Beyond the seven worlds, above the Sadashiva, the dark-throated one, taking a form of grace, danced with delight for Uma to see.) The mantra explaining Porpathik Kuththu is as follows: "Therkkum vadakku kizhakku mel kutchiyil Arputhamadhor anju mugaththilum Oppil perinpaththu ubaya ubayaththul Tharparan endru thaninadam seyyume." (In the south, north, east, and west, in the five wonderful faces, in the unparalleled supreme bliss, in both the internal and external, the Supreme One performs His unique dance.) Portillaik Kuththu is as follows: "Andangal or ezhulum ambar pathiyaaga Pandai akasangal aindhum pathiyaaga Thondinil sakthi thiru ambalamaaga Kondu paranjothi kuththu ugandhane." (With the seven worlds as the sky, with the five ancient akashas as the sky, with Shakti as the sacred hall of service, the Supreme Light danced with delight.) The mantra for Arputha Kuththu is as follows: "Netrikku nere puruvaththu idaiveli Uttrutru paarkka olividum mandhiram Patrukkup patraay paraman irundhidum Sitrambalam endru serndhu kondene." (Directly at the forehead, in the space between the eyebrows, when one intently gazes, a mantra shines. The Supreme One resides as the support of all supports. I have attained that Chidambaram.) "Ambalamavathu akila sarasaram Ambalamavathu anjezhuththame." (The hall is the entire universe, the hall is the five letters.) Now, in this Tantra, there is a section called "Sunya Sambhashanai." In Tamil, it is called "Maraiporul Kilavi," and in Sanskrit, "Sunya Sambhashanai." In this, highly profound gnana-inducing topics are discussed in other languages in a way that is not easily understood. Sunya does not mean nothingness. It means that which is hidden and not manifest. We see this in medical texts in our Indian tradition. It is customary to understand these by receiving direct instruction from an experienced teacher. Otherwise, there is a danger that its meaning will be distorted and lead to peril. This is the tradition followed by our elders. For example, a mantra from this Sunya Sambhashanai: "Vazhudhulaiyai viththida paagal mulaiththathu Puzhudhiyai thondinen poosani pooththathu Thozhudhukondu odinar thottakkudigal Muzhudhum pazhuththathu vazhaikkaniye." (When the vazhudhulai was sown, bitter gourd sprouted. When I dug the dust, pumpkin blossomed. The garden dwellers ran, worshipping. The banana fruit ripened completely.) Vazhudhulai is a type of herb belonging to the brinjal family, called vazhudhunai. It is a sattvic food consumed by sadhakas. Sowing vazhudhulai refers to the initial practices of Saiva sadhana, such as dietary discipline and the Chariya stage. Paagal (bitter gourd) is bitter. "When vazhudhulai was sown, bitter gourd sprouted" means that upon the initial practice, the state of iruvinai oppu (equality of two karmas), where both karmas are equally disliked, arises. This is called sukha dukha abhedam (indifference to pleasure and pain) in Sanskrit, and iruvinai oppu in Tamil. That is the meaning of "Vazhudhulaiyai viththida paagal mulaiththathu." Now, "digging the dust" refers to performing actions conducive to Sivabhogam in the higher sadhanas of Kriya and Yoga. By "dust," it implies a state that has already been refined by some practice. "Digging" means further achieving it. If one does so, "I dug the dust, and pumpkin blossomed." "Pumpkin blossomed" means that Shiva begins to manifest. By "blossoming," the initial stage conducive to further results is indicated here. "The garden dwellers ran, worshipping" refers to those who previously performed actions conducive to results in the land, who are called dwellers. Here, "garden dwellers" refers to the instruments (karuvis and karanas) of the soul, which were previously associated with bondage and aided the soul's efforts. These are the internal instruments (antahkaranam) and external instruments (bahirkkaranam). The meaning of "these garden dwellers ran, worshipping" is that when the soul, which was previously subject to the senses, undertook Shiva practices, these instruments and karanas subsided to the extent that they aided it, and then, in the actionless state of Sivabhogam, these instruments and karanas lost their power and merged into that state. Now, "the banana fruit ripened completely." This indicates the blissful result of the maturity of Sivabhogam. Thiruvachagam says, "Vazhaippazhaththin manam kasiviththu" (making the banana fruit's fragrance flow). Similarly, the 2874th mantra, which begins with "Pattippasukkall," states: "Pattippasukkall 24la Kuttip pasukkall or ezhula aindhula Kudappal soriyinum pattip pasuve panavarkku vaiththadhe." (Even if the 24 large cows and the seven or five small cows yield milk, the large cow is reserved for the wealthy.) In this, "24 large cows" refers to the 24 Atma Tattvas. "Seven or five small cows" refers to the seven Vidya Tattvas and the five Shiva Tattvas, respectively. "Kudappal" refers to the abundance of benefit. "Panavan" refers to those who are wealthy at the level of Atma Tattvas, and also to the Mayavada Ekanmavadhigal (monists who believe in the oneness of the soul with Brahman). Similarly, many other things can be understood. Now, a little information about the commentaries on Thirumandiram. Most Thirumandiram commentaries have followed this commentary. Ummapathi Sivachariar classifies Thirumooladevar Nayanar as one of the masters of Iyal Tamil (literary Tamil). Chapter 02 — Dhandi Adigal Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Volume 909, Vamparaa Varivandu Sarakkam, by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar Puranam. In Periyapuranam, the history of Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar follows the history of Thirumooladevar Nayanar. The fifth verse of Thiruththondaththogai, composed by Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, begins with "Vamparaa Varivandu." Accordingly, the chapter in Periyapuranam is named "Vamparaa Varivandu Sarakkam." In that fifth verse, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal mentioned the histories of Thirugnanasambandar Peruman, Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar, and Thirumooladevar Nayanar, and we reflected on them in the order they appear in Periyapuranam. Now, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, who sang "Nambiran Thirumoolan adiyarkkum adiyen" (I am a servant to the servants of our Lord Thirumoolan), next sings "Naattamigu Thandikkum Moorkarkkum adiyen" (I am a servant to the discerning Dhandi and to Moorkar). In this half-line, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal praises two Nayanmars. By "Naattamigu Thandi," he praises Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar, and by singing "Moorkarkkum adiyen," he praises Moorka Nayanar. Therefore, this should be understood as "Naattamigu Thandikkum adiyen" and "Moorkarkkum adiyen." Accordingly, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal has sung about Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar as "Naattamigu Thandikkum adiyen" in the collection of hymns, Thiruththondaththogai. "Naattamigu Thandikkum adiyen" means "I am a servant to Dhandi Adigal, who has abundant vision." The word "Naattam" here refers to the eye. "Naattam miguthal" means to acquire something that was not present before. Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar was born blind. Therefore, he incarnated in this way so that he could contemplate the sacred feet of Lord Shiva entirely within his heart, without seeing the external world. Later in this Puranam, we will see that by the grace of the Lord, he is blessed with external vision in addition to his internal vision. Therefore, having experienced the Lord's feet with his inner eye, he is now going to experience and see the Lord's feet with his outer eye as well, by the grace of the Lord. Thus, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal sings "Naattamigu Thandikkum adiyen." This is the meaning of "Naattamigu Thandikkum adiyen" found in the collection of hymns. Now, in the detailed work, Thiruththondar Thiruvandhadhi, composed by Nambiyandar Nambigal Perumanar, the 37th verse about Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar is as follows: "Kannaar mani ondrum indri kayiru pidiththu ararkkuth Thannaar punal thadam thottalum thannai nagum amanar Kannaangu izhappu amanar kalakkam kandu am malarkkan Vin nayaganidaippettravan Arur viral Thandi enbadhum." This is the verse about Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar in the detailed work, Thiruththondar Thiruvandhadhi. Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar, who was born blind in Thiruvarur, was performing sacred service when the Kamalalayam Theertham in Thiruvarur was encroached upon by Jains and had become overgrown. Distressed by this, Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar, though blind, tied a rope as a guide and descended into the Kamalalayam sacred tank to perform the sacred service. When the Jains mocked him, by the grace of our Lord, Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar regained his eyesight, and the Jains lost their eyesight and left Thiruvarur as per their vow. Such is the glorious history of Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar. Nambiyandar Nambigal Perumanar briefly sings about it in the detailed work. The meaning of the verse in the detailed work is as follows: "Even without a speck of light in his eyes, he held a rope and groped his way to dig a tank with cool water for Lord Shiva. Seeing the Jains, who spoke harsh words, mocked him, and caused him distress, lose their eyesight and become confused, he, Dhandi Adigal, who received his flower-like eyes from the Lord of the heavens, is the mighty Dhandi who incarnated in Thiruvarur." This is the meaning of the verse in the detailed work. In accordance with the collection of hymns and the detailed work, the history of Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar is the 31st history in this Vamparaa Varivandu Sarakkam, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, following the history of Thirumooladevar Nayanar. The Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar Puranam is the section that narrates the history and character of the Nayanar named Dhandi Adigal. In chronological order, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman begins to narrate the history of Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar as the fourth history in the Vamparaa Varivandu Sarakkam. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman composes the Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar Puranam in 26 verses. Now, let us reflect on the history in the detailed work, Periyapuranam. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman has structured the first verse of this Puranam to begin with the Nayanar's sacred name. We saw this previously in the history of Thirunavukkarasu Peruman. We saw there that the history began with the sacred name of Thirunavukkarasu Peruman, "Thirunavukkarasu valar Thiruththondin neri vazha" (May the path of sacred service, nurtured by Thirunavukkarasu, flourish). Similarly, this Puranam also begins with the sacred name of Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar, as composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. The first verse is: "Dhandi Adigal Thiruvarur pirakkum perumai thavam udaiyar Andavanar marai paada aadum semponkkazhal manaththukkonda Karuththin agan nokkum kurippe anri puram nokkum Kanda unarvu thurandhar pol pirandha pozhudhe kan kanaar enbadhu thiruppadal." (Dhandi Adigal, who was born in Thiruvarur, possessed the greatness of penance. He was born blind, as if he had renounced the external vision that perceives objects, with the sole intention of seeing with his inner eye the red golden feet of the Lord, which the celestial beings sing and dance to.) Our Nayanar, whose sacred name is Dhandi Adigal, possessed the great penance of being born in Thiruvarur. Being born in Thiruvarur grants liberation. Therefore, being born in Thiruvarur is possible only for those who are worthy of liberation through mature penance. To such individuals, the Lord grants the divine grace of being born in Thiruvarur. Therefore, being born in Thiruvarur itself bestows the qualification for liberation. Among all penances, the greatest penance is being born in Thiruvarur. Therefore, that great penance bestows the great good of liberation. Thus, it grants birth in Thiruvarur. Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar, who incarnated in such a Thiruvarur, was born blind, as if he had renounced the external vision that perceives worldly objects, with the sole intention of seeing with his inner eye the red golden feet of the Lord, which he held in his mind. He was born blind. Therefore, he desired only the undivided inner vision. He did not desire the divided external vision. When did he renounce this? He was one who had attained such great previous penance. He had wished for this in previous births and now received this greatness. He incarnated with the intention of firmly establishing the Lord's feet in his mind and meditating on them as his sole goal. He incarnated in Thiruvarur with the intention of seeing only the sacred feet of Thyagesa Peruman, who dances in Thiruvarur while the gods chant the Vedas, with his inner eye. Inner vision means meditating only on the Lord's feet. It is a state of not seeing anything other than the Lord's feet. In this sacred history, we will see the Jains belittling his state of being born blind. We will also see that they were ruined because of it. Therefore, to ensure that people who read this history do not easily misunderstand the Nayanar's state of having only inner vision and make mistakes, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, with his divine poetic skill, reveals to us the true intention of the Nayanar's incarnation. That is the divine grace of Sekkizhar Peruman. Now, the second verse: "Kaanum kannal kaanbadhu meyththonde aana karuththu udaiyar Penum selvaththuth Thiruvarur peruman adigal thiruvadikke Poonum anbinnal paravi potrum nilaimai purindhu amarar Senum ariya ariya thiruththondil seriyach sirandhu ullar." (Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar, who believed that what is to be seen with the seeing eye is only true service, was greatly distinguished in the sacred service, which even the celestials find difficult to comprehend, by praising and worshipping the sacred feet of the Lord of Thiruvarur, who resides in the cherished and prosperous Thiruvarur, with the love he embraced.) Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar believed that what is to be seen with the seeing eye is only the true service of the Lord. As expressed in sacred sayings like "Kaanadha kankalellam kaanadha kankale kaanakkann vai pesadha peykallode kankall kaanminkallo" (All unseen eyes are unseen eyes; O eyes, see with the dumb devils who do not speak), what is to be seen with the eyes is indeed the sacred service of Lord Shiva. Therefore, whether with the inner eye or the outer eye, one must see only the Lord's feet. One must see only the Lord's sacred service. However, the inner eye is more special than the outer eye. It is revered as the eye of knowledge. The verse states that one must see only the true service of the Lord. This means that first, a desire arises to perform service to the Lord. Then, actions follow, thinking "how shall we do this?" Therefore, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman has stated this because the desire that precedes the action becomes the cause. "Meyththondhu" means "service of the truth." "Mei" (truth) refers to Sat (Existence), which is the Lord. Therefore, "meyththondhu" means service of the Lord, service rendered to the Lord. If "mei" is taken as the body, then "meyththondhu" also means service performed with the body. Therefore, Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar, desiring the state of praising and worshipping the sacred feet of Thyagesa Peruman, who resides in the cherished and prosperous Thiruvarur, with the love he embraced, is greatly distinguished in the sacred service rendered to Thyagesa Peruman. The service to Shiva and the service to devotees are sacred services that even the celestials find very difficult to comprehend. The celestials are those who perform minor good deeds and, as a result, enjoy heavenly pleasures in the celestial world. After they have enjoyed the fruits of their minor good deeds, they return to birth again. Moreover, they experience many sufferings amidst those pleasures and take many births as a result. But the devotees of Shiva perform major good deeds, remove birth, and attain the state of eternal bliss, which is liberation. That bliss of liberation is a distant bliss for the celestials. That is why this major good deed, service to Shiva, and service to devotees, is of a greatness that even the celestials cannot comprehend. Our Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar is greatly distinguished in such service. We will continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 03 — Dhandi Adigal Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Volume 910, Vamparaa Varivandu Sarakkam, by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar Puranam. The third verse: "Poovaar sadila thirumudiyar magizhndha selvap poongoyil Devaasiriyan mun irainji valam seyvaarayaai Semmaipurin naavaal inbamurum kaadhal Namasivaya narpadhame Ova anbil eduththu odhi oru naal pola varum naalil Sengann vidaiyar thirukkoiyil kudapaal theerththak kulaththin paangu engum amanar paazhigalaai Idaththaal kuraipaadu eydhudhal angu annilaimai thanai Dhandi Adigal arindhe aadharavaal ingu naan ikkulam perugak kallavenndum endru ezhundhaar." These are the third and fourth verses. The Lord is one who has a sacred head adorned with matted locks intertwined with flowers. "Poo" (flower) here refers to the Konrai flower, but by generally saying "poo," it can also include flowers like Thumbai and Vellierukku. However, the special privilege belongs to the Konrai flower. Such a Lord, whose sacred head is adorned with flowers including Konrai, resides joyfully in the prosperous temple of Poongoyil in Thiruvarur. There is a Devaasiriyan mandapam there. When one enters the Thiruvarur temple through the gopuram (tower gate), the first thing one sees is the Devaasiriyan Thirumandapam. It is a sacred hall always filled with devotees. Therefore, it is the first to be worshipped. After that, one should go inside and worship the Lord residing in the Poongoyil. This is the rule for visiting the Thiruvarur temple. Therefore, the tradition of worshipping devotees first, and then worshipping the Lord by associating with the feet of devotees, is shown here. Thus, he first worships and circumambulates the Devaasiriyan Thirumandapam, and then worships the Lord in the Poongoyil. Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar is one who, with a tongue that performs righteousness, continuously chants the sacred word "Namasivaya" with joyful love and unwavering devotion. His sacred tongue is a tongue that performs righteousness. His tongue is accustomed to speaking only of the Lord Shiva's qualities without interruption. We see here that the idea of "Naan marakkinum sollum naa Namasivayave" (Even if I forget, my tongue will say Namasivaya) is relevant. When chanting the Sri Panchaksharam, one should do so with joyful love. One should chant it with desire. One should chant the Sri Panchaksharam remembering the favors the Lord does for us. In accordance with Thirugnanasambandar Peruman's words, "Kaadhalaagi kasindhu kannir malki odhuvaar" (Those who chant with love, melting and shedding tears), Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar chants the sacred word "Namasivaya" with such joyful love. The Sri Panchaksharam, the five letters, is the sacred name of the Lord. Therefore, it is a word. It is a good word. It is a word that bestows good. Goodness is liberation, which is moksha. He chants it according to the proper rules. The Sri Panchaksharam can be chanted in three ways: Manasam (mental), Mandam (muttering), and Urai (spoken). Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar chants it in the "Urai" (spoken) manner. Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar circumambulates the temple and chants the Sri Panchaksharam every day, just like any other day. To the west of the temple of the Lord, who has a red-eyed bull, the Thiruvarur temple, lies the Kamalalayam Theertham. It is a beautiful sacred tank. Lakshmi performed penance here, so it is named Kamalalayam. This tank is located to the west of the temple. Therefore, the east side of the Kamalalayam Theertham is the temple, and the other three sides are streets. In such a situation, the Jains encroached upon the other three sides of the Kamalalayam Theertham. The place was occupied by Jain monasteries, caves, and seats, becoming Jain dwellings. The Jains encroached upon the vast expanse of the sacred tank, gradually reducing its area. They filled up the depth of the sacred tank and occupied the raised ground. It is a well-known fact that the Thiruvarur temple, tank, and canal each cover five acres. The saying is "Koyil aindhu veli, kulam aindhu veli, sengazhunir odai aindhu veli" (Temple five acres, tank five acres, Sengazhunir canal five acres). Thus, the Thiruvarur Kamalalayam Theertham covered five acres. The Jains were encroaching upon it in this manner. Such selfish encroachment of public places for personal gain is one of the most corrupt human traits observed in both ancient and modern times. Dhandi Adigal Nayanar became aware of this situation. He resolved in his heart that the area of this Kamalalayam Theertham should be as vast as it once was. He decided that efforts should be made to achieve this. He resolved to desilt the sacred tank and dig out the overgrown areas. He undertook efforts to do so. We must understand that all this arose from his immense love for the Lord, and not from evil thoughts such as envy or hatred towards the Jains. It is a known truth, supported by historical evidence, that the Jains caused great harm to the Saiva path in those days, just as they did in the Nadu Nadu, Pandya Nadu, and Thondai Nadu regions. We also saw this in the history of Naminandhi Adigal Nayanar. Now, the fifth and sixth verses: "Kuzhivaai adhanil kurinattu kattum kayiru kulakkaraiyil Izhivaai puraththu naduththariyodu isaiyak katti idai thadavi Vazhiyaal vandhu mann kalli eduththu mariththum thadavi poy Ozhiyaa muyarchiyaal uyththaar odhum ezhuththu anjudanuyppar. Nanni naalum nal thondar nayandha viruppaal migapperugi Annal theerththakkulam kallak kanda amanar poraaragi Enni Dhandiyadigal paal eydhi mun nindru iyambuvaar Mannai kallil pirani padum varuththam vendam endru uraiththaar." The divine Sekkizhar Peruman explains the sacred deeds performed by Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar, who resolved to desilt the sacred tank. The details of the sacred work he performed are described here: how Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar, though blind, descended into the tank and systematically dug the tank and its banks, and how he brought the soil out. Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar first planned and resolved it in his mind's eye, and then performed the sacred deed externally. We will see the histories of Poosalar Nayanar and Vayilar Nayanar, who built temples in their minds and worshipped. In the Sivagamas, the section on Antaryagam (internal sacrifice) in the method of Sivapuja (worship of Shiva) is of this nature. Sivagnana Siddhiyar states that Antaryagam is proclaimed as a means to liberation. Therefore, before performing an external action, an internal action must be performed. Thus, here, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman elaborately describes the unique greatness of Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar's sacred service, performed with his inner eye, even though he was externally blind, by dividing it into separate sections in one verse. So, how did Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar perform this sacred task? He dug the earth inside the tank to create a pit. He then planted a pole, a staff, in it. This was to mark the place from where the earth needed to be dug out. Then, he planted another pole on the bank of the tank. This was the place where the dug-out earth from the tank was to be deposited. After planting these two poles, he took a good rope, first tied it to the pole on the bank, and then tied the rope to the pole planted inside the tank. Since he was blind, he thought he could perform this sacred task by holding onto this rope. Thus, he descended into the tank, dug the earth, and carrying the earth, he touched and felt the rope, and following its path, he went up and deposited the earth on the bank. He did this with unceasing effort. How did he do it? He did it while continuously chanting the sacred five letters. As Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar's sacred body performed the sacred service of digging and depositing the earth, he also performed the service of chanting the Sri Panchaksharam with his sacred mouth. All those who engage in divine service should always perform it by chanting the Sri Panchaksharam as a mantra. We saw earlier that Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar did this while circumambulating the temple. Therefore, performing service by speaking and thinking differently with the mouth and mind is not only unproductive but also carries the danger of becoming an offense. Therefore, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman emphasizes and instructs us here that those who perform sacred service should keep this in mind and perform the sacred service of the temple by chanting the Sri Panchaksharam with both mind and mouth. Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar, a good devotee, performs this sacred task with a mind completely devoted to the feet of Lord Shiva, without any differing thoughts. After the king worshipped and departed, Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar worshipped the golden feet, which even Vishnu and Brahma do not know, completed the remaining part of his sacred service, and with a meditative mind, chanted the sacred five letters without fail. Remaining steadfast in this discipline, he attained the great glory of the shade of the feet of Lord Shiva, who has shining matted locks. Attaining the shade of the Lord's feet is the greatest of all blessings. It is specifically referred to as Mukti or Moksha. Thus, Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar worshipped and attained the Lord's feet. The 26th verse, the concluding verse: "Kannin manigal avaiyinri kayiru thadavi kulam thotta Ennil perumai thiruththondar paadham irainji idar neengi Vennil vaazhvaar tham vendap purangal vegundhaar verkaattur Ull nilaavum pugazh thondar moorkkar seygai uraikindrom." The 26th verse states: "Having worshipped the feet of Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar, the sacred devotee of immeasurable greatness, who, without the light of his eyes, groped with a rope and dug the tank, and having been freed from obstacles – obstacles such as ignorance and other internal hindrances that may arise in the upcoming sacred histories, and external hindrances caused by others – by the grace of Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar's feet, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman now proceeds to narrate the deeds of Moorka Nayanar, the renowned devotee who resides in Thiruvirkattur, where the Lord, who burned the three cities at the request of the celestials residing in the heavens, is enraged." Thus, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman concludes the history of Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar and begins to narrate the history of Moorka Nayanar. Let us also conclude the history of Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar, and with the support of his sacred feet, continue to reflect on the history of Moorka Nayanar. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of our Lord Dhandi Adigalar Nayanar. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of Nambiyandar Nambigal. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Praise, praise to the sacred feet of the great commentator. Thiruchitrambalam.
