Chapter 01 — Eanati Nathar Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Having reflected upon the Puranam of Eripattar Nayanar in the Third Canto, "Ilai Malinda Sarukkam," of Periya Puranam, we shall now reflect upon the Puranam of Eanati Nathar Nayanar. As usual, we will first reflect upon a summary of Eanati Nathar Nayanar's story and then delve into a detailed reflection on the Nayanar's Puranam. Eynanur is a very ancient village in the Chola country, made fertile by its fields. Eanati Nathar Nayanar was born into the Ilakula Sanrar lineage in that village and lived there. Eanati Nathar Nayanar always excelled in the worship of the sacred ash (Thiruneeru), never falling short. His profession was teaching swordsmanship to kings and those of royal lineage. He performed this profession and used the earnings to provide for the needs of Shiva's devotees. Eanati Nathar Nayanar lived a life of good conduct, admired even by his enemies. Similarly, there was a man named Atisuran who was also engaged in the profession of teaching swordsmanship. This Atisuran was arrogant, believing that no one was more skilled than him in this profession. However, his income from this profession was dwindling. But Eanati Nathar Nayanar's income continued to increase. Because of this, Atisuran became jealous of Eanati Nathar Nayanar. Driven by jealousy, he dared to confront Eanati Nathar Nayanar. So, he gathered others who would assist him and went to Eanati Nathar Nayanar, challenging him to a fight. He stood before Eanati Nathar Nayanar, proposing a contest: whoever won the fight would continue to teach swordsmanship to the kings. Eanati Nathar Nayanar agreed to the battle with him. Eanati Nathar Nayanar and Atisuran, along with their respective armies, reached a common ground. A great battle ensued between the two sides. Then, Eanati Nathar Nayanar and Atisuran joined the armies and fought face to face. Atisuran, unable to withstand Eanati Nathar Nayanar's swordplay, faltered, was defeated, and fled, showing his back. Atisuran could not sleep that entire night. He lamented that he had been defeated by Eanati Nathar Nayanar, losing all his honor and respect. Therefore, he decided that Eanati Nathar Nayanar could only be defeated by deceit. He realized that he could not defeat him in a direct battle. So, he began to plot treachery. The next morning, as soon as he woke up, he went to Eanati Nathar Nayanar and proposed that their two armies should not clash. He suggested that only he and Eanati Nathar Nayanar should fight alone. Eanati Nathar Nayanar agreed to this and, carrying his sword and shield, stood waiting for Atisuran at the secluded spot he had designated, unknown to anyone. Before this, Atisuran had already learned about Eanati Nathar Nayanar. He knew that Eanati Nathar Nayanar would not harm anyone who had applied sacred ash. Therefore, intending to defeat Eanati Nathar Nayanar by deceit, Atisuran smeared sacred ash all over his forehead. He had never applied sacred ash before. Yet, to conquer Eanati Nathar Nayanar through treachery, he now applied sacred ash. Although he applied sacred ash to his forehead, deceit remained in his heart. With this appearance of sacred ash, carrying his sword and shield, he too went to the designated secluded spot. Eanati Nathar Nayanar was waiting for him there. Until he approached Eanati Nathar Nayanar, he concealed the sacred ash on his forehead with his shield and went close to Eanati Nathar Nayanar.

Eanati Nathar Nayanar, seeing Atisuran approaching him, attempted to strike and kill him. Just as Eanati Nathar Nayanar came very close to Atisuran, Atisuran removed the shield that had been covering his forehead. There, Eanati Nathar Nayanar saw the sacred ash on Atisuran's forehead. Upon seeing the sacred ash on Atisuran's forehead, Eanati Nathar Nayanar thought, "Aha! This Atisuran, who never applied sacred ash before, has now come here with the brilliance of sacred ash! He has become a devotee of Lord Shiva." He decided, "I will abide by the intention of this person who has appeared as a devotee of Shiva by applying sacred ash." He thought of throwing away his sword and shield. However, if he were to throw away his sword and shield, he would stand disarmed. If Atisuran were to attack him then, the sin of attacking a disarmed person would fall upon Atisuran. Thinking that such a sin should not befall a Shiva devotee who wears sacred ash, Eanati Nathar Nayanar merely held his sword and shield in his hands as a formality. Atisuran committed the wicked act he had intended when he came with the sacred ash. Eanati Nathar Nayanar, upon seeing the Shiva devotee with sacred ash, recognized that his former enemy had now become a Shiva devotee and bowed before him. To ensure that not even a slight sin would befall the Shiva devotee, he did not stand disarmed but held his sword and shield as a formality. Atisuran committed the wicked act he had intended. At that moment, Lord Shiva appeared, severed all of Eanati Nathar Nayanar's bonds, and bestowed upon him the state of loving union, eternally inseparable from Him. Lord Shiva granted Eanati Nathar Nayanar the rare liberation (mukti). Arumuga Navalar writes the following commentary on the Puranam of Eanati Nathar Nayanar: "Those who wear the sacred ash, the symbol of Shiva, with true devotion, will attain Shiva's abode. If one joyfully applies the sacred ash, the armor worn by the Lord of Skulls, without fail, then karmic bonds will not remain, and one will attain the path to Shiva, reaching the beautiful feet of Shiva," says the Thirumandiram. Those who have great devotion to this glorious sacred ash, upon seeing Shiva devotees wearing it, will worship them without distinguishing between the Shiva devotees and Shiva. Eanati Nathar Nayanar excelled in this boundless devotion to the sacred ash. He spent all the wealth he acquired from his profession of teaching martial arts on devotees who wore sacred ash. When his enemy, Atisuran, came to kill him, armed and disregarding all rules of war, Eanati Nathar Nayanar, despite having the power to kill him, stood allowing Atisuran's desire to be fulfilled, seeing the sacred ash on his forehead. This magnanimity arose from Eanati Nathar Nayanar's devotion to the sacred ash. Eanati Nathar Nayanar was such that whatever Shiva's devotees did, he considered it pleasing to himself, out of immense devotion. The compassionate Lord Shiva granted us this rare human body so that we may worship Lord Shiva and attain liberation. Without this body, that would not be possible. That is, we would not have the means to attain liberation. Therefore, we must protect this body to serve Lord Shiva. Eanati Nathar Nayanar did not attempt to protect his body from his enemy; he stood without protecting his body. This was due to his devotion to Shiva.

The Shiva Agamas state that it is meritorious to sacrifice one's life out of intense devotion when there is an obstacle to Guru, Lingam, or Sangamam. We see this same devotion in Meypporul Nayanar. Now, we see the same devotion in Eanati Nathar Nayanar. Mutthanathan, who committed a great wicked act against Meypporul Nayanar, and Atisuran, who committed a great wicked act against Eanati Nathar Nayanar, are believed to have fallen into hell for their great sin of deceitfully killing true devotees. Those who, without any devotion to Lord Shiva, wear His symbols and deceive and harm others, will suffer a much greater punishment from Lord Shiva Himself than those who harm without wearing those symbols. From this, it is understood that wearing Shiva symbols like sacred ash as mere disguises is useless for those who do not follow the path prescribed by Lord Shiva. Did not Meypporul Nayanar and Eanati Nathar Nayanar, without any other thought, stand allowing their enemies' intentions to be fulfilled, merely considering that those who came before them were Shiva devotees? Devotion to Shiva was the reason for this. Thus, we clearly understand the greatness of sacred ash and the greatness of Shiva's guise from the Puranam of Meypporul Nayanar in the second canto and the Puranam of Eanati Nathar Nayanar, which we see in this third canto. We will continue to reflect on the Puranam of Eanati Nathar Nayanar in detail. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 02 — Eanati Nathar Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. We are reflecting on the Puranam of Eanati Nathar Nayanar in the Third Canto, "Ilai Malinda Sarukkam," of Periya Puranam, Part 120. We have reflected on the summary of Eanati Nathar Nayanar's Puranam. Now, we will reflect on the Nayanar's Puranam in detail. Sekkizhar Swamigal expands the Puranam of Eanati Nathar Nayanar in 42 Thiruppadalgal (verses). As is our custom, we will first reflect on the Nayanar's Puranam through the Thogai Nool (summary work), then the Vagai Nool (classified work), and then the Viri Nool (expanded work) of Periya Puranam. First, in the Thogai Nool, the patikam (decad) called Thiruththondaththogai, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal sings about Eanati Nathar Nayanar as follows: "Eanati Nathan than adiyaarkkum adiyen" (I am a servant to the devotees of Eanati Nathan). The meaning of this is, "I am a servant to the devotees of Eanati Nathar Nayanar." The Puranam of Eanati Nathar Nayanar is the second Puranam in the third canto. In the order of succession, the Puranam of Eanati Nathar Nayanar is the ninth Puranam. This Puranam is the section that describes the history and character of the Nayanar named Eanati Nathar. Next, in the Vagai Nool, Thiruththondar Thiruvandathi, Nambiandar Nambigal has composed the following Thiruppadal for Eanati Nathar Nayanar: "Patthanai Eanati Nathanai Paarneedu Eyinai than ullaththanai thannodum amar malainthaan et netri neer kandu kai thani vaal veedu ozhinthavan kandippa nindrarulu nitthanai Eezhakkula theepan enbar innee nilaththae" (They say that Eanati Nathan, the devotee, the one who is within Eyinai, who abandoned the single sword in his hand upon seeing the sacred ash on the forehead of him who fought with him, and who stands firm in grace, is the lamp of the Eezhakula on this good earth). Now, let us reflect on the Puranam of Eanati Nathar Nayanar through the Viri Nool, Periya Puranam. The Chola kings were those who planted their tiger flag on the Himalayas, extending their kingdom up to the Himalayas. The Chola kings ruled the Chola country, wearing the white royal umbrella and their Aatthippoo garland, making it very prosperous. In their glorious Chola country, there was an ancient village called Eyinannur, surrounded by groves and fields. Eyinannur is located on the south bank of the Arisil River. It is near Kumbakonam, and is now called Alagaththiri Pudur. This ancient village of Eyinannur is now known as Enanallur. There is a very old Shiva temple here. This Shiva temple was renovated with stone by a Vaishnava lady. The idol of Eanati Nathar Nayanar and his consort's idol were placed in the northeast direction, opposite the sanctum, and worshipped. Now, these idols are placed in the northern prakaram (circumambulatory path), next to the idol of Sri Manickavasagar Swamigal. Here, the Lord's name is Brahmapureeswarar, and the Goddess's name is Karpagambigai. To the west of this temple, about half a nazhigai (a unit of time/distance), on the Andaankarai, are the battleground where Eanati Nathar Nayanar fought with Atisuran. This is said to be the place where the battle took place between the two. Even now, on the Guru Puja day of Eanati Nathar Nayanar, the Lord is specially brought to this place. This place is currently known as Salaikkarai. In this village, there is also a math (monastery) named after Eanati Nathar Nayanar. This Eyinannur is a village rich in field resources. Many varieties of sugarcane grow here, adding to its prosperity. Two varieties of sugarcane, Vezhakkarumbu (hard sugarcane) and Menkarumbu (soft sugarcane), thrive here. The soft sugarcane is so tender that people can bite it with their teeth to extract juice and enjoy its taste. Thus, there is plenty of sugarcane. Yet, the paddy (rice) here grows so tall that it overshadows the sugarcane. The paddy plants here are thicker than sugarcane.

This is because of the excellent water, fertile land, rich fertilizer provided by the people, and the strength of the seeds, which make the paddy stalks grow thick and erect like sugarcane stalks. Therefore, Sekkizhar sings, "Karumbu thaazha saali oongum thanmaiyathaa" (The sugarcane bows as the paddy rises). Thus, the people living here are prosperous due to the abundance of sugarcane and paddy. The citizens are considered one of the six limbs of a government. "Padai, kudi, koozh, amaichchu, natpu, aran aarum udaiyaar arasarul eeru" (Army, citizens, wealth, ministers, allies, and fortresses – one who possesses these six is a king among kings) says the Thirukkural. Thus, due to the fertility of the Marutham land, the people here excelled in wealth, knowledge, discipline, and devotion to God. In this Eyinannur, Eanati Nathar Nayanar was born in the Eezhakulam, the Eezhakula Sanrar lineage. The Eezhakulam is said to be the lineage that undertook the profession of tapping toddy. It is also said to be a division within the Sanrar lineage. In Kerala, a hilly region, those who perform this profession are known as Eezhuvar, a caste. In Kongu Nadu, those who perform this profession are known as Sanrar, a lineage name. Sanrar has evolved into Sanar. Therefore, Eanati Nathar Nayanar was born in the Eezhakula Sanrar lineage. Eanati Nathar is his name. Moreover, Eanati was formerly the name of an official position, that of the chief of royal armies, and it was customary for kings to bestow upon such individuals an Eanati golden forehead plate and an Eanati ring for the finger as symbols of this office. We learn this from ancient Tamil texts such as Chintamani, Manimekalai, and Purananuru. Eanati, Ettti, Kavidi, etc., are honorary titles. Sekkizhar Swamigal mentions that Eanati Nathar Nayanar undertook the profession of teaching swordsmanship to kings. Therefore, Sivakavimani writes that it is doubtful whether Eanati Nathar was his official title or his given name. Eanati Nathar Nayanar excelled in the worship of the ancient sacred ash (Thiruneeru) and never deviated from it. Sacred ash is older than the most ancient things. Sacred ash is mentioned in the Vedas. It is eternal. It is the form of Parasakti. Therefore, Sekkizhar Swamigal refers to this sacred ash as ancient sacred ash, primordial sacred ash. God is called Aran because He destroys the sins of souls who reach Him. Because the result of that fire causes the sins of those who reach Him to be reduced to ash, it is called Neeru (ash). "Payilappaduvathu Neeru, Paavam Arruppathu Neeru" (That which is practiced is ash, that which severs sin is ash) says Thevaram. Those who served with the sacred ash applied to their bodies were called Neetru Thondu (service of ash). Worshipping devotees who wear sacred ash is the highest quality of Shiva devotion. Thus, Eanati Nathar Nayanar was one who understood the greatness of sacred ash, had devotion to it, and revered Shiva's devotees. He practiced the profession of teaching swordsmanship. In those days, the custom of war was to wield a sword and fight closely, one against another, to achieve victory. However, in these times, war does not occur face-to-face in that manner. Instead, it involves hiding and fighting from a distance, using modern weapons to cause widespread destruction to people, without regard for children, the elderly, the sick, or women. This kind of war is happening today. Sivakavimani condemns this as a cowardly war. The profession Eanati Nathar Nayanar held was teaching swordsmanship to kings. Therefore, it was a teaching profession. Eanati Nathar Nayanar used the earnings from this to provide for the holy devotees of Lord Shiva who wore sacred ash. The Nayanar excelled in good conduct, admired even by his enemies. Similarly, there was a man named Atisuran who lived by this profession. He lived with the arrogance that no one was superior to him in the art of swordsmanship, valuing himself highly. This pride is considered ego. To consider oneself great is ego. Generally, Tholkappiyam lists pride as one of the eight emotional expressions. Texts refer to this pride as valor. Meyppadu (emotional expression) is defined as the bodily manifestation that occurs within the mind and becomes outwardly apparent to others. Thus, are there not eight such emotional expressions that occur within the mind and manifest in the body for others to see? They are: laughter, weeping, disgust, wonder, fear, pride, anger, and joy. Pride is one of these. Pride is also called valor. It is called pride because it stands as the ultimate limit, transcending all other forms of greatness, not being equal to them. To stand superior to ordinary people in terms of knowledge, courage, wealth, and other excellences is pride. Furthermore, Ilampooranar states that pride is considering oneself great. The Tholkappiyam sutra states that there are only four types of pride: education, courage, fame, and generosity. Scholars say that there are four reasons for pride to arise in a person. Education, courage, fame, and generosity are the four causes of pride in a person. Among these four, education includes knowledge of dharma, artha, and kama (virtue, wealth, and pleasure), knowledge of arts like music, drama, and sculpture, knowledge of warfare, and other forms of learning. The second, courage, is fearlessness. That is, the strength of mind that does not fear enemies or things that cause enmity is called courage. The third is fame. This is the praise that comes from living a virtuous life, adhering to dharma, and possessing high qualities like compassion, and from the willingness to reject even the whole world if it brings blame, and from generosity and benevolence. Next, generosity is the quality of giving without limit. This generosity is not like charity, which only involves giving away one's wealth, but rather the act of giving away all of one's possessions, including one's life and body. Thus, Tholkappiyam states that pride arises in a person due to four reasons: education, courage, fame, and generosity. However, the pride that Atisuran possessed here was not the pride mentioned in such texts. Sekkizhar Swamigal states here that it arose from the arrogance that there was no one more learned or more skilled than him. In reality, true value is what others appreciate. Even if others consider one great, one should not think so, but rather consider oneself small and act accordingly. This is the path of righteousness. Therefore, one should not praise oneself or consider oneself great with arrogance. Poets and others may praise themselves, but those are exceptions. However, Atisuran here, without falling into any of these categories, considered himself great and acted with arrogance. Therefore, according to the dictum of justice, he suffered loss in his profession, which was the immediate consequence he received.

He suffered loss as a result of his arrogance, as Sekkizhar Swamigal explains in the upcoming verses. Let us continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 03 — Eanati Nathar Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 120, Ilai Malinda Sarukkam, Eanati Nathar Nayanar Puranam. Atisuran was also born in the same lineage as Eanati Nathar Nayanar. Therefore, he too was engaged in the teaching profession of swordsmanship, which was the traditional occupation of that lineage. However, Atisuran's income from this profession dwindled day by day. For Eanati Nathar Nayanar, the income from teaching swordsmanship continued to increase. Eanati Nathar Nayanar possessed great skill in teaching swordsmanship. He excelled in that art. Because of this, he lived prosperously. He lived a life admired even by his enemies. He spent all the wealth he acquired on Shiva devotees who wore sacred ash. But for Atisuran, the income from this profession decreased day by day. Therefore, Atisuran became jealous of Eanati Nathar Nayanar. Thirukkural, a book of ethics, calls such jealousy arising in one's mind "Azhukkaaru." Thiruvalluvar, in the chapter "Aran Valiyuruththal" (Emphasizing Virtue), when defining the nature of virtue, states in the 35th Kural: "Azhukkaaru avaa veguli innaachchol naankum izhukkaa iyandradhu aram" (Virtue is that which is free from these four: envy, desire, anger, and harsh words). He says that virtue is to act continuously, avoiding these four faults. Among these four faults, he places envy, or jealousy, as the first. Parimelazhagar, when defining what Azhukkaaru or jealousy is, says that it is envy upon seeing the prosperity of others. Parimelazhagar defines jealousy as the inability to tolerate or bear the prosperity, wealth, and other advancements of others. Thus, upon seeing the prosperity of others, jealousy arises in the mind, making one unable to tolerate it. This jealousy will lead to a desire to acquire the object or quality that one is jealous of. This desire will then follow the senses. Thiruvalluvar calls this "avaa" (desire) as the second fault. Thus, due to jealousy, desire for others' possessions arises. If that desire is not fulfilled, anger arises towards others. Thiruvalluvar calls this "veguli" (anger). As a result of this anger, "innaachchol" (harsh words) arise. Thus, jealousy is the fundamental cause of these faults that arise one after another: envy, desire, anger, and harsh words. Therefore, Parimelazhagar, in his commentary on this Kural, states that virtue is to act by eliminating these four faults, including jealousy. This jealousy, or "azhukkaaru," is contrary to the quality of patience. That is why Thiruvalluvap Peruman, in the order of chapters, placed a separate chapter called "Azhukkaaraamai" (Freedom from Envy) immediately after the chapter "Poraiyudaimai" (Patience). Generally, all faults that occur in the mind can be called "azhukku" (dirt). However, Thiruvalluvar here specifically defines "azhukku" as the jealousy that arises in the mind. Here too, Atisuran becomes jealous upon seeing the wealth of Eanati Nathar Nayanar. This causes desire in him. When that desire is not fulfilled, anger arises in him. This also leads to harsh words. As a result, his virtue diminished. His wickedness grew. Consequently, his wealth diminished. That is, Atisuran's prosperity decreased. So, Sekkizhar Peruman states that Atisuran's wealth decreased day by day, and the income from his profession as a swordsmanship teacher decreased day by day, isn't that right? The underlying reason for this was the jealousy he harbored towards Eanati Nathar Nayanar. That jealousy was the cause. "Avviththu azhukkaaru udaiyaanai seyyaval thavvaiyai kaattividum" (The goddess of wealth will abandon the envious person and show him to her elder sister, poverty) is Kural 167 from the chapter "Azhukkaaraamai." Its meaning is that the goddess of wealth will not associate with one who is envious upon seeing the prosperity and advancement of others. Not only that, but the goddess of wealth will send her elder sister, Thavvai (poverty), to him. Therefore, Valluvar states here that those who are envious will not only lose their wealth but also be afflicted by poverty, the elder sister. According to this Kural from the ethical text Thirukkural, due to Atisuran's jealousy, Sridevi (goddess of wealth) gradually left him and sent her elder sister (poverty) to him. Therefore, it becomes clear to us that the quality of jealousy, or "azhukkaaru," is something that a righteous person must certainly avoid. Now, if we want to avoid this quality of jealousy, what should we do? The 15th verse from "Neethineri Vilakkam," a book of ethics by Kumaraguruparar Swamigal, explains this to us. "Thammil meliyaarai nokki thamadhu udaimai amma perithendru agam magizhga. Thammilum katraarai nokki karuththazhiga katradhellaam etrae ivarkku naam endru." This is the verse. We should look at those who are less fortunate than us, those who are less wealthy than us, those who are in a lower financial position than us, and compare ourselves to them, and rejoice that our wealth is great. We should not be jealous of those who are wealthier than us. Therefore, if one wants to compare one's wealth, one should compare it with those who are less fortunate and rejoice. One should not compare oneself with those who are wealthier and become jealous. So, if we feel jealous when we see those who are wealthier than us, to remove that jealousy, we should look at those who are suffering in poverty more than us and rejoice in the wealth we possess. Next, there is a kind of pride that comes from learning, isn't there? That is, the thought or arrogance that "I have learned a lot," isn't there? How should we overcome that? He says that we should look at those who have learned more than us and think, "There are such great scholars here; the knowledge I have acquired is nothing compared to theirs," and thus suppress our intellectual pride. Therefore, when it comes to wealth, when comparing wealth, we should compare our wealth with that of those less fortunate than us and rejoice that we possess more wealth. At the same time, when we feel proud of our education and knowledge, we should think of the great scholars who have learned more than us and reduce our intellectual pride, thus Kumaraguruparar Swamigal offers us a remedy for the disease of jealousy and the disease of pride or arrogance. Here, Atisuran was also arrogant in the first verse, as we saw.

Now, in this verse, we see him being jealous of Eanati Nathar Nayanar's wealth. However, Atisuran did not rejoice by looking at those in a lower state of poverty than himself, as stated in Neethineri Vilakkam. Nor did he stand humbly, his arrogance subdued, by looking at Eanati Nathar Nayanar, who was more skilled than him. Jealousy burns within him upon seeing Eanati Nathar Nayanar's wealth. This becomes the foundation for the great sin he is about to commit later. Because of this jealousy, he intended to defeat Eanati Nathar Nayanar through deceit. He committed that great sinful act. Therefore, he fell into hell. "Azhukkaaru ena oru paavi thiruchchetrru thee izhi uththuvidum" (The sinner called jealousy will destroy one's wealth and lead one to a fiery hell) is Kural number 168 on "Azhukkaaraamai." Its meaning is that the sinner called jealousy will destroy the wealth of the envious person in this life and lead him to hell in the afterlife. Atisuran here serves as an example of this Kural. Due to the evil quality of jealousy he possessed, his wealth in this life and his income from his profession continued to decrease. This quality of jealousy also became the reason for him to commit an inappropriate act due to his jealousy towards Eanati Nathar Nayanar, leading him to hell in the afterlife. Thus, Atisuran, who was jealous of Eanati Nathar Nayanar, gathers his relatives and goes to fight Eanati Nathar Nayanar. His reason for fighting Eanati Nathar Nayanar now is solely greed for wealth. Atisuran gathers his relatives, that is, strong and brave warriors, to assist him and stands before Eanati Nathar Nayanar's house, challenging him. He proclaims angrily that whoever is skilled in swordsmanship will henceforth be able to continue the profession of teaching swordsmanship and earn income from it. Therefore, he challenges Eanati Nathar Nayanar to a battle, stating that whoever wins will henceforth be able to teach swordsmanship to kings and royal families. Atisuran is also a swordsmanship teacher, so he brings his students, the brave warriors, and issues this challenge to Eanati Nathar Nayanar. Sekkizhar here sings that Atisuran, who is cunning like a fox, going to the house of Eanati Nathar Nayanar, who is like a tiger, and issuing such a challenge, is like a cunning fox going to a tiger's den and challenging the tiger to a fight. Hearing this challenge, Eanati Nathar Nayanar, like a male lion, rose with fervor, asking, "Who has challenged me to battle?" He tied a waistband around his waist, fastened a warrior's anklet to his foot, and, taking his sword and shield, came out of his house, ready for battle. We will continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 04 — Eanati Nathar Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 121, Ilai Malinda Sarukkam, Eanati Nathar Nayanar Puranam. Eanati Nathar Nayanar, asking "Who has challenged me to battle?", comes out of his house in warrior's attire. Therefore, his students who are with him also come with him. Hearing his approach, Eanati Nathar Nayanar's relatives who were nearby also joined Eanati Nathar Nayanar. Seeing Eanati Nathar Nayanar come out, Atisuran says, "Let us both make our armies stand opposite each other and fight. Whoever wins this battle will have the right to continue this traditional profession of teaching swordsmanship and receive the earnings from it." Atisuran challenges him to fight in a public place. In that public place, that open ground, martial arts training takes place. Therefore, if any training or fight takes place there, it will be convenient for everyone to watch. So, Atisuran tells Eanati Nathar Nayanar, "Let us fight this battle in that public ground where everyone can see." Hearing these words, Eanati Nathar Nayanar replies, "You say that whoever is stronger and wins the battle between us two will be able to pursue the profession of teaching swordsmanship and keep the earnings from it. This is not good; this is not the right way. But if it seems good to you, I will do the same." Why does Eanati Nathar Nayanar say this is not good? That is, what does Atisuran say? He says that whoever is stronger between the two of them will have the right to engage in the profession of teaching swordsmanship and enjoy its income. Eanati Nathar Nayanar says that this principle is not a good principle. It is not fair that only the strong in battle should have the right to possessions. It is not fair or just. This act of the strong seizing the possessions of the weak is the dharma of animals. This is the law of the jungle. Animals that lack the ability to distinguish between good and evil act in this manner. That is, strong animals attack and live off weaker animals. Therefore, this law of the jungle is a path of theft. The principle that Atisuran now proposes is based on the predatory actions of the human community, which are animalistic. That is why Eanati Nathar Nayanar says that this is not a good principle; it is not fair. The scriptures of justice also state this. However, from that day until today, this very principle has been prevalent in human society. That is, the strong suppressing the weak and claiming their possessions as their own is happening in most places today. The proverbs "small fish are food for big fish," "only the strong survive," and "only the powerful remain, all others perish" clearly illustrate the current state of people based on these laws of the jungle. However, the wise and the noble will not accept this principle. The scriptures of justice condemn the principle that the strong survive and the weak perish. This is one of the cruelties that the noble despise. Sekkizhar Swamigal, who was the chief minister, conveys this truth to us through the words of Eanati Nathar Nayanar. After stating all this justice, why did Eanati Nathar Nayanar agree to the battle that Atisuran challenged him to? It is the nature and characteristic of warriors to accept a challenge to battle when one warrior challenges another. It is considered cowardly in the warrior tradition to not accept such a challenge from a warrior. That is why Eanati Nathar Nayanar, after stating that Atisuran's principle was not a good one, then agreed to the battle. Moreover, Eanati Nathar Nayanar agreed to the battle challenged by Atisuran due to his confidence that his own valor would prevail, his conviction that dharma, justice, and nobility would win, and his firm belief that his love and devotion to Lord Shiva and His devotees would triumph. Thus, as Atisuran and Eanati Nathar Nayanar agreed to battle, their two armies gathered in the public ground. The two armies gathered like two large clouds. The swords in the hands of the two great warriors flashed like lightning between the clouds. Crows waited there, anticipating the outcome of the battle. On two opposing sides, warriors stood in formation, wielding swords and shields. Spear-wielding warriors stood facing each other. Archer warriors, holding bows, also stood facing each other. Thus, the respective warriors were ready for three types of battles: sword fighting, spear fighting, and archery. Swords clashed with swords, spears clashed with spears. Arrows flew from the bows of the archers on both sides. A fierce battle ensued. Blood flowed like a river. As a result of the battle, severed bodies lay scattered everywhere. Vultures gathered, searching for the fallen bodies from the fierce battle. Many warriors died in this fierce battle. Many warriors perished on both sides after fighting fiercely. Now, Eanati Nathar Nayanar rises with anger and comes forward. He comes to the front of his army. Generally, leaders who lead battles stay behind the armies at the beginning of the war and direct them. The armies fight in front, guided by them. This is the tradition of warfare. This is the tradition at the beginning of a war. When the war intensifies and their armies weaken, the commanders come forward themselves, directly engaging in battle, encouraging their troops, and leading them. This tradition can also be seen in the histories of great Puranas like Kandapuranam. Here, many of his warriors have fallen, and those who remain are weakened in battle. In this situation, Eanati Nathar Nayanar comes forward with anger to fight at the forefront, encouraging them. Here, Eanati Nathar Nayanar came to fight with anger, not due to anger arising from the three gunas. It is anger that arose due to the battle, as a battle cannot occur without anger. We should not mistake this for the kind of anger that ordinary people like us experience due to the three gunas. If we were to assume that Eanati Nathar Nayanar also experienced anger due to the three gunas, that anger would have consumed him, taken control of him, and destroyed the purity of the Nayanar's mind. However, we can understand that Eanati Nathar Nayanar was not controlled by anger through the state he adopted when he saw the sacred ash on Atisuran's forehead during their later individual battle. Eanati Nathar Nayanar, a devotee of Lord Shiva, who has a throat adorned with poison, became very angry. Fire flew from his sword, and his warrior's anklet jingled on his foot. He came to the forefront of the battlefield and struck down the heads and shoulders of the warriors who stood against him in battle. All those who stood against Eanati Nathar Nayanar were killed by his sword. Seeing all those who stood against Eanati Nathar Nayanar killed, the other enemy warriors fled the battlefield. Here, Sekkizhar Swamigal gives a beautiful simile. Those who stood against Eanati Nathar Nayanar fled the battlefield, didn't they? Sekkizhar Swamigal says that just as desires and other faults flee and disappear when confronted by the steady true knowledge (Shiva Gnanam), unable to stand against it, so too did the warriors who opposed Eanati Nathar Nayanar flee. The other warriors, seeing those who opposed Eanati Nathar Nayanar being killed, fled from the field. Therefore, they were not killed. That is, those who fled were not killed. They were not found on the battlefield. They disappeared. When true knowledge, or Shiva Gnanam, arises in a person, desires and other faults cannot stand before that true knowledge and flee and disappear, don't they? The same thing happens here. "Thalaippattar ellaarum thani veerar vaalil kolaippattar. Muttaathaar kol kalaththai vittu nilaippatta mey-unarnu nerppatta pothil alaippatta aavam muthal kutram pol aayinaar" (All who confronted him were slain by the sword of the solitary warrior. Those who did not confront him fled the battlefield, becoming like desires and other faults that are agitated and flee when steady true knowledge appears) is the Thiruppadal composed by Sekkizhar Swamigal. It is the 634th verse in Periya Puranam and the 27th verse in Eanati Nathar Nayanar Puranam. Sekkizhar Swamigal here refers to "nilaippatta mey-unarnu" (steady true knowledge), doesn't he? He says that faults will not stand but will flee before this steady true knowledge, doesn't he? What is this steady true knowledge? First, let's see what true knowledge is. It is called true knowledge, true wisdom, and ultimate knowledge. This true knowledge is the understanding that realizes the true reality. It is the true knowledge that perceives the ultimate reality, which is the cause of liberation. That is, it is the true knowledge that, illuminated by the eye of grace, perceives the realities of Shiva, soul, and bonds. Before such knowledge, desires, attachments, and other faults will not sprout and stand, but will disappear like cotton before a forest fire, or like darkness before light. However, Sekkizhar Swamigal states that this knowledge, this true knowledge, must be "nilaippatta mey-unarnu" (steady true knowledge). What is steady true knowledge? It means that this true knowledge should be eternal, not like knowledge that appears for a while and then disappears. For example, algae spread over the surface of a pond. If a stone is thrown into it, the algae will move aside where the stone falls. Then, after the stone sinks into the pond and the vibrations caused by the thrown stone subside, the algae that were displaced by the stone will gather together again. Similarly, knowledge that appears occasionally and by which desires and other faults are occasionally driven away. This is not permanent knowledge, not steady knowledge. Only before steady true knowledge, not like this temporary knowledge, will desires and other faults permanently disappear. That is why Sekkizhar Swamigal sings that faults flee and disappear as if before steady true knowledge. In Saiva Siddhanta, this steady true knowledge is the state of never forgetting the feet of the Lord. Sekkizhar Swamigal states here that desires and other faults will flee and disappear before the true knowledge of such enlightened beings. Thiruvalluvar also expresses the same idea in the last Kural of the chapter "Meyyunarthal" (True Knowledge) in "Thevarum Meyyunarthal," stating that as a result of true knowledge, lust, anger, and delusion—the three—will be destroyed and disappear. "Kaamam, veguli, mayakkam ivai moonran naamam keda kedum noi" (Lust, anger, and delusion—when the names of these three are destroyed, disease will be destroyed) is Kural number 360 of Meyyunarthal. Parimelazhagar, in his commentary on this, says that for those who have attained maturity in continuous knowledge and yoga, these three faults—lust, anger, and delusion—will be destroyed to the extent that not even their names remain. These three faults are the causes that create karma for living beings. The karmas performed by living beings are the effects of these faults. Therefore, if these three faults are removed, the karmas of living beings will be destroyed as a result. Since the karmic impurity of living beings is destroyed in this way, living beings will not be born again. Thus, Parimelazhagar makes us understand that those who possess true knowledge will not be reborn. Lust, anger, and delusion are called Raga, Dvesha, and Avidya by Northern scholars. These three faults will not be present in those who have attained knowledge. The meaning of this is that they will flee before knowledge. Generally, Northern scholars speak of five faults called "Panchakleshas": Avidya, Asmita, Raga, Dvesha, and Abhinivesha. In Tamil, these are called Avichchai, Ahankaram, Ava, Aasai, and Kobam by Tamil scholars. Here, Ahankaram is included in Avichchai. Aasai is included in Ava. Thus, Valluvap Peruman includes these five faults within the three faults of lust, anger, and delusion. Here, Avichchai is called perverted understanding or distorted understanding. That is, the perverted understanding that perceives good as evil, evil as good, impermanent things as permanent, and permanent things as impermanent is called Avichchai. This is the effect of the Anava mala (ego impurity). We have already seen its detailed explanations when we defined the word "marul" (delusion). Thus, Saiva Siddhantins explain these as five faults: Avichchai from eternity, then Ahankaram (ego) which considers "I" due to Avichchai, then Ava (desire) which says "I want this" due to Ahankaram, then Aasai (attachment) to objects due to Ava, and then Kobam (anger) when that attachment is not fulfilled. These five faults are also called six: Kama, Krodha, Lobha, Moha, Mada, Matsarya by scholars. Sekkizhar Swamigal here groups all these faults, which are described as six, five, or three, under the single fault of "aarvam" (ardent desire/attachment). If this fault of "aarvam" is expanded, it will multiply into three, five, or six. Therefore, as mentioned earlier, Sekkizhar Swamigal uses the simile that just as all these faults, like "aarvam," flee and disappear before steady true knowledge, so too did Atisuran and his armies flee in defeat before Eanati Nathar Nayanar. The appropriateness and beauty of Sekkizhar Swamigal's comparison of Eanati Nathar Nayanar to true knowledge and Atisuran and his warriors to desires and other faults are worthy of our joyful contemplation and praise. Let us continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 05 — Eanati Nathar Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 122, Ilai Malinda Sarukkam, Eanati Nathar Nayanar Puranam. Atisuran and his remaining forces, unable to withstand Eanati Nathar Nayanar, flee and hide. Atisuran, who had turned his back and fled, due to his defeat in battle, spends the entire night sleepless, pondering this event. He realizes that he cannot fight Eanati Nathar Nayanar face-to-face, and even if he did, he would not achieve victory. Therefore, he plans and contemplates that he must defeat him through deceit. Truly righteous individuals will always desire what is honest and good. They will have noble aspirations. Moreover, they will believe that the path to achieving those aspirations must also be honest, and they will strive to achieve them through honest means. However, those with base character will not have noble aspirations, nor will the path they choose to achieve those aspirations be honest. This tendency to achieve one's desires by any means is a characteristic of those with a base mindset. The higher the purity and nobility of the goal to be achieved, the more pure and honest the path to achieving that goal must also be. This is the nature of noble individuals. But here, Atisuran plans to defeat Eanati Nathar Nayanar by any means of treachery. Earlier, in the Puranam of Meypporul Nayanar, Mutthanathan also similarly planned to defeat Meypporul Nayanar through deceit. However, Mutthanathan had been defeated by Meypporul Nayanar many times and then finally plotted to defeat him through treachery. Here, Atisuran, being many times more wicked than Mutthanathan, decides to defeat Eanati Nathar Nayanar through deceit immediately after being defeated by him once. That is why Sekkizhar Swamigal refers to this Atisuran as a wicked person and a villain. Thus, Atisuran, who had planned all night, sends a message to Eanati Nathar Nayanar through his messenger as soon as dawn breaks. He says that in their battle, all their respective followers engaged in the fight and perished. Therefore, instead of that, let only the two of them fight in a secluded place. At the end of that battle, whoever wins can continue the profession of teaching swordsmanship. Eanati Nathar Nayanar accepts this, thinking it is good to do so. He accepts it and comes to the secluded place designated by Atisuran, carrying his sword. Previously, the place where Atisuran's armies and Eanati Nathar Nayanar's armies clashed was a public place, a public ground. But now, Atisuran wants to defeat Eanati Nathar Nayanar through deceit. Therefore, he asks him to come and fight in a secluded place unknown to anyone. So, the place where Eanati Nathar Nayanar comes is a secluded place unknown to anyone. Eanati Nathar Nayanar arrives at that place without anyone knowing, because if his departure for the fight were known, all his relatives would gather. Atisuran plans to come to that place. The plan he had thought about all night to defeat Eanati Nathar Nayanar through deceit at that place was this: he knew that Eanati Nathar Nayanar would never, under any circumstances, harm devotees whose foreheads were adorned with sacred ash. Therefore, he plans to use this quality of Eanati Nathar Nayanar to his advantage and defeat him. Eanati Nathar Nayanar worshipped, served, and revered the true sacred ash applied by true Shiva devotees. Similarly, he shows the same love and devotion even to those who wear sacred ash merely as a disguise. Lord Shiva gave everyone a forehead solely for the purpose of applying sacred ash. The forehead was created only for applying sacred ash. That is why Avvai Piratti says, "Neeru illatha netri paazh" (A forehead without sacred ash is barren). "Neerilaa netri paazh, neyilaa undi paazh, aarilaa oorukku azhagu paazh, maaril udan pirappilaa udambu paazh, paazhe madakkodi ilaa manai" (A forehead without sacred ash is barren, food without ghee is barren, beauty without a river for a village is barren, a body without a sibling is barren, a home without a chaste woman is barren) is the 24th verse of Nallvazhi by Avvaiyar. Therefore, it is the conviction of our ancestors that a forehead without vibhuti is a barren forehead. Similarly, Thirunavukkarasu Peruman also states in the fifth verse of the 95th general Thiruppathikam in the Sixth Thirumurai as follows. In this verse, he describes the characteristics of a village. "Thirukkoil illatha thiru ill oorum, thiruvenneeru aniyaatha thiru ill oorum, parkkodi patthimaiyal paadaa oorum, paanginodu pala thaligal ilaa oorum, viruppodu vensangam oothaa oorum, vithaanamum ven kodiyum ilaa oorum, aruppodu malar pariththittu unnaa oorum, avaiyellaam ooralla adavi kaade" (A village without a Shiva temple is not a village, it is a forest. A village where people do not lovingly wear sacred ash is not a village, it is also a forest. A village where people do not sing with immense devotion is not a village, it is also a forest. A village without many beautiful temples is not a village, it is also a forest. A village where the sound of the white conch is not joyfully blown is not a village, it is a forest. A village without canopies and white flags is not a village, it is a forest. A village where people do not pluck flowers and offer them to Lord Shiva before eating is not a village, it is a forest) says Thirunavukkarasu Peruman. In this, he states as the second point that a village where people do not wear sacred ash is not a village, it is merely a forest, he states firmly. Through this, we understand the greatness of sacred ash from the words of Thirunavukkarasu Swamigal. Now, Thirugnanasambandar Peruman has composed a separate patikam called Thiruneetru Pathikam (Decad on Sacred Ash). Next, shall we see how Manickavasagar Peruman has sung the greatness of this sacred ash? In Thiruvachagam, Manickavasagar Swamigal has composed a Thiruppathikam called Achchapaththu (Ten Verses on Fear). In it, he states what he fears. In the fifth verse of this patikam, he sings: "Pinaiyellaam varinum anjen, pirappinodu irappum anjen, thuni nilaa aniyinaan than thozhumbarodu azhunthi amma, thini nilam pilanthum kaanaa sevadi paravi venneeru anikilaathavaraik kandaal amma naam anju maarre" (Even if all diseases come, I fear not; I fear not birth and death. But, oh mother, if I see those who do not praise the sacred feet of the Lord who wears the crescent moon, which cannot be seen even by piercing the dense earth, and do not wear sacred ash, how greatly I fear!). Manickavasagar says, "I will not fear any severe diseases that may come to me. I will not even fear birth and death. But if I see those who do not praise the feet of Lord Shiva and do not wear sacred ash, I will be greatly afraid. The extent of that fear is so immense that it cannot be described." He says, "If I see those who do not praise the feet of Lord Shiva and do not wear sacred ash, I am so afraid."

The Vedas say to burn a forehead without water. Lokakshi Smriti states that if one, with a mind devoted to Shiva, wears the Tripundaram, then the three worlds are sustained by that great soul. Sacred ash is called Vibhuti in Sanskrit. Bhuti means wealth. Vibhuti means supreme wealth. Supreme wealth is the attainment of liberation. It is called Vibhuti because it grants the highest liberation. "Mukti tharuvathu Neeru" (Ash grants liberation) is the Thiruneetru Pathikam of Thirugnanasambandar Peruman. Sacred ash is produced by burning cow dung with fire. What is its significance? "Pasu" means that which is bound. "Pasu" here refers to the soul bound by the three impurities (malas) of Anava (ego), Karma (action), and Maya (illusion). Thus, Vibhuti or sacred ash is the attainment of Shiva Tattva (the essence of Shiva) that results from burning the three impurities of the soul—Anava, Karma, and Maya—with the fire of Shiva Jnana (knowledge of Shiva). This reveals the truth that those who wear it will attain liberation. Sacred ash can be applied all over the forehead as "utthoolanam" or as three lines called "tripundaram." The sacred ash applied as three lines in "tripundaram" will remove all faults that stand in threes. It will remove the three faults of lust, anger, and delusion. It will destroy the three gunas of sattva, rajas, and tamas. It will eliminate the three karmas of sanchita, prarabdha, and agamiya. It will remove the three types of intellect: doubt, perversion, and confusion. It will grant knowledge, transcending the three paths of charya, kriya, and yoga. It will enable one to cross the three worlds: upper, lower, and middle. It will eliminate the three states of jagrat (waking), svapna (dreaming), and sushupti (deep sleep). It will grant the three types of liberation—Shiva Salokyam, Shiva Samipyam, Shiva Sarupyam—and lead to the even higher Shiva Sayujyam. It will remove the three limitations of time, space, and object. It will eliminate the three practices of good and evil: doing, causing to do, and consenting. It will remove the three diseases of vata, pitta, and kapha. It will eliminate the three distinctions of sajatiya bheda, vijatiya bheda, and svagata bheda. It will purify the three instruments of mind, speech, and body. Thus, sacred ash has the power to remove all faults that exist in threes, grant all joys that exist in threes, and, above all, grant the joy of liberation. God wears sacred ash. Therefore, we too should wear sacred ash, which is the Saiva tradition. Lord Paramasivan, who is older than the most ancient things, at the time of the great dissolution, when the five kalas (nivrittis, pratishthas, vidyas, shantis, shantyatitas) under the five deities (Brahma, Vishnu, Rudra, Anantesvara, Sadashiva) are annihilated, and the tattvas and bhūvanas up to the end of the pure Maya bhūvanas are destroyed in the five types of dissolution (Brahmanda pralaya, Prakrita pralaya, Madhyama pralaya, Mahapralaya, Prathama Mahapralaya), at that time of great dissolution, Lord Shiva burns all the bodies of the deities, including the Anusadashiva, with a spark of fire from His third eye, reducing them to ashes. He then smears that sacred ash all over His divine body and shines forth. Similarly, Lord Shiva ordained that we, the souls, should also wear the sacred ash, which comes from burning the dung of the cow, in which all those deities reside as parts, as a symbol.

Vibhuti is mentioned in the Vedas and also in the Agamas. Among the 108 Upanishads that explain the essence of the Jnana Kanda of the Vedas, 15 Upanishads specifically praise Vibhuti. Among them, the Bhasma Jabala Upanishad, Kalagni Rudra Upanishad, and Jabali Upanishad explain the glory of Bhasma with the rules of wearing it. The Brihat Jabala Upanishad not only elaborates on the method of wearing sacred ash as an integral part of Brahma Vidya but also describes the Vedic method of producing sacred ash. In this Upanishad, wearing sacred ash is established as an important and knowledge-giving part of all rituals. The glory of this sacred ash is highlighted in the Isavasya Upanishad, the first of the ten principal Upanishads, which are the essence of the 108 Upanishads, as well as in the Brihadaranyaka Upanishad, the tenth Upanishad, and the Taittiriya Upanishad. Furthermore, the greatness of this sacred ash is extensively described in the 28 Shiva Agamas and their sub-Agamas, in Smritis like Parasara Smriti, Gautama Smriti, Manu Smriti, in Puranas like Shiva Purana, Kurma Purana, Linga Purana, Skanda Purana, and in Itihasas like Mahabharata, Ramayana, and Shivarahasya. To make us understand the greatness of sacred ash, which possesses so many glories, and to show us, the people of the world, the true devotion that Eanati Nathar Nayanar had towards sacred ash and Shiva devotees who wore it, and thereby guide us to the right path, Lord Shiva continues this divine play with His divine will. We will continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 06 — Eanati Nathar Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 123, Ilai Malinda Sarukkam, Eanati Nathar Nayanar Puranam. Eanati Nathar Nayanar arrives at the secluded spot designated by Atisuran. Atisuran, who had never applied sacred ash until this day, and had no habit of doing so, now smears sacred ash all over his forehead, intending to defeat Eanati Nathar Nayanar through deceit. With sacred ash on his forehead and deceit in his heart, he comes to that place, carrying his sword and shield. Until he approaches Eanati Nathar Nayanar, Atisuran conceals his forehead, adorned with sacred ash, with his shield, and then approaches Eanati Nathar Nayanar. The shield, a weapon carried by warriors, is a defensive tool. But here, Atisuran uses that defensive tool, the shield, to conceal his deceitful act. He holds it. There, Eanati Nathar Nayanar stands like a powerful bull. A bull is full of pride, majesty, and immense strength. It excels in goring and killing. In previous verses, Sekkizhar Peruman compared Eanati Nathar Nayanar to a tiger and a lion. In this verse, he compares Eanati Nathar Nayanar to a bull. This is because a bull has the strength to charge and kill its enemy. However, when such a bull charges, if it sees its master there, it will stop charging. That bull possesses that power, skill, and understanding. In this historical event too, Eanati Nathar Nayanar, who was ready to charge Atisuran and had begun to charge, stops upon seeing the sacred ash when Atisuran removes his shield and reveals his forehead adorned with sacred ash. That is why Sekkizhar here compares Eanati Nathar Nayanar to a bull. Thus, Eanati Nathar Nayanar prepares to charge and fight Atisuran. At that moment, Atisuran removes the shield that was covering his forehead. There, Eanati Nathar Nayanar sees the brilliance of the sacred ash. Upon seeing the sacred ash on Atisuran's forehead, he thinks, "He never applied sacred ash before. Now I see the brilliance of sacred ash on his holy forehead. Today, he has become a great devotee of Lord Shiva, the God of gods, and has worn sacred ash." Eanati Nathar Nayanar, who had immense love and devotion for sacred ash, resolves that he will fulfill whatever desire is in the mind of Atisuran, who stood before him wearing sacred ash. Eanati Nathar Nayanar laments, "I tried to kill a devotee wearing sacred ash, thinking him an enemy, not a relative." Upon seeing the sacred ash on Atisuran's forehead, he casts aside all his previous thoughts about him and, at that very moment, considers him a Shiva devotee, harboring good thoughts towards him. He thinks that because he wears sacred ash, he is a Shiva devotee. Eanati Nathar Nayanar sees the sacred ash on Atisuran's forehead as brilliant. This is because, due to Eanati Nathar Nayanar's love for sacred ash, he perceives the sacred ash on Atisuran's forehead as brilliant. Eanati Nathar Nayanar was one who, upon seeing anyone wearing sacred ash, would immediately offer devoted service to them, wasn't he? "Evarenum thaamaaga vilaadath thitta thiruneerum saadhananum kandaal ulgi uvaraadhe uvar avaraik kandapodhu uvandhu adimaith thiran ninaindhu angu uvandhu nokki ivar thevar avar thevar endru solli irandaattaath thozhindhu eesan thirame peni thavaraadhe thozhum adiyaar nenjinulle kanraappoor naduththaraiyaik kaanalaame" (If anyone sees sacred ash and the means of worship, they should not hesitate but remember the devotion of the devotees and joyfully look at them, not distinguishing between this god and that god, but worshipping only the Lord, and in the hearts of such unfailing devotees, one can see the central pillar of Kanraappoor) is from Thiruththandaagam.

We must live to witness the Nayanar's state, which was a complete embodiment of the defined ethical principles in the world. Kazharitrrarivar Nayanar, seeing a washerman whose body was whitened by salt water, coming towards him, perceived him as a devotee. When the washerman, frightened, asked who he was and said, "I am a washerman," Kazharitrrarivar Nayanar, knowing him to be a washerman and realizing that the attire he had assumed was the sacred ash attire, said, "I am Cheran's servant. You have reminded me of the sacred ash attire. Go without worry," and departed. We should also recall that story, its significance, and his devotion here. Eanati Nathar Nayanar resolves that he will fulfill whatever Atisuran had intended and abide by it. Due to the sacred ash on Atisuran's forehead, Atisuran's intention was to deceitfully target Eanati Nathar Nayanar's life and thereby gain the right to the swordsmanship profession. Eanati Nathar Nayanar understands this intention, doesn't he? What kind of person was Eanati Nathar Nayanar? He would dedicate all his possessions to Shiva's devotees. Eanati Nathar Nayanar would now defeat Atisuran and, through that victory, undertake the profession of teaching swordsmanship. Even if he were to defeat Atisuran and undertake that swordsmanship profession, all the income and possessions derived from it would be spent on Shiva's devotees. He would consider that wealth as belonging to Shiva's devotees. Thus, Eanati Nathar Nayanar was one whose body and life, like other possessions, belonged to the devotees. Therefore, if Atisuran desired to gain the right to this swordsmanship profession by taking his life, then Eanati Nathar Nayanar resolves, "I will offer my life to Atisuran, who has become a Shiva devotee wearing sacred ash, according to his intention. I will give my life to this Atisuran, who is a Shiva devotee wearing sacred ash." Therefore, to facilitate this, Eanati Nathar Nayanar thinks of throwing away his sword and shield and preparing to give his life to Atisuran. However, if Eanati Nathar Nayanar were to throw away his sword and shield and stand disarmed, and Atisuran were to harm Eanati Nathar Nayanar at that moment, the sin of killing a disarmed person on the battlefield would fall upon the Shiva devotee. Therefore, Eanati Nathar Nayanar held his sword and shield in his hands merely as a formality, without throwing them away. Who can comprehend the devotion and the inner thoughts of Eanati Nathar Nayanar, that great devotee, towards sacred ash and Shiva devotees who wear sacred ash? This would not be witnessed by any living being, but only by Shiva. Therefore, Sekkizhar Swamigal says that no one but the Lord, who stands as the all-witness, can know the greatness of the Nayanar. The wicked Atisuran, who stood before Eanati Nathar Nayanar, accomplished the deed he had intended and plotted. The conclusion of this historical event is that the Nayanar joyfully offered his three possessions—his possessions, body, and life—in exchange for sacred ash. Since the Lord, who is the life of all lives, stands everywhere and knows all that is conceived in the mind, Lord Shiva, knowing this quality of the Nayanar, appeared before Eanati Nathar Nayanar to bestow His grace upon him. Lord Shiva graciously appeared from the pure space of consciousness, the divine grace that shines in a state free from bonds. No one but the Lord, who is the life within the heart, can know the greatness of a heart that loves sacred ash. Therefore, the Lord, who knows this, will bestow the appropriate reward. Eanati Nathar Nayanar

appears before the Nayanar. How can we praise the divine grace of Lord Shiva, the God of gods? The Lord, who has the Goddess as a part of His body, severed the Nayanar's bonds with the enemy's sword, bestowed grace, and made the Nayanar, who was His own, attain a state of loving union, forever inseparable from Him, and then reached Ponnambalam (Chidambaram). The sword in Atisuran's hand became an instrument of the Lord's gracious action. That sword, which became an instrument of the Lord's gracious action, severed Eanati Nathar Nayanar's bonds. Here, "bond" refers to the body, which is a product of Maya. The Nayanar was bound to this world only by his body. It was his body that served as the basis for all these actions: his profession as a swordsmanship teacher, his prosperity, Atisuran's decline in prosperity despite being the rightful owner, and Eanati Nathar's engagement in the battle Atisuran challenged. Therefore, Lord Shiva uses the sword in Atisuran's hand as His instrument of grace to sever Eanati Nathar Nayanar's bond, which is this body, a product of Maya. Just as an object tied by a rope returns to its natural state when the rope is cut, so too, when the Lord severed Eanati Nathar Nayanar's remaining body, the bond of the body that connected him to this world was severed. As a result, Eanati Nathar Nayanar attained his natural state of liberation. Lord Shiva bestowed that liberation upon Eanati Nathar Nayanar. "Not even an atom moves without Him" is the wise saying of the elders. Therefore, even the wicked act that the defeated enemy intended to commit against Eanati Nathar Nayanar through deceit was also the grace of the Lord. The grace of the Lord always removes bonds according to the maturity of the souls. Here, the Lord's grace stood as the means to remove the Nayanar's bond, which was the illusory body. For this, the enemy's sword in the hand of the adversary became an instrument for the Lord. The Lord's grace, like that sword, bestowed the benefit of Shiva's merit upon Eanati Nathar Nayanar and the consequence of sin upon Atisuran. That is why Sekkizhar Peruman said, "Kai vaalaal paasam aruththu aruli" (Severed the bonds with the sword and bestowed grace). Thus, severing bonds and granting liberation is the grace of the Lord. That is why Sekkizhar Peruman says in this verse, "Paasam aruththu aruli utravarai endrum udan piriyya anbu aruli pottrtodiyal paaganaar ponnambalam anainthaar" (He severed the bonds, bestowed eternal inseparable love upon His devotee, and the Lord, who has the golden-bangle-wearing Goddess as a part of Him, reached Ponnambalam). Here, he refers to Lord Shiva as "Pottrtodiyal Paaganaar" (the Lord who has the golden-bangle-wearing Goddess as a part of Him). "Pottrtodiyal" means Goddess Uma. It is a tradition we see that whenever the Lord comes to bestow grace upon souls, He comes with Goddess Uma. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman, intending to say that Lord Shiva granted Eanati Nathar Nayanar the eternal joy of liberation, which is never separated from Him, refers to Lord Shiva in this verse as the Lord who has Goddess Uma as a part of Him. Eanati Nathar Nayanar also attained the eternal joy of liberation, never to be reborn, and revels in the ocean of Shiva's bliss. In this narrative, Atisuran, knowing Eanati Nathar Nayanar's devotion to sacred ash, attempted to deceive him by applying sacred ash himself. Mutthanathan also deceived Meypporul Nayanar. Many people in this world commit deceitful acts while wearing sacred ash. But for that, we should not despise the sacred attire of sacred ash. Whoever applies sacred ash, we should worship that sacred attire without considering their other evil qualities or scrutinizing them. This is the highest level of worship of sacred attire. Here, the Nayanar saw new sacred ash on the forehead of his enemy. Despite knowing his enmity, jealousy, fierce battle, treachery, and previous defeat, Eanati Nathar Nayanar, seeing only the sacred ash, considered him a great devotee of Shiva. He stood firm, saying, "Even if he intends to take my life, I will give my life to him according to his intention." Not only that, but to ensure that no blame would befall this devotee who wore sacred ash, he held his sword and shield merely as a formality. Eanati Nathar Nayanar, who was in a state where the strength of his arm and sword could bring him victory, upon seeing only the sacred ash on his opponent, offered his very life as a sacrifice for the sake of worshipping sacred ash. We must worship, praise, and remember this integrity. Eanati Nathar Nayanar became an embodiment of integrity and valor, and moreover, an embodiment of love. Love always wins, treachery loses. In this narrative, we might all think that Atisuran, through deceit, achieved what he intended. There is a possibility for all of us to think so. But he was not victorious. Because Atisuran, who had never applied sacred ash before, applied sacred ash to defeat Eanati Nathar Nayanar, descending from his principles. Was it not Eanati Nathar Nayanar's great devotion to sacred ash that made Atisuran apply sacred ash? Therefore, what triumphed here was Eanati Nathar Nayanar's love for sacred ash. Because of that, Atisuran, who had never applied sacred ash before, also applied sacred ash. In all previous battles, Eanati Nathar Nayanar's valor and bravery triumphed. In this final battle, Eanati Nathar Nayanar's love and sacred ash triumphed. In any case, Atisuran's deceitful intention failed. Therefore, the true victor was Eanati Nathar Nayanar. His love for sacred ash triumphed. All that appears in this world as external appearances, seemingly detrimental to our love for God, is actually bestowed by God's grace to sever our worldly bonds and elevate us to a higher state. Just as a doctor uses a knife to cut a patient's body to do him good and cure him, so too does God use the sword in Atisuran's hand as His instrument of grace to bestow upon Eanati Nathar Nayanar the eternal joy of inseparable love for Him. Killing is evil, says justice. It is also a principle allowed by Dharma Shastras that one may kill even one who comes to kill oneself. Killing an enemy and achieving victory on the battlefield is also a principle of war. Suicide is a sin. Similarly, when others come to kill one, possessing the strength to defend oneself but not defending oneself and being killed by others is equivalent to suicide and is a sin. But all these are ethical rules that lead to merit and demerit in the worldly state. These rules do not apply to Eanati Nathar Nayanar. Because the ordinary worldly ethical rules cannot and will not be the yardsticks to measure the actions of our great Nayanmars, who are in a high state, transcending all these worlds and philosophies. Furthermore, the dharmas stated in these worldly ethical texts are only for a good worldly life and for earning merit and demerit in the afterlife, and do not lead to the ultimate benefit of liberation. Moreover, liberation is the ultimate goal. Worldly life is the means. That is, this worldly life and body are the means. When the ultimate goal of liberation is to be attained, we should not cling to this world and body, which are the means. We should let go of them. Thus, letting go of this body, which is the means, when attaining the ultimate goal of liberation, is the most appropriate action. Here, Atisuran, by opposing and deceiving a Shiva devotee, fell into the fiery hell. Similarly, Mutthanathan, who deceived Meypporul Nayanar, also fell into hell. But Atisuran and Mutthanathan committed these wicked acts while wearing sacred ash. In that case, do the Vedic rules, which state that ash removes sin and grants liberation, not apply to Atisuran? These rules do not apply to him. Because he wore sacred ash not with love, thinking of it in his heart, but with deceit in his heart. "Vedam neriyillaar vedam poondu enna payan" (What is the use of those who are not on the path of the Vedas wearing the attire of the Vedas?) is the 128th verse of the First Tantra of Thirumandiram. If those who wear sacred ash do not follow the rules prescribed in the Shiva Agamas, then wearing sacred ash and other such means is of no use to them. Moreover, they harbored mental faults such as deceit, jealousy, and desire. Mutthanathan and Atisuran deviated from the rules of war. They were swallowed by greed for possessions. They did not think of Lord Shiva even a little. Therefore, how could the benefit of sacred ash reach them? The meaning is that they did not receive any benefit from the sacred ash they applied. According to Saiva Siddhanta philosophy, souls do not stand alone. They always depend on something. Therefore, the soul should not cling to the impermanent worldly dependencies such as body, wealth, and relationships, but should follow the path of good deeds, having received scriptural knowledge and the Guru's teachings. This will lead to the discipline of following the path of Shiva's means. Moreover, if any obstacle arises to this discipline of Shiva's means, and one adheres to the discipline of the Shiva Agamas without deviation, that discipline will unite us with Lord Shiva. Here, Eanati Nathar Nayanar abandoned the false dependencies of body, wealth, and so on, and embraced the true dependency of sacred ash worship. By adhering to it, he attained the state of eternal love, never separated from the Lord. Sacred ash is as ancient as Lord Shiva. Just as Lord Shiva is eternal, so too is sacred ash eternal. The true worship of sacred ash grants the soul liberation from bonds and the attainment of Shiva. Worshipping sacred ash is equivalent to worshipping Shiva. Sacred ash is the form of the gracious power of Parasakti. Thirugnanasambandar Peruman refers to sacred ash as the form of Parasakti, saying, "Paravanamavathu Neeru" (Ash is the divine form). The gracious power of Lord Shiva uses sacred ash as an instrument and acts through it. The Lord possesses the gracious power as an inseparable quality. Therefore, just as He is the possessor of qualities, His gracious power, which is a quality, and sacred ash, which is its instrument, are also eternal. Thus, Sekkizhar Swamigal concludes the sacred history of Eanati Nathar Nayanar, our leader, who had the sacred ash worn by his Lord as his only support, by saying, "We will now proceed to narrate the sacred service rendered by our great Lord Kannappar to Lord Thirukalaththi, the God of gods, as we have understood it." We too will worship the sacred feet of Eanati Nathar Nayanar and, as a result, reflect on the sacred Puranam of Kannappar Nayanar. Praise to the sacred feet of Eanati Nathar Nayanar. Praise to the sacred feet of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Praise to the sacred feet of Sekkizhar Swamigal. Praise to the sacred feet of Sivakavimani Ayya, the commentator of Periya Puranam. With the prayer that sacred ash, the embodiment of Parasakti, may protect us, we will continue to reflect. Thiruchitrambalam.
