Chapter 01 — Tirunilakanta Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. We have contemplated the first part, the Puranam of Thillai Vazh Anthanar, in the section called Thillai Vazh Anthanar Sarga, which is the second sarga in Periya Puranam. The first line of Thiruthondathogai Thiruppathigam is "Thillai Vazh Anthanartham Adiyarkkum Adiyen". Therefore, Sekkizhar first sang the section called Thillai Vazh Anthanar Puranam. There, he extensively described the greatness of the 3000 Thillai Vazh Anthanars. The Thillai Vazh Anthanars are Thogai Adiyars, meaning devotees of a group. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal has collectively sung about the 3000 Thillai Vazh Anthanars, those who have lived, are living, and will live, in this one line: "Thillai Vazh Anthanartham Adiyarkkum Adiyen". Periya Puranam narrates the history of 63 Nayanmars and nine Thogai Adiyars. The Thillai Vazh Anthanars are one of these nine Thogai Adiyars. Now, the second line of Thiruthondathogai is "Thiruneelakandaththu Kuyavanarkku Adiyen". That is, Thiruthondathogai continues with "Thillai Vazh Anthanartham Adiyarkkum Adiyen, Thiruneelakandaththu Kuyavanarkku Adiyen", and this second line, "Thiruneelakandaththu Kuyavanarkku Adiyen", was sung by Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Therefore, this line is the section that narrates the historical account and character of the Nayanar named Thiruneelakandam, who lived in Thillai. This Nayanar, known as Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar, Thiruneelakandaththu Kuyavanar, or Thiruneelakanda Nayanar, is the first Nayanar to be sung among the 63 Nayanmars. As his name suggests, he was a Nayanar from the potter community (Kuyavar Kulam). The term "Kuyavanar" in "Thiruneelakandaththu Kuyavanar" refers to the name of the community in which he was born. "Thiruneelakandam" is a descriptive name given to this Nayanar because he constantly chanted the sacred name "Thiruneelakandam" of Lord Shiva throughout his life. "Thiruneelakandaththu Kuyavanarkku Adiyen" is from Thiruthondathogai. Nambiyandar Nambi describes this Nayanar in Thiruthondar Thiruvandathi with the following verse: "Sollach Sivan Thiru Aanai Than Thoomozhi Tho Nasaiyai Ollai Thuranthu Uru Moothu Atharpin Umaikon Arulaal Villaippurai Nuthalaal Odu Ilamai Petru Inbam Mikkan Thillaith Thiruneelakandak Kuyavanaan Sey Thavane." This is the verse about Thiruneelakanda Nayanar in Thiruthondar Thiruvandathi, written by Nambiyandar Nambi, which is the next type of text we should see after the collection of Thiruthondathogai. Now, let us see the history of the Nayanar according to the elaborate text, Periya Puranam. First, let us contemplate a summary of the Nayanar's history, and then we will see his history in detail through Sekkizhar's path and his verses. Sekkizhar Swamigal extensively described the glory of Thillai, also known as Chidambaram Kshetra, in the Puranam of Thillai Vazh Anthanar, which refers to the collection "Thillai Vazh Anthanartham Adiyarkkum Adiyen". After describing the greatness of Chidambaram Kshetra and the Thillai Vazh Anthanars living there, Sekkizhar now begins the history of Thiruneelakanda Nayanar. In the renowned, famous, and blessed Chidambaram, where the incomparable Thillai Vazh Anthanars lived, a Nayanar was born in the potter community. That Nayanar always chanted the unique sacred name of the Lord, "Thiruneelakandam". Whether standing, walking, sitting, or sleeping, he constantly thought of and chanted the unique sacred name of the Lord, "Thiruneelakandam", all the time.

Because he lived by constantly remembering and chanting the unique sacred name of the Lord, "Thiruneelakandam", he came to be known as Thiruneelakandaththu Nayanar. He was born into the Kuyavar Kulam, which is also called the Kulalar Kulam. This means he belonged to the community that kneads and processes clay to make various earthenware, pots, and sacred tiles, thus serving the people. The name of that community is Kulalar, which in Tamil is Kuyavar Kulam. Thus, because he was born into the Kuyavar Kulam and always kept the Lord's name Thiruneelakandam in his mind, he was called Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar. This Nayanar excelled in his domestic duties (illaram dharma). According to his community, he practiced the ancestral profession of a Kulalar, or potter, making earthenware and selling them for his livelihood. He also joyfully gave sacred tiles to devotees of Shiva. Thus, Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar lived his domestic life with his wife. He had immense devotion to the Lord's name Thiruneelakandam and lived his life centered on it. This name Thiruneelakandam is a name that proclaims the Lord's supremacy. Once upon a time, when the devas and asuras churned the Ocean of Milk, the Halahala poison arose. To save the lives of all beings in this world, Lord Paramasivan consumed that poison. He held the poison in his throat, and as a result, his throat appeared blue. Thus, the Lord came to be known by the sacred names Srikandan, Neelakandar, and Kalakandar. Why did the Lord not swallow the poison but held it in his throat, making it appear blue forever? It is so that when we, as souls, look at the Lord's form, we see this blue throat on his reddish body. This sight should remind living beings that just as the Lord swallowed the poison to bless the devas who surrendered to him, he will similarly remove the sorrows of all souls who approach him and save us. For this reason, he appears with the Thiruneelakandam. Our devotee lived his life with this sacred name Thiruneelakandam as its very purpose. Our Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar, such a special devotee, lived his domestic life in his youth. In his youth, he was simple in matters of pleasure. One day, he went to the house of a courtesan outside the town and returned home. His wife, who was of great chastity, came to know of this. She suppressed the anger that arose in her mind towards her husband, Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar, and continued to perform all other domestic duties for him. Even while performing other duties and services for her husband, Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar, that virtuous lady refused to unite with him physically and lived without consenting to it. This was due to the anger caused by his visit to a courtesan's house one day. Therefore, Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar, to appease his wife's anger, went near her, offered various reconciliations, and tried to touch her. At that moment, his chaste wife, seeing Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar approaching to touch her, said with great anger, "You must not touch me anymore. This is an oath upon that Thiruneelakandam." She swore an oath upon Thiruneelakandam. Hearing that, the Nayanar, with his devotion to Lord Paramasivan's Thiruneela

Thiruneelakandam overflowing, withdrew without touching his wife. Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar, having moved far away from his wife, addressed her. Since she said "You must not touch *us*" (plural) instead of "You must not touch *me*" (singular), he vowed, "Not only you, but I will not even think of touching any woman of your kind, even in my mind." Thus, the Nayanar distanced himself from his wife both mentally and physically. Even after this vow, the Nayanar, Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar, and his virtuous wife continued to live together in the same house. They excellently performed their domestic duties in the same house. However, based on the oath sworn upon Thiruneelakandam, they lived separately, without touching each other physically or having physical union. Moreover, this oath was kept very private, unknown to anyone else besides the two of them. As they lived according to this oath, made in their youth, they gradually reached old age. Throughout this time, they lived their lives without any कमी in their devotion to Shiva and their domestic duties. At this time, Paramasivan, the embodiment of compassion, took the form of a Sivayogi and went to their house, so that the people of the world might know the greatness of the devotee without any doubt and praise Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar for their salvation. Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar invited the Lord, who came as a devotee of Shiva, into his home, offered him a seat, performed puja with love according to the rules, prostrated, and stood with folded hands along with his wife. Then, Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar asked the devotee, "Swami, what service should I render to Your Holiness?" To this, the Sivayogi gave him a sacred begging bowl (thiruvodu) that he held in his hand and said, "Keep this in your house. Later, when I come and ask for it, give it back. You must carefully preserve this sacred begging bowl. This sacred begging bowl is unique and incomparable. This sacred begging bowl purifies everything that comes into contact with it. Therefore, it should be protected more carefully than gold or gems. Keep this excellent begging bowl. Later, when I come and ask for it, you must return the same begging bowl to me." Hearing this, Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar saluted him, received the sacred begging bowl, placed it in a corner of his house, and bid farewell to the Sivayogi. After a long time, one day, the Lord made that sacred begging bowl disappear from Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar's house. Then, on the same day, he appeared as a Sivayogi at Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar's house. As before, Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar worshipped the Sivayogi, offered his respects, and stood before him. At that time, the Sivayogi asked, "Give me the same sacred begging bowl that I gave you earlier." Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar went inside the house and searched for the sacred begging bowl in the place where it was kept and in all other places. He could not find the sacred begging bowl anywhere. The Nayanar, who had gone saying he would bring it in a moment, returned empty-handed after a long time and stood before the Sivayogi. The Sivayogi became angry and asked, "You said you would return in a moment, why are you delaying so much?" Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar bowed to the Sivayogi and said, "Swami, I searched for the sacred begging bowl that Your Holiness gave me in the place where it was kept and in all other places, but I could not find it. It seems to be lost. I will make a new sacred begging bowl, better than the old one. Please accept it and forgive my mistake." Immediately, the angry Sivayogi said, "What are you saying? I told you before that it was an incomparable sacred begging bowl, and I would not accept gold or precious gems in its place. I told you before that I wanted the same

sacred begging bowl. Therefore, bring me back the same begging bowl." "Swami, it is not available. I will give you another sacred begging bowl instead," said Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar. Enraged, the Sivayogi said, "You have stolen the item I entrusted to you and are committing a great sin. Therefore, I will not leave without getting that begging bowl, without getting that sacred begging bowl, by complaining to everyone," and he became angry. But Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar said, "Swami, I did not steal that begging bowl. How will I prove it?" To that, the Sivayogi said, "Hold your son's hand and immerse yourself in the pond, and swear that you did not steal it." Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar asked, "I do not have a son to do that. What shall I do?" To that, the Sivayogi asked, "Hold your wife's hand and immerse yourself and swear." To that, the devotee said, "Due to a vow between my wife and me, I cannot hold my wife's hand and immerse myself in the pond and rise. Therefore, I will immerse myself alone in the pond and rise and swear." To that, the angry Sivayogi said, "You are not returning the sacred begging bowl I gave you earlier, and you are keeping it stolen and lying. If you did not steal it, I asked you to swear by holding your wife's hand, but you say you cannot even swear to that. Therefore, I am going to discuss this matter in the great assembly of the Thillai Vazh Anthanars," and he went to the assembly of the Thillai Vazh Anthanars. Thiruneelakanda Nayanar followed him. The Sivayogi presented the case to the Thillai Vazh Anthanars. The Thillai Vazh Anthanars, having learned the details of the case from the Sivayogi and Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar, told Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar, "If you did not steal the sacred begging bowl he gave, then it is just that you prove it by immersing yourself in the pond with your wife and swearing." Hearing this verdict, he said, "I will swear accordingly, come," and taking the Sivayogi, he went to his house, called his wife, went to the temple called Thirupuleeswaram, and there, in the sacred pond in front of it, he took a bamboo stick and entered the pond. His wife followed him. Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar held one end of the bamboo stick, and Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar's wife held the other end of the bamboo stick, and both prepared to immerse themselves. But seeing that, the Sivayogi said, "Hold your wife's hand and immerse yourself." Therefore, now the Nayanar had no other choice. He was forced to reveal the truth of what had happened. So, before the Sivayogi and the people surrounding the sacred pond, Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar recounted how he had once visited a courtesan's house, and as a result, his wife had sworn an oath on Thiruneelakandam that he should not touch her, and how, based on that oath sworn on Thiruneelakandam, he had lived all these days without touching his wife or any other woman even in his mind. Therefore, he said that even now he would not touch his wife's sacred hand. Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar held one end of a bamboo stick, and his wife held the other end, and the elderly couple immersed themselves in the sacred pond. When they emerged from the pond, Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar and his wife shed their old age and regained their youth. Immediately, devas and sages showered flowers from the sky upon the Nayanar. They praised his greatness. Everyone who witnessed this miracle – that Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar and his wife, who had immersed themselves in the pond as old people, emerged young again – searched for the Sivayogi who was there. He was nowhere to be seen; he had disappeared. Seeing this, they stood bewildered and confused. At that moment, Lord Shiva, with Parvati, appeared in the sky on his bull (Rishabam) to Thiruneelakandar and his wife. Thiruneelakanda

Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar and his wife fell to the ground, prostrated before him, rose, and stood praising him. Lord Shiva addressed them, "Beloved ones, you have attained greatness by conquering the five senses, which are difficult for anyone to overcome. Remain with us forever, without losing your youth." Saying this, he disappeared. Thiruneelakanda Nayanar, strong in devotion, and his wife performed a great deed that no one else could do, attained Shivlokam, and lived enjoying supreme bliss. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 02 — Tirunilakanta Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. In Periya Puranam, we are contemplating the Puranam of Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar Nayanar. We have seen a summary of his story. Now, let us first contemplate the commentary written by Arumuga Navalar for the Puranam of Thiruneelakanda Nayanar, which narrates the history of Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar, and then we can contemplate the Nayanar's history through the path of Sekkizhar Peruman. Sekkizhar Peruman narrates the sacred history of Thiruneelakanda Nayanar in 44 verses. The commentary written by Arumuga Navalar for this Puranam is as follows: Lust (Kama) is what obstructs living beings from being freed from the cycle of birth and attaining liberation. Lust is extremely difficult and hard for even the greatest of people to eradicate. Whether one thinks of it, sees it, hears it, or rejects it, lust is like poison taking over the head; it has the nature to corrupt the intellect of even the most discerning. Even if it enters the hearts of great people who are excellent in education, knowledge, and conduct, it will obscure their intellect and diminish their character, lineage, and conduct. It will obscure their intellect, preventing them from discerning what is a suitable path and what is not. Due to this obscuring of intellect, lust prevents those who are under its sway from even contemplating the blame, sin, and other consequences that arise from their actions. That very lust is the cause of all sins like murder, theft, and drinking alcohol. This lust becomes the cause of all other sins. Therefore, this lust has the great cruelty of casting souls into hells and tormenting them for countless ages. There are stories and ideas in many texts as evidence for this. Therefore, one must suppress lust so that it does not arise even a little in the mind. If lust arises in our mind, to remove it, to drive it away, souls must associate with devotees of Shiva. They must listen to Shiva Puranas. They must contemplate the meanings of Shiva Shastras. If one performs such good deeds, one can not only protect oneself from lustful thoughts entering the mind but also accumulate Shiva merits. Therefore, when lust arises in our mind, we must spend time performing Shiva merits. Only union with one's wife is prescribed in the Vedas and Agamas. Even union with one's wife should only be for the sake of having children. Otherwise, union for the sake of pleasure is also a fault, a sin. Thiruneelakanda Nayanar, who was excellent in Shiva devotion and devotee devotion and lived a domestic life, engaged in union with a courtesan, as explained in texts, due to the excess of lust that arose in his mind even while his wife was present. However, when he tried to touch his wife to appease her displeasure, his wife pointed to Shiva and swore an oath, "If you touch us, Thiruneelakandam." As soon as he heard that oath, he withdrew without touching her. Not only that, but since his wife said "If you touch *us*" (plural) instead of "If you touch *me*" (singular), he resolved that he would not even think of any other woman, let alone his wife. Even when lust arose in Thiruneelakanda Nayanar's mind, his Shiva devotion was far greater, many crores of times greater. It is because of this that his Shiva devotion

prevented even a trace of lust from ever arising in the Nayanar's mind later, which is learned from this history. Thiruneelakanda Nayanar and his wife, being young, lived in the same house day and night. However, after this incident, after this vow took place, they began to live according to that vow. When we marvel at their greatness in living without deviating even a little from the vow and completely eradicating lust, it becomes clear that the strength of their Shiva devotion was the reason for this. Lord Vishnu entered the body of Jalandarasura after his death and united with his wife. Later, Jalandarasura's wife realized that the one who came was Vishnu and cursed him, which caused him distress. Brahma, infatuated by the beauty of Tilottama, whom he had created, tried to unite with Tilottama without considering that she was his daughter. Indra united with Ahalya, the wife of Sage Gautama, and was cursed by Sage Gautama to have a thousand yonis on his body. Chandra united with the wife of his guru, Brihaspati, and contracted consumption. Many other sages, while performing severe austerities in mountains and forests, were infatuated by celestial women, asura women, and rakshasa women, united with them, and lost their austerities. In many previous yugas, when dharma flourished greatly, devas, sages, and others committed great and terrible sins like adultery, incest with daughters, and incest with guru's wife due to excessive lust. But Thiruneelakanda Nayanar, who lived in this Kali Yuga, considered it an act of treachery against Shiva to violate the oath sworn by his wife, pointing to Lord Shiva, and conquered lust even without uniting with his own wife. How glorious is his devotion because of this! Pattinaththu Pillaiyar also speaks of this glory, saying, "Maadhu sonna soolaal ilamai thurakka vallēn allēn" (I am not capable of renouncing youth due to an oath spoken by a woman). In this verse, Pattinaththu Pillaiyar praises three Nayanmars. The verse is: "Vaalaal makavarithu ootta vallēn allēn maadhu sonna soolaal ilamai thurakka vallēn allēn thondu seythu naal aaril kan idanthu appa vallēn allēn naan inich chenru aalavathu eppadiyo Thirukkalaththi apparukkē." In this verse, Pattinaththu Pillaiyar sings about three Nayanmars and yearns that he cannot be like them. He sings about Siruthondar, saying, "Vaalaal makavarithu ootta vallēn allēn" (I am not capable of cutting my son with a sword and feeding him). He yearns that he has not attained the maturity to cook and offer his son's flesh as food when a devotee of Shiva asks for it, like Siruthondar Nayanar. Similarly, secondly, he praises Thiruneelakanda Nayanar, saying, "Maadhu sonna soolaal ilamai thurakka vallēn allēn" (I am not capable of renouncing youth due to an oath spoken by a woman). Pattinathar yearns and sings that he has not attained the maturity of Thiruneelakanda Nayanar, who renounced his youth and controlled his senses due to a vow, an oath spoken by his wife, an oath sworn on Lord Shiva. Thirdly, he sings about Kannappa Nayanar. Kannappa Nayanar was one who had such great love that he plucked out his own eye and offered it to the Lord within six days of developing affection for him. Pattinathar yearns that he has not attained that maturity either. Thus, our Thiruneelakanda Nayanar is a Nayanar praised by Pattinaththu Pillaiyar. Was it only Thiruneelakanda Nayanar who controlled his senses, conquered lust, and immersed himself in Shiva devotion? His wife also

similarly controlled her senses, practiced Shiva devotion, and led a domestic life. That is why Lord Shiva, with Uma Devi, appeared on his bull vehicle to both Thiruneelakanda Nayanar and his wife and granted them life in Shivlokam. Even chaste women, upon seeing handsome men, may waver in their resolve and cause those men to waver as well. But Thiruneelakanda Nayanar's wife, even though she was very young and lived in the same house with her husband, Thiruneelakanda Nayanar, day and night, upheld her vow by not uniting physically with her husband. The greatness of their Shiva devotion is immeasurable. When Paramasivan came as a Sivayogi and claimed that Thiruneelakanda Nayanar had stolen the begging bowl he had entrusted to him, and that if he had not stolen it, he should prove it by holding his wife's hand and immersing himself in the pond, even then, they stood firm in their previous vow of not touching his wife. They both held the two ends of a bamboo stick and immersed themselves in the pond. Not only that, but the vow between Thiruneelakanda Nayanar and his wife was kept secret from everyone else for all this time. Finally, when the Sivayogi put immense pressure on them, he revealed that vow and the shameful act he had committed earlier that led to the vow, before the world. Thus, if he had not revealed the past events—that Thiruneelakanda Nayanar had gone to a courtesan's house, and then his wife had sworn an oath, and he had been living by that oath—the Sivayogi's doubt would not have been cleared. It was in that critical situation, when there was no other way, that the Nayanar finally announced this vow to the world. Furthermore, it is astonishing how Thiruneelakanda Nayanar and his wife upheld that vow all this time, not by taking other living beings as witnesses, but by taking the sacred feet of Lord Shiva, who is the life of all lives, as their witness. It is with this astonishment in mind that Sekkizhar, the author of Periya Puranam, uttered, "Ilamaiyin mikkuḷḷārkaḷ iruvarum ariya ninra aḷavil sīr āṇai pōtri" (Praising the glorious oath that stood firm even as both were in the prime of youth). Without taking living beings as witnesses, that is, without taking other beings as witnesses, but by taking His own sacred feet as witness, for those who possess the great quality of performing Shiva merits, Lord Shiva Himself appears, illuminates their fame throughout the world, makes all other beings worship them, shows the world the fruit of their penance, and grants Shivlokam to the Nayanar and Thiruneelakanda Nayanar and his wife. Lord Shiva Himself directly appeared to them, revealed their extraordinary deeds for the whole world to know, and praised Thiruneelakanda Nayanar and his wife with His own sacred words, "You have excelled by conquering the five senses." Thus, the Nayanar, Thiruneelakanda Nayanar, and his wife, praised by the very words of Lord Shiva, lived eternally in Shivlokam. This is the commentary written by Arumuga Navalar for the Puranam of Thiruneelakanda Nayanar. Now, let us contemplate the history of Thiruneelakanda Nayanar very extensively and subtly, following Sekkizhar's Periya Puranam. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 03 — Tirunilakanta Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. In Periya Puranam, in the second sarga, Thillai Vazh Anthanar Sarga, we are contemplating the section called Thiruneelakanda Nayanar Puranam. We have so far contemplated the summary of Thiruneelakanda Nayanar's story and the commentary written by Arumuga Navalar. Now, let us contemplate the Nayanar's history very extensively and subtly through Sekkizhar's Periya Puranam, following the commentary of Sivakavimani. Sekkizhar, who sang the Puranam of Thillai Vazh Anthanars, places and sings the history of this Nayanar, Thiruneelakanda Nayanar, as the second, following the order sung by Sundaramoorthy Swamigal in Thiruthondathogai. The Thillai Vazh Anthanars are Thogai Adiyars (group devotees). Sundaramoorthy Swamigal places and sings Thiruneelakanda Nayanar as the first among the Thani Adiyars (individual devotees). It expands as Thiruneelakanda Nayanar Puranam. Similarly, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal places and sings Thiruneelakanda Yazhpanar Nayanar as the concluding Nayanar in Thiruthondathogai. Thus, Thiruthondathogai and the Periya Puranam that followed it begin with the history of Thiruneelakanda Nayanar and conclude with the history of Thiruneelakanda Yazhpanar. This sacred name "Thiruneelakandam" serves as a protection and bestows grace upon devotees. Therefore, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal places the praise of Nayanmars who associated the sacred name Thiruneelakandam with their own names, at the beginning and end of Thiruthondathogai. Accordingly, Sekkizhar also places the Puranam of Thiruneelakanda Nayanar as the first Puranam among the individual devotees and the Puranam of Thiruneelakanda Yazhpanar Nayanar as the concluding one. These are the Puranams of sixty individual devotees. After that, Sekkizhar Swamigal sings the Puranam of Sadaiya Nayanar, the father of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, and then the Puranam of Isaignani Ammaiyar, the mother of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, as the 71st Puranam. It is well known that the Puranam of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal naturally appears as the first, in between, and at the end of Periya Puranam. Now, Sekkizhar, beginning the Puranam of Thiruneelakanda Nayanar, starts singing about this Nayanar as "Vediyar Thillai Moothoor Vetkover Kulaththu Vanthar" (He came from the Vetkover community in the ancient city of Thillai, where Brahmins reside). Sekkizhar had already sung about the Thillai Vazh Anthanars. So, continuing from that, he begins to sing that in such a special Thillai, in the ancient city of Thillai where such special Thillai Vazh Anthanars live, a Nayanar was born in the Vetkover community. Thiruneelakanda Nayanar was born in Chidambaram, in the great city of Thillai. Thiruneelakanda Nayanar was a virtuous and excellent Nayanar who daily worshipped, praised, and adored Lord Shiva, who has Uma Devi as one part of his body. He went to Thiruchitrambalam, where Shiva dances, and praised and worshipped the sacred feet of the Lord, who performs the wondrous, unique, and beginningless and endless sacred dance. The community in which Thiruneelakanda Nayanar was born is the Vetkover Kulam, which is the Kuyavar Kulam. In Sanskrit, it is known as Kulalar Kulam, and in Tamil, it is called Kuyavar Kulam. Kulalars are those who bring clay, knead it, process it to the right consistency, and then make earthenware, lamps, sacred begging bowls for devotees, and other items from that clay, thereby serving society. This Kulalar Kulam is a community with an ancient and excellent tradition. The work of kneading clay and making earthenware, performed by these Kulalars, is considered comparable to the creative work of God.

a traditional profession. Sri Rudram, an important part of the Vedas, praises Lord Paramasiva in its fourth Anuvaka as "Kulalebhyaka". It praises the Lord as one who is in the form of a Kulalar in the fourth Anuvaka of Sri Rudram. The explicit meaning of this is that the Lord manifests in the forms of people from various communities who perform different professions and thus benefit society. However, the scriptures explain that "Kulalebhyaka" or the Lord being in the form of a Kulalar signifies His creative activity, indicating that the Lord is the creator. Among the 14 Saiva Siddhanta scriptures, Sivagnana Siddhiyar is an important Sivagnana scripture. It compares the creative work of the Lord to the pottery making work of Kulalars and establishes that the Lord is the creator. This is the 38th verse in Sivagnana Siddhiyar Supakkam. Its explanation is that for any action to occur, three causes are needed: the Upadana Karana (material cause), the Nimitta Karana (efficient cause), and the Sahakari Karana (auxiliary cause). It is a scriptural principle that an action takes place through these three causes. For example, for a pot to come into existence, the Upadana Karana, the primary cause, is clay. Clay is the primary cause. This clay does not transform into a pot by itself. Therefore, an intelligent cause is needed to make this clay into a pot. That is the Nimitta Karana. Only an intelligent entity can transform clay into a pot. Such an intelligent entity that transforms ordinary clay into a pot is the Nimitta Karana. That is, here, the intelligent potter kneads the clay and makes a pot. But the potter cannot directly knead the clay and make a pot. He needs tools like a wheel and a stick to help him. Therefore, the wheel, stick, and other such items are called Sahakari Karanas. Thus, for a pot to be formed, the primary cause, clay, is needed. An intelligent potter, a Kulalan, is needed to transform that clay into a pot. And that Kulalan needs a wheel and a stick to transform the clay into a pot. These are the auxiliary causes. Similarly, there are three causes for the creation of this universe. The primary cause, the Upadana Karana, for the creation of this universe is Maya. The Lord creates universes by taking a part of this Maya. Therefore, just as a Kulalan is needed as the Nimitta Karana to make a pot, the one who is needed as the Kulalan to create universes from Maya is the Lord Himself. Just as a Kulalan needs tools like a stick and a rod to transform clay into a pot, the Lord uses His power, Sakti, as an auxiliary to create the universe, which is like a pot, from Maya, which is like clay. Therefore, just as clay is the primary cause, the Kulalan is the efficient cause, and the wheel, stick, etc., are the auxiliary causes for the creation of a pot, so too, Maya is the primary cause, the Lord is the efficient cause, and the Lord's gracious power (Sakti) is the auxiliary cause for creation. Therefore, using the pottery-making profession, clay is compared to Maya, the primordial substance of the universe; the wheel, stick, etc., are compared to Shiva's Sakti; and the Kulalan who makes the pot is compared to the Lord. Thus, Sivagnana Siddhiyar

describes. Thus, the pottery-making profession practiced by the Kulalar community is glorious, being compared to the creation of the Lord. Our Thiruneelakanda Nayanar came from such a glorious tradition. The Kulalar community is such a glorious and traditional community. Our Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar came from this potter community. This great one constantly chanted the name of the Lord, Thiruneelakandam. Therefore, by chanting the name Thiruneelakandam, his name became Thiruneelakandam, and with his community name following, he is called Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar. Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar thus becomes his descriptive name. This Nayanar is one who understands what is false and what is true, rejects falsehood, and lives according to the true path of Saivism. Living in this Saiva path, this Nayanar serves Lord Shiva and Shiva's devotees. He has great love for Lord Shiva and performs the services desired by Shiva's devotees. Furthermore, he makes sacred begging bowls (thiruvodu) as part of his ancestral profession of pottery, and dedicates them to Shiva's devotees, thus living his life. Not only that, but he also perfectly practices the domestic dharma (manaiyaram), which is praised by the world. He leads this sweet domestic life with his wife. Thirukkural states 11 virtues as important duties for householders. The first Kural says, "Ilvaazhvaan enbaan iyalbudaiya moovarkku nallaatrin ninra thunai" (He who leads a domestic life is a good support for the three who are naturally virtuous). In this, Thiruvalluvar states three virtues in this first Kural in the chapter on domestic life, saying that householders who follow the Grihasthashrama should support those belonging to the other three ashramas: Brahmacharya, Vanaprastha, and Sannyasa. In the second Kural, he states three other virtues: "Thurandharkkum thuvvaathavarkkum irandharkkum ilvaazhvaan enbaan thunai" (The householder is a support for ascetics, the poor, and the deceased). That is, householders should help the helpless who are abandoned by everyone. Secondly, they should help the poor. Thirdly, they should perform water rituals (neerkadan) and other ancestral rites for those who have died without any support and have taken refuge with them. Thus, in the second Thirukkural, he states three virtues. Thiruvalluvar has so far stated six virtues, three in the first Kural and three in the second. In the third Kural, he states five duties for householders. Thiruvalluvar says that one should help five types of people: "Thenpulaathaar, Deivam, Virundhu, Okkal, Thaan" (Ancestors, God, Guests, Relatives, Self). That is, Thiruvalluvar prescribes that those who provide for ancestors, gods, guests, relatives, and themselves are performing essential duties for householders who lead a domestic life. These five duties mentioned in the third Kural are praised as Pancha Yagna in Sanskrit. Thus, Thiruvalluvar states that Grihasthashramis (householders) have a duty to support 11 types of people. These 11 types of people are: those living in Brahmacharya ashrama, those in Vanaprastha ashrama, those who have taken Sannyasa, providing necessary food, clothing, shelter, etc., to abandoned beings, performing water rituals and other ancestral rites for those who have taken refuge and died, helping others suffering from poverty, ancestors,

gods, guests, relatives, and oneself. These are the 11 people that householders should support, as stated by Thiruvalluvar in the chapter on domestic life in Thirukkural. Thus, Thiruneelakanda Nayanar and his wife lived an excellent domestic life according to the definition of domestic life given by Thiruvalluvar. They lived their domestic life without deviating from the worldly dharmas stated in the scriptures of justice. Not only that, but they lived with great devotion to Shiva, serving Shiva and Shiva's devotees, and adhering to the true principles of Saivism, such as sacred ash (Thiruneeru), rudraksha, matted hair, and the sacred Panchakshara mantra. Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar performed his ancestral profession of pottery only to the extent necessary for his livelihood and domestic life, dedicating the rest of his time and effort to his love for Lord Shiva and Shiva's devotees. Chapter 04 — Tirunilakanta Nayanar

Though association with courtesans is forbidden by scriptures and moral codes, the Nayanar became simple in matters of pleasure due to the intensity of his youth. Therefore, he lived unable to explain it. At the same time, he lived with immeasurable devotion to the Lord, holding onto the sacred name Thiruneelakandam, and excelling in devotion to God and devotees. The Lord appears as Thiruneelakandar only to express his compassion for living beings. Though the Lord consumed poison for the sake of the devas, he held that poison in his throat to manifest his compassion so that all beings in all worlds might realize it and be saved. Since the love that should have been entirely hers was shared with another, Thiruneelakanda Nayanar's wife became angry and displeased. Therefore, unable to bear the insult, she performed her duties for her husband with great anguish. Self-respect (manam) means never deviating from one's position and not living if one experiences degradation. This is an essential quality for those of noble birth. Furthermore, self-respect is of three types: not living in a degraded state from one's high position, not going to those who insult one, and not tolerating humiliation if it occurs. These are the three types of self-respect. Here, the loss of self-respect for Thiruneelakanda Nayanar's wife was her perception that she had been humiliated. Therefore, unable to bear the insult, she refused to unite physically with her husband, while at the same time performing all other necessary duties for him without any कमी. This is what Tamil literature calls "oodal" (feigned quarrel or displeasure). That is, Thiruneelakanda Nayanar's wife, being displeased with him, refused only physical union with him, but otherwise continued to nurture her husband, perform services for him, entertain guests, and fulfill all other necessary domestic duties without any कमी. At this point, Sivakavimani offers commentary that resolves various doubts that arise in us. Thiruneelakanda Nayanar was also very exalted in Shiva devotion. He had immeasurable devotion to the name Thiruneelakandam and had performed numerous austerities in previous births and in this one. The incident where he once went to the courtesan's quarter outside the town is mentioned here. Association with "porutpendir" (women for money), "vilaimagalir" (prostitutes), or "varavin magalir" (women of income) as they were called in earlier days, is condemned by worldly moral texts. It is an act condemned by worldly moral texts. However, judging and concluding on the Nayanar's actions based on the rules and exceptions of ordinary worldly moral codes is an inappropriate analysis. Some, forgetting the aim of Thiruneelakanda Nayanar's history, may try to find fault in the Nayanar's actions using the measure of worldly moral texts. Therefore, it is the correct approach to avoid finding fault and committing an offense in the Nayanar's history. This is because his wife swore an oath, pointing to the Lord's name Thiruneelakandam, and commanded him not to touch her. Generally, those who swear an oath do so by invoking things or people that the person being sworn to holds dear. For example, it is common practice to swear, "By the oath of your family deity, you must not do this anymore," or "By the oath of your father, you must not do this," or similarly, "By your mother, by your child, you must not do this." Here, since our Nayanar has devotion to the name Thiruneelakandam of Lord Shiva, the lady says, "By the oath of Thiruneelakandam," and tells him not to touch her. Thus, when his wife swore an oath upon Thiruneelakandam, which he held in great devotion, Thiruneelakanda Nayanar made a firm resolve from that moment onwards: "I will not touch any woman, including my wife, even in my mind." Thiruneelakanda Nayanar and his wife lived in the same house, but they lived by this vow. Both were young. We also see that the Nayanar was simple in matters of pleasure due to the intensity of his youth. Furthermore, the Nayanar had the right to unite with his wife. Nevertheless, due to the vow, from that moment onwards, he lived without touching any woman, including his wife, even mentally. He overcame the power of lust, which even deities like Vishnu found difficult to overcome. He lived without touching his wife for pleasure or even for procreation, based on the vow he took not to touch any woman, even mentally.

Uniting with one's wife for the sake of procreation is a practice permitted by the scriptures. Even that, this holy man set aside, living without offspring. Not only that, but they continued to uphold that vow until the Lord came as a Sivayogi to test them, and until they reached old age and the culmination of old age. Can the rules, exceptions, and grammar of this world be the yardsticks to measure and find fault or perfection in the Nayanar's character? No. Not only that, Nayanmars, even though they are in this world, live in a state beyond this world, and only appear like ordinary humans to others. But they are very exalted in penance and Shiva merits. They are very close to liberation. Therefore, merely because they wear an ordinary human body and appear like ordinary humans in this world, judging the Nayanar's character by the rules and exceptions of the world that apply to ordinary people is ignorance and even an offense against Shiva. Like Thiruneelakanda Nayanar, Moorka Nayanar offers food to devotees of God with money earned through gambling. Kotpuli Nayanar kills all his close relatives who violated his command not to eat the paddy reserved for the Lord's sacred food. By what rule of which text are we to measure all these actions of the Nayanmars? Therefore, we must try to understand the very high state of the Nayanmars, and realize that even though they wore human bodies like us, their spiritual maturity is one close to liberation. We should not measure them by the exceptions mentioned in ordinary texts and avoid offending them, says Sivakavimani in his commentary. This commentary is very important. With this explanation, we must approach the histories of all Nayanmars, including Thiruneelakanda Nayanar. In the history of each Nayanar to come, similar strange events, that is, many events that appear strange from our perspective, are going to happen. Therefore, with the request that devotees of Shiva should apply this same explanation there and seek Shiva's merit, we will continue the history. While Thiruneelakanda Nayanar's wife was living in displeasure, to avoid that displeasure, to appease it, he approached her, offering reconciliation and attempting to touch her. In times of displeasure, it is a characteristic of Akam poetry for the heroine to gain superiority and the hero to submit. In times of displeasure, we have seen in literature that the husband would even caress his wife's feet to appease her anger. Therefore, to appease the displeasure, the Nayanar approaches his wife. An oath or a vow is an attempt to prevent the person in front from proceeding further on that path. It is that stopping power. If we look at literary convention, during a period of displeasure, the wife holds a superior position. It is common for the wife to be in a superior position. Because his wife swore an oath on Thiruneelakandam, and the Nayanar's love and devotion for Thiruneelakandam were many crores of times greater than this worldly pleasure, the Nayanar did not proceed further and took this vow. Thiruchitrambalam. In Periya Puranam, Thillai Vazh Anthanar Sarga, we are contemplating the history of Thiruneelakanda Nayanar. Thiruneelakanda Nayanar's wife, being displeased, has been living without uniting physically with the Nayanar. So, to appease her displeasure, when the Nayanar tries to touch her, she says, "By the oath of Thiruneelakandam, do not touch us." Now, let's continue our contemplation. Sekkizhar Swamigal says that Thiruneelakanda Nayanar, who had immense devotion and love for the name Thiruneelakandam, withdrew upon hearing that oath. Sekkizhar Swamigal sings, "Aathiyaar Thiruneelakandaththu alavu thaam konda aarvam pedhiyaa aanai ketta periyavar peyarnthu neengi" (The great one, hearing the unwavering oath sworn on the primordial Thiruneelakandam, which he held in such devotion, moved away). Here, Sekkizhar Swamigal refers to Thiruneelakanda Nayanar as "Periyavar" (a great person). This is because this Nayanar overcame and conquered the desire of lust, which is difficult for even many great deities and others to overcome, solely by the oath sworn on Thiruneelakandam, apart from Lord Shiva himself. Therefore, this Nayanar performed an extraordinary feat. Hence, Sekkizhar Swamigal calls him "Periyavar". Thirukkural states, "Seyarkariya seyvar periyar" (Great ones perform difficult deeds). Sekkizhar Swamigal notes that the Nayanar, hearing his wife's command, moved away. Both "peyarthal" (to move) and "neenguthal" (to withdraw) mean to leave that place, to move a little distance away from that place. If so, why does Sekkizhar Swamigal use two words with the same meaning, "peyarnthaar" and "neenginaar"? It is because Thiruneelakanda Nayanar, who was standing close to his wife, trying to touch her, moved and withdrew the moment he heard the command. That is, he physically moved away from there. It also means he mentally withdrew and moved away. That is why Sekkizhar Swamigal uses two words here, say the elders who understand the subtlety. Thus, the great Thiruneelakanda Nayanar moved a little distance away from his wife, both physically and mentally. Now, after this happened, Sekkizhar says that the Nayanar looked at his wife as if she were a stranger. "Ethilar" means a stranger. Sekkizhar Swamigal says that he looked at his wife like a stranger, not with the intimacy of a husband. Furthermore, Sekkizhar composes the verses as if Thiruneelakanda Nayanar says, "Since she said 'us' (plural), I will not even touch other women in my mind." Why does the Nayanar say, "I will not touch even in my mind"? Even thinking of touching would produce the result of touching. Actions done mentally are also included in our karma. Therefore, this thought itself becomes karma, which becomes the seed for action, and that itself gives rise to a birth, as we can see in many histories. Therefore, the Nayanar says, "I will not touch even in my mind." Moreover, the act of Alalasundarar having thoughts of women in Thirukailayam caused him to be born in this world, as we have already seen in Periya Puranam. Therefore, the Nayanar says that even if he were to touch mentally, it would be a violation of his wife's command, and so he would not do it even mentally. Furthermore, "I will not think in my mind, I will not touch in my mind" also means "I will not consider women as objects of my experience." Thus, it means "I will not consider women as objects of my experience." Not only that, it also means "I will not even think of women." As soon as this vow took place, from that moment onwards, the chaste wife of the Nayanar performed all the necessary services for her husband.

But she performed those services without her body touching the Nayanar's body. Furthermore, Sekkizhar Swamigal says that both of them lived in the same house and led a domestic life. For women who possess chastity as their quality, the appropriate place for them to reside is where their husband is. Therefore, even when Thiruneelakanda Nayanar's wife was displeased and after this vow occurred, she did not separate from her husband but continued to serve him. Thus, both of them avoided physical intimacy, which is a special characteristic of married life, and lived according to their vow. Consequently, they also lost the blessing of children, which is a fruit of married life. Even though she was deprived of the blessing of children by her husband, that chaste queen upheld her chastity, cherished her husband, and lived by fulfilling all his needs. Not only that, but this husband and wife lived without anyone else knowing about the vow they had made between themselves. This incident was a private matter that occurred between the two of them one night. It is not the tradition of noble families, nor is it good conduct, to let others know about private matters such as displeasure between a husband and wife, or other intimate matters. Therefore, this vow that occurred between them was known only to these two. It remained known only to them. They lived virtuously without anyone else knowing about it. The only third person who knew about this vow was Lord Shiva, the owner of the name Thiruneelakandam. The Nayanar's wife made the oath by taking Him as witness, due to her devotion to that Thiruneelakandam. Therefore, both of them upheld this vow by taking Thiruneelakandam as witness. By the power of that Thiruneelakandam, they conquered and transcended lust, which is difficult to overcome, and lived their lives. Furthermore, this command was an oath made in the name of Thiruneelakandam. Therefore, this was a private matter between Lord Shiva and His devotees, Thiruneelakanda Nayanar and his wife. Just as it is not good character or good conduct to reveal private matters between a husband and wife, so too it is not proper to reveal private matters that occur between oneself and God. Therefore, they also lived without revealing it. It is precisely because they guarded this secret and lived upholding this vow in their private lives without telling anyone outside that the Lord Himself comes and reveals this truth to the world. Thus, living by upholding the vow without outsiders knowing is one of the most excellent underlying philosophies of this Puranam. Thus, they lived without touching each other, having taken this vow among themselves, without any outsiders knowing. As they upheld this vow made in their prime youth, many years passed. Sekkizhar describes this as, "Valamali ilamai neengi vadivuru mooppu vanthu thalarvodu saainthu anbu thambiraan thiraththu saayaar" (Their rich youth departed, beautiful old age arrived, and though they stooped with weakness, their love for the Lord did not wane). This means that their youth departed, and beautiful old age arrived. Due to that old age, they became stooped in their bodies. But their love for the Lord did not wane, says Sekkizhar. Here, Sekkizhar

says that youth departed and beautiful old age arrived. The departure of youth is one state. The arrival of old age is another state. Therefore, Sekkizhar Swamigal sings about both these states here. It is a rule that those born on this earth, regardless of their nature, due to having received this birth of a body subject to decay in the world, will naturally experience youth, then old age, ailments, and diseases as time passes, and then perish. Furthermore, Sekkizhar Peruman describes their old age as "vadivuru mooppu" (beautiful old age). That is, he calls it beautiful old age. How can old age be beautiful? It is said that because both of them upheld their vow and had immeasurable devotion to Thiruneelakandam, even though they reached old age, that maturity manifested beautifully. Furthermore, our traditional medical texts and ethical texts state a conclusion. What is it? If anyone in their youth indulges excessively in pleasures according to the rules of youth, old age will come quickly. Along with that, old age will destroy the beauty of their form and disfigure them, say medical and ethical texts. But here, the Nayanar and his wife have been living without physical union since their youth. Therefore, they did not experience any deficiency in their vital fluids. Thus, there was no deficiency in their vital fluids. Therefore, even though old age came due to their age, their youthful beauty remained undiminished, can also be interpreted. Thus, even though old age came and their bodies weakened, their love for the Lord did not weaken for either of them. In this state, the Lord decided to reveal their greatness to the world. With the compassion that their ascetic life should be shown to the world, so that people may know this is the good path, develop devotion to the Nayanar, and attain salvation, the Lord comes here to reveal their greatness to the world. In what form does he come? He comes in the form of a Sivayogi. The Sivayogi's matted hair shines like fire growing like tender shoots. Thus, with his matted hair shining like fire, the Lord comes in the form of a gracious Sivayogi. The Sivayogi wears a loincloth around his waist. This loincloth is referred to as "keezhkōvaṇam" (lower loincloth). "Kīḻ" refers to a piece of cloth woven with thread in a plate-like shape, meant to be tied as a waist-cord. So, the Sivayogi comes wearing a loincloth, a "kīḻkōvaṇam", embodying the form of a yogi. This is the sacred form appropriate for a Sivayogi. Therefore, there is no other clothing apart from the loincloth. On that sacred body, the radiant sacred ash (Thiruneeru) shines. The radiance of this sacred ash is such that it does not diminish itself. Similarly, it keeps those who see it from diminishing and averts any harm that may come to them. This is the nature of sacred ash. This is the nature of the sacred radiance of sacred ash. Thus, the sacred ash shines on the Sivayogi's forehead in three lines, as a Tripundra. On the Sivayogi's shoulders and chest, the sacred thread (Poonool) shines. On his forehead, the Tripundra of sacred ash radiates. The Lord has concealed his natural long matted hair. Instead of his usual long matted hair, now, as a Sivayogi, his hair is curled and stands like a "pambai". "Pambai" refers to hair that does not hang down but sticks out in tufts here and there. Thus, where there should have been matted hair, there was "pambai". This Sivayogi comes with a golden smile, as if radiating moonlight. He carries a sacred begging bowl in his sacred hands. He walks on foot, leaving his bull, his usual vehicle. Thus, matted hair, "pambai" hair, loincloth, sacred ash, Tripundra, sacred thread, and a begging bowl in hand are the marks of a Sivayogi. Why does this Sivayogi come? He comes to reveal Thiruneelakanda Nayanar. Here, "to reveal" means to reveal the greatness of this Nayanar to the living beings of the world. The Lord is one who knows everything that happens at all times and in all places. Therefore, he does not come to see or to test. He comes so that the living beings of the world may see and attain salvation, to reveal the devotion of that Nayanar to other living beings. "Arivaanum thane arivippaan thane" (He is the knower, and He is the one who makes known) is from Karaikkal Ammaiyar's Arputha Thiruvandathi. The Lord knows by Himself. But only if He makes it known will living beings have the ability to know. Therefore, to make known the nature of this Nayanar to living beings, the Lord now comes as a Sivayogi. Similarly, in the histories of other Nayanmars, when the Lord comes, it should be understood that He comes only to reveal the greatness of those Nayanmars to living beings, and through that revelation, for those living beings to attain salvation, writes Sivakavimani in his commentary. He further states that it should not be understood that the Lord comes to test His devotees. This is because if the Lord comes to test His devotees, it would compromise the Lord's omniscience. Therefore, the Lord comes not to test His devotees, but to reveal their greatness to other living beings. What is the good path that the Lord came to bestow through Thiruneelakanda Nayanar? It is to explain the good path that all worldly attachments are inferior when compared to the love of God. This pertains to devotional detachment (Bhakti Vairagya). Before the love of God, the pleasure of a wife, the pleasure of children, or the pleasure of a father, or even one's eyes or head, are not considered superior. This is the philosophy of the lives of the Thiruthondars. In this Puranam, we find the exalted histories of souls who, when the world pulled them down on one side and God lifted them up on the other, humbled the world and held onto God. Therefore, due to an oath sworn on one of God's names, due to the vow made on Thiruneelakandam, Thiruneelakanda Nayanar and his wife lived their entire lives completely severing youthful acts and lust. Thus, making the pleasures of the five senses pale before the love of God is the good path that God came to show us. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 06 — Tirunilakanta Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. In Periya Puranam, we are contemplating the Puranam of Thiruneelakanda Nayanar. Due to the vow made on Thiruneelakandam, Thiruneelakanda Nayanar and his wife, though living in the same household, live without touching each other. They have also reached old age. Now, the Lord, in the form of a Sivayogi, comes to their house. The moment Thiruneelakanda Nayanar sees the Sivayogi, he praises him mentally. He praises him with his words. Then, he goes before him, with great love for him, thinking that he is a virtuous devotee, praises him, greets him, bows, and welcomes him into his house. It is an important rule prescribed by Saiva Shastras that whenever one sees a devotee of Shiva, one should worship that devotee as Shiva himself. "Maalara neyam malinthavar vedamum aalayam thaanum aran ena thozhume" (Worship the form of those filled with pure love and the temple itself as Hara) is the 12th Sutra of Sivagnana Bodham. Its explanation is that one should worship devotees of Shiva as Shiva himself. But here, for Thiruneelakanda Nayanar, Shiva himself has come as a devotee of Shiva. What a great blessing of penance Thiruneelakanda Nayanar performed! Thus, taking the devotee of Shiva into his house, he performed all the pujas with immense heartfelt desire, as the Saiva Siddhanta Shastra texts prescribe how to worship and serve devotees of God. He bowed before him with humility and stood shyly, and then Thiruneelakanda Nayanar addressed the Sivayogi as follows: "My Lord, what should I do now? Please tell me what my service is," he pleaded. To that, the Sivayogi looked at Thiruneelakanda Nayanar and said, "My dear one, keep this begging bowl with you and return it to me when I ask for it," and he gave the sacred begging bowl he held in his hand to Thiruneelakanda Nayanar. Furthermore, while giving it, the Sivayogi described the special qualities of the sacred begging bowl he held in his hand. This sacred begging bowl is very exalted. There is nothing equal to it. Therefore, you must keep it safely, said the Sivayogi. That sacred begging bowl is the one held by the Lord. Therefore, just as the Lord is incomparable, so too is the sacred begging bowl he holds. Furthermore, the Sivayogi said that this sacred begging bowl has the power to purify everything that comes into contact with it. He said that it purifies all impure things placed in this sacred begging bowl. That is, the scripture states that if souls surrender all their karmas to the Lord, if they dedicate them to the Lord, the Lord purifies them and redeems those souls. That is the inner meaning of "this sacred begging bowl purifies everything that comes into contact with it." Furthermore, the Sivayogi said that this sacred begging bowl should be cherished more than gold or gems. As soon as the Sivayogi said this, Thiruneelakanda Nayanar humbly received the sacred begging bowl, placed it in an important place in his house, and then came and stood before the Sivayogi, making his plea. How glorious is that sacred begging bowl held by the Lord? The seventh Thirumandiram, 184th verse of Thirumoolar's Thirumandiram, describes the Lord's act of begging as follows: "Pichaiyathu etran Piraman thalai thannil Pichaiyathu etran piriya Aran seyya Pichaiyathu etran Piraman sirangaatti Pichaiyathu etran Piraman paramagave"

Why does the Lord beg, carrying a begging bowl? The Lord holds Brahma's head as his begging bowl. When Lord Brahma became arrogant, thinking he was the Supreme Being, Lord Shiva plucked one of Brahma's five heads and held it as a Brahma Kapala, a begging bowl. Similarly, the Lord holds the Brahma Kapala to signify that it is wrong for living beings to think they are the masters of everything. Next, to subdue the arrogance of the sages of Darukavana, he sent Vishnu as Mohini to the sages and went to the wives of the sages with a begging bowl. The Lord went to Darukavana to prove that there is a Supreme Being and to make it known that He Himself is that Supreme Being. Like Lord Shiva, his devotees also take the form of holding a begging bowl, the sacred begging bowl. Why do Shiva's devotees hold a sacred begging bowl? Generally, people who are inclined towards righteousness and dharma will make great efforts themselves to give what is desired to those who ask. They will also go to various places to perform dharma. But people who are not inclined towards dharma will not go anywhere to perform dharma. Even such people, who are not inclined towards dharma and are lazy, should perform dharma. To create an opportunity for them to perform dharma, Shiva's devotees go to the homes of ordinary people and make even those who are not thinking of dharma, but are at home, perform dharma, thereby bestowing Shiva's merit upon them. This is the inner meaning of this Thirumandiram. The Lord, coming as a Sivayogi, entrusts that sacred begging bowl to Thiruneelakanda Nayanar. Thiruneelakanda Nayanar, born in the ancient and glorious lineage of Vetkover, also known as Kulalar, humbly receives and preserves it. Sekkizhar Peruman mentions Thiruneelakanda Nayanar's community, the potter community, the Kulalar community, in many places. This community began to help humans by providing them with pots, earthenware, and vessels for cooking rice, from the very day humans started building houses and cooking food. Therefore, Sekkizhar Swamigal praises this community. Furthermore, the sacred begging bowl is a companion to renunciates, Sannyasis. And it is this Vetkover community, the Kulalar community, that makes these sacred begging bowls. Moreover, the profession of making earthenware is one that involves no cruelty or violence. Considering all these excellences, Sekkizhar Peruman praises and speaks highly of Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar's community in many places. Thus, when Thiruneelakanda Nayanar preserved the sacred begging bowl in a place inside the house and informed the Sivayogi, the Sivayogi also took leave from there and returned to the temple (Ambalam). Sekkizhar Swamigal says that it was the Lord who performs the dance in the Jnana Akasha in Thillai who came in this manner. Thus, after many days passed, the Lord, in the same form as the Sivayogi who had come before, stood before Thiruneelakanda Nayanar's house. As before, Thiruneelakanda Nayanar bowed to the Sivayogi, served him, performed puja, and stood making his plea. Before the Lord appeared as a Sivayogi, he did one thing. He made the sacred begging bowl that he had previously entrusted to Thiruneelakanda Nayanar disappear from that place. Only then did he come to Thiruneelakanda Nayanar's house in the form of a Sivayogi. The Lord, who has the power to place and retrieve all things,

the Lord who came in the form of a Sivayogi, asked Thiruneelakanda Nayanar, "Give me now that sacred begging bowl that I entrusted to you one day." Sekkizhar Peruman here refers to the Sivayogi who came as Lord Shiva as "Ellam than vaithu vaanga vallaan" (One who can place and retrieve everything). "Vichathindriye vilaivu seykavai vinnu mannakam muzhuthum yavaiyum vaichu vaanguvai" (You create without seed, and you place and retrieve all things in heaven and earth) is from Thiruchathagam, Thiruvachagam. The Lord has the power to create all worlds from His own possessions, the pure and impure Mayas, and the objects of experience within them, the instruments capable of experiencing them, and the bodies possessing those instruments, give them to living beings, remove their impurities, and then again make those worlds and other things merge into those primal causes. This is the conclusion of true scriptures. Thus, the Lord, who places and retrieves all universes, places the sacred begging bowl with Thiruneelakanda Nayanar and then retrieves it. Placing the sacred begging bowl is creation. Retrieving it is dissolution. Thus, when the Sivayogi who came asked for the sacred begging bowl he had given before, Thiruneelakanda Nayanar went very quickly. He said, "I will bring your begging bowl, the sacred begging bowl, in a moment," and went inside the house. He went quickly. But the sacred begging bowl was not found in the place where it was kept in the house. Not only that, but even after searching everywhere, Thiruneelakanda Nayanar could not find the sacred begging bowl anywhere. As Thiruneelakanda Nayanar did not return for a long time, the angry Sivayogi looked at Thiruneelakanda Nayanar and shouted loudly, "You went inside saying you would return here quickly, why are you delaying so much?" Hearing the yogi's words, Thiruneelakanda Nayanar quickly came and told the yogi, "My father-like one, the sacred begging bowl you entrusted to me is not found even after searching in the place where it was kept and in other places. So, leave that old begging bowl. I will make a new and better sacred begging bowl for you. Please accept it," he pleaded. He also pleaded for his mistake to be forgiven. In the sense that it is the duty of parents to forgive the mistakes made by children, Thiruneelakanda Nayanar here calls the Sivayogi "Enthai, Em Thanthai" (my father). It is the duty not only of parents but also of elders to forgive mistakes. Therefore, the Nayanar here calls the Sivayogi "my father." The Sivayogi spoke to Thiruneelakanda Nayanar, who stood bowing before him, as if he were very angry. "What did I tell you when I gave you that sacred begging bowl before? I will not accept a begging bowl made of gold or one made of precious gems instead of this. I said that I would only accept the same begging bowl that I gave you before, didn't I? So, bring me that same begging bowl that I gave you," he asked. Here, Sekkizhar Swamigal beautifully mentions something. He says, "The Sivayogi spoke as if he was angry," instead of "The Sivayogi was angry." The Lord's anger burned Manmatha. Therefore, unlike that anger, here the Sivayogi speaks these words to Thiruneelakanda Nayanar as if he is angry. Sivakavimani writes in his commentary that this angry look, this look as if he was angry, is a gracious look that came from the power of concealment (Tirodhana). We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 07 — Tirunilakanta Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. In Periya Puranam, we are contemplating the Puranam of Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar. Lord Shiva, who came as a Sivayogi, asks for the sacred begging bowl he had given earlier. As the sacred begging bowl was lost and could not be found anywhere, Thiruneelakanda Nayanar looked at the Sivayogi and pleaded, "Great one, the sacred begging bowl you gave me is lost. It cannot be found anywhere. Therefore, I will make another begging bowl, better and more durable than that one." He begged and pleaded with the Sivayogi, asking him not to be angry. Sivakavimani writes in his commentary that this state of the Nayanar is a state where the "Tharpodham," or the self-awareness of the soul, has been destroyed and vanished. Even after the Nayanar said this, the Sivayogi looked at Thiruneelakanda Nayanar and said, "You have stolen the sacred begging bowl I entrusted to you. You are performing many deceitful acts. You are not at all afraid of the blame that will come from this. Therefore, I will leave from here only after taking my begging bowl, for everyone to see. I will not take even one step from here without my begging bowl, the sacred begging bowl," said the Lord, who is the great essence of all virtues. Hearing this, Thiruneelakanda Nayanar bowed to the Sivayogi and said, "Great one, I did not steal your sacred begging bowl. Not only that, but not even a trace of the thought of stealing it was in my mind. Tell me what I should do to prove this," he pleaded humbly. To that, the Sivayogi looked at Thiruneelakanda Nayanar and said, "Hold your beloved son and immerse yourself in the pond, and swear." Swearing by immersing oneself in water is a worldly custom. Water, fire, etc., are the sacred forms of the Lord's Ashtamurtis. The Lord's Ashtamurtis are the five elements, the sun, the moon, and the soul as the master. Saiva Siddhanta Shastra states that the Lord has these eight things as his sacred body. It is a Saiva tradition to celebrate the Lord as having these eight things—the five elements, the sun, the moon, and the soul—as his sacred body. "Prithivyo Bhava Apas Sarva Agne Rudra Vayul Bhima Akasasya Mahadeva Suryasya Ugra Chandrasya Soma Atmana Pasupatih" is a Vedic statement. According to this, the Ashtamurtis of Lord Shiva are mentioned here. The eight names and their corresponding eight names are mentioned. This means that Lord Shiva is called Bhava when he takes the earth element (Prithvi) as his form, Sarva when he takes the water element (Jalam) as his form, Rudra when he takes fire (Agni) as his form, Bhima when he takes air (Vayu) as his form, Mahadeva when he takes space (Akasha) as his form, Ugra when he takes the sun (Surya) as his form, Soma when he takes the moon (Chandra) as his form, and Pasupati when he takes the soul (Atma) as his form. Therefore, swearing in water, fire, etc., is a worldly custom. This is because water, fire, etc., are the sacred forms of the Lord's Ashtamurtis. That is why crossing fire, putting one's hand in fire, immersing oneself in water, etc., have traditionally been considered suitable for swearing before the Lord. Not only that, but the custom of swearing at the temple entrance and in front of the sanctum has also existed and continues to exist. Therefore, the yogi said this to Thiruneelakanda Nayanar.

He tells Thiruneelakanda Nayanar, "Immerse yourself in the pond with your son and swear that you did not steal that sacred begging bowl." Even though this statement and action of the Sivayogi may seem very cruel to us, it is not actually cruel. These are acts of grace performed by the Lord out of compassion to reveal the devotee. Hearing this, Thiruneelakanda Nayanar said to the Sivayogi, "Sir, you ask me to hold my son and immerse myself in the pond and swear. But I do not have such a son. So, tell me what else I should do." To that, the yogi said, "If you don't have a son, hold your wife and immerse yourself in the pond and swear." Hearing this from the Sivayogi, Thiruneelakanda Nayanar said to the Sivayogi, "Due to a vow between my wife and me, I am unable to hold my wife's hand and immerse myself in the pond. To prove my innocence, I will immerse myself alone in the pond and swear. Come." Hearing this, the Sivayogi said, "You are not returning the item I gave you. You have stolen it. If you are not stealing it, I asked you to swear by holding your wife's hand and immersing yourself in the pond, but you say you cannot do that either. You say you will immerse yourself alone and swear. This is not acceptable to me. For this, I will go to the assembly of the Thillai Vazh Anthanars and complain about this case," said the Sivayogi. Saying this, the Sivayogi walked ahead. Thiruneelakanda Nayanar, drawn by love and desire, followed behind the Sivayogi. Both went and stood in the assembly where the Thillai Vazh Anthanars, who were excellent in good conduct and fully proficient in the four Vedic paths, were gathered. The Sivayogi stood before the assembly of the Thillai Vazh Anthanars and complained, "This potter refuses to return the sacred begging bowl I entrusted to him. When I asked him to swear by holding his wife's hand and immersing himself in the pond to prove that he did not steal it, he refuses to do that too." Thus, when the Sivayogi spoke, the Thillai Vazh Anthanars asked Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar, "O Vetkover, tell us what truly happened." Hearing that, Thiruneelakandar said, "Elders, this Sivayogi had previously given me a sacred begging bowl and asked me to guard it safely. He then said that whenever he came to ask for it, I should return it. After many days, he has now come and asked for it. I searched everywhere for that sacred begging bowl, but I could not find it. It has disappeared," said Thiruneelakanda Nayanar. Having heard explanations from both, the Thillai Vazh Anthanars gave the following verdict: "If you have lost the sacred begging bowl given by the Sivayogi, then to prove that you did not steal it, this Sivayogi himself asks you to immerse yourself in the pond with your wife and swear. Therefore, it is the correct procedure for you to immerse yourself in the pond with your wife and swear. So, you shall immerse yourself in the pond with your wife and swear," the Thillai Vazh Anthanars ruled. Hearing this verdict, Thiruneelakanda Nayanar, in a very difficult situation where he could not tell everyone about the oath he had made to his wife not to touch her, looked at the Sivayogi, who had won the case, and said, "As you have said, I will immerse myself and swear. Please come." Saying this, he took him and reached his home from there. Commands are of many kinds: the command of elders, the command of a king, the command of Ishwara. The command of elders is very high, but the command of a king is greater than that. Among all these, the command of Ishwara is the greatest. Thiruneelakanda Nayanar here had taken the command of Ishwara, the command sworn on Thiruneelakandam. But now, the Thillai Vazh Anthanars had given a royal judgment, a king's command. Therefore, to fulfill the royal command to the extent that it was consistent with the greater command of Ishwara, Thiruneelakanda Nayanar hastened. Thiruneelakanda Nayanar took his wife from his home, and also the Sivayogi, that devotee of Shiva, and to swear the oath, he reached the sacred pond in front of the sacred temple called Thirupuleeswaram, where Lord Shiva resides. Ambalam. This Thirupuleeswaram, also called Thirupuleeswaram or Thirupuleechuram, is a Shiva temple located a short distance west of the Thillai Natarajar temple. This temple was established and worshipped by Vyagrapada Mamunivar for his own spiritual benefit. Hence, it is called Puleeswaram. Since Vyagrapadar, the sage with tiger feet, worshipped here, it is called Puleechuram. Just as the temple where Thadakai worshipped is called Thadakeswaram, and where Ananthar worshipped is called Anantheswaram, this temple is called Puleeswaram. We will later see the history of Kanampulla Nayanar, who attained blessings by burning his hair as a lamp in this temple, in his Puranam. This Thirupuleechuram temple is now called Ilamaiyaakinaar Kovil (Temple of the One Who Restored Youth). It is currently known as Ilamaiyaakinaar Kovil because they regained their youth by immersing themselves in this sacred pond. The glories of this Thirupuleeswaram temple can be found in the Vyagrapada Sarga of the text called Koyil Puranam. The sacred pond of this Thirupuleechuram temple is one of the ten sacred bathing places in Thillai where the Lord grants sacred water during special occasions. Sivaganga Theertham, Paramananda Koopam, Pulimadu, Anantha Theertham, Nagaseri, Brahma Theertham, Sivapriya, Thirupparkadal, and Kuyya Theertham are the other nine sacred bathing places. Thus, Thiruneelakanda Nayanar took a bamboo stick, a bamboo rod, and entered the sacred pond of Thirupuleechuram with his wife. According to his vow, he should not touch his wife. But according to the verdict given by the Thillai Vazh Anthanars for the Sivayogi's case, he had to hold his wife's hand, immerse himself, and swear. Therefore, to fulfill both, he took a bamboo rod, and Thiruneelakanda Nayanar's wife held one part, one end, of the bamboo rod, and Thiruneelakanda Nayanar held the other part, the other end. As they tried to immerse themselves holding both ends of the bamboo rod, the Sivayogi told them to touch his wife, hold her hand, and immerse themselves. He also pointed out that that was the verdict. At this critical moment, with no other option, the Nayanar revealed the old story to everyone, so that the world might hear about the vow he had made not to touch his wife. Until this day, the vow-bound life led by Thiruneelakanda Nayanar was unknown to anyone in the world. The people of the world could neither see nor hear about Thiruneelakanda Nayanar's life. Now, as the Nayanar spoke, the people of the world came to know.

The Nayanar is not immersing himself in the pond while touching his wife, isn't he? Because of that, the Sivayogi asks. That is why Thiruneelakanda Nayanar says this. Similarly, the reason Thiruneelakanda Nayanar is immersing himself in the pond without touching his wife is the oath sworn on Thiruneelakandam. His devotion to Thiruneelakandam was the cause of that oath. Furthermore, the incident of his visit to the courtesan's house also contributed to this. Therefore, the Nayanar was forced to recount all the old incidents. Thus, to reveal the Nayanar's life to the world, the Lord came as a Sivayogi and imbued Thiruneelakanda Nayanar with divine grace. Thiruneelakanda Nayanar, like a straw caught in a flood before the Lord's divine grace, lost his self-control, that is, lost his self-awareness, and stood in the path of divine grace, which is Shiva-awareness. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 08 — Tirunilakanta Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. In Periya Puranam, in the section called Thillai Vazh Anthanar Sarga, we are listening to the history of Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar. The assembly of Thillai Vazh Anthanars rules that Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar and his wife should immerse themselves in the pond and prove that they did not steal the sacred begging bowl given by the Sivayogi. When Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar and his wife try to immerse themselves in the sacred pond holding a bamboo stick, the Sivayogi tells them to hold hands and immerse themselves. At that moment, with no other option, the Nayanar recounts all the past events for the world to know. After recounting this, Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar and his wife hold the two ends of the bamboo stick, immerse themselves in the pond, and emerge. At that time, Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar and his wife shed their old age, which they had before immersing themselves in the pond, and appear in their youth. Devas and sages shower Kalpaka flowers. Everyone around the pond, witnessing this miracle, is bewildered as they no longer see the Sivayogi who was standing there until then. As the Nayanar and his wife are bewildered by this miracle, Lord Shiva appears in the sky on his bull vehicle with Uma Devi to Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar and his wife. Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar and his wife immersed themselves in this pond and emerged. Not only that, but Sivakavimani beautifully says that they emerged from the great ocean of birth. Thus, both of them, having emerged, attained the youth that everyone desires. Witnessing this miracle, devas and sages shower Kalpaka flowers. It is a tradition that when such a divine grace event occurs and a great divine incarnation takes place, devas and others shower Kalpaka flowers and sound divine drums in praise. In this state, the Sivayogi, who had come to reveal his devotee, who had come to make known the greatness of his devotee to the whole world, suddenly disappeared, and everyone was bewildered, unable to see him. Thus, the people of the world witness the miracle of Thiruneelakanda Nayanar and his wife regaining their youth. They also witness the miracle of the Sivayogi suddenly disappearing. At the same time, Lord Shiva and Uma Devi appear in the sky on their bull vehicle to Thiruneelakanda Nayanar and his wife. But this vision is not visible to other people of the world. This is a manifestation of the Lord's divine power. Therefore, this manifestation of divine power occurs only for mature souls. Therefore, only Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar and his wife see this vision of the Lord on his bull. Furthermore, appearing on his bull in the sky here should be understood as appearing in the Chidakasha (the sky of consciousness). Thus, the Lord and the Goddess appearing in the Chidakasha means that this vision is available only to mature souls, who are sages. That is why other people of the world around the pond could not see that divine vision. But the Lord's coming as a Sivayogi was in the form of the power of concealment. Therefore, other people of the world happened to see that power of concealment of the Lord. But since the power of divine grace is known only to mature souls, it was not visible to other living beings. It is known that whenever the Lord bestows grace upon living beings, he appears with Uma Devi. Thiruneelakanda Nayanar and his wife, bewildered by their old age disappearing and youth returning, stand bewildered. At that moment, the Lord appears in the sky with the Goddess on his bull. Then their bewilderment disappears. Initially, when the Lord came as a Sivayogi to Thiruneelakanda Nayanar's house and played by giving him the sacred begging bowl, Thiruneelakanda Nayanar did not know that it was Shiva's divine play. Later, when the Sivayogi suddenly disappeared and their old age disappeared and they attained youth, they stood in bewilderment, knowing and yet not knowing. Then, when the Lord appeared in the sky with the Goddess on his bull, he realized that this was the Lord. Therefore, the Nayanar first experienced a state of not knowing. Then he experienced a state of knowing and yet not knowing. Thirdly, he experienced a state of knowing. Thus, one can understand the nature of these three states and the state of self-awareness occurring in them, and the state where the Lord's divine grace fills and directs the soul's consciousness. The nature of the Guru, the nature of grace, and the nature of the soul are explained by this history, and the path for living beings to follow is shown here. Devas and sages praise and extol Thiruneelakanda Nayanar's greatness, seeing that Thiruneelakanda Nayanar, by upholding the divine command, crossed the ocean of lust, which no one else can cross, protected his vow, and thus the Lord appeared to them. They also saw them regain their youth and immerse themselves in the wealth of divine grace. They saw that the greatness of the devotees' love for the Lord shines above all else. Thus, the Lord, who appeared to Thiruneelakanda Nayanar and his wife, addressed Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar and his wife, "Beloved ones, who have attained greatness by conquering the five senses, remain with us forever, without losing this youth," and then disappeared. Thus, Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar and his wife were praised by the very words of the Lord as those who had conquered the five senses. Both of them conquered the five senses, so those five senses became their obedient servants. But for others, those five senses act as masters, and other beings become slaves to the five senses and follow their dictates. Therefore, for others, the five senses dominate, and others are subservient to them. But Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar and his wife, by conquering the five senses, stand superior in their state. What does it mean to control the five senses? Humans have five senses, that is, five organs of perception (gnanendriyas). These five senses are touch, taste, sight, smell, and hearing. These five senses have five objects: taste, light, touch, sound, and smell. In Sanskrit, these are called Shabda, Sparsha, Rupa, Rasa, and Gandha. The mind experiences these five objects through these five senses, these five organs of perception. Therefore, although desire is one for humans, for souls, this one desire expands into five desires when it passes through these five organs of perception. That is, it experiences pleasures through seeing, hearing, smelling, touching, and tasting. Therefore, desire also becomes five desires when it passes through these five senses. So, as indicated by the Lord here, if they conquered the five senses, it does not mean that Thiruneelakanda Nayanar and his wife did not have the five objects—Shabda, Sparsha, Rupa, Rasa, and Gandha—or the five senses—touch, taste, sight, smell, and hearing. They also had the five objects. They also had the five senses and the five organs of perception. But they eradicated those five desires, where their desire would pass through these five senses and grasp the five objects. Therefore, Thiruneelakanda Nayanar and his wife had the five senses. But they did not have the desire that passes through them and the enjoyment that comes from that desire. Not only that, but they perform all the virtues necessary for domestic life, except for the enjoyment of physical union. Not only that, but they also serve Shiva's devotees and Shiva, and perform Shiva's dharmas. Therefore, the meaning is that their senses lost their function and humbly submitted to their control. That is why Lord Shiva himself praises them, saying, "You have conquered the five senses, which are difficult to overcome." Yogis control the five senses through yogic practices and remain motionless. That yogic state is different. But to subdue the five senses, bring them under one's control, and direct the desire that passes through them towards the worship of God, is a state even higher than that. Furthermore, what does the Lord say here? He bestows grace that Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar and his wife will remain eternally young. Thus, to remain eternally young and unchanging in nature is unique to the Lord alone. Similarly, just as the Lord has an unchanging nature, those who are with the Lord will also have that quality. Therefore, just as the Lord is eternally young, Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar and his wife will also be eternally young, says the Lord. The Lord is the death of death. Therefore, by being associated with the Lord, who is the death of death, Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar and his wife also conquered time, which is inferred from this. How does Sekkizhar praise the Lord who came here to explain the ascetic life of Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar and his wife to living beings? Sekkizhar says that the Lord dances in the hall and goes to the homes of devotees. Sekkizhar describes Lord Shiva as one who has the greatness of going to the homes of every devotee of Shiva and revealing their true state to the world. The Lord himself goes to every place where devotees are and graciously takes them as his own. By thus taking Shiva's devotees as his own, the Lord also instructs and takes as his own the rest of the world and other living beings associated with Shiva's devotees. Thus, because the Lord himself directly came to the homes of Nayanmars, including Thiruneelakanda Nayanar, and took them as his own, this glorious Puranam of Thiruthondars was born. By listening to it, learning it, and reciting it, the Lord bestows grace upon all other living beings and makes them attain salvation. Is this not the unique great compassion of our Lord? In Thiruvempavai of Thiruvachagam, Manikkavachagar also sings with devotion, "Here, having appeared in our homes, the one who bestows his golden lotus feet, the hero," and in Thiruppalli Ezhuchi, "O Lord, who has graciously appeared from the ancient lineage of your devotees, along with your beloved with bangles on her fingers." Thus, Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar and his wife, who were taken as his own and blessed by the Lord, performed the difficult deeds mentioned above. As a result, they attained Shivlokam and lived in supreme bliss in Shivlokam, having attained youth that no one else can attain. When the Lord and Goddess appeared on the bull, they said, "Be with us," didn't they? Therefore, at that very moment when the Lord said that, Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar and his wife attained Shivlokam. When the Lord said "with us," he meant Shivlokam. Souls who attain Shivlokam will not take birth again. Therefore, Shivlokam is the world of liberation from which there is no return. Sekkizhar Swamigal says that Thiruneelakanda Nayanar and his wife, having attained Shivlokam, lived in supreme bliss. Worldly pleasures are those experienced by ordinary animalistic senses. These are worldly pleasures. They give only a little pleasure. Once experienced, they lead to suffering. But supreme bliss, that is, the supreme bliss attained by Thiruneelakanda Nayanar and his wife in Shivlokam, is a world that cannot be measured by any measures like time, space, etc., where their senses have transformed into divine senses by the abundance of Shiva's grace. In that great world, having attained divine senses through Shiva's grace, they revel in supreme bliss. This supreme bliss is praised in the Vedas as Paramānanda, Brahmānanda, and Shivānanda. Thirunthiyar also states this idea: "The worldly pleasure attained itself becomes supreme bliss there, the complete gift that comes, O Unti Par, Maya does not sprout," says Unti Par. Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar renounced worldly pleasures, meditated intensely on Thiruneelakandam, experienced Thiruneelakandam, and finally attained the state of supreme bliss, which is liberation. Swearing an oath on the Lord's name Thiruneelakandam, upholding that oath, and upholding that oath without anyone else knowing, these are the underlying philosophies of this Puranam. To show these, the Lord came here directly. Sivakavimani states the things we learn from this Puranam as follows: In the community of devotees, one should not think of greatness or lowliness based on lineage. Everyone should live by following their respective ancestral profession and ancestral conduct without abandoning them. Those who perform ancestral professions or other professions should limit them to the extent necessary for their livelihood and domestic life, and dedicate the rest of their time and efforts to Lord Shiva and Shiva's devotees. Association with courtesans is an evil conduct condemned by scriptures. The Lord's name Thiruneelakandam is worthy of praise and devotion by everyone. It explains the Lord's gracious act of protecting all worlds from destruction and harm. Therefore, by chanting the name Thiruneelakandam, people of the world can also protect themselves from destruction and harm. They can attain the Lord's grace. The sacred begging bowl held by the Lord is of great glory. It is held for the salvation of the world. Similarly, the sacred begging bowl carried by Shiva's devotees also has the same nature. Lust is very cruel. For those who follow it, it can bring all evils. It is difficult for anyone to conquer. For devotees who have the support of the Lord and devotees and are engaged in sacred service, conquering lust is possible. Otherwise, it is very difficult for even deities like Brahma, Vishnu, Indra, and sages to conquer it. The rare greatness of this Nayanar and his wife, who were in their prime youth, lived in the same house, completely renounced lust, and upheld the oath made on Thiruneelakandam throughout their lives, is worthy of the world's praise. One can unite physically with one's wife for the sake of procreation, according to the rules. This is a rule stated by Dharma Shastras. But before the Lord's command, before the oath made on the Lord, even this is to be set aside. Similarly, other worldly experiences are also to be set aside before the oath made on the Lord. Conduct is not to be upheld for fame or glory or for the sake of human witnesses. We must uphold conduct uniformly, both internally and externally, always and everywhere, by taking the omnipresent Lord as witness. Worldly pleasure is small, supreme bliss is great. Those who renounce worldly pleasure for the love fixed on the Lord will attain eternal supreme bliss. Thus, due to their love and devotion for the Lord, their Shiva devotion, their unwavering adherence to Shiva's dharmas, their renunciation of worldly pleasure for the love of the Lord, their devotion to Thiruneelakandam, their upholding of that divine command, that oath, and their performance of difficult deeds,

because they attained such great blessings, this great one was named Thiruneelakanda Nayanar. Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar, also known as Thiruneelakanda Nayanar, and his wife attained the imperishable state of supreme bliss, which is liberation. Sekkizhar Swamigal, having extensively narrated the Puranam of Thiruneelakanda Nayanar, states that he has not fully narrated the Puranam of Thiruneelakanda Kuyava Nayanar, and that he has narrated this Puranam to the best of his knowledge and ability, praising the greatness of Thiruneelakandar, and thus concludes this Puranam. Just as the Lord's greatness cannot be fully described, so too the greatness of the Lord's devotees cannot be fully described, can it? Therefore, Sekkizhar Swamigal, saying this, praises that this glorious history of our Thiruneelakanda Kuyava Nayanar may grow and endure forever, and concludes the Puranam of Thiruneelakanda Nayanar. He then says that with its support, he will begin to narrate the sacred service performed by Iyarpagai Nayanar, a devotee of Shiva, who was a merchant living in Kaveri Poompattinam. We too will contemplate the sacred history of Iyarpagai Nayanar. Thiruchitrambalam.
