Chapter 01 — TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 200, Mummaiyāl Ulagāṇḍa Sarukkam, TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar Puranam. After the Puranam of Uruttira Pasupati Nayanar, we are now going to contemplate the Puranam of TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar. First, let us contemplate a brief summary of the Nayanar's life. In the Chola country, on the banks of the Kollidam river, surrounded by fertile fields, was the ancient prosperous city of Adhanur. All around it were lush paddy fields, flower gardens, groves of coconut, mango, jackfruit, and other trees. It was a densely populated city with mansions. Next to it, there was a pulaippāḍi, a settlement of small houses densely inhabited by the great Kizhiyars, who were hereditary agricultural laborers. Those who lived in this pulaippāḍi raised dogs and chickens. Around their small houses were coconut trees, maruta trees, and vanji trees. The pulai women engaged in pounding paddy. Their pastime was to beat drums with stones, sing to their rhythm, and dance. In such a pulaiyar settlement, in a family that held the traditional right to pulai work, a great man named Nandanar was born, endowed with a consciousness cultivated in a previous birth, focused on the sacred feet of Shiva. From the day his consciousness awakened, his mind became pure due to his intense desire for Lord Shiva, and without ever thinking of anything else, he followed the path of sacred service that was in accordance with the dharma of his birth-caste. He lived by the income from the grants (māṇiyam) for the drum-beating work assigned to the village. He excelled in sacred service. He performed various services in Shiva's temples, such as providing hides for covering percussion instruments like the perigai, and strings for the veena and yazh. Outside the temples, he rejoiced by dancing and singing with great love for Lord Shiva. Nayanar thought deeply of Sivalokanathar of Tiruppunkur with great desire and set out from Adhanur, reaching there. At the entrance of the temple, he wished to worship the Lord directly, singing and dancing with excellent music. To grant him a vision as he desired, Sivalokanathar commanded the Rishabha Devar (sacred bull) in front of Him to move aside and granted a direct vision. Nandanar saw that direct vision of Sivalokanathar, bowed, praised, glorified, danced, sang, and worshipped. Afterwards, seeing a low-lying area behind the temple, he eagerly dug a deep and wide pond there. He circumambulated the temple, bowed, stood outside, sang and danced, took leave of the Lord, and returned to his village. Later, he visited several nearby temples, worshipped the Lord, and performed whatever service he could. One day, while he was thus engaged, a strong desire arose in him to see and worship the sacred hall of Tillai (Chidambaram). Because of this, he could not even sleep. However, the thought arose that going to Tillai would not be appropriate for his birth-caste, and considering this to be the Lord's command, he avoided going to Tillai. But as the desire to go to Tillai did not subside, he would say, "I will go tomorrow, I will go tomorrow." Many days passed in this manner. Realizing that many days had passed thus, and unable to bear it any longer, unable to restrain himself, he resolved to go, so that the transient birth, like a pulai flower, might cease. He set out and reached near Tillai. He bowed to the sacred boundary of Tillai and rose. Seeing the sacrificial halls and the schools where Vedas were chanted, he feared to enter there, considering the lowliness of his caste. Beyond these, if one entered the main gate of the town's surrounding wall, they say there were three thousand sacrificial fires in every mansion.

"If this is the case for ordinary people, is it fitting for me to go there? It is very difficult," he thought. But his great love for Tillai grew and intensified, an uncontainable love. His heart melted. He folded his hands in worship. He circumambulated the sacred boundary of Tillai day and night. Thinking of his inability to enter there, he became very weary and slept, distressed, wondering how he could worship the dance of Lord Shiva in the sacred hall. As he slept, thinking that "this lowly birth, which causes suffering, is an impediment to me," the Lord appeared in his dream. The Lord appeared and, with a gentle smile, graced him, saying, "So that this birth may cease, you shall plunge into fire and come before Me with the Brahmins." Similarly, the Lord appeared to the Tillai Brahmins individually in their dreams and graciously commanded them to prepare a fire for TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar. All the Tillai Brahmins arose and, with reverence, gathered before the sacred gate of Tillai, resolved to carry out the Lord's command, and informed the devotee. At that moment, he bowed and said, "I am saved." The Tillai Brahmins then went and prepared the fire. They prepared the fire before the sacred gate on the southern side of Tillai's wall. Nandanar, holding the Lord's sacred feet in his mind, circumambulated the fire. He folded his hands in worship. Remembering the sacred feet that perform the dance of grace, he entered the fire. At that moment, in that fire, he cast off his illusory false form and emerged from the fire as a holy sage with a sacred thread and matted locks. As he rose, he shone like Brahma. Celestial drums resounded. Devas showered flowers. The Tillai Brahmins folded their hands. Devotees prostrated. Accompanied by the Tillai Brahmins, TirunAlaippOvAr bowed to the gopuram and swiftly entered to worship Lord Shiva dancing in the sacred hall. He reached the limit of that dance, which saves the world. After he entered Tillai, no one could see him again. Everyone was amazed and praised. The Lord bestowed upon TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar the blissful state of worshipping His sacred feet. This is the brief summary of TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar's life. Adhanur, where TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar was born, is located northwest of Tiruppunkur, across the Kollidam river. It is now known as Adhamangalam. A Shiva temple once existed here. Old signs of the pulaippāḍi and other features are not visible. There is also a land area of 40 veli, a distinct circle, known as TirunAlaippOvAr Kāṇi. Adhanur is west of Kāṇāṭṭam Puliyur. One can also reach Adhanur by coming southwest from Karuppariyalur. One can also reach Adhanur by coming west from Omāmpuliyur. In Tillai, there is an area called Omakulam. This is the place where Nandanar plunged and rose. It is southwest of the Nataraja temple. There is also a separate temple for Nandanar here. This is the brief summary of TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar's life. Now, let us extensively contemplate the Puranam of TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar through Sekkizhar Peruman's Periya Puranam. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 02 — TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 201, Mummaiyāl Ulagāṇḍa Sarukkam, TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar Puranam. In the Mummaiyāl Ulagāṇḍa Sarukkam, the Puranam of TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar follows the Puranam of Uruttira Pasupati Nayanar. According to our tradition, we shall contemplate the Nayanar's life in the order of concise work, then categorized work, then extensive work. First, the phrase about TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar in the Tiruttoṇḍat Tokai composed by Sundaramurti Swamigal is "Seṇmaiyē TirunAlaippOvārkkum Aḍiyēn" (I am a servant even to TirunAlaippOvAr, who is of pure mind). Sundaramurti Swamigal, who sang "I am a servant to Murti who ruled the world with three (qualities), and to Murugan and Uruttira Pasupati," has included TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar in the third verse of his Tiruttoṇḍat Tokai, singing "Seṇmaiyē TirunAlaippOvārkkum Aḍiyēn." The meaning of this is, "I am a servant to the great one named TirunAlaippOvAr, who has a pure mind." The purity (seṇmai) mentioned here refers to the unwavering feeling of love that arises from a pre-existing consciousness in a mind elevated towards Shiva. Seṇmai means mental purity and integrity. Nayanar performed his sacred service to the Lord while adhering to his caste dharma. He resolved to go to Tillai. But due to the lowliness of his caste, he refrained from going. Later, as his love intensified, many days passed with him saying, "I will go tomorrow, I will go tomorrow." Unable to bear the waste of these days, Nayanar resolved to go to Tillai so that his birth might cease. Thus, he thought of going to Tillai. But he did not go. He said, "I will go tomorrow." Then again, he resolved to go. Nayanar performed these actions with a pure mind at each stage. Even when he reached the boundary of Tillai, he did not dare to enter the city of Tillai, but stood at the outskirts of the town and circumambulated and worshipped the town itself. This too is a special quality of the purity that Nayanar possessed. Then, by the command of the Lord, he plunged into fire and emerged as a Vedic sage, which also came from the pure state that Nayanar held. Therefore, all these actions, being "seṇmaiyē" (pure), are arranged thus, and his entire life story is a result of his pure nature. That is why Sundaramurti Swamigal sang, "Seṇmaiyē TirunAlaippOvārkkum Aḍiyēn," to show all these. TirunAlaippOvAr is the name of the Nayanar. Nayanar had the desire and the readiness to go to Tillai. Even so, considering the lowliness of his birth-caste, he did not dare to go. Later, as his love became very intense, he stood thinking, "I will go to Tillai tomorrow, I will go tomorrow, I will go tomorrow," didn't he? Therefore, because of that special characteristic, the Nayanar was named TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar. His birth name is Nandanar. This is the explanation of the phrase "Seṇmaiyē TirunAlaippOvārkkum Aḍiyēn" found in the concise work. Now, in the categorized work, Tiruttoṇḍar Tiruvantāti, Nambiāṇḍār Nambigal has composed the following verse about the Nayanar: "Nāvār pugazh Tillai Ambalattān aruḷ peṭṭu nāḷaippōvān avaṇām puṟattiruttoṇḍaṇ taṇ puṇ pulai pōy mūvāyiravar kaikkūppa muṇiyāy avaṇ patitāṇ māvār pozhi tigazh Ādaṇūr eṇpar im maṇḍalattē." This is the verse composed by Nambiāṇḍār Nambigal about TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar in Tiruttoṇḍar Tiruvantāti. Now, let us contemplate the history of the Nayanar through Sekkizhar Peruman's Periya Puranam, which is the extensive work. TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar Puranam.

This section narrates the life history and character of the Nayanar named TirunAlaippOvAr. In chronological order, the life story of TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar is presented as the fourth story by Sekkizhar Peruman in the Mummaiyāl Ulagāṇḍa Sarukkam. Sekkizhar Peruman has sung 37 verses in the Puranam of TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar. Sekkizhar Peruman, following his adopted tradition, begins the Nayanar's history by first singing about the prosperity of the country and village where the Nayanar was born. The village where TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar was born is Adhanur. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman sings about its prosperity. Adhanur is the place where Nandanar was born. It is an ancient and prosperous place. However, its greatness became known to the world only after Nandanar's birth. Adhanur is a village renowned for the water wealth of the sacred Kollidam river. The Kollidam river, with its waves as hands, gathers gold and precious gems, and the lotuses in the ponds on both sides of the river receive them with their flower-like hands. Such is the prosperous village of Adhanur. When the Kaveri river overflows, the excess water is diverted into the Kollidam, and the Kollidam is designed to carry it to the sea. The name Kollidam (meaning "place that receives") is prevalent there because it is the place that receives the overflowing Kaveri. Such a sacred Kollidam river always has clear water. Driven by the force of the wind, the surface of this water is pushed, and the water curls up and rises. As the water curls up and rises, it carries up gold, sand, pearls, and gems lying at the bottom of the river. Then, it crashes against the banks and deposits the gold, gems, and pearls it carried there. The lotuses in the ponds in the wide fields on both banks receive these deposited gems and pearls with their hands. Just as generous people scatter and give away their possessions as they go, the sacred Kollidam river piles up gems on both its banks as it flows. Just as the needy receive the possessions scattered by generous people as they go, the lotuses in the ponds and fields on the banks of the Kollidam receive the pearls and gems scattered by the Kollidam river. Such a glorious Adhanur is located within Kana Nadu, one of the inland regions of the Chola country. In Adhanur, people live with the great light of sacred ash shining brightly. This is a fertile marutam land. Therefore, paddy, plantains, areca nuts, and sugarcane are cultivated. Sugarcane fields are abundant. Because of the abundance of sugarcane, when it is processed, its juice and other residues mix in the fields, making them muddy. Varal fish, shaped like plates, leap about. People there yoke buffaloes and engage in farming. They yoke buffaloes and plow with plows. In those muddy fields, there are pregnant crabs. When people plow those muddy fields with plows, the iron tip of the plow creates a path. The pregnant crabs follow that path until the field is completely plowed, and then they take refuge in the lotus flowers in the ponds next to the field. Thus,

the pregnant crabs that have climbed onto the lotus flowers lay their eggs within those flowers. As the crabs climb onto the flowers and lay their eggs within them, the flowers sway, and their pollen (tāttu) falls, emitting fragrance. This sight is like a mother crab, having given birth, drying herself and being given medicine suitable for childbirth. Sekkizhar Peruman sings thus. Here, Sekkizhar Peruman, in his description of nature, alludes to the medicinal uses of gorocanai (a yellow pigment from a cow's gall bladder) and other substances provided by Nandanar's lineage, which were used as medicine for women during childbirth. It is Sekkizhar Peruman's tradition to subtly weave references to the Nayanars' life stories into the descriptions of the natural wealth of the land where they lived. In each Nayanar's story, for Sekkizhar Peruman, who sings with a divine vision, the land, city, river, pond, field, trees, and other elements where the Nayanars lived all manifest as part of the Nayanar's story, isn't that wonderful? Sekkizhar Peruman sings that the trees in Adhanur are very lush and tall, reaching up to the solar orb (dinakara maṇḍalam). Dinakaran means the sun. Its meaning is "one who makes the day." Dinam means day, karan means maker. Dinakaran means one who makes the day as a period of time. The implication is that the sun creates the day and night divisions for the world by revolving and rotating around itself. Now, in Adhanur, there are many coconut trees. On those coconut trees, young spathes have bloomed, emitting fragrance. Coconut spathe refers to the flower clusters of the coconut tree. These coconut trees are very strong. They are coconut trees with strong bunches. Strong bunches imply the strength to bear the fruits as they grow larger and heavier day by day, without letting go. Below the coconut trees, near them, there are ponds. There are pits. In the water of these pits, there are vaalai fish. These vaalai fish leap and jump up to the height of the coconut trees. As these vaalai fish leap and jump up to the height of the coconut trees, the fruits at the top of the coconut trees fall down. The young bunches of the coconut tree hold on very strongly. Ripe, mature nuts fall off with a slight shock. These are the characteristics of a coconut tree. Thus, the vaalai fish jump and leap from the pit, striking the coconut tree growing on the bank, causing it to shake. As if the coconut tree, angered by the vaalai's mischievous act of leaping onto its trunk, punished the vaalai fish, the coconut tree dropped its fruits onto it. So, the coconut nuts struck the vaalai fish and fell into the muddy field with it. The vaalai fish was buried in the mud, pressed under the fruit. When the coconut nuts fell with such force, they landed on a ripe jackfruit that had ripened at the base of a nearby jackfruit tree. As a result, the jackfruit split open and poured out its juice. The jackfruit juice flowed abundantly. Previously, the vaalai fish was buried in the mud by the fruit that fell from the coconut tree, wasn't it? Now, the jackfruit juice flows there. As the jackfruit juice flows, the hold of the mud gradually loosens, and now the vaalai fish, which was trapped under the coconut nut, easily came out. So now it leaps with even more joy.

The coconut tree mentioned here is depicted as a tree that simultaneously bears many stages of ripeness, such as young flowers and tender coconuts (pūmpālai), strong bunches with green fruits (pasiya kāy koṇḍa valiya kulai), and mature fruits (mutupālai). Similarly, great people are those who help people of various levels of maturity according to their respective standards. This is the implication. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman vividly describes the prosperity of the country by showing together the state of coconut trees growing lushly on the banks due to Adhanur's water wealth, the state of their fruits maturing and falling naturally without being picked due to their abundance, the state of vaalai fish leaping and jumping up, and the nature of jackfruits bursting open and pouring out their juice. Let us continue our contemplation. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 03 — TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 202, Mummaiyāl Ulagāṇḍa Sarukkam, TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar Puranam. Sekkizhar Peruman sings about the prosperity of Adhanur, where TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar was born. There is abundant wealth derived from agriculture. This wealth is of two types: nansei (wetland) and punsei (dryland). This agricultural wealth can also be divided into two: field wealth and garden wealth. Field wealth refers to land where river water or tank water flows naturally without the effort of farmers, nurturing crops. However, here, farmers' labor is still required for tasks like making mud, planting, and weeding. Secondly, garden wealth. Unlike field wealth, river water does not flow naturally here. From the beginning to the harvest season, the farmer's efforts are required in many ways, such as daily irrigation, weeding, scaring away birds, and protecting crops. Next is the intermittent wealth. This refers to the second type, dryland gardens. Many crops are cultivated mixed together in dryland gardens. Therefore, the produce from dryland gardens does not come all at once like in nansei land, but rather as intermittent wealth. That is why Sekkizhar Peruman refers to it as intermittent wealth. Next, he mentions "selpaḍu tuḍavai" (cultivated garden). Gardens are of two types: those that are plowed and cultivated, and those on mountain slopes where seeds are sown without plowing. The garden mentioned here refers to those crops that are plowed and sown. This is because, even today in the Kaveri region, some areas are kept as elevated lands within nansei fields to grow dryland grains and other crops. Sekkizhar Peruman sings "iḍaivaru vaḷam" (intermittent wealth) to indicate that these crops appear intermittently in the fields. Thus, Adhanur is seen to be full of wealth derived from agriculture. Generally, wealth derived from agriculture is considered true wealth. Moreover, it is concluded that all types of wealth originate only from agriculture. "Suzhaṇḍum ēr piṉṉatu ulakam adaṇāl uzhaṇḍum uzhavē talai" (Though it revolves, the world follows the plow; therefore, though one toils, agriculture is supreme) is Kural number 1031 from the chapter on Agriculture. Parimēlazhagar, who wrote a commentary on this, states that even if wealth is earned through all other professions, all those engaged in other professions must follow farmers for food. That is why it is said, "Suzhaṇḍum ēr piṉṉatu ulakam." Therefore, he also says, "uzhaṇḍum uzhavē talai." Here, the people of Adhanur are also very wealthy due to agriculture. Wealth automatically reaches those who are diligent, as stated in Kural number 594 from the chapter on Diligence: "Ākkam atarviṇāy sellum asaivilā ūkkam uḍaiyāṇ uḷai" (Prosperity will seek out and go to the diligent one who never tires). Therefore, according to the Tirukkural we saw earlier, farmers are the most diligent. Combining the conclusions of these two Kurals, we can interpret that wealth, seeing the diligence of farmers, goes and resides with them. It reaches them. Such an Adhanur is full of mansions. Those mansions are very tall, reaching up to the clouds. Moreover, new settlements are constantly being built around the town. As the town becomes dense, new settlements are created outside the town to expand it. Having sung about Adhanur in this manner, Sekkizhar Peruman now begins to sing about the pulaippāḍi outside Adhanur, where our Nandanar was born. Settlements called pulaippāḍi or pulaicceri are located outside the town, as suburban settlements. Places ending with "ceri" or "pāḍi" refer to suburban residential areas. Near Adhanur, there is a settlement called pulaippāḍi. This is a suburban area where pulaiyar live. Pulaiyar is a caste name derived from their profession of handling and consuming meat and flesh of dead animals. This pulaippāḍi, located near Adhanur, is situated next to agricultural fields, which are marutam land. It is located in a large open area next to the fields. There, people who are agricultural laborers, belonging to the farming profession, live closely with their relatives. Those who live in these pulaippāḍi and other ceris build small huts and live there. The roofs of these huts are thatched with straw. They are agricultural laborers. Therefore, they use the straw they obtain as their right to thatch their huts. To ensure that such a roof lasts for many years without damage, they let gourd vines grow over it. A gourd vine has tendrils that grow long like hands from the stem below the leaves, which enable it to cling to roofs, fences, and other structures and climb upwards. Therefore, those who live in these ceris let these gourd vines grow over their hut roofs for support. They also let gourd vines grow over the roofs to obtain fruits for food. Gourd is suitable for cooking and eating with the meat (pulāl) consumed by pulaiyar and others. Furthermore, their landlords and others would not prefer or eat these gourds. Therefore, it is common for pulaiyar to cultivate gourds. As mentioned above, these vines also help protect the straw-thatched huts from damage by clinging to them. Having thus explained the structure of the pulaippāḍi, Sekkizhar Peruman then proceeds to sing about the lifestyle, customs, food, and occupations of the pulaiyar living in the pulaippāḍi, as well as the types of games played by children and the ornaments they wear. In the huts of the pulaiyar, they raise chickens for food. The small chicks of these chickens follow their mother hen wherever she goes. Thus, the hen takes her chicks with her. This is because, by doing so, the chicks learn how and where to peck for food. The chicks also follow her. Furthermore, the mother hen takes her chicks with her to protect them from birds of prey like kites. The people there raise dogs. Children play by snatching puppies, called parals. When the mother dog is not around, the children quickly take the puppies from their place and run away before the mother dog sees them. As these children run with the puppies, the puppies' small yelps can be heard. If the mother dog hears that sound, she will relentlessly pursue them. She will chase after the children. Therefore, to prevent the small yelps of the puppies from being heard, the iron bells tied to the children's waists make noise. When the children run with the puppies, these iron bells make noise. The small yelps of the puppies are drowned out by the sound of the iron bells. Thus, the iron bells tied to the children's waists help the children in this way. The people living in the pulaippāḍi are poor. Therefore, they adorn their children with iron ornaments. Those who are wealthier than them adorn themselves with silver, and those who are even wealthier adorn themselves with gold, as much as they can afford. Thus, we can see here the truth that wealth is the fulfillment of the mind. Just as gold, gems, and silver bring joy to the wealthy, the iron ornaments worn by the people living here bring them the same joy. Even very wealthy people sometimes wear iron ornaments to ward off evil spirits and the like. In that pulaippāḍi, in the courtyards of the huts, the hides of dead animals like sheep and cattle are split into strips and laid out to dry for tanning, to be used for making leather instruments. In that pulaippāḍi, there are marutam trees, vanji trees, mango trees, and coconut trees. These are all products of the marutam land, trees that grow in places rich in water. This indicates the water wealth and land wealth of that region. In the shade of these trees, the pulatti women of that pulaippāḍi put their children to sleep. They use animal hides as mats for their children to sleep on. Their profession is to skin and process the hides of dead cattle, buffaloes, and sheep to make sandals and leather for musical instruments. This is the traditional occupation of the pulaiyar. Thus, they would process and prepare hides for making various drums and other musical instruments, as well as sandals and other items. The pulai women use strong, small hides for their children to sleep on. The place where they live is marutam land, so it is always damp. Therefore, to protect their children from the dampness, they take one of the hides they use for their work, a small hide, and lay their children on it. Thus, because the mat is made of hide, the dampness will not affect the children. Furthermore, in the shade of these trees, female birds have buried wide-mouthed pots to incubate their eggs. The occupation of these people is guarding crops and announcing by drum. For this work, they also have the right to receive grants from agricultural lands. Their job is to announce by drum. If any news needs to be announced in the village, they take a drum, beat it, and announce it. When there is no drum-beating work, it is their custom to hang their drums on the branches of mango trees near their small huts to prevent rats, termites, and other pests from damaging them. In these pulaippāḍi areas, there are also some old coconut trees. It is natural for dogs to give birth to and raise their puppies in the hollows at the base of these trees. Furthermore, the people living in this pulaippāḍi also perform village guard duties. To engage in their agricultural work, village guard duties, and other tasks, the roosters they raise wake them up early in the morning. Red-crested male chickens are called roosters. These roosters crow early in the morning and wake these people up. It is customary to pay them paddy as wages for their agricultural work. The pulai women pound such paddy in a mortar to make rice. This paddy-pounding work is usually done by the women in the early morning and evening so as not to interfere with their natural work of agriculture. While pounding paddy, these women, the pulai women, sing. The birds in the ponds in that pulaippāḍi make sounds. The pulai women adorn their hair with paddy stalks. They have a custom of drinking liquor. Due to drinking liquor, their steps falter.

Thus, they walk swaying. These women drink liquor and also engage in revelry. Drums resound to accompany them. In this way, Sekkizhar Peruman sings about the structure of the pulaippāḍi near Adhanur, the people living there, the birds, animals, the occupations of the men in the pulaippāḍi, the work of the women, and the games of the children. In such a pulaippāḍi, Nandanar, a Nayanar who had great love for Lord Shiva's sacred feet, was born. Let us continue our contemplation. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 04 — TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 203, Mummaiyāl Ulagāṇḍa Sarukkam, TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar Puranam. Sekkizhar Swamigal, having described the pulaippāḍi near Adhanur, sings that a holy man named Nandanar lived in such a pulaippāḍi. Nandanar had true love for Lord Shiva's sacred feet. What does it mean to have true love for Lord Shiva's sacred feet? What does true love mean? It means that his love did not go to anything else except Lord Shiva's sacred feet. That is true love. Sekkizhar Peruman composed the following verse: "Ippadittākiya kaḍiñar iruppiṉiṉ varappiṉiṉ vāzhvār mey parivu Sivaṉ kazhaṟkē viḷaitta uṇarvoḍum vandār appatiyil ūr pulaimai āṇṟa tozhil tāyattār oppilavar Nandanār eṉa oruvar uḷarāṇār." In this, Sekkizhar Peruman sings about Nandanar, "mey parivu Sivaṉ kazhaṟkē viḷaitta uṇarvoḍum vandār" (he came with a consciousness that had cultivated true love for Shiva's sacred feet). Sekkizhar Peruman sings that he came with the consciousness of having true love for Shiva's sacred feet. Regarding "viḷaitta uṇarvoḍum vandār," Sivakavimaṇi Ayya explains as follows: "Oru maikkaṇ tāṉ kaṭṟa kalvi oruvarkku ezhumaiyum ēmappuḍaittu" (The education one learns in one birth will be a protection for them in seven births) is a Kural. Its meaning is that the education one learns in one birth will follow them and be beneficial in many subsequent births. This is Tirukkural, chapter on Education, Kural number 398. Just as education, once learned in one birth, follows through many births, so too the merits and demerits (puṇṇiya pāvam) accumulated by a soul will follow that soul through many births and be experienced by it. Although the soul takes different bodies in various births, it carries with it the knowledge it learned, the wisdom it gained, and the merits and demerits from its previous births. This is why if someone in a particular caste or family is superior in knowledge to anyone else in that caste or family, it means that they have learned that knowledge and gained that wisdom in their previous births. This is what Sekkizhar Peruman refers to here as "viḷaitta uṇarvu" (cultivated consciousness). Thus, Nandanar, having cultivated love for Lord Shiva's sacred feet in his previous births, even though he was born in a pulaiyar settlement in this birth, continued to show love for Lord Shiva's sacred feet due to the continuation of that love from his previous births. Even though he was born in a pulaippāḍi, this cultivated prior consciousness is what Sekkizhar Peruman describes in the Puranam of Chaṇḍīsar Nayanar as "sintai malara uḍaṉ malarum sevvi uṇarvu" (a pure consciousness that blossoms along with the blossoming of the mind). In the Puranam of Tirugnanasambandar, he also sings, "Īsar kazhaḷ muṟai purinda muṉ uṇarvu mūlam" (the root of the prior consciousness that performed service to the Lord's feet). In the Puranam of Kaṇṇappa Nayanar, which we already contemplated, he also sings, "muṉbu sey tavattiṉ īṭṭam" (the accumulation of previous penance). With such a cultivated prior consciousness, the holy man Nandanar was born into this world. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman sings that he is incomparable. Sekkizhar Peruman sings "oppilavar" (incomparable). It means peerless. From the moment he was born into the very low pulaiyar lineage, Nandanar remained in the very high state of devotion to Lord Shiva's sacred feet. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman, who sang "oppilavar Nandanār," also sings "Nandanār eṉa oruvar uḷarāṇār" (a person named Nandanar came into being). Many people who did not perform penance towards Shiva in previous births, no matter what they are like or what possessions they have in this birth, are considered to be without. Those who performed penance and showed love towards Lord Shiva in previous births, even if they have no possessions in this birth, are truly those who exist. This is the ultimate conclusion of spiritual texts. "Ilar palar āgiya kāraṇam nōṟpār silar palar nōlātavar" (Many are those who are without, because few perform penance; many are those who do not perform penance) is Kural number 270 from the chapter on Penance. Parimēlazhagar comments on this: In this world, there are only a few wealthy people, but many poor people. The reason for this is that only a few perform penance, while many do not. Therefore, those few who performed penance are wealthy, and the many who did not perform penance are poor. This Kural speaks about the general dharma in the worldly state. If we apply this to Shiva's merit, those who did not perform penance towards Shiva in previous births, no matter what wealth they possess in this birth, are considered to be without. Those who performed penance towards Shiva in previous births, even if they possess nothing in this birth, are truly those who exist. They are "uḷar" (existing ones). "Nalamilarāka nalamatuṇḍāka nāṭavar nāṭaṟugiṉṟa kulamilarāka kulamatuṇḍāka" (Even if they are without welfare, welfare exists; even if they are without lineage, lineage exists, as the people of the world know) is from Tirugnanasambandar's Tiruvāḷavāy Tevaram. According to this, even though Nandanar did not have the welfare, lineage, and wealth that the world sees, because he loved Lord Shiva's sacred feet and possessed that cultivated consciousness, Sekkizhar Peruman writes that Nandanar became one who possessed everything. Now, Sekkizhar Peruman composes the next verse. "Piraṇdu uṇarvu toḍaṅgiya piṉ piṟaikkaṇṇi peruntakai pāl siṟaṇda peruṅkādalināl seṇmai puri sintaiyarāy maṟaṇdum ayal niṇaivu iṉṟi varu piṟappiṉ vazhi vaṇda aṟam puri koḷkaiyarāyē aḍit toṇḍiṉ neṟi niṇṟār" (After his consciousness began from birth, with great love for the great Lord with the crescent moon, he became of pure mind, without even forgetting to think of anything else, and stood in the path of service, adhering to the dharma of his birth). Nandanar, born in this world, in that pulaiyar settlement, from the time his memory began, from the day his consciousness awakened, lived with a mind that desired that special great purity, which is great love for Lord Shiva's sacred feet. He lived without even forgetting to think of anything else. He stood firm in the path of Shiva's service, adhering to the principle of performing the Shiva dharmas appropriate to his pulaiyar lineage. Sekkizhar Peruman sings, "Piraṇdu uṇarvu toḍaṅgiya piṉ" (after his consciousness began from birth). It is impossible for a person, from birth until they gain understanding and awareness, to discern what is good and what is evil and to act accordingly. That is why Sekkizhar Peruman sings, "uṇarvu toḍaṅgiya piṉ." Similarly, in Karaikkal Ammaiyar's Tiruvākkākiya Arputat Tiruvantāti, Ammaiyar sings, "Piraṇdu mozhi payiṇṭa piṉ ellām kādal siṟaṇdu niṉ sēvaḍiyē sērṇdēṉ" (After being born and learning to speak, with great love, I reached your sacred feet). We will contemplate this extensively in the Puranam of Karaikkal Ammaiyar. Here too, we recall it and see that Nandanar, similarly, from the time his consciousness awakened, from the time he began to understand, stood firm in the path of Shiva. Generally, even after their consciousness awakens for a long time, many humans do not direct their consciousness towards the path of Shiva, but fall into other worldly ways until death. Many such people exist in the world. But to direct one's mind towards purity, which is love for Lord Shiva's sacred feet, from the day one's consciousness awakens, is appropriate only for great souls like Nandanar. Nandanar lived with a pure mind. Purity (seṇmai) means the state of being Shiva. "Seṇmaiyē āya Sivapadam aḷitta selvamē" (The wealth that bestowed the state of Shiva, which is purity itself) and "seṇmai nalam aṟiyāda sitaḍar" (fools who do not know the goodness of purity) are from Tiruvāsakam. "Tiruniṇṟa seṇmaiyē seṇmaiyāy koṇḍa Tirunāvukkaraiyaṉ" (Tirunāvukkaraiyaṉ, who held the firm purity as purity itself) is the fourth verse of Tiruttoṇḍar Tokai. Regarding this,

"Naṟṟavā uṇṉai nāṉ maṟakkiṉum sollum nā Namasivāyavē" (O good ascetic, even if I forget you, my tongue will utter "Namasivaya") is Sundaramurti's Tevaram. We can see this in Nandanar's state here. Such a Nandanar adhered to the principle of performing dharma according to his birth. He held the principle that he should perform whatever Shiva dharmas are possible and prescribed by the scriptures for the lineage, caste, and family in which he was born. Sekkizhar Peruman is now going to sing in the next three verses how he put this into practice. Some believe that to perform service to Lord Shiva and His devotees, one must abandon the customs prescribed by the scriptures for one's birth lineage. However, Nandanar held the principle that one should perform Shiva dharmas and sacred services while adhering to the conduct appropriate to the family in which one was born. In Saiva Siddhanta, people are divided into three categories. The first category is Samucittar, the second is Vainayakar, and the third is Prakirutars. Samucittars are those whose minds are illuminated by special dharma saṃskāras. The quality of their minds shining as before even after the body is shed is called Samucittiham. The second category is Vainayakar. They possess the quality of Vainayaka, which is illuminated by their intellect, guidance from a guru, and scriptures. The third category is Prakirutars. They possess the quality of Prakrutam, which appears only when the body exists, like in dreams, and then disappears. These Prakirutars are those who lack the ability, knowledge, and desire to attain the state of Shiva. Therefore, the scriptures that describe the method of attaining the state of Shiva are of no use to them. The Vainayakar, who belong to the second category, are those who wish to attain liberation by learning from scriptures, considering all enlightened beings as authorities. They have four stages according to their maturity: mandam (slow), mandataram (slower), tiviram (intense), and tivirataram (more intense). For these Vainayakar who strive in this way, the method of attaining the state of Shiva is described in the Sivagnana Siddhiyar, a philosophical text. The Samucittars, who belong to the first category, are those who are born with the quality of Samucitta due to the special penance they performed in previous births. Lord Shiva Himself comes forward and admits such Shiva devotees, who are born with the desire to attain liberation (moksha), into the state of Shiva. Among these three categories, our Nandanar belongs to the Samucittar category. Therefore, he is born with the qualification to attain liberation due to the special penance he performed in previous births. Lord Shiva Himself comes forward and admits them into the state of Shiva. All Nayanars, including Nandanar, belong to this Samucittar category. Regarding these Samucittars, Arunanandi Sivachariar Peruman states in Sivagnana Siddhiyar: "Paṇḍai nal tavattāl tōṇṭi Paramaṇai bhakti paṇṇum toṇḍarai tāṇē tūya gatiyiṉil tokkuppaṉ" (He Himself gathers the devotees who appear through ancient good penance and worship the Supreme Lord into the pure state). The three types of people—Samucittar, Vainayakar, and Prakirutars—are described in Bhoga Karikai. Therefore, all our Nayanars are Samucittars. That is why Lord Shiva Himself comes and admits them into the state of Shiva, which is liberation. Even though he was born as such a Samucittar, Nandanar performed service to the Lord without deviating from his caste tradition, in accordance with his birth lineage. Thus,

without deviating from his caste tradition, he performed service to the Lord. We can also understand the truth by seeing the stories of great people like Kannappa Nayanar, who, transcending the limits prescribed for their caste tradition, strived for liberation in a Samucitta state. Sekkizhar Peruman continues to sing about how Nandanar performed Shiva service in accordance with his birth lineage. Let us continue our contemplation. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 05 — TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 204, Mummaiyāl Ulagāṇḍa Sarukkam, TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar Puranam. Nandanar, adhering to the customs of his birth-caste, performed Shiva service in a way that was possible according to that dharma. Sekkizhar Peruman explains how he performed Shiva's sacred work. The word "paṟai" (பறை) means "to speak" or "to announce." In those times, there was a custom of announcing news of public and social events to the general public by beating a drum. Therefore, those who announced in this manner were called "paṟaiyar" (பறையர்), meaning "announcers." This became the name of the profession. This name then came to be associated with the lineage that performed this profession. Those who announced by drum would hold a type of musical instrument in their hands, make a sound, and when people gathered, they would announce the news. Therefore, the musical instrument that made such a sound came to be called "paṟai." Thus, those who announced news using the instrument called "paṟai" were called "paṟaiyar." It is noteworthy that the Tamil word "paṟaital" (பறைதல்) is still used in Malayalam with the same meaning of "to speak." The grant given to those who announced by drum for public social service was called "paṟaittozhil māṇiyam" (drum-beating profession grant). Therefore, those who received this "paṟaittozhil māṇiyam" and announced news were called "paṟaiyar." However, it is said that the derogatory connotation now associated with this name for this term is due to the "pulai" (lowly) work performed by this lineage. But in those times, there was no such derogatory meaning associated with these names. It was a time when it was considered a profession, and the people who performed that profession were respected. With the intention of showing how the absence of derogatory connotations in such caste names was delightful, Kacciyappa Munivar composed a verse in the Perur Puranam. Perur is a sacred place located near Coimbatore today. There, the Lord is Paṭṭīswarar, and the Goddess is Maragathavalli. When Sundaramurti Swamigal came to Perur, Lord Shiva, wishing to play with Sundaramurti Swamigal, left the Perur temple where He resided with His consort, the green-hued Nayaki (Pachchai Nayaki), and both of them engaged in farming as a farmer and a farmer's wife in the fields. Lord Shiva and the Goddess had commanded Rishabha Devar not to tell Sundaramurti Swamigal that they were farming in the fields when he asked where the Lord was, and then they left. Sundaramurti Swamigal came there, bathed in the Kanchi river, and when he came to the temple, he was perplexed not to find the Lord and the Goddess. He asked Rishabha Devar, but Lord Shiva had commanded Rishabha Devar not to tell Sundaramurti Swamigal that they were in the fields. Therefore, Rishabha Devar, without answering Sundaramurti Swamigal, indicated with his eyes that the Lord and the Goddess were in the fields as a farmer and a farmer's wife. Sundaramurti Swamigal also looked into the fields. Seeing Paṭṭīswarar, who is the profound meaning of the great Vedic statements, and Maragathavalli, whose sacred lotus feet were immersed in the mud of the fields as they engaged in farming, Sundaramurti Swamigal worshipped them there, delighted, and prostrated before them. After worshipping thus, he sang to the Lord: "Maṟaivaṉ arasaṉ seṭṭi taṉ tātai vayaṅgu nūl sūttiraṉ puvaṉam paṟaitaru nalla Saṅkaraṉ vēḍaṉ pa" (You are a Brahmin, a king, a merchant, a father, a brilliant thread-wearing Sudra, a good Shankara who announces to the world, a hunter, a) "aṇi Sivaṉ vilaṅgu mukkaṇṇaṉ arai sey ambaṭṭaṉ akam atu puṟattē ēkāli āṭṟu aravamār iḍaiyaṉ paṟaiyaṉum muṉṉarāṇa nī iṉṟu pallaṉ āṇamai teriṇdēṉ yāṉ" (You are Shiva adorned with three eyes, a barber, a washerman, a herdsman wearing a snake as a girdle, and a drummer. I now understand that you have become a farmer today) – this is how Kavirāṭsasar Kacciyappa Munivar structured this verse in the Perur Puranam, depicting Sundaramurti Swamigal singing to the Lord. Here, the verse is composed in such a way that Sundaramurti Swamigal sings, applying various caste names to the Lord, with two meanings (siledai) to enhance the taste of Tamil. Sundaramurti Swamigal sings to the Lord: "You are a Brahmin, for the Vedas declare that Parameswara alone is a Brahmin. Therefore, you are a Brahmin. You are a king, having ruled as the Pandya king, crowned in Madurai. You are a Vaisya, having traded bangles and gems in Madurai. Moreover, you are a Vaisya who performs an unusual trade, giving yourself to beings and taking away their self-conceit (tarpōtam), which is the soul's ego. You are a Vellala, who cultivates the crop of devotion in beings. You are a Sudra, a brilliant thread-wearing Sudra. You are the Sudra who is the essence of the sutras (aphorisms) in texts like Sivagnana Bodham. You are the good Shankara whom this whole world speaks of. You are a hunter, a hunter who assumes many forms. You, who are in your true form (svarūpa nilayil), are a hunter who assumes many forms like formless (aruvam), form-formless (aruvuruvam), and form (uruvam). You are the hunter who came in the form of a hunter to bestow the Pasupata arrow upon Arjuna. You are an Ambattan (barber). 'Am' means beautiful, 'paṭṭaṉ' means one who wears silk. One who wears beautiful silk garments. Therefore, you are an Ambattan. You are Shankaran, of the Shankara caste. You are Shankaran, the one who brings happiness. You are a herdsman who wears a snake as an ornament around his waist. You, who have appeared in all these various castes, appear to me today as a Pallan (farmer). This act of becoming a Pallan is more beautiful than your being a Brahmin," sings Sundaramurti Swamigal. Therefore, Kavirāṭsasar Kacciyappa Munivar structured this verse in the Perur Puranam, depicting Sundaramurti Swamigal singing to the Lord with all caste names and their inner spiritual meanings, to make us understand that there is no inferiority in any caste or caste name. Similarly, the Sri Rudram, which is in the heart of the Vedas, praises the Lord as a potter, a carpenter, and as belonging to all sections of society, doesn't it? Therefore, it is clear to us from this that in those times, there was no discrimination based on caste or caste names. Our Nandanar also performed the work appropriate to the caste in which he was born. He performed the pulaiyar work that was done by his birth lineage. Nevertheless, he excelled in Shiva service. Nandanar provided the necessary hides for the perigais (drums) needed for the temples of Lord Shiva, who wields the trident as a weapon. In temples, there are percussion instruments like perigai. Their mouths are covered with hide, and nerves are used to connect the hide to the body of the instrument. These percussion instruments like perigai have various numbers of faces, such as one face, two faces, five faces, and so on. Therefore, Nandanar provides the hides that cover the faces of these musical instruments. In addition, he also provides the straps and other materials used to tightly bind the hide to the instrument for Shiva temple service. Furthermore, he also provides the strings needed for instruments like the veena and yazh, which are played in Shiva temples. In ancient times, the strings used in string instruments like the veena and yazh were made from the nerves of dead animals. Nowadays, these are made from metal wires. But in those times, they used the nerves of dead animals for this purpose. Therefore, Nandanar, using these materials appropriate to his birth-caste, the pulai caste, performs Shiva service for the Shiva temple. He also lived by offering gorocanai (a yellow pigment from a cow's gall bladder) for Shiva temple service. Gorocanai is a substance, a yellow fragrant substance extracted from the stomach of a cow. It is used in the mixture of sandalwood paste for the Lord's sacred anointing. They extract these from the stomachs of cows after they die. Therefore, Nandanar, even though he performed the pulai profession, which was appropriate to his birth-caste, the pulai caste, he used it for the service of Lord Shiva and the Shiva temple. He lived by performing Shiva service in this manner. Skins, straps, strings, and gorocanai – all these, being helpful for Shiva's worship and service, Nandanar offered them with the abundant desire that arose in his heart due to his consciousness from previous births. Others, that is, others born in his caste, saw these skins, straps, strings, and gorocanai as commodities to sell for their livelihood. But Nandanar saw all these as materials for Shiva service and used them accordingly. Similarly, Chandesa Nayanar, moved by compassion, performed ablution for Lord Shiva with milk that flowed spontaneously from cows without being milked. We can also recall here the Shiva service of Tirunilakanta Nayanar, whom we saw first, who used the earnings from his pottery work for his sustenance and offered the sacred begging bowls he made to Shiva's devotees. Thus, all the Nayanars were great people who lived by adapting the professions appropriate to their birth-castes to the service of Lord Shiva. Nandanar, who lived in this manner, due to his immense love for the Lord, would go to the temple, stand outside the temple gate, and, due to the overflowing abundance of his true love for Lord Shiva, would dance and sing. When immense love for the Lord overflows, one loses self-control and dances, sings, and performs other actions. These are all characteristics of love. Thus, dancing and singing, remembering the Lord, from outside the temple, are appropriate actions. Therefore, our Nandanar also had the nature of worshipping the Lord by dancing and singing from outside the temple, remembering Him. While thus engaged, he thought of going to Tiruppunkur, desiring greatly to contemplate the sacred feet of Sivalokanathar, who resides in Tiruppunkur, a sacred place near his village. Let us continue our contemplation. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 06 — TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 205, Mummaiyāl Ulagāṇḍa Sarukkam, TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar Puranam. Nandanar, from Adhanur where he lived, thought of going to Tiruppunkur, a nearby sacred Shiva temple. He desired to go to Tiruppunkur and have a darshan of Sivalokanathar. So, he set out from Adhanur and reached Tiruppunkur. At this point, let us briefly contemplate the greatness of the Tiruppunkur Shiva temple and its sthala puranam (legend). This sacred place called Tiruppunkur is located west of the Vaithiswaran Koil temple. The sthala viruksham (sacred tree) here is the Pungu tree, hence it is named Tiruppunkur. The Lord's sacred name is Sivalokanathar, and the Goddess is Sokkanayaki. This sacred place has been sung about by Tirugnanasambandar, Tirunavukkarasar, and Sundaramurti Swamigal. It is also known as Karinjaranyam. It is endowed with the three special features of Murti (deity), Sthalam (place), and Teertham (sacred water). Once, when Parameswara was seated with Uma Devi on Mount Kailash, Brahma Devar went there. He saw many people resembling Ishwara there. Amazed by this, he asked Lord Shiva the reason. Then Lord Shiva told Brahma Devar, "In the earthly realm, there is a place called Karinjavanam, a Pungavanam. In that place, for Soundarya Nayaki's Shiva puja, we have created a Paranjothi Lingam and bestowed five Lingams, which are manifestations of the Panchakshara mantra. In that place, for Uma Devi and Us to bathe, we have established the Rishabha Teertham from Kailash. Those who bathe in that teertham and worship Sivalokesan and Soundarya Nayaki (Sokkanayaki) there according to the prescribed rites will attain our Sarupa Padavi (the state of having the same form as the Lord)." Lord Shiva thus graced Brahma Devar. Brahma Devar, hearing this, was greatly delighted. At that very moment, he ascended his swan vehicle with Saraswati Devi and arrived at Karinjaranyam, also known as Tiruppunkur. As the Lord had said, he bathed in the Rishabha Teertham, entered the temple, worshipped Sivalokesan and Soundarya Nayaki, performed ablutions, offerings, and archana, and offered praises. Then Lord Shiva appeared there, granted darshan to Brahma Devar, and bestowed the Panchakshara Upadesam (initiation into the five-syllable mantra). After that, Brahma Devar returned to Satyaloka. Once, Indra, having fallen in love with Ahalya, the wife of Sage Gautama, and committed an improper act, was cursed by Sage Gautama. To be freed from that curse, Indra traveled to various sacred places (Kshetra-adanam and Teertha-adanam). He came to Tiruppunkur and performed Shiva puja here. Then Sri Sivalokanathar appeared, removed Indra's curse, and commanded him to celebrate a festival for Him every year. Similarly, Devendra, with a joyful heart, celebrated a festival for the Lord on Vaikasi Visakam and has continued to do so every year on Vaikasi Visakam. Once, during the divine marriage of Parvati and Parameswara, the North tilted and the South rose. To balance this, Agastya Mahamuni was sent to the South. Then, Agastya Peruman walked towards the South, crossed the Vindhya mountain, came to this Tiruppunkur temple, bathed in the Rishabha Teertham, had a darshan of Sivalokanathar, chanted the Panchakshara mantra 12,000 times, and attained samadhi. Then Ishan, pleased, granted him a vision of His wedding form. After this, Agastya Peruman went to Tirumaraikkadu, also known as Vedāranyam. Once, when there was a drought and famine in the Chola country, the Chola king came to Tiruppunkur, had a darshan of Sivalokanathar, and performed puja. At that time, Sundaramurti Swamigal came to Tiruppunkur. The Chola king requested Sundaramurti Swamigal to bring rain. Sundaramurti Swamigal commanded that if it rained, 12 veli of land should be given to Sivanokanathar. Then, by the grace of Ishan, rain poured everywhere, and the king was delighted and worshipped the Lord. Later, as the rain continued incessantly, Sundaramurti Swamigal was worshipped by the king to prevent damage from the rain. Sundaramurti Swamigal said that he would stop the rain, but if he did, another 12 veli of land should be given to Sivaloka Peruman and he should be worshipped. The king agreed to this. Then the rain stopped. The country prospered. Thus, this Karinjaranyam, Tiruppunkur, is of such greatness. When Daksha, disrespecting Lord Shiva, performed a great yajna with Vishnu and other devas, Veerabhadra, born from the fiery third eye of Lord Shiva, went to that yajna and scattered all the devas. He also knocked out the teeth of Surya. Surya, thus maimed, went to many sacred places but found no relief. He came to Tiruppunkur, bathed in the Rishabha Teertham, worshipped Sivalokesan, and praised Him. By the grace of this Lord, he regained his teeth, consecrated a lingam in his name, and returned to his world. In the same Daksha Yajna, Agni Deva's hand was cut off by Veerabhadra. Agni Deva came here to Tiruppunkur and worshipped the Lord. Then his severed hand grew back as before. In the Treta Yuga, when the monkey army was sent to the South to search for Sita Piratti, these monkeys came to Tiruppunkur, worshipped Sivalokanathar, bathed in the teertham, and praised Him. They received boons that they would have abundant food on their way and would find Sita Piratti, and then they left. Sage Patanjali also came here and worshipped the Lord. Sage Vyagrapada also came here and worshipped the Lord. Uma Devi, to destroy Mahishasura, worshipped Lord Shiva and on the tenth day received the necessary weapons from Lord Shiva and killed Mahishasura. Later, to kill Raktabija, Vanyasura, and others, she took the form of the Sapta Kanniyars (Seven Maidens), worshipped Sivalokanathar of Tiruppunkur, received boons, and killed and conquered those asuras. Therefore, this is a sacred place where the Sapta Kanniyars performed puja. The place where Uma Devi stayed at that time is now known as Kanniyakudi. It is located near Tiruppunkur. There are many more stories and legends related to this sacred place. We are now going to look at the history of Nandanar's worship. We will see the history of Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar and Sundaramurti Swamigal singing here in the Puranam of Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar. Nandanar reached Tiruppunkur and arrived at the temple. "Sīrēṟum isai pāḍi tiruttoṇḍar tiruvāyil nērē kumpida vēṇḍum eṉa niṇaittārkku atu nērvar kārēṟum eyil puṇkūr kaṇṇudalār tirumuṉbu pōrēṟṟai vilaṅga aruḷ purindu aruḷip pulappaḍuttār" (The devotee, singing glorious music, desired to worship directly at the sacred gate. Having reached Tiruppunkur, Nandanar stood before the sacred place and sang songs praising the Lord with music. The music that praises the Lord is glorious music. All other music, without this quality of glory, is mere sensual pleasure that deludes souls and leads them into rebirth. Nandanar performed dance and song outside the temple. He worshipped the Lord by dancing and singing. He worshipped the Lord from outside the temple, in accordance with his caste tradition. The Rishabha Devar in Tiruppunkur was of immense size. Therefore, when Nandanar looked from outside the gate, he could not see Sivalokanathar, the Lord inside, directly. But Nandanar desired to see Him directly. However, he could not go inside due to his caste tradition. Nandanar wished to see Sivalokanathar inside directly and worship Him. Rishabha Devar stood in the way, obstructing his view.

Therefore, Lord Shiva, who grants whatever His devotees desire, graciously commanded the Rishabha Devar in front of Him to move aside so that Nandanar could have a darshan. Rishabha Devar moved a short distance from his place. Rishabha Devar moved aside. Sivalokanathar, who was inside, granted a direct darshan to Nandanar, who was at the outer gate. Tears of joy flowed from Nandanar's eyes. He folded his two sacred hands above his head, and with a melting heart, immersed in bliss, he worshipped the Lord, danced, and sang, saying, "Shankara Potri, Jaya Jaya Potri." As evidence of this event, even now in the Tiruppunkur temple, the very large Rishabha Devar in front of the Lord's sanctum is still slightly moved aside. Even now, those outside the temple can have a direct darshan of the Lord inside, as Rishabha Devar remains moved aside, making way. The Rishabha Devar in Shiva temples represents the soul. In the eighth chapter, 16th section, "Pati Pasu Pasam Verinmai" (The non-difference of Lord, Soul, and Bondage) of the Tirumantiram, composed by Tirumular Nayanar, the seventh verse is as follows: "Āya patitāṉ aruḷ Sivaliṅgamām. Āya pasuvum aḍal ēṟeṉa niṟkum. Āya palipīṭam āgum naṟpāsamām. Āya aṟaṉnilai āyndu koḷvārkaṭkē." (The Lord is indeed the gracious Sivalingam. The soul stands as the mighty bull. The sacrificial altar is indeed the good bondage. For those who understand the state of dharma.) This verse means that the Lingam in the sanctum sanctorum of Shiva temples is the Lord (Pati). The Rishabha (bull) in front of the Lingam is the soul (Pasu). Therefore, the spiritual truth that the darshan of the Lord (Pati) is attained only when the soul's (Pasu) animalistic nature (pasuttuvam) is removed is conveyed to us by Nandanar's Shiva darshan, which he received when Rishabha Devar moved aside. Now, the next verse composed by Sekkizhar Peruman. "Sivalōgam uḍaiyavar tam tiruvāyil muṉ niṉṟu pavalōgam kaḍappavar tam paṇiviṭṭu paṇiṇdeluṇdu suvalōḍu vār alaiya pōr vārpiṉ poru sūzhal avalōḍu maḍuttatu kaṇḍu ādarittu kuḷam toṭṭār." (Nandanar, who would transcend the world of birth, bowed and rose from before the sacred gate of Sivalokanathar. Seeing a low-lying area behind, suitable for a pond, he desired it and dug a pond.) Nandanar, who worshipped Sivalokanathar of Tiruppunkur, the possessor of Sivaloka, is referred to by Sekkizhar Peruman as "pavalōgam kaḍappavar" (one who transcends the world of birth). "Pavalōgam kaḍappavar" means one who strives to attain a state beyond birth, or one who stands in the path of Shiva. "Pavam" means birth. "Pavalōgam" means the world of birth. This means that Nandanar, who came with a consciousness cultivated towards Lord Shiva's sacred feet in previous births, performed Shiva service in a way that would prevent future births. Thus, remembering the Lord's grace that granted him a direct darshan of Sivalokanathar, he circumambulated the temple. At that time, he saw a low-lying area behind the temple. Therefore, he thought of digging it into a sacred pond. Generally, digging ponds and improving or creating water bodies for the benefit of all beings is one of the universal dharmas (pasu-dharma). But here, Nandanar thinks of digging a sacred pond for Lord Shiva's temple. This pond would be a temple pond and would be useful to Shiva's devotees. Therefore, this Shiva service is a pati-dharma (Lord's dharma). Therefore, Nandanar, with love for the Lord, with love for the Lord's devotees, and with the desire for the Lord's devotees to come and bathe, thought of digging a pond. And so, he created the pond. As mentioned above, Nandanar was one who performed service to the Lord without transgressing the limits of his tradition. Therefore, he considered this work of digging a pond outside the temple as Shiva service and performed it. Nandanar, having thus created the pond as he wished, circumambulated the temple, bowed, rose, danced with joy, took leave of the Lord, and returned to his village, Adhanur. Let us continue our contemplation. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 07 — TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 206, Mummaiyāl Ulagāṇḍa Sarukkam, TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar Puranam. Nandanar, having returned to Adhanur from Tiruppunkur, visited nearby sacred places such as Tirunintriyur, Tirunidur, and Tiruppullirukkuvelur (Vaithiswaran Koil), dancing and singing. The love to have a darshan of Tillai kept Nandanar awake at night, and that irresistible desire arose within his mind. But at dawn, his mind contemplated that thought. The desire he had to have a darshan of the sacred hall in Tillai would not be appropriate for his birth-caste and tradition, his intellect advised. Therefore, this advice suppressed that thought. Nandanar considered this suppression to be the Lord's command. We can recall here the sacred words, "Vēṇḍattakkatu aṟivōy nī vēṇḍa muzhuvatum taruvōy nī vēṇḍum parisu oṉṟu uṇḍu eṉṉil atuvum uṇdaṉ viruppam aṉṟē" (You know what is to be desired; you grant everything desired; if there is anything to be desired, that too is not your desire). Every night, the desire to go to Tillai would arise intensely. At dawn, he would think of his situation and his birth-caste tradition, and regret deeply that it was not proper to go to Tillai. Then, as his love intensified again, he would think and desire, "I will go tomorrow, I will go tomorrow." Thus, Nandanar's mind experienced many states: the resolve to go to Tillai, then the suppression of that resolve because it was not in harmony with his caste, and then again the resolve. Because Nandanar thought "I will go tomorrow, I will go tomorrow" every day, remembering the Lord, he was named "Nalaippovar" or "TirunAlaippOvAr." It is the nature of great people not to delay good deeds. The reason for this is their realization of the transient nature of this birth. This birth is very short-lived, like a pulai flower. Pulai is a type of plant with flowers. Since its life is very short, the pulai flower is used as a simile for our birth. Nandanar also resolved to see Tillai to end his bondage of birth. In accordance with "seeing Tillai brings liberation," Nandanar set out from his ancient village of Adhanur. He set out from Adhanur, his ancient birthplace, surrounded by areca palms with abundant spathe flowers, and reached near Tillai, surrounded by fields where vaalai fish leap and jump. As soon as he saw Tillai, as soon as he saw its boundary, he bowed to that sacred boundary and rose. It is a truth established by sacred texts that bowing to the boundary of Tillai removes rebirth. At the boundary of Tillai where Nandanar arrived, there were public sacrificial halls and places where Vedas were studied. To Nandanar, who stood at the boundary of Tillai and bowed to it, the smoke of sacrifices and the places where Vedas were chanted became visible. Seeing them, he feared to go near those sacred places where Vedas were chanted, thinking of his low caste. Therefore, he did not go further but stood at the boundary of Tillai. What Nandanar saw at the boundary of Tillai were the sacrificial halls where many Vedic sages and Brahmins together performed sacrifices. Sekkizhar Peruman is going to describe in the following verses that these sacrificial halls are places where everyone comes together to perform sacrifices. Moreover, the three fires called "mutti," which the Brahmins maintain daily in their homes, are also present in the homes of the Brahmins within the city of Tillai. Nandanar, thinking of his birth-caste, stood without entering Tillai, adhering to a rule that one should not go near sacrificial halls and places where Vedas are studied, having been born in such a caste. The rules regarding which professions are appropriate for which lineage, and how one should behave when members of one lineage interact or stay with members of another, and who should be where, who should go where, and who is allowed to go where, all these rules are prescribed in Agamas and Smritis. In those days, people followed these rules and lived according to them. They performed dharma by adhering to these traditional rules. Nandanar was one who performed dharma by adhering to these traditional rules. Therefore, he also thought in this manner. Nandanar's situation is different. Similarly, Kannappa Nayanar's situation is different. He had immense love for the Lord with the topknot, forgetting caste, lineage, and other distinctions. Therefore, we must carefully understand that his situation was different. Thus, Nandanar, who stood at the boundary of Tillai, thought that if he crossed this boundary and these walls outside Tillai and went inside, there would be three thousand Tillai Brahmins who maintain sacrificial fires in their homes. They say there would be three thousand sacrificial fires in three thousand homes. If this is the case, it is not at all appropriate for him to go inside. It would be very difficult, Nandanar feared. Therefore, outside the wall of Tillai, his uncontainable great love grew more and more, his heart melted, he folded his hands, and he circumambulated the sacred boundary of Tillai many times, standing there, deeply moved. Nandanar, having set out from Adhanur and reached the boundary of Tillai, is thus at the southern gate of Tillai. Sekkizhar has composed, "Māḷigaigaḷ tōṟum kulavum vēdigaigaḷ oṉṟiya āgutigaḷ mūvāyiram uḷ eṉpār" (They say there are three thousand sacrificial fires united with altars in every mansion). By "vēdigaigaḷ oṉṟiya āgutigaḷ" (sacrificial fires united with altars), it is explained here that the three fires of the perpetual fire (nitya agni) maintained in each Brahmin's household, along with the altar and pit for those three fires, are meant. A "vēdigai" is a small platform placed in front of the sacrificial pit for placing sacred pots and other items for worship. The three thousand Tillai Brahmins perform the agnikāryam (fire ritual) in their respective three thousand mansions, each individually. Thus, by performing individual agnikāryam in three thousand homes, three thousand offerings are made there. "Kaṭṟāṅgu eri ōmpi kaliyai vārāmē seṭṟār" (They nurtured the fire as learned and prevented Kali from coming) is from Tirugnanasambandar's Koil Tiruppadigam. Nandanar, when he was staying in Adhanur, would think of going to Tillai. Then he would change his mind. He would say, "I will go tomorrow," because of his caste. Then he would change his mind again. After all this, he finally resolved and now came to the boundary of Tillai. Even when he came to the boundary of Tillai, he saw the sacrificial halls, the Brahmin homes, and the places where Vedas were studied, and he was mentally disturbed, thinking it would be very difficult to go past all these and enter. That thought prevented him from going further into Tillai. Because of this hindrance, his love for the Lord grew more and more, becoming an unquenchable great love. It grew, it intensified, his heart melted. Therefore, he circumambulated the wall many times and stood at the very boundary of Tillai, worshipping. Because he could not go inside, Nandanar's mind was agitated and melted, and this great love that arose grew immeasurably and overflowed, becoming indescribable. Unable to go inside Tillai, and unable to leave Tillai, Nandanar circled around and around like ants around a pot of honey. Just as a calf circles around a house when its mother cow is kept inside and it is left outside, so too Nandanar, unable to go and see the Lord who was dancing in the sacred hall inside Tillai, and unable to leave that place, circled around the city of Tillai like a calf searching for its mother. Thus, he circumambulated Tillai day and night. He was disheartened, thinking repeatedly about the nature of this lowly birth that prevented him from entering Tillai. He slept in sorrow, wondering how he could worship the sacred dance in the sacred hall of Lord Shiva, who has a dark throat. He slept, thinking that this lowly birth, which causes suffering, is an impediment to having a darshan of the Lord. He calls this birth a lowly birth because it is an impediment to having a darshan of the Lord. Thus, he slept, thinking that this lowly birth, which causes suffering, is an impediment to having a darshan of the Lord. In this state, Ambalavanar, who dances in the sacred hall, knowing Nandanar's condition, graced Nandanar. To alleviate all the sorrows of that pure devotee Nandanar, Tillai Kuttapperuman appeared in his dream. He appeared with a gentle smile and graced him. The Lord appeared in Nandanar's dream and told Nandanar, "So that this birth may cease, you shall plunge into the fire and come before Me with the Brahmins wearing the sacred thread." Nandanar, thinking this lowly birth was an impediment, had fallen asleep exhausted. Therefore, the Lord, realizing that Nandanar's sorrows would not cease unless his thought was removed, graced him thus to remove it. "You plunge into the fire and come. And when you come after plunging into the fire, come to Me with the Brahmins wearing the sacred thread." That is why the Lord graced him thus. The Lord's intention here was to alleviate the sorrow of Nandanar, the devotee who was distressed and slept, unable to worship the Lord dancing in the sacred hall of Tillai. The phrases "ippiravi pōy nīṅga" (so that this birth may cease) and "eriyiṉ kaṇ im māyap poyttakaiyum oru vazhittu" (this illusory false body will also be removed in the fire) imply that Nandanar's previous body ceased to exist, and his body was renewed as a meritorious body. That is why, after "ippiravi pōy nīṅga," Nandanar rose from the fire in the form of a sage, with the sacred thread shining on his chest and matted locks on his head. Therefore, the Lord did not say this to confirm that there is highness, lowness, or inferiority in caste. The thought about his birth was in Nandanar's mind. Therefore, the Lord's intention was to change that thought. Therefore, it is very wrong to make new explanations that the Lord spoke of highness or lowness in castes. However, this should not be interpreted to mean that there are no distinctions in castes, lineages, and other categories. Caste, lineage, and other distinctions are inherent realities. Saiva Siddhanta states that the fruits of our prarabdha karma (destiny) come to us through three things: caste, lifespan, and experience (bhoga). These are prescribed by the Agamas and other scriptures given by the Lord, and by the rules of Tamil grammar. It is our scripture that people, by following these prescribed rules, will gradually attain a higher state that transcends these rules. Therefore, those who make new researches, saying that the Lord smiled with a hint of disdain, seeing Nandanar's state of mind, who performed service according to the rules of the traditions He Himself established, are making erroneous researches, not correct ones. The Lord's gentle smile (punmuruvāl) is the natural smile that arises to bestow grace. "Nāyakaṉ tiruvuḷḷattu magizhndē aṅgaṇ eytiya muṟuvalum tōṇṭa" (A smile appeared on the Lord's joyful sacred face) is from Tirukkuṟippu Toṇḍar Puranam. "Tirumuga muṟuval seyya oṉṟiya viḷaiyāṭṭōrār" (They did not know the unified play when a smile appeared on His sacred face) and "aruḷ pozhiyum tirumugattil aṇi muṟuval" (a beautiful smile on the grace-showering sacred face) are some of the places where the Lord's gentle smiles are mentioned. Appar Swamigal sings, "Siritta mugam kaṇḍa kaṇ koṇḍu maṟṟu iṉi kāṇpatu eṉṉē" (Having seen the smiling face, what else is there to see?) desiring to have the bliss of seeing this sweet gentle smile of the Lord. Appar Swamigal sings that after seeing the Lord's gentle smile, what else can one see besides that? Let us continue our contemplation. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 08 — TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 207, Mummaiyāl Ulagāṇḍa Sarukkam, TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar Puranam. The Lord says, "This birth, which you consider lowly, and which you believe is an impediment to worshipping the dance in the sacred hall, shall cease. Plunge into the fire and come forth." We should recall here that Appar Swamigal, to purify his body after being in the Jain religion, prayed to the Lord to brand him with the trident and bull, saying, "Puṉṉeṟiyām amaṇ samayattoḍakkuṇḍu pōṇda uḍal taṉṉuḍaṉ uyir vāzha taraiyē nāṉ taripatataṉukku eṉṉuḍaiya nāyaka niṉ ilacsiṉai iṭṭu aruḷ" (This body, entangled in the evil Jain religion, let me live on earth with it, my Lord, grace me by branding me with your mark). This is because the command to kindle the fire and have Nandanar enter it was conveyed by the Lord to the Tillai Brahmins in their dreams. But they feared what the citizens, the king, and others, who did not know the greatness of this being the Lord's command and Nandanar's greatness, would say. Later, in the same Tillai, Umapati Sivachariar, having received a written command from Nataraja Peruman, initiated and granted liberation to Pettan Samban. But they feared what the other people and kings, who did not know this, would think. Then, love overcame all that fear. Therefore, with great love for Nandanar, all the Tillai Brahmins came to Nandanar, the devotee. The three thousand Tillai Brahmins came to Nandanar and spoke as follows. Sekkizhar Peruman composed the following verse: "Ayyarē Ambalavar aruḷāl ippozhutu aṇaiṇdōm veyya azhal amaittu umakku tara vēṇḍi eṉa viḷamba naiyum maṉa tiruttoṇḍar nāṉ uyṇdēṉ eṉa tozhutār. Now, the commentary on this verse. The three thousand Tillai Brahmins came and addressed Nandanar as "Ayyarē." "Ayyarē" means "O great one." Therefore, "Ayyar" is a term used to mean a great person. It is a recent custom for this to be used as a caste name. In ancient times, "Ayyar" was used to mean a great person. It is a later custom for it to be used as a caste name, not just for greatness, but as a conventional term. We can also recall here that Aludai Pillaiyar (Tirugnanasambandar) addressed Tirunilakanta Yazhppanar as "Ayyar nīr uḷam magizha iṅgaṇainda uruti uḍaiyōm" (O Ayyar, we are firm in our resolve to come here to make your heart rejoice) and "Ayyar nīr avatariṭṭiḍa ippatiṉ aḷaviṉ māṭavam muṉbu seyṭavāṟu" (O Ayyar, how much great penance this place performed before you were born) and "Ayyar nīr yāzh itaṉai muṟikkum" (O Ayyar, you will break this yazh). "Ai" is an exclamation of wonder, according to Tolkappiyam. "Ayyar" means one who is worthy of wonder. Therefore, Sivakavimaṇi Ayya writes that one should not introduce new researches into this story that are inconsistent with the conclusions of spiritual texts, thereby causing trouble and committing sacrilege. Therefore, the Tillai Brahmins also addressed Nandanar, the pure and great devotee, as "Ayyarē." Addressing him thus, they said, "Ayyarē, you are a great person. But we did not realize your greatness. Ambalavanar has graciously revealed it to us. Therefore, we have now had the opportunity to see you." They said this with the implication that they have now come, realizing it because the Lord revealed it. If not for the Lord's grace, they would not have been able to come even now. They said they have come to kindle the fire as the Lord desired. That fire is the fire desired by the Lord. Therefore, it is also the fire desired by you. Fire is something that others would consider heat and avoid with fear. But that fire is something that you desire to enter. Furthermore, it is our tradition to refer to birth as heat. The fire that helps to alleviate the heat of such a birth is also fire. Just as poison is countered by poison, and the dirt of cloth is removed by another dirt, which is alkaline earth, it seems the Lord intended to remove the dirt of low birth from Nandanar's heart with fire. Therefore, the Tillai Brahmins said that they have come to prepare the fire as per the Lord's command. The Lord could have directly informed Nandanar alone to prepare the fire and enter it. But the Lord informed the Tillai Brahmins and had them prepare the fire. Why? Because Nandanar had the consciousness of his birth. To make Nandanar realize that he would come with a meritorious body, having shed his previous body, between the sacrificial halls, Vedic schools, and three thousand offerings, for Nandanar, who grieved how he could worship the Lord dancing in the hall, and to enable the devotees and the rest of the world to witness the power of grace and the power of his love, the Lord commanded the Tillai Brahmins to prepare the fire. Thus, Nandanar, hearing the announcement from the Tillai Brahmins, felt his mind melt with the emotion that he, a very humble and simple person, had received a great blessing. "Ittaṉaiyum emparo Ayya Ayya Emperumān tirukkaruṇai iruṇdavārē" (All this, O Lord, O Lord, how great is the grace of our Lord!) – thinking thus, his mind melted and softened. Nandanar says, "I am saved," with the conviction that he has received the great blessing of shedding this lowly birth, attaining a good body, and worshipping the Lord dancing in the hall, surrounded by the Tillai Brahmins. Furthermore, he says, "I am saved," having seen the Brahmins come in reality to fulfill the Lord's grace that he had seen in his dream. Thus, having received Nandanar's consent, the Tillai Brahmins prepare the fire as commanded by the Lord. They then inform Nandanar of this. When one sees the Lord's gracious intention and a soul ready to receive that grace, the action of the Acharya is to perform the appropriate ritual and unite that soul with the Lord's feet. That action of the Acharya is taking place here through the Tillai Brahmins. Furthermore, we must remember that Nataraja Peruman is also one of the Tillai Brahmins. Let us continue our contemplation. Chapter 09 — TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 208, Mummaiyāl Ulagāṇḍa Sarukkam, TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar Puranam. As commanded by the Lord, the Tillai Brahmins, having obtained Nandanar's consent, prepared the fire outside the southern wall of Tillai, in front of the sacred gate. The place where the Tillai Brahmins prepared this fire is now a sacred pond called Omakulam. We can understand that this pond is the place where the Tillai Brahmins prepared the fire, and that in ancient times, the outer wall of Tillai and its fortifications, along with a sacred gate, existed on the southern side of Tillai, near this place. Sekkizhar Peruman composes the verse: "Kai tozhudu naṭamāḍum kazhal uṉṉi azhal pukkār eytiya appozhutiṉ kaṇ eriyiṉ kaṇ im māyap poyttakaiyum uru ozhittu puṇṇiya māmuṉi vaḍivāy mey tigazh veṇṇūl viḷaṅga vēṇi muḍi koṇḍu ezhundār." (Nandanar, folding his hands, remembering the dancing feet, entered the fire. At that very moment, in the fire, he cast off this illusory false form and rose in the form of a meritorious great sage, with the sacred white thread shining on his chest and matted locks on his head.) Nandanar, folding his hands, remembering the sacred feet of the Lord who performs the five divine functions, entered the fire. At that very moment of entering, in the fire, he cast off this illusory false form, which is a product of maya, and rose in the form of a meritorious sage, with the sacred white thread shining on his chest and matted locks on his head. This body is an illusory false body, a product of maya. What is that falsehood? It is the body that is prone to pride in caste, lineage, and birth, and is also impermanent. Therefore, it is an illusory false body. Nandanar cast off that body, which he considered a lowly birth, a pulai form. Nandanar, having cast off that false body in the fire, rose from the fire, having obtained a true body, the form of a meritorious sage. How did he rise? With the three-stranded white sacred thread shining on his chest and matted locks on his head, which are the excellent symbols of Saivism, he rose from the fire. Sekkizhar Peruman composes the next verse: "Seṇtī mēl ezhum pozhutu seṇmalar mēl vaṇd ezhuṇda aṇdaṇaṉ pōl tōṇḍiṇār. Aṇdara tuṇdupi nādam vaṇd ezhuṇdatu uyar visumbil. Vāṉavarkaḷ magizhndu ārtu paiṇduṇar maṇdārattiṉ paṇimalar māṟigaḷ pozhiṇdār." (As he rose from the red fire, he appeared like the Brahmin who rose from the red lotus. The sound of celestial drums arose in the high sky. The celestials rejoiced and showered fragrant Mandara flowers.) Thus, Nandanar, having entered the red fire and risen as a meritorious sage, appeared like Brahma, seated on a red lotus flower. At that time, the sound of celestial drums arose in the vast sky. The celestials rejoiced and showered Mandara flowers. The red fire is compared to a red lotus, and Nandanar, who rose as a meritorious sage from that fire, is compared to Brahma, the Brahmin seated on a red lotus. Brahma is said to have emerged from the red lotus that arose from Vishnu's navel during creation. This is the scriptural tradition that Brahma emerges from Vishnu's navel during creation. Sekkizhar Peruman uses this simile here to explain that Nandanar, having cast off the illusory false form of low birth, appears in the form of a sage by the grace of Lord Shiva. This simile is used to illustrate that Nandanar shone like Brahma, who comes forth to create the world during creation. Furthermore, Sekkizhar Peruman sings that when he rose from the fire, he appeared like Brahma. He does not use this simile when he goes to worship the Lord dancing in the hall. This implies that even Brahma is not equal to him. The Tillai Brahmins, who were blessed, seeing Nandanar thus rise from the fire as a meritorious sage, folded their hands in worship. After the Lord had graced them in their dream, when they saw Nandanar outside the wall, a fear arose in the Tillai Brahmins, wondering what this was. The Brahmins stood bewildered, not knowing what to do. Now, seeing the wonder of the Lord's manifestation of grace, they folded their hands. All the devotees fell down and prostrated, immersed in great joy. The Vedic sage, TirunAlaippOvAr, comes to worship the sacred golden feet that perform the great dance of grace in the sacred hall, which is surrounded by the chanting of the Vedas in Tillai. He comes with eagerness, his inner desire to worship those sacred golden feet, which he had wished to worship for many days when he lived in Adhanur, now about to be fulfilled. Nandanar, who had previously said, "I will go tomorrow, I will go tomorrow," referring to a future event, now comes, knowing that that future event is about to be realized in the present. He comes accompanied by the three thousand Tillai Brahmins. Nandanar, TirunAlaippOvAr, goes directly, without any hindrance, not expecting anything else, now that the lowly birth, which he considered an impediment to worshipping the Lord dancing in the hall, has ceased. The Brahmins also followed him. For those coming from Adhanur, the first thing they see is the southern gopuram. Therefore, TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar crosses the southern wall and comes to the southern gopuram. He enters through the southern gopuram. It will be understood later that Tirugnanasambandar also came through the southern gopuram of this temple. TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar enters through the southern gopuram. Like the force with which water, long dammed and imprisoned, bursts forth when the embankment breaks, so too TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar entered, with the intensity of his desire, which had been suppressed by the thought of his lowly birth, now rising to its peak. After crossing the sacred gate and the gopuram, one should circumambulate the sacred street, and then worship the mansions and the Perambalam (great hall). The method of worship is to cross the inner sacred gate and then see the Nataraja in front of the Chidambaram (Chitrambalam). We must understand that our great Acharyas (spiritual teachers) have shown this method of darshan. Without that, TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar, after bowing to the gopuram and crossing it, quickly went to see the dancing limit. This indicates his intensity. For TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar, whose birth had ceased as the Lord had graced him, "ippiravi pōy nīṅga eriyiṉ iḍai nī mūzhgi muppuri nūl mārpuḍaṉ muṉ aṇaiyāy" (so that this birth may cease, you plunge into the fire and come before me with the sacred thread on your chest), only attaining the Lord remained. Therefore, TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar quickly went and merged. He reached the sacred feet of Chidambaram, which is the dancing limit, and merged completely into the pervasive grace, disappearing. All those who accompanied him saw him enter through the gopuram. They could not see where he went after entering. They became unable to see TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar, who had merged completely with the Lord. Thus, when TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar crossed the gopuram and entered, and then disappeared, the Brahmins were amazed. The great sages praised him. Ambalavanar, the endless blissful great dancer, graced TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar, the devotee who had come to Him, to remain worshipping His beautiful lotus-like feet, having removed the impurities of karma. Seeing the wonder of "aruḷum mey neṟi poy neṟi nīkkiya atisayam kaṇḍōmē" (we have seen the wonder that removed the false path and revealed the true path of grace) from Tiruvāsakam, those who saw TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar's form disappear, unable to be seen, experienced an indescribable sense of wonder. That is what is called a wonder. "Nalaippovar" (one who will go tomorrow) was a future event. "Varugiṇṟār" (he is coming) became a present event earlier. That too has now reached its complete fulfillment. Therefore, the poetic beauty with which Sekkizhar Peruman now sings "vaṇdaṇainda tiruttoṇḍar" (the devotee who has come and reached) is worthy of our praise. The Lord is a sentient being, and the soul is also a sentient being. Therefore, they can unite with each other, which is a principle of Saiva Siddhanta. "Aṇaindōr taṉmai" (the nature of those who have united) is a scriptural truth. The Brahmins were amazed. The great sages praised. Sekkizhar Peruman composed the verse: "Vaṇdaṇainda tiruttoṇḍar tammai viṉai māsu aṟuttu suṇdarattāmarai puraiyum tuṇai aḍikaḷ tozhutirukka aṇdamillā āṇanda peruṅkūttar aruḷ purindār." (The great blissful dancer, who is endless, graced the devotee who had come and reached Him, to remain worshipping His beautiful lotus-like feet, having removed the impurities of karma.) Sekkizhar Peruman sings about the Lord accepting Nandanar, TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar, as "vaṇdaṇainda tiruttoṇḍar tammai viṉai māsu aṟuttu." We should interpret "viṉai māsu aṟuttu" as "removing karma and impurity." The karma mentioned here is the two karmas, which is the karma mala (impurity of action). Here, it refers to the mūla karma (root karma). In "viṉai māsu aṟuttu," "viṉai" is the mūla karma. "Māsu" is the āṇava mala (impurity of ego). "Viṉai aṟuttu" should be understood as removing the mūla karma. "Māsu aṟuttu" should be understood as overpowering the āṇava mala. Āṇavam, kaṇmam, māyai are the three impurities. We can see that the māyā mala (impurity of illusion) was removed in the verse "im māyap poyttakaiyum oru ozhittu" (this illusory false body was also removed), where Nandanar, TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar, shed his old body and rose in the form of a meritorious great sage. Here, it is stated how the other two impurities were destroyed and removed. Thus, because all three impurities were removed and the removal of bondage (pāsa nīkkam) occurred, the great dancer, the Lord, graced TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar with His beautiful lotus-like feet. He is the endless blissful great dancer. As a concluding remark to this story, Sivakavimaṇi Ayya writes as follows: Many people are doing new researches into this story of Nandanar. Many new thoughts and movements regarding caste, lineage, tradition, and customs are arising in these days, causing conflict. We should not introduce these into this story and cause trouble. We should not confuse it. We cannot deny that among human beings, there are always high and low statuses based on birth and distinction, according to time and place. This is also ordained by divine texts. It is also known from human experience. Due to differences in karma and maturity, people and other beings are indeed of various kinds. This difference seen among people cannot be changed. These differences are included in caste, lifespan, and experience (bhoga). It is a truth discovered by spiritual texts that these prarabdha karmas come to us as ordained by the Lord, according to the karma of each soul. But it is also defined where this caste should stand. It is our duty to follow the rules prescribed in the dharma texts until one reaches the state where one has no actions of their own and stands as Shiva. When the state comes where self-awareness, also called soul-consciousness, falls away like a possession from a sleeping person's hand, then these states of caste and others also fall away and cease to exist. For Kannappa Nayanar, self-awareness ceased as soon as he saw the Lord's feet. After that, he transcended the state of caste-based conduct. Here, Nandanar's self-awareness was in his memory. The state of removing it would come only after removing that body. Therefore, to destroy his self-awareness, which is soul-consciousness, the Lord destroyed the body he possessed and graced him to rise from the fire, granting him a meritorious body. Caste, lineage, and other distinctions should be understood and followed only to the extent necessary. We should not allow them to become a source of pride, arrogance, and ego. This is emphasized by the Tiruvāsakam, "Sāti, kulam, piṟappu eṉṉum suzhippaṭṭu taḍumāṟum ātamili nāyēṉai" (me, a dog without self-control, who struggles caught in the whirlpool of caste, lineage, and birth). As a result of new researches, some new people have come forward, claiming that Sekkizhar Swamigal did not compose the part where TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar plunged into the fire and rose, and that these are later interpolations, and they try to find a place for their own theories by rejecting these parts. Many people from various castes became Nayanars and were praised by Sundaramurti Swamigal. Therefore, some conclude that there is no caste discrimination among people. Others claim that the Lord commanded Nandanar to plunge into the fire to show the true nature of the Tillai Brahmins, whom they consider to be hard-hearted people who, filled with caste arrogance, would not let anyone near them and kept them outside. These new researchers are conducting many new researches. TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar, from the time the thought of Tillai came to him in Adhanur, feared that it was not proper for him to enter Tillai, thinking of his caste, and remained outside. It was Nandanar himself who stood thus. No one prevented Nandanar from approaching the Lord. Similarly, no one told Kannappa Nayanar to come near the Lord and offer Him food. Furthermore, the Lord, who held Kannappa Nayanar and Tirukkuṟippu Toṇḍar by hand even without their sacred threads, brought our Nandanar near Him, making him a Vedic sage with a sacred thread and matted locks. This is because Nandanar's state of mind and conduct, who feared and lived by the thought that only if he were a Vedic sage or a Brahmin could he go near the Lord, was the reason for the Lord's grace.

Therefore, the Lord, as Nandanar wished, made him a Vedic sage with a sacred thread on his chest and graced him to come to Him. Therefore, we must identify all those who conduct new researches in this story that are inconsistent with the conclusions of spiritual texts and reject them. We should not listen to their opinions and commit sacrilege, writes Sivakavimaṇi Ayya. Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman, stating that TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar, who plunged into the fire to shed his impure body and rose as a faultless Vedic sage, attained the sacred feet of Ambalavanar, and that he will now narrate the nature of the sacred service of Tirukkuṟippu Toṇḍar Nayanar with the help of the Lord's glorious sacred feet, concludes the Puranam of Nandanar, TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar, and begins the Puranam of Tirukkuṟippu Toṇḍar Nayanar. Let us also continue our contemplation. Praise to the sacred feet of TirunAlaippOvAr Nayanar. Praise to the sacred feet of Sundaramurti Swamigal. Praise to the sacred feet of Nambiāṇḍār Nambigal. Praise to the sacred feet of Sekkizhar Peruman. Praise to the sacred feet of Sivakavimaṇi Ayya. Thiruchitrambalam.
