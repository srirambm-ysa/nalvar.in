Chapter 01 — IyarPakai Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. In the Periya Puranam, in the section called Thillai Vazh Andanar Sarukkam, after contemplating the section on Thillai Vazh Andanar Puranam and then the Thiruneelakanda Nayanar Puranam, we are now about to enter the IyarPakai Nayanar Puranam. First, let us briefly look at the history of IyarPakai Nayanar, and then we can contemplate it in great detail and subtlety through Sekkizhar’s path. Now, a summary of IyarPakai Nayanar’s history. In the Chola country, where the Kaveri river merges with the sea, there is a special holy bathing place called Kaveri Sangamam. The town located at this confluence of the Kaveri river with the sea is Kaveri Poompattinam. Here, in the Vaishya lineage, lived a devotee of Shiva named IyarPakaiyar, who was excellent in devotion to Guru, Lingam, and Sangamam, and possessed great generosity. He led a householder’s life. IyarPakai Nayanar was a great lover who joyfully gave everything desired by Shiva devotees adorned with Vibhuti and Rudraksha. One day, Lord Shiva, the Lord of Kailasa, in order to make known to all beings this generosity of IyarPakai Nayanar – that is, his quality of never refusing anything desired by Shiva devotees – took the form of a Brahmin, with Vibhuti shining on his sacred body, and with a guise of a dissolute person, came to IyarPakai Nayanar’s house. IyarPakai Nayanar lovingly welcomed that Shiva devotee, prostrated before him, led him inside, performed worship according to custom, and stood praising him, saying, "Swami, your presence here is due to the penance I performed in my previous birth." Hearing this, the Brahmin said to IyarPakai Nayanar, "I have heard of your quality of joyfully giving whatever Shiva devotees desire without refusing, and today I have come here desiring something from you. If you agree to give that thing, if you joyfully agree, I will tell you what that thing is." To this, IyarPakai Nayanar replied, "Whatever kind of thing you ask for, if that thing is with me, then that thing is not mine. It belongs exclusively to the devotees of the Lord. Therefore, do not doubt this. Please tell me what your heart desires." The Brahmin who had come then said, "I have come desiring your wife." At that, IyarPakai Nayanar was even more delighted than before, bowed, and said, "Swami, you have asked for something that is already mine. This is a blessing I have received." He then went inside the house. There, he addressed his chaste wife, saying, "Today I have given you to this Shiva devotee." Immediately, his wife was distressed. Then, regaining her composure, she said, "My beloved Lord, whatever you command, I will do that. Is there any other duty for me? There is no other duty," and she bowed to IyarPakai Nayanar. Immediately, IyarPakaiyar’s wife bowed before the Brahmin Shiva devotee and stood by his side. Thus, the Nayanar, delighted at having given his wife, asked the Brahmin, "What further service should I perform?" To this, the Shiva devotee, who had received IyarPakai Nayanar's wife, said, "As I take this woman alone, you must accompany me so that no obstruction arises from your relatives or the people of your town." To this, IyarPakai Nayanar said, "It is my mistake that I delayed until you told me to do this task which I should have done myself." He immediately went inside the house, put on a breastplate, and, carrying a sword and shield, set out valiantly. He let the Shiva devotee and the lady go ahead, and he followed behind as their escort. Hearing this news, the relatives and townspeople wondered, "Why did IyarPakai do this? Is he mad? Even if he is mad, should someone take his wife?" In order to protect their town and lineage from disgrace, all the relatives, armed with spears, swords, and daggers, intercepted the Shiva devotee and said, "Do not go! Leave this woman, who is the lamp of our lineage, here and go!" The Shiva devotee looked at the lady as if frightened. The lady said, "Lord, do not be afraid. IyarPakai will defeat them." Hearing this, IyarPakaiyar said, "I will stand alone and defeat all of them. Do not falter," to the Shiva devotee. He then became extremely angry with the relatives who had come to oppose them and warned them, "All of you, run away from here! Otherwise, you will fall victim to my sword!" To this, the relatives replied, "Why do you speak so shamelessly? You gave your wife to that Brahmin, and now you speak so bravely? Even if we die, we don't care. We will not agree to you giving your wife." IyarPakaiyar became extremely angry and rose, saying, "I will defeat all of you and send you to heaven, and then I will let this ascetic go." The relatives, however, tried to attack the Brahmin. IyarPakaiyar, in great anger, cut down the opposing relatives and townspeople with his sword, sending them to heaven. Only those who managed to escape survived. The others fell victim to IyarPakai Nayanar's sword and died. Now, IyarPakai Nayanar said to the Brahmin, "Cross this grove without fear. I will accompany you." So, the Brahmin went ahead, followed by the lady, and then IyarPakai Nayanar behind them. They crossed the grove and reached the sacred place called Thiruchaikkadu. As soon as they reached Thiruchaikkadu, which was the boundary of Kaveripoompattinam, the Brahmin said to IyarPakaiyar, "You may now go back." IyarPakai Nayanar bowed at the Brahmin's feet, rejoiced, saying, "I have received your divine grace," and turned back. As IyarPakai Nayanar was returning, the Brahmin rejoiced, thinking, "He has a mind completely devoid of falsehood. He is going back without even looking behind." He then called IyarPakai Nayanar again, crying out, "IyarPakai! Come quickly! Run quickly!" Hearing the Brahmin's cry, IyarPakai Nayanar rushed back, saying, "Here I am, I have come. If there is any more trouble, tell me. I will cut them down with my sword too." At that moment, the Brahmin disappeared. The Brahmin who had come was not to be seen. He saw only the lady. Then, in the sky, Lord Shiva appeared on his bull, with Goddess Uma. Seeing the vision of the Lord and Goddess mounted on the bull, IyarPakai Nayanar fell and rose, praising them many times. He sang "Pottri Pottri" (Praise, Praise). He melted with emotion, remembering the great grace the Lord had bestowed upon him. The Lord addressed IyarPakai Nayanar, saying, "IyarPakai, you who have unblemished love for us and for our devotees, come with your wife and join us!" and then disappeared. IyarPakai Nayanar and his wife reached Shiva Loka and lived experiencing supreme bliss. The relatives and townspeople who had fought with IyarPakai Nayanar and died in that battle also reached the celestial world, Devaloka, and experienced joy. This is the sacred history of IyarPakai Nayanar. "I am a servant to IyarPakai who never says 'no'" is from Thiruthondathogai. Jaffna Arumuga Navalar explains this history as follows: Lord Shiva, who is the embodiment of compassion, bestows the supreme bliss of liberation upon true devotees who understand Him and love Him incessantly. True devotees of Shiva, who possess true understanding, consider Lord Shiva, who is the life of their lives, to be dearer to them than their wife, children, and other living attachments, and also dearer than their house, gold, and other material attachments. They have true love only for Lord Shiva. Such devotees, even though they are involved in worldly experiences with things like wife, children, house, and gold, remain detached from them like water on a lotus leaf, and have incessant true love for Shiva. They worship Shiva devotees and the Shiva Lingam as Shiva himself. Although Lord Shiva is omnipresent, He manifests clearly in the Shiva Lingam and among Shiva devotees, like ghee in curd, and remains hidden in other places, like ghee in milk. In all his previous births, IyarPakai Nayanar performed countless acts of Shiva Punya (meritorious deeds for Shiva) without expecting any reward. In this birth, he was born as IyarPakai Nayanar and lived without the egoistic sense of "I" and "mine." He considered Lord Shiva alone as his sole relative and worshipped the Shiva Lingam and Shiva devotees as Shiva himself. Generally, people in the world experience greater joy when their loved ones enjoy something than when they themselves enjoy it. For example, there is a father who has immense love for his child. Sweets are very dear to him. Similarly, his child also loves sweets very much. So, what will this father, who has immeasurable love for his child, do? When he gets a sweet, instead of eating it himself and enjoying it, he will give it to his beloved child. Seeing the joy the child experiences, he will feel greater happiness than he would have by eating the sweet himself. Similarly, IyarPakai Nayanar's mind was consumed by an intense, growing love for Shiva devotees, whom he saw as Shiva himself. Therefore, he found greater joy in seeing Shiva devotees experience his possessions than in experiencing them himself. That is why, when any devotee asked for any of his possessions, whether small or large, he would give them with unwavering, genuine joy, excelling in generosity. Lord Shiva, the ocean of grace, desired to make this true love of IyarPakai Nayanar known to all souls. So, He came to IyarPakai Nayanar's house in the guise of a Shiva devotee. Among worldly possessions, there is nothing dearer to souls than a wife. Therefore, Lord Shiva, who came in the guise of a Shiva devotee, asked IyarPakai Nayanar for his wife. IyarPakai Nayanar, with great joy, gave his wife, who was renowned for her chastity, without hesitation. That is why Lord Shiva appeared and praised IyarPakai Nayanar as "dear to me." IyarPakai Nayanar was highly respected by the world due to his noble birth and great wealth. He did not even care about the disgrace that might come upon him from the world if he gave his wife to another in this manner. His mind was completely consumed by devotion to Shiva. Desiring another's wife is a sin explained in the Shiva Agamas. IyarPakai Nayanar also knew this. However, when a Shiva devotee asked for his wife in this way, upon seeing the Shiva guise adorned with Vibhuti and Rudraksha, IyarPakai Nayanar, immersed in Shiva devotion, considered it as seeing Shiva himself and performed devotion without scrutinizing the qualities or faults of that Shiva devotee. This highly advanced state of not scrutinizing the qualities or faults of devotees comes only to those with intense Shiva devotion. That is why IyarPakai Nayanar is praised by Paramashiva, the Lord of all worlds, as a brave one who performed an impossible act. Let us now continue to contemplate the sacred history of IyarPakai Nayanar in detail through Sekkizhar's Periya Puranam verses. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 02 — IyarPakai Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. In the Periya Puranam, in the second chapter, Thillai Vazh Andanar Sarukkam, in the section on Thillai Vazh Andanar Puranam, we contemplated the collective devotees known as Thillai Vazh Andanars. Secondly, in the section on Thiruneelakanda Nayanar Puranam, we contemplated the sacred history of Thiruneelakanda Kuyavanar. Thirdly, IyarPakai Nayanar Puranam. In the Thiruthondathogai composed by Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, the line about this Nayanar is "Illaiye ennaatha IyarPakai-kkum adiyen" (I am a servant to IyarPakai who never says 'no'). "Thillai Vazh Andanar-tham adiyarkkum adiyen, Thiruneelakandathu Kuyavanarkku adiyen, Illaiye ennaatha IyarPakai-kkum adiyen" are the first three lines of the Thiruthondathogai. Among these, the third Nayanar is IyarPakai Nayanar. His Puranam is presented as the third Puranam under the name IyarPakai Nayanar Puranam. "Illaiye ennaatha IyarPakai-kkum adiyen" is the line about this Nayanar from the Thiruthondathogai composed by Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Now, there is a verse about this Nayanar in the Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi composed by Nambiyandar Nambi. Let us also look at that. The Thiruthondathogai composed by Sundaramoorthy Swamigal is a collection (Thogai Nool), and the Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi composed by Nambiyandar Nambi is a classification (Vagai Nool), and the Periya Puranam is an elaborate work (Viri Nool). Therefore, "Illaiye ennaatha IyarPakai-kkum adiyen" is from Thiruthondathogai. Now, let us contemplate the verse about this Nayanar in the classification work, Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi by Nambiyandar Nambi, and then we can proceed to the elaborate work, the Periya Puranam. In the classification work, Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi, the verse about the Nayanar is as follows: "Sei thavar vendiyathu yaathum koduppa Sivan thavanai kai thavam pesi nin kaathaliyai tharuga enrathume mai thigazh kanniyai eenathavan vaayntha perum pugazh vanthethiya Kaveri Poompattinathtul IyarPakaiye" (IyarPakai of Kaveri Poompattinam, who, when a Shiva ascetic, speaking deceitfully, asked for his beloved dark-eyed maiden, gave her, thereby attaining great fame). Now, let us contemplate the elaborate work, the Periya Puranam. IyarPakai Nayanar Puranam is the section that describes the history and qualities of the Nayanar named IyarPakai. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal's mention of "Illaiye ennaatha IyarPakai" (IyarPakai who never says 'no') means that if Shiva's devotees ask for anything he possesses, he is a Nayanar who will not say 'no' to that thing. This is the inner meaning of this Puranam. Now, we can begin to contemplate the Nayanar's Puranam in the Periya Puranam. The Chola king Anapaya Chola Chakravarti was the reason for the creation of the Periya Puranam. Anapaya Chola Chakravarti, who was the reason for Sekkizhar to compose the Periya Puranam, ruled with a white umbrella and a righteous scepter. The city where his ancestors, the Chola kings, ruled was Kaveri Poompattinam. Currently, it is known as Poompuhar. IyarPakai Nayanar was a Nayanar who lived in this Kaveri Poompattinam, also known as Poompuhar, in ancient times. Sekkizhar Swamigal here praises the rule of Anapaya Chola, who supported him, and sings that Kaveri Poompattinam was the town where his ancestors lived. In order to express gratitude for having this Periya Puranam sung, Sekkizhar Swamigal mentions Anapaya Chola Chakravarti here and there in the Periya Puranam, expressing his gratitude. This place is also one such instance. The northern scholars call this expression of gratitude "Upakara Smriti." Kaveri Poompattinam, also known as Poompuhar, is a city in the Chola country, made fertile by the flowing Ponni river, Kaveri. The Kaveri river branches into many tributaries, and from these tributaries, many streams, rivulets, and canals branch off, thereby enriching the entire Chola country. Therefore, for the people of the Chola country, even though the earth is their mother, Sekkizhar Peruman has already praised the Kaveri river as nurturing and cherishing them like a foster mother. It is appropriate to recall that here. Furthermore, the Kaveri river is a river that possesses the wisdom to bestow charity, considering time, place, and suitability. Therefore, the Kaveri river, branching into various streams, fertilizes the fields and then the remaining water flows into the sea. If too much water accumulates in the fields, it harms the crops. Therefore, only the excess floodwaters of the Kaveri flow into the sea. Thus, after doing immense good to all people, the Kaveri river merges with the sea with its remaining water. According to "Nadeenam Sagaro Gathiki" (the ocean is the destination of rivers), it finally merges with the sea. The ocean is the source of all holy waters. Therefore, according to the rule that what originates from where it dissolves, the ocean water is the source of all holy waters, just as rivers ultimately merge with the sea. The city of Kaveri Poompattinam is located where the Kaveri river merges with the sea. By the Kaveri river merging with the sea, the sea becomes sacred here. According to Puranic history, as a curse removal for the sin that came upon Indra due to his association with Vritrasura, Indra's sin was divided into four parts. One part came as foam in the water. Naturally, there is foam in seawater. Moreover, seawater is unfit for drinking and bathing due to its salinity. Therefore, it is our tradition to bathe in the sea only during certain auspicious times. "Masi Kadalattu Kandan Kapalichcharam Amarndhan" (He who saw the Masi sea bath and resided in Kapalichcharam) is from Thirugnanasambandar's Thevaram. Thus, it was our tradition to bathe in the sea only on auspicious days, including the Masi sea bath. However, here in Kaveri Poompattinam, since the Kaveri merges with the sea with its sacred water, this place is always suitable for holy bathing. The place where the Kaveri merges with the sea is called Kaveri Sangamathurai. This is a sacred bathing ghat. Because the holy and meritorious Kaveri merges with the sea here, this place becomes a great sacred bathing place. Therefore, one can always bathe in the sacred waters at this Sangamathurai where the Kaveri merges with the sea. The sea, which cannot even cleanse the body due to its salinity, gains the merit of purifying the soul here through its association with the Kaveri. Thus, Kaveri Poompattinam, now called Poompuhar, is a town excelling in all prosperity. This Poompuhar, which was once a great port, city, and metropolis, now exists as a small town on the coast. This Poompuhar, or Kaveri Poompattinam, is a city praised in ancient Tamil literature, including Silappathikaram. IyarPakai Nayanar, a great man, lived in this Poompuhar, belonging to the merchant lineage. It is customary to say that there are 48,000 merchants of good lineage. They are of three types: Ippar, Kavippar, and Perunkudi Vanibar. IyarPakai Nayanar was born into the third type, the noble lineage of Perunkudi Vanibar. IyarPakai Nayanar was a great wealthy man, possessing all the riches that came from immeasurable wealth. He excelled in great devotion to Shiva and devotion to Shiva devotees. He was a Nayanar who would never say 'no' to any item asked by any Shiva devotee who loved Shiva, but would give them whatever they desired. Generally, wealth leads to suffering. But when does wealth truly become beneficial? It is when we use that wealth in the service of Shiva, for Lord Shiva and Shiva devotees. Only then does that wealth yield its true benefit. IyarPakai Nayanar was one who used all his wealth, including gold and other possessions, for Shiva devotees. Generally, it is very rare for wealthy people to have the quality of serving Shiva. But for IyarPakai Nayanar, it came naturally. Therefore, due to his immense love for Shiva devotees and his abundant wealth, IyarPakai Nayanar was accustomed to giving them whatever they desired without saying 'no'. If one has love for Lord Shiva, that love should also extend to Shiva devotees. Therefore, when Sekkizhar Peruman states that IyarPakai Nayanar loved Shiva devotees, it implies that he also loved the Lord. Thus, IyarPakai Nayanar, who had great love for Shiva devotees, lived without distinguishing between their lineage, characteristics, or qualities, and his love for devotees grew immensely. IyarPakai Nayanar was one who would give Shiva devotees what they desired and serve them. Giving what is desired is the excellence of charity. Giving unwanted things, no matter how great the value, is not charity, say the elders. That is why even the Lord, as Thiruthandagam reminds us, "He gives what those who desire, desire." Thus, IyarPakai Nayanar was one who would give Shiva devotees what they desired, when they desired it, and where they desired it. This giving was his nature. The Nayanar did not cultivate this practice through effort. It came naturally to him. This came naturally to the Nayanar due to the Shiva penance he performed in previous days and the Shiva Dharma he followed. IyarPakaiyar is the birth name of the Nayanar. However, his later history showed that the world considered his natural disposition as contrary to worldly custom, as an enmity. Therefore, IyarPakaiyar became a conventional metaphorical name. People in this world consider all things and all possessions as "mine, mine." This is the worldly nature. But IyarPakai Nayanar, however, had the nature of considering all possessions as belonging to Shiva devotees. Therefore, he was an enemy to the worldly nature of considering things as "mine, mine." Thus, he acquired the name IyarPakai Nayanar. Furthermore, it can also be interpreted that he lived without enmity towards the true nature of this world, which is that all possessions truly belong to Shiva devotees. Therefore, the name IyarPakaiyar suited him in both affirmative and negative senses. Thus, IyarPakai Nayanar lived engaged in the duties of a householder and Shiva Dharma. We contemplated the 11 duties of householders according to Thirukkural in the Thiruneelakanda Nayanar Puranam, didn't we? Applying that here, he lived without deviating from the householder's dharma, which is the worldly dharma taught by worldly moral texts. And he also lived upholding the higher dharma of Shiva Dharma. If one lives without failing in the duties of householder life as stated in the moral scriptures, the benefit of that unwavering householder life will give ordinary merits, and it will stop there, giving meritorious results to live in this birth, or in future births, or in the celestial worlds. Therefore, these are "Pasu Dharmas" (duties related to the individual soul). The benefits given by Pasu Dharmas are perishable. Therefore, these too are a kind of bondage. Thus, the householder dharmas, which are Pasu Dharmas, as stated in the moral scriptures, give only ordinary benefits. But Shiva Punya, which is Pati Punya (merit related to the Lord), that is, the service to devotees, stands as the cause of knowledge, bestows Shiva experience, and leads to the eternal bliss of Shiva Sayujya (union with Shiva). Therefore, all Shiva devotees considered Shiva Dharma to be supreme and lived by it. Along with that, they also upheld the householder's dharma. Thus, the Lord decided to make known to all beings this nature of IyarPakai Nayanar, who would give Shiva devotees what they desired without saying 'no'. Let us continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 03 — IyarPakai Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. In the Periya Puranam, in the second chapter, Thillai Vazh Andanar Sarukkam, we are contemplating the IyarPakai Nayanar Puranam. IyarPakai Nayanar was born into the merchant community in the town of Kaveri Poompattinam, and he leads a householder's life with immense wealth and prosperity. Upholding Shiva Dharma as his very life, he lives revering and serving Shiva devotees, never saying no to anything they desire and ask for, but immediately granting it. Lord Shiva decides to reveal the greatness of such an IyarPakai Nayanar to the world. What kind of being is Lord Shiva? He is the extremely subtle essence that can only be understood through Shiva Gnanam (divine knowledge). Lord Shiva is an extremely subtle, extremely subtle being. No matter how much we meticulously research, He possesses the quality of being even more subtle beyond all that. The Upanishads praise Him as "Anoraneeyan Mahato Maheeyan" (subtler than the subtle, greater than the great). The Lord has the nature of being subtler than atoms and beyond the beyond. Thiruvachagam's Thiru Andappakuthi praises the Lord's subtlety, saying, "Il Nuzhai Kathirin Thun Anuppuraiya Chiriya Aga Periyon" (He is smaller than the smallest atom in a sunbeam, yet greater). That is, the Lord is so subtle that even the smallest atoms appear as vast universes. The same Thiruvachagam, in Thiruvempavai, marvels at the Lord's immense cosmic form, saying, "Paathalam Ezhinum Keezh Sorkazhivu Paathamalar Pothaar Punai Mudiyum Ella Porul Mudive" (His lotus feet extend below the seven netherworlds, beyond words, and His adorned crown encompasses the end of all things). That is, all worlds, universes, cosmos, and realms are contained below His sacred crown and above His sacred feet. Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam states that atoms are all universes. Thus, the Lord is an extremely subtle being. Even though He is such a subtle being, He dances in the cosmic hall for all to see, performing His divine dance out of compassion for living beings. The Lord cannot be grasped by the intellect of a living being, which is rooted in the egoistic sense of "I" and "mine," born of Anava (ego) - the intellect that says, "I research, I know." He cannot be caught by such research. But when He, out of His immense love for us, manifests and reveals Himself to us with the help of the supreme knowledge-power (Paragnana Chitshakti Oli) that says, "He reveals, and I know," completely subjugating the ego, then we can see Him, catch Him, and hold Him. Thus, Lord Shiva, the subtle essence beyond the reach of the mind and words of living beings, known only through Shiva Gnanam, manifests Himself in the sacred cosmic hall and performs the dance of grace for the salvation of all beings. This Lord now comes to IyarPakai Nayanar's house. How does He come? He comes in the guise of a dissolute person. Why does He come? He comes to ask for IyarPakai Nayanar's wife. Therefore, Sekkizhar Swamigal here marvels at these two contrasting qualities of the Lord. Sekkizhar is amazed that the Lord now comes in a form that embodies contradictory qualities. How so? He is the subtle essence that those with Shiva Gnanam investigate. But He Himself comes out and dances in the cosmic hall. He has no form, but here He comes in a disguise. He comes in the guise of a dissolute person, but pure sacred ash shines on His golden body. Therefore, Sekkizhar Swamigal wonders if He came in such a way that Uma Devi knew, or if He came without her knowledge. Why does Sekkizhar Swamigal wonder so? The Lord comes here to ask for IyarPakai Nayanar's wife. If He came with Uma Devi's knowledge, Uma Devi would never have consented to the Lord coming to receive another's wife as a gift. But can it be said that He came without her knowledge? That too is not possible. For she is always inseparable from a part of the Lord. So that too is not possible. Therefore, Sekkizhar Swamigal wonders what to say about the Lord coming with such contradictory qualities. That is, the inner meaning of this is that the power of grace (Arul Shakti) and the power of concealment (Tirodhana Shakti) operated simultaneously. Sekkizhar marvels here, contemplating how Arul Shakti and Tirodhana Shakti operated together. Just as IyarPakai Nayanar appeared to be an enemy to his own nature, so too the Lord's sacred form, which He assumed, makes one think that He manifested in this manner, contrary to His own nature. The Lord now comes to IyarPakai Nayanar's house in the guise of a devotee. His body shines like gold. He is adorned with pure sacred ash on that body. Pure sacred ash means that it is pure itself, and it also purifies everything else. Therefore, it is pure ash. Thus, He comes with pure ash shining on His golden body. But He comes in the guise of a dissolute person. What is the guise of a dissolute person? It is a guise that displays signs of lust, indicating a person who engages in illicit relations with other women. The guise of a dissolute person would have nail marks, teeth marks, yellow paste marks, etc. It is a guise of someone acting in a degraded manner, driven by lust. Therefore, the implication is that He wears sacred ash on His body as an atonement to remove the degradation of that dissolute guise. And He comes in the guise of a Brahmin, adorned with sacred ash, in this dissolute guise. Thus, Lord Shiva comes to IyarPakai Nayanar's house in the guise of a Shiva devotee, with pure sacred ash shining on His pure golden body, in a dissolute guise, and in the form of a Brahmin. Thus, Lord Shiva comes to IyarPakaiyar's house, located on one side of the merchant street in Kaveri Poompattinam, also known as Poompuhar, in the aforementioned guise. IyarPakai Nayanar's house was located on the merchant street. We already know that IyarPakai Nayanar was a wealthy merchant. In those old days, it was customary for different communities, based on their profession and other reasons, to live in separate streets. That is, the city planning of that time was such that people of the same profession lived in the same streets, not based on caste. Sivakkavimani mentions that it is regrettable that this custom is now disappearing. Thus, IyarPakai Nayanar welcomes the Shiva devotee who came to his house, that is, the Lord who came in the guise of a Shiva devotee, addressing him as "My Father, My Lord." By saying "Embirane" (My Lord), it is implied that the Lord, Shiva himself, came in the guise of a devotee. Furthermore, Saiva Siddhanta scriptures state that one should worship those who bear the Shiva guise as Shiva himself. Therefore, we must understand that IyarPakai Nayanar worshipped this devotee as Shiva, according to his principle. Because he had complete love for Shiva devotees, that love resulted in joy, and he welcomed the Shiva devotee with complete joy. Joy and love are inseparable. The sign of love is to go forward and bow. Therefore, IyarPakai Nayanar also goes forward to welcome him. IyarPakai Nayanar rejoices, saying, "You have come to our house because of the penance we performed before." Even though the Shiva devotee who came was in a dissolute guise, this Nayanar, without considering his lineage, qualities, etc., looked only at the sacred Shiva guise that the devotee wore, and it was his nature to worship that Shiva guise. Therefore, he saw the devotee in the Shiva guise as Shiva himself and loved him. Even if a person is a Chandala (outcaste), if he wears Rudraksha on his body and sacred ash on his forehead, he is worthy of worship. He is superior to all excellent people of all castes, states the Sri Shiva Mahapurana, Sri Vidyeswara Samhita, 24th chapter, 62nd verse. Therefore, Shiva devotion is important here. Lineage, qualities, caste, or appearance are not important, is what is understood here. Thus, the Brahmin in the dissolute guise, having been lovingly welcomed and offered extensive worship by IyarPakai Nayanar, who stood humbly before him, said to IyarPakai Nayanar, "I have heard of your true nature, that you never refuse any item desired by Shiva devotees, but give it with great willingness. Today I have come here desiring something from you. If you agree to give that item, then I will tell you what that item is." He said this to IyarPakai Nayanar. The Shiva Brahmin who came himself states that IyarPakai Nayanar is one who gives what Shiva devotees desire without refusing, and with great willingness. It is the conclusion of the Shastra texts that giving something with willingness is truly beneficial. Therefore, when we give anything, giving it without refusing is one nature, but giving it with joy, willingness, and happiness is also very important, as understood from this. The Lord, in the guise of a dissolute Brahmin, has come to IyarPakai Nayanar's house and is asking for something. While all other beings approach the Lord and ask for what they need, the Lord Himself comes to a devotee, IyarPakai Nayanar, and asks for something. What greater proof is needed for the Lord's humility and great compassion? Lord Shiva, the supremely compassionate one who grants what those who desire, desire, comes here to honor His devotee, IyarPakai Nayanar, to see his ability to give His devotees what they desire, just as He Himself does. Our Lord Shiva is free from desire and aversion. Yet, He came to the Nayanars here, asking for something, only to bestow grace upon IyarPakai Nayanar and his wife, and to show the world the true nature of the Nayanars. Thus, the dissolute Brahmin who came did not explicitly state what he desired. Instead, he said that he had come desiring something, and if IyarPakai Nayanar joyfully agreed, he would tell him what that thing was. Thus, the Shiva Brahmin did not initially reveal what the item was. This is because, out of humility regarding asking for something from another, he did not explicitly ask what the item was at first, but asked generally. This is the natural humility of one who receives charity. Kamban also beautifully describes how Thirumal, who was in the form of a dwarf (Vamana) when receiving charity, grew into His cosmic form (Vishwaroopam) after receiving the charity. Thus, the Brahmin who came in the dissolute guise said that there was something he desired from IyarPakai Nayanar, and if IyarPakai Nayanar would give it with great joy, he would accept it. What does IyarPakai Nayanar reply to this? Let us continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 04 — IyarPakai Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. In the Periya Puranam, in the section called Thillai Vazh Andanar Sarukkam, we are contemplating the Puranam of IyarPakai Nayanar. Lord Shiva, in the guise of a dissolute Brahmin, comes to the house of IyarPakai Nayanar, who is accustomed to joyfully giving whatever Shiva devotees desire, without hesitation. The Lord, in the guise of a dissolute person, says that he has come desiring something from IyarPakai Nayanar, and if he agrees to give it with joy, he will accept it. Hearing this from the Brahmin, IyarPakai Nayanar says to the Brahmin, "Whatever thing you desire, if that thing is with me, I will give it to you with great joy. Do not have any doubt about it. Nothing that I possess belongs to me; it all belongs to the devotees of Lord Shiva, my Lord." IyarPakai Nayanar also asks him to reveal what that thing is. IyarPakai Nayanar states that whatever he possesses belongs to the devotees of Lord Shiva, it is their property. All beings in the world are servants of Lord Shiva. Everything non-living is Lord Shiva's property, that is, His possession. "Everything is Your servant, everything is Your possession" is the saying of the elders. Therefore, IyarPakai Nayanar says, "There is nothing that is my own property. I am a servant of Lord Shiva. Therefore, I am not the owner of that property. All the things I possess belong to the Lord, who owns me as His servant. Thus, the things I possess belong to the Lord. Therefore, those things belong to the Lord's devotees," is IyarPakai Nayanar's view. As IyarPakai Nayanar stood asking what that thing was, the Brahmin in the dissolute guise said, "I have come here desiring your beloved wife." Even though the Brahmin spoke these words, which should never be uttered in any time or place, directly to IyarPakai Nayanar, IyarPakai Nayanar not only endured them but also became happier than before. The Brahmin in the dissolute guise openly spoke these words, which should not be uttered, thought of, or even related to a deviant act, directly to IyarPakai Nayanar, even though they should not be thought of in the mind. Hearing this, IyarPakai Nayanar became even happier than before. Sekkizhar states that the joy he felt before was due to a Shiva devotee coming to his house. Now, IyarPakaiyar felt even greater joy than that. The reason for this great joy is that the Shiva devotee, the Brahmin who came in the dissolute guise, asked for something that IyarPakai Nayanar possessed. Therefore, this was the reason for his joy. IyarPakai Nayanar was afraid that the Brahmin might ask for something he did not possess, and he would be unable to give it. Now, the dissolute Brahmin asks for the Nayanar's beloved wife. Therefore, thinking that he was asking for something he possessed, IyarPakai Nayanar felt great joy. Because of this joy, IyarPakai Nayanar bowed to the Brahmin and rose. Thus, he said, "You have asked for something that I already possess. That is a great blessing you have given me, my Lord." With these words, he quickly went inside the house and said to his wife, who was renowned for her chastity and was dear to the Nayanar, "My wife, whom I married according to custom, today I have given you to this true ascetic who has come here." If a wife were lacking in chastity or love, a husband might easily give her away. But this lady was of supreme chastity and deeply loved IyarPakai Nayanar. Therefore, the Nayanar quickly goes to give away such a wife, supreme in chastity and love, to the devotee. This shows IyarPakai Nayanar's intense devotion to Shiva devotees. When IyarPakai Nayanar tells this to his wife, how does he address her? He addresses his wife as "My wife, whom I married according to custom and tradition." When marrying, a wife promises her husband that she will act without disobeying his words. This is a part of a Vedic marriage. Thus, IyarPakai Nayanar reminds his wife that she promised to obey his words during the marriage and tells her that he has given her to the true ascetic who had come. He says this with great subtlety. Even though the Shiva devotee who came was in a dissolute guise, IyarPakai Nayanar calls him a true ascetic, one who possesses true penance. Without rejecting the true sacred guise adorned with sacred ash due to the dissolute guise, and in order to honor the guise adorned with sacred ash, IyarPakai Nayanar calls that Brahmin a true ascetic, one who possesses true penance. Sekkizhar Swamigal states that the wife, upon hearing these words of IyarPakai Nayanar, first became distressed and then regained her composure. The wife first becomes distressed upon hearing IyarPakai Nayanar say that he has given her to the Shiva devotee who had come. Why is she distressed? During the marriage, her husband, IyarPakai Nayanar, as part of the marriage, promised his wife that he would protect her until her last day, didn't he? Therefore, she is distressed that if he gives her to the ascetic Brahmin, the promise IyarPakai Nayanar made that day might be broken. And she is also distressed that if IyarPakai Nayanar gives his wife to the Brahmin who came, the sin of desiring another's wife might befall the Brahmin. After that, she regains her composure. How does she regain her composure? She realizes that her duty is to obey her husband, IyarPakai Nayanar, and that it would be wrong not to obey his command. Thus, the wife, who first became distressed and then regained her composure, bowed to IyarPakai Nayanar and said to him, "If this is the command you have given me today, then is it not my right to do whatever command you, my Lord of life, give?" and she stood bowing to the incomparable great IyarPakai Nayanar. Just as she had carried out countless commands given by IyarPakai Nayanar throughout this time, she says that she will carry out this command that has occurred today. She says that it is her great blessing. IyarPakai Nayanar's wife calls IyarPakai Nayanar the Lord of her life. Generally, in worldly custom, a husband is merely the lord of the body. But IyarPakai Nayanar is not just the lord of his wife's body, but also the incomparable lord of her life, ensuring her life. Life is superior to the body. Therefore, she says that she will fulfill the command that ensures her life and will not refuse this command concerning the body.

Furthermore, IyarPakai Nayanar's wife states that her duty is to fulfill her husband's words. The rule is that chastity means not failing in one's word. Here, "word" generally refers to the words of God, elders, etc., but specifically refers to the husband's word. Sivakkavimani further states: For true Shiva devotees, giving Shiva devotees what they desire is the best Shiva Dharma. Even if it is one's beloved wife, who is rare and chaste, she is secondary compared to the excellence of giving Shiva devotees what they desire. And any other possessions are also secondary. It is very special and noble to give this beloved object, love, and any other possessions to receive the grace of Shiva devotees. This is the high standard of IyarPakai Nayanar's life, to believe that such service to Shiva devotees is the ultimate means of liberation for living beings. Thus, accepting her husband's intention and standing by it is the definition of chastity for IyarPakai Nayanar's wife. Sivakkavimani states that it is the Lord's divine act to reveal their virtues to the world. Thus, IyarPakai Nayanar's wife, just as her husband, IyarPakai Nayanar, had embraced and protected her until now, now commanded her to go forth in this act that ensures her life. Therefore, IyarPakai Nayanar's wife bowed to her husband. As the wife bowed, IyarPakai Nayanar also bowed back to his wife. IyarPakai Nayanar bowed to his wife because she upheld his nature of service, which never said 'no'. Thus, since IyarPakai Nayanar had given his wife to the Brahmin who came, his wife now became the property of the Shiva devotee. Therefore, IyarPakai Nayanar bowed to his wife, considering that things belonging to Shiva devotees are also worthy of worship. After this, IyarPakai Nayanar's wife, who was superior to Lakshmi, went near the Brahmin and stood bowing with hesitation. Here, why does IyarPakai Nayanar's wife stand with hesitation before the Brahmin? This is due to the quality of modesty, one of the inherent feminine qualities. Tamil texts describe the inherent feminine qualities as fear, innocence, shame, and modesty. What is this quality of modesty? It is the aversion that chaste women feel towards things they are not accustomed to or familiar with. Sekkizhar describes IyarPakai Nayanar's wife, who stood before the Shiva devotee, as "Thiruvinun Periyal" (greater than Thirumagal, i.e., Lakshmi). Thirumagal means Lakshmi. Sekkizhar Swamigal states that IyarPakai Nayanar's wife is greater than Lakshmi. Lakshmi is a symbol of material wealth. Worldly wealth. Ordinary wealth goes to everyone who strives and works hard for it, whether they are excellent in grace or ordinary people. But IyarPakai Nayanar's wife possessed spiritual wealth. Spiritual wealth that comes from joining Shiva devotees. This spiritual wealth is found only in Shiva and Shiva devotees. Therefore, Sekkizhar states here that IyarPakai Nayanar's wife, who attained unattainable spiritual wealth, is superior to Thirumagal, who is symbolized by ordinary material wealth. "Arutchelvam Selvathtul Selvam Porutchelvam Pooriyar Kannum Ula" (Spiritual wealth is wealth among wealth; material wealth is found even among the lowly) is the 241st Kural from the chapter on Aruludaimai (Possession of Grace) in Thirukkural. Parimelazhagar's commentary on this states that the best wealth among many kinds of wealth is that which comes from grace. All other material wealth is found even among the lowly. Therefore, Sekkizhar describes IyarPakai Nayanar's wife, who attained spiritual wealth, in this manner. Let us continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 05 — IyarPakai Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. In the Periya Puranam, in the second chapter, Thillai Vazh Andanar Sarukkam, we are contemplating the Puranam of IyarPakai Nayanar. Lord Shiva, who came in the guise of a dissolute Brahmin to IyarPakai Nayanar's house, asked for the Nayanar's wife, and he, with a willing heart, entrusted his wife to the Brahmin. Having entrusted her, he stood, filled with even greater joy than before, and humbly asked the Brahmin, "What more should I do now? What service should I perform now?" The Brahmin said to IyarPakai Nayanar, "As I go with this woman, if your relatives or the people of this town, who care about her and you, cause any obstruction, you must come and protect me, you must accompany me." In this verse, Sekkizhar refers to IyarPakai Nayanar as a "Maathavar" (great ascetic). This means that IyarPakai Nayanar performed great penance. Thiruvachagam praises the Lord as "Atthan" (Father) who makes the mind Shiva and makes all actions penance. "Chittam Shivamaakki Seithanave Thavamaakkum Atthan" (The Father who makes the mind Shiva and makes all actions penance). Here, "Chittam Shivamathal" refers to the transformation of the individual faculties (Jiva Karanas) into Shiva's faculties (Shiva Karanas). Thus, Lord Shiva, who is the Father who transforms the Jiva Karanas into Shiva Karanas, comes here to IyarPakai Nayanar's house in the guise of a true ascetic. IyarPakai Nayanar gives his wife to that true ascetic. By giving his wife, the lady, to Lord Shiva, who is the true ascetic, IyarPakai Nayanar becomes a Maathavar. Therefore, Lord Shiva, who came asking for something, is a Maathavar, and IyarPakai Nayanar, who gave his wife to Him, also becomes a Maathavar. The Lord Shiva who came has two consorts. Even though Lord Shiva has two consorts, He concealed His consorts and came here in the guise of an ascetic, desiring another woman. But IyarPakai Nayanar has one consort. However, by giving that consort to the Shiva devotee, abandoning his desire for her, IyarPakai Nayanar also becomes a Maathavar, some commentators note. Sekkizhar writes that IyarPakai Nayanar, by giving his wife to the Brahmin who came in a dissolute guise, became even happier than before. IyarPakai Nayanar gives his wife as a gift. But his wife also accepts that command and fulfills it. Therefore, the Nayanar's act of giving is completed by his wife's action of accepting the command and joining the devotee. When he saw that his act of giving was completed by his wife's action, IyarPakai Nayanar was filled with even greater joy than when he had given her. In Thirukkural, in the chapter on Charity, Valluvar mentions "Eethu Uvakkum Inbam" (the joy of giving). That is, Valluvar speaks of the joy that comes from giving something. Parimelazhagar, in his commentary on this, writes that when something is given to someone who needs it, and that person receives it and rejoices, the compassionate person who gave it will also rejoice upon seeing the recipient's joy. Similarly, here, the joy IyarPakai Nayanar felt when he gave his wife as a gift is the first stage. The second stage of joy comes when he sees the Brahmin experiencing joy through the completion of the act of giving. This results in an even greater joy for him than before. This is what Sekkizhar describes about IyarPakai Nayanar, saying, "Manam Magizhndhu Peruvakaiyin Malarndhe" (his mind rejoiced and blossomed with great delight). Here, "Malarthal" (blossoming) means all faculties excelling with joy. Here, Sekkizhar Peruman refers to Lord Shiva, who came as a Shiva devotee, as a "Saathi Vediyar" (a Brahmin by nature). Here, "Saathi" means nature, inherent quality. The Lord who came is naturally a Brahmin. That is, Sekkizhar states that Lord Shiva came here in the guise of a Brahmin. And he also says that the one who came is naturally a Brahmin. What is the proof that Lord Shiva is the only natural Brahmin? The Vedas speak of Lord Shiva as "You alone are the Brahmin." "Tvam Deveshu Brahmano Asi" is from the Samaveda Shatapatha Brahmana. "Vipra Viprasya" is from the Svetasvatara Upanishad. "Brahmano Adhipati Shivam" is from the Narayana Upanishad. "Vipranam Daivam Shambhu Kshatriyanam Tu Madhava Vaishyanam Tu Bhavet Brahma Shudranam Devendrakah" is from Manusmriti. Therefore, these scriptures prove that Lord Shiva alone is naturally a Brahmin. Furthermore, the Shastras describe Brahmins as "Shatkarma Nirathars" (those engaged in six duties), don't they? That is, they perform six duties, and one of them is "Pratigraham," receiving charity. Here, Lord Shiva has come in the guise of a Brahmin to perform this act of receiving charity, which is one of the six duties of Brahmins. Therefore, Lord Shiva, who is naturally a Brahmin, comes here in the form of a Brahmin and performs the act of receiving charity, which is one of the six duties of Brahmins. Does the degradation associated with receiving charity, regardless of who receives it, not apply to this Brahmin, the Lord? It does not apply to the Lord. Because when the Lord receives something, it is not for Himself. He receives it to bestow grace upon living beings. Therefore, the degradation that comes from begging does not apply to this Brahmin, the Lord. Thirumoolar's Thirumandiram, in the seventh Tantra, in the section on Bhikshavidhi, has a verse: "Parandhu Ulagezhum Padaitha Piranai Irandhunni Enbar Etrkku Irakkum Nirantharamaga Ninaiyum Adiyaar Irandhundu Than Kazhal Ettach Cheithane." (Those who do not recognize Shiva as the Lord who created the seven worlds will scorn Him as a beggar. But those devotees who constantly meditate on Him will understand that He begs not for Himself, but to make us reach His feet.) Those who do not recognize Lord Shiva as the primeval Lord of the world will only see Him holding a begging bowl and receiving charity, and will speak ill of it. But those who recognize Lord Shiva as the primeval Lord of the world will properly understand that He receives charity not for Himself, but for the salvation of us, living beings. Now, the Brahmin, Lord Shiva, having received IyarPakai Nayanar's wife as a gift, said to IyarPakai Nayanar, "If your relatives or the people of your town cause any harm to me or to the woman I received as a gift while I am going with her, you must come as my escort." Hearing this from the Brahmin, IyarPakai Nayanar said, "Shouldn't I have realized this and done it before you even asked? It is indeed my mistake that I delayed until you told me." Regretting this, he went to another room in his house, took up a sword and shield, put on the appropriate attire, and then came before the Brahmin. He let the two of them go ahead, and he began to follow behind them. IyarPakai Nayanar was one who lived considering himself and all his possessions as belonging to Lord Shiva and Shiva devotees. Therefore, the Nayanar was a servant. He lived considering Lord Shiva and Shiva devotees as his masters. The characteristic of a servant is to anticipate the master's command and act before being told. That is the characteristic of a servant. But here, Sivakkavimani explains that IyarPakai Nayanar regrets his mistake for not realizing it until Lord Shiva, who came as a Brahmin, asked him to accompany him and the woman he had received as a gift. IyarPakai Nayanar immediately took up his sword and shield, put on the appropriate attire, and prepared for battle like a lion. Then, he let the Brahmin and the lady walk ahead, and he followed behind them as their protector. We already know that IyarPakai Nayanar was a great man from a merchant community. This shows that in those days, merchants also learned military skills and were proficient in them. Unlike now, in those days, people were generally allowed to possess weapons and were skilled and brave enough to use them. We can also understand from this that they kept weapons and were trained in them only to protect themselves and their possessions. Now, the Brahmin who received the gift and the lady walk ahead. They begin their journey from Kaveri Poompattinam. IyarPakai Nayanar, with his sword and shield, in battle attire, follows behind them as their protector. Hearing about this incident, the lady's relatives, IyarPakai Nayanar's relatives, his clan members, and all the townspeople gathered together and tried to stop the Brahmin in the dissolute guise. They became angry with IyarPakai Nayanar for doing this. No one had ever done such a thing before. IyarPakai Nayanar dared to do something that no one else had done. The Brahmin asked for something that should not be asked for as a gift. And IyarPakaiyar gave something that should not be given. Even if IyarPakai Nayanar gave her away because he was mad, is it right for this Brahmin to take his wife? This is very wrong. We will not allow this wrong to happen. Saying this, IyarPakai Nayanar's and his wife's relatives, the townspeople, and all the clan members took up bows, swords, and daggers and, with great uproar, went before the Brahmin and tried to surround him. IyarPakai Nayanar openly and shamelessly performed a disgraceful act that no one else had done or even thought of doing, namely, giving his wife as a gift. Therefore, his relatives scorned him as a madman and became angry. It seems IyarPakai Nayanar took his sword and shield and came as a protector, anticipating that his relatives would come to stop them with weapons. The Brahmin walks ahead, followed by the lady, and then the Nayanar walks behind them. IyarPakai Nayanar follows behind the two of them as their escort. Lord Shiva, in the guise of a Brahmin, walks ahead, with IyarPakai Nayanar as his escort. Lord Shiva, who came in the guise of a Brahmin, asked IyarPakai Nayanar to accompany him as his escort, didn't he? Therefore, this Brahmin, who came in a dissolute guise, considered IyarPakai Nayanar as his escort. Not only that, Lord Shiva, who came in the guise of a Brahmin, is the escort for all living beings. Therefore, Sekkizhar composes the verse here to convey both these meanings. Lord Shiva is the one who accompanies living beings throughout the countless births they take. Saiva Siddhanta scriptures state that Lord Shiva is one with, the body of, and separate from living beings, bestowing grace. Therefore, Lord Shiva alone accompanies living beings throughout the journeys of the countless births they take. The Lord also has the name "Maarga Sahayar" (Helper on the Path). "Vaananaadanai Vazhithunai Marundhe" (The medicine that is the escort of the Lord of the heavens) is from Sundarar's Thevaram, Thiruvannainallur. Thus, the Lord Himself is the one who comes as an escort for all living beings. He now comes in the guise of a dissolute Brahmin and asks IyarPakai Nayanar to accompany him as his escort. Therefore, IyarPakai Nayanar also accompanies the dissolute Brahmin and the lady who follows, as their escort.

Generally, parents, spouse, children, relatives, and friends protect us from suffering and accompany us while we live. But when we journey to another world, they only come to see us off. This applies not only to other relatives but also to a husband. All husbands in the world accompany their wives on their journey of life until their own lifespan, and then they depart. But IyarPakai Nayanar not only accompanied his wife as long as she lived in this world but also stood as a support for her soul, entrusting her to the Lord, and thus supported her in attaining Shiva's grace. The support IyarPakai Nayanar provided was a life-long, unique support. Just as Lord Shiva accompanies souls through their births and bestows liberation, so too IyarPakai Nayanar supported his wife's soul and helped her attain Shiva's grace. Therefore, just like the Lord, devotees also guide us, living beings, and ensure our lives. Thus, for us, living beings, only Lord Shiva and His devotees will always be the support for our lives and lead us to the path of liberation. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 06 — IyarPakai Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. In the Periya Puranam, we are contemplating the Puranam of IyarPakai Nayanar. Lord Shiva, who came in the guise of a dissolute Brahmin and received IyarPakai Nayanar's wife as a gift, walks ahead. Behind him walks the lady who was IyarPakai Nayanar's wife. IyarPakai Nayanar walks third, protecting them. Sekkizhar Swamigal describes Lord Shiva, who came in the guise of a dissolute Brahmin, as walking ahead with "Kaliperum Kadal" (great, overflowing love). Love generally refers to desire for women. Here, Sekkizhar Swamigal states that the Lord walks with "Kaliperum Kadal." "Kaliperum Kadal" means immense love. This is not like love for the body, but love for the soul. Lord Shiva Himself loves our souls immensely, becomes the Lord of our souls, and ensures our souls. Therefore, this is the inner meaning of Sekkizhar singing that the dissolute Brahmin walks ahead with immense love. Knowing that these three were traveling, IyarPakai Nayanar's relatives, his wife's relatives, and all his clan members gathered together, took up bows, swords, daggers, and other weapons, and went before the Brahmin in the dissolute guise. They stood as if to stop him and said to the Brahmin, "Wicked one, stop! Don't go! Stop! Leave this lady, who is the lamp of our lineage, here and go. If you leave this lady with us and go, you can remove the disgrace caused by your action. So, remove the disgrace that has come upon you and leave this lady here and run away!" What is disgrace? Generally, in common parlance, we use the terms "disgrace" and "sin" together. Here, disgrace refers to the ignominy that comes upon us in this life when we perform actions that are forbidden by the Dharma Shastras, that is, actions that are said not to be done. The result of this disgraceful action is sin. Therefore, we should fear disgrace. By fearing disgrace, we can avoid committing sin. Here, the Nayanar's relatives say to the dissolute Brahmin, "Disgrace has come upon you because of your action. Therefore, to remove that disgrace, leave this lady, who is the lamp of our lineage, here and go." What is the disgrace that the relatives refer to? The disgrace that comes upon IyarPakai Nayanar is that he gave his wife to another. The disgrace that comes upon IyarPakai Nayanar's wife is that she joined another man, not her husband. The Nayanar belonged to a merchant community. If a member of this community performs such a disgraceful act, other communities will speak ill of this community. Therefore, this also becomes a disgrace that comes upon that community. Furthermore, people from other towns and countries will speak of someone living in this town, in this country, having performed such a disgraceful act. Therefore, this is a disgrace that comes upon that town and country. In addition, the fact that someone who came in the guise of a Shiva devotee received a wife as a gift is a disgrace that came upon that Shiva guise and Shiva's service. Thus, the relatives believe that disgrace will befall IyarPakai Nayanar and his wife, the lineage they were born into, the town and country they live in, and also the Shiva devotee who received IyarPakai Nayanar's wife as a gift, and thereby all those who wear the Shiva guise. That is why they say to the Brahmin, "To remove this disgrace, leave this lady, the lamp of our lineage, with us and go." Furthermore, the relatives say to the Brahmin that the disgrace had already occurred when IyarPakai Nayanar gave his wife. Therefore, to remove that existing disgrace, they tell him to leave the lady, the lamp of their lineage, and go. We saw that disgrace comes upon us when we do things that are forbidden by the Dharma Shastras, the moral texts, didn't we? Here, what is the disgrace that comes upon the Brahmin by receiving IyarPakai Nayanar's wife? It is the disgrace that comes from violating the rule stated in Thirukkural, in the chapter on "Pirannil Vizhaiyamai" (Not desiring another's wife), which says one should not desire another's wife. Therefore, desiring another's wife is a moral principle stated in Thirukkural, a book of ethics. By violating that principle, disgrace comes upon the Brahmin here. Thiruvalluvar does not only say that disgrace will come in this chapter. He says that disgrace, enmity, sin, and fear will come to one who desires another's wife. "Pagai, Paavam, Achcham, Pazhi ena naangum Igavaavaam Il Izhaappan Kan" (Enmity, sin, fear, and disgrace—these four will not depart from him who desires another's wife) is the 146th Thirukkural in the chapter on "Pirannil Vizhaiyamai." As all the relatives raised their voices and came towards the Brahmin with an uproar, he acted as if he was afraid and looked at the lady standing behind him. Sekkizhar states here that the Brahmin acted as if he was afraid and looked at the lady. He was not actually afraid, not actually scared. He acted as if he was afraid, in accordance with the dissolute guise that Lord Shiva had assumed and the situation that was unfolding. Seeing the Brahmin's apparent fear, the lady addressed the Brahmin as "Lord." This is because, accepting her husband IyarPakaiyar's command, she had taken this dissolute Brahmin as her master. Therefore, she addresses the Brahmin as "Lord." Furthermore, it is implied that He is the Lord of all. The lady, seeing the Brahmin acting afraid, said, "Lord, do not be afraid. IyarPakai will defeat all these relatives." Generally, it is customary and a rule for a wife not to utter her husband's name. But now, the lady has been given to the Brahmin by IyarPakai Nayanar. Thus, the lady has been given as property to the Brahmin. Therefore, she stands having completely abandoned the thought that IyarPakai Nayanar is her husband. Therefore, she calls IyarPakai Nayanar by name and says, "IyarPakai will win, do not be afraid." Hearing this from the lady, IyarPakai Nayanar said to the Brahmin, "Do not worry. I will protect you from any harm from them and defeat them. Do not falter." Saying this, IyarPakai Nayanar, like a great male lion, with the fire of anger in his eyes, went towards the opposing relatives and said, "All of you, run away from my sight! If you run away, you will escape with your lives. If you don't, you will be cut into pieces by my sword and writhe in pain!" He said this angrily as he advanced towards them. Among the wild animals, the lion is the most excellent in bravery and majesty. Therefore, Sekkizhar states here that IyarPakai Nayanar roared like a lion. Generally, it is a rule of war to give a final warning to one's enemies before starting a battle. Therefore, IyarPakai Nayanar now gives a final warning to his relatives, following that rule. Seeing IyarPakai Nayanar coming thus, the assembled relatives said, "What have you done? By your action, you have brought disgrace upon this country and our lineage. Our enemies mock and laugh at us because of this. Even though our enemies laugh at us, you are not ashamed at all. You not only gave your wife to this Brahmin, but you also boast of your strength. We will certainly stop you. Even if we have to die in this act of stopping, we will die, but we will not let this Brahmin take your wife." They said, "We will die, but we will not agree to this." These relatives are all about to fall victim to IyarPakai Nayanar's sword in a short while. Thus, it is the nature of worldly people to speak beforehand of what is to come. That is why elders say that our mouths should always be filled with auspicious words. Moreover, here, the relatives say that they will not agree to the Brahmin taking IyarPakai Nayanar's wife. IyarPakai Nayanar gave his wife as a gift to the Brahmin. But the relatives say that they will not agree to him taking her. This is because, at the time of his marriage, IyarPakai Nayanar, in the presence of the sacrificial fire, gods, relatives, and everyone, with gods and relatives as witnesses, married this lady. Not only that, he also vowed to protect this lady according to dharma as long as he lived. This is a common ritual during marriages. Therefore, since IyarPakai Nayanar made a promise to protect that woman as long as he lived, in the presence of his relatives, with them as witnesses, the relatives now object, saying they will not agree to this, as he is breaking that promise. Towards these objecting relatives, IyarPakai Nayanar's mind fills with anger. He says to them, "If you stop this Brahmin, I will send all your lives to the heroic heaven and let this good ascetic, this ascetic, go." Sekkizhar writes here that IyarPakai Nayanar becomes angry, doesn't he? It is stated in the Dharma Shastras and moral texts that one should not become angry. Thirukkural also has a chapter called "Vegulaamai" (Not getting angry). This chapter is specifically about not getting angry. If so, when Sekkizhar states that anger arose in IyarPakai Nayanar's mind, the anger that arose in the Nayanar's mind was not anger born of the Maya qualities of Rajas or Tamas. This anger arose to prevent any obstruction to the Shiva Punya that IyarPakai Nayanar was performing, and to ensure that the Shiva Punya was fully completed without interruption. Therefore, this was not anger born of the Maya qualities of Rajas, Tamas, etc., that come to other humans. Therefore, the anger IyarPakai Nayanar felt here is beyond the moral principles of not getting angry, as stated in the worldly Dharma Shastras. Furthermore, the anger IyarPakai Nayanar felt here takes the lives of the opposing relatives. As a result, the lives of the opposing relatives go to Devaloka (celestial world) and experience joy there. Therefore, the Nayanar's anger brought good to the lives of his relatives. This also makes it clear to us that the anger IyarPakai Nayanar felt is not like the anger described in the moral scriptures. Furthermore, here IyarPakai Nayanar says that he is going to kill his relatives. But in reality, killing is not his intention. The relatives are obstructing the Shiva Punya he is performing. By obstructing the Shiva Punya, they will incur sin. Because of that sin, they will experience various sufferings. Therefore, the purpose of IyarPakai Nayanar's killing here is to prevent them from committing such a sin. Therefore, IyarPakai Nayanar's statement that he is going to kill them

The relatives, by obstructing the Shiva Punya, were saved from sin, and their lives were guided to a good path, allowing the Brahmin to proceed. This was the goal of the Shiva Punya. That is why Sekkizhar Peruman praises IyarPakai Nayanar as "Nallor Nallavar" (a good person, a good one) in this verse. Let us continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 07 — IyarPakai Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. In the Periya Puranam, we are contemplating the sacred history of IyarPakai Nayanar. IyarPakai Nayanar gives his wife as a gift to the Brahmin who came as a Shiva devotee. Not only that, IyarPakai Nayanar accompanies them as their protector to the boundary of Kaveri Poompattinam. We have contemplated how his relatives, knowing this, came to oppose them. As IyarPakai Nayanar confronts the relatives, the assembled relatives try to go towards the Shiva Brahmin and stop him. The relatives, thinking that it is better to stop the Shiva Brahmin and rescue the lady first, and then they can fight IyarPakai Nayanar, proceed towards the Brahmin to stop him. As the relatives tried to stop him, IyarPakai Nayanar, in anger, swung his sword alternately to the left and right, fighting so that the legs, shoulders, and heads of the opposing relatives were severed from their bodies. Sekkizhar Swamigal describes IyarPakai Nayanar as playing a game of war like a male tiger. What was IyarPakai Nayanar's anger? It was that these relatives were not opposing him, but were opposing the Brahmin whom he revered and worshipped. One cannot tolerate suffering that comes to those one reveres and loves more than suffering that comes to oneself. That is why IyarPakai Nayanar flared up. The nature of love is to protect the beloved at all times. It doesn't matter what suffering comes to oneself. The quality and action we see in true lovers is that they think no suffering should come to those they love. "Anbudaiyaar Enbum Uriyarpirarkku" (Those who have love even their bones belong to others) is from Thirukkural. Thus, IyarPakai Nayanar, in anger, severed the shoulders, legs, and heads of his relatives with his sword. When fighting enemies, the first thing to be cut is the enemy's shoulder. This is because enemies fight with the help of their shoulders. Therefore, cutting the shoulder first is a method of warfare. Even after cutting the shoulders, enemies will walk with their legs and come to fight. Therefore, cutting their legs is the second action. Even if their legs are cut, they will fall and fight. Therefore, only if absolutely necessary, severing the enemy's head is our rule of war. That is why Sekkizhar also sings here, systematically, that IyarPakai Nayanar first cut the shoulders of the opposing relatives, then their legs, and then their heads. Thus, IyarPakai Nayanar severed the limbs of his relatives' bodies into pieces. He states that he separated and severed the limbs, the organs that are attached to the body. In Tamil, Yama (the god of death) is also called "Kootruvan." The body and soul are always together. By separating the soul and body, which are always together, into body and soul, he gets the name Kootruvan. Furthermore, Sekkizhar states here that IyarPakai Nayanar plays as he fights these relatives. This implies that IyarPakai Nayanar performed this act effortlessly, as if in play. In this battle, the Nayanar's relatives and kinsmen came to oppose him individually. They came as a crowd to oppose him. Each one came from a different direction to oppose him. Even though many came in various ways to oppose him, the brave and courageous IyarPakai Nayanar stood alone against all of them, defeated them all, and emerged victorious. Sekkizhar praises IyarPakai Nayanar's bravery in a verse: "Sorindhana Kudalkal Engum Thunindhana Udalkal Engum Virindhana Thalaigal Engum Midaindhana Kazhugum Engum Erindhana Vizhigal Engum Ethirppavar Oruvarindhre Thirinthanar Kalanil Engum Sivan Kazhal Punaitha Veerar" (Intestines were strewn everywhere, bodies were severed everywhere, heads were scattered everywhere, vultures gathered everywhere, eyes burned everywhere; there was no one to oppose him, the brave one adorned with Shiva's feet wandered everywhere). What is bravery? It is to stand fearless against anything that goes against one's principles and goals, and to emerge victorious. "Pulanainthum Venraan Than Veerame Veeram" (His bravery is the bravery of one who has conquered the five senses) is the saying of Avvai Pirattiyar. For IyarPakai Nayanar, who had dedicated all his faculties, mind, and love solely to the Lord and His devotees, and who had thus conquered the five senses, defeating these relatives was a very easy task. It is a truth we see that no one can defeat devotees who have surrendered themselves to Shiva. This is also established in the histories of great saints like Thirunavukkarasar. "Anjuvathu Yaathonrum Illai Anja Varuvathum Illai" (There is nothing to fear, and nothing to cause fear) is the saying of Thirunavukkarasu Peruman. "Naam Aarkkum Kudiyallom Namanai Anjom" (We are not subjects to anyone, we do not fear Yama) is also the saying of Thirunavukkarasu Peruman. It is the nature of devotees that they need not fear anyone, including animals, humans, or celestials, due to their love for the Lord and His devotees. "En UlamE Pugundha Adhanaal" (Because He entered my heart) - just as Thirugnanasambandar Peruman and Thirunavukkarasu Peruman, great individuals who excelled in the service of Lord Shiva, did not need to fear even the days and planets, as evidenced by Gnanasambandar Peruman's Thevaram: "Nyaayiru Thingal Sevvaay Pudhan Viyazhan Velli Sani Paambu Irandum UdanE Aasaru Nalla Nalla Avai Nalla Nalla Adiyaar Avarkku MigavE" (Sunday, Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday, and the two snakes (Rahu and Ketu) are all good, good, very good for devotees). Thus, the bravery of devotees is very special. Sekkizhar Peruman, in the section on "Thirukkoottach Chirappu" (Excellence of the Holy Assembly), asks if he can describe it. It is appropriate to recall that verse here. "Aaram Kandigai Aadiyum KanthaiyE Paaram Eesan Pani Allathu OnrilAr Eera Anbinar Yaathum KuraivilAr Veeram Ennaal Vilambum Thagaiyatho" (Their garland is Rudraksha, their garment is a loincloth; their burden is nothing but the service of the Lord; they are full of love, lacking nothing; can their bravery be described by me?) This is a verse from Sekkizhar Peruman in the section on "Thirukkoottach Chirappu," praising the greatness of devotees, verse number 144. Thus, IyarPakai Nayanar, with great bravery, sent all his opposing relatives to the heroic heaven. Only a few who fled in fear of their lives survived. Thus, IyarPakai Nayanar, having given his virtuous wife to the Shiva Brahmin, and having sent all the great relatives who obstructed the completion of that gift to the heroic heaven with his sword, then said to the sage, "O Adigal (Revered One), you need not fear anymore. I will accompany you until you cross this grove and see you off." He then went with him. We can understand that this grove was located outside Kaveri Poompattinam. Here, IyarPakai Nayanar addresses the Shiva Brahmin as "Adigal." The title "Adigal" is a traditional name given to great sages who have abandoned worldly attachments and are devoted to God. We should remember here that we refer to Manikkavachagar as Thiruvathavooradigal. We can understand here that this battle took place near a grove outside Kaveri Poompattinam. Therefore, IyarPakai Nayanar, having removed all those who opposed them, thought that someone might be hiding in this grove and cause trouble to the Brahmin after IyarPakai Nayanar left. Therefore, IyarPakai Nayanar says, "I will accompany you until you cross this grove." So, the three of them cross the grove. Sekkizhar Swamigal describes the three of them crossing the grove as follows: the hidden sage, unknown to the two, walks ahead. Behind him walks the lady. And behind them walks IyarPakai Nayanar, the brave one who defeated all his opponents.

He says that he walks third. Generally, it is the nature of a wife to walk behind her husband. But here, since IyarPakai Nayanar has given his wife to the Brahmin, the lady walks behind the Brahmin. The Brahmin walks first. The lady walks behind the Brahmin. And IyarPakai Nayanar walks third, behind her. The wife, who should have walked behind IyarPakai Nayanar, listened to her husband IyarPakai Nayanar's words and surrendered herself to the Lord. By dedicating herself to the Lord's devotee, she walks ahead of her husband. That is, Sekkizhar subtly implies here that she attained the Lord even before her husband attained Him. The epithet Sekkizhar uses for the Brahmin walking ahead is "one unknown to the two." The Lord has come here in the form of a Brahmin. Thus, the lady and IyarPakai Nayanar, who walk behind the Lord, do not know that the Lord Himself has come in the form of a Brahmin. Therefore, the Brahmin walking ahead is a Brahmin unknown to the two, a person unknown to the two. Not only that, there is also an inner meaning here that He is the incomparable one unknown even to Brahma and Vishnu. He says "one unknown to the two." "One" in Tamil does not only mean the number one, but also "incomparable." Therefore, Sekkizhar Swamigal addresses and describes the Brahmin in two senses: as the Lord unknown to the lady and IyarPakai Nayanar who walk behind Him, and as the incomparable one unknown even to Brahma and Vishnu. Thus, the three of them cross that grove outside Kaveri Poompattinam and proceed near the sacred place called Thiruchaikkadu. As soon as they reached Thiruchaikkadu, the Brahmin said to IyarPakai Nayanar, "You may now go back." After the Brahmin, who came as an ascetic, told him to return, IyarPakai Nayanar fell at his feet and bowed with his head. He joined his hands in salutation. Thus, bowing before the devotee, he praised the Brahmin, who had manifested for the salvation of the three worlds, with his mouth, and said that he had received the blessing of the grace of this devotee. IyarPakai Nayanar then returned from there. In this verse, Sekkizhar Peruman refers to the Brahmin as "Poosur." Brahmins are also called "Poosuras." This means "gods living on earth." Thus, IyarPakai Nayanar returns again. The Brahmin sees IyarPakaiyar, the good devotee who performed a great deed difficult for anyone else to do, returning in this manner. Who is that Brahmin? Is it not Lord Shiva, with the blue throat and eight arms? That Lord Shiva, looking at IyarPakai Nayanar with joy, praises IyarPakai Nayanar, thinking, "He has a heart completely devoid of falsehood. He is going back without even looking behind." He then begins to call the Nayanar again. Sekkizhar Swamigal praises IyarPakai Nayanar in the verse, describing him as a good devotee who performed a difficult act. Great people are those who perform difficult acts. "Seyarkariya Seivaar Periyar" (Great are those who do difficult things) is from Thirukkural. "Seyarkariya Seyal Palavum Seithu Silar Eytharku Ariyadhanai Eythinaar" (Some, having performed many difficult acts, attained what was difficult to attain) is from Thirukkalippattiyar, verse 61. Here, the difficult acts performed by IyarPakai Nayanar include abandoning attachment to his possessions, considering all possessions as belonging to the Lord's devotees, detaching himself from his relatives, giving up attachment to his wife, giving whatever Shiva devotees ask for without hesitation, opposing and eliminating any obstruction to the charity he performs for Shiva devotees, and even when giving his wife as a gift to a Shiva devotee, walking back without looking behind. Every action performed by the Nayanar is indeed a difficult act. Saivism classifies souls into three types: Prakritikas, Vainikas, and Samuchittas. Among these, Samuchittas are those who have eliminated all their karmas, that is, they have removed the karma mala (impurity of karma) and, for the sake of the remaining small karma, they take on a body and eliminate that remaining karma. All the actions they perform are not done as their own actions, due to their self-consciousness, but are performed as Shiva's actions. These are the ones whose actions transcend worldly rules and prohibitions. Here, IyarPakai Nayanar is also a Samuchitta. Therefore, his actions became actions rooted in Shiva. Therefore, he stands here as a great person who performed difficult acts, acts that are difficult for anyone else to do. Therefore, Sekkizhar praises him in the verse as a "Nattrondar" (good devotee). Let us continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 08 — IyarPakai Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. In the Periya Puranam, we are contemplating the Puranam of IyarPakai Nayanar. IyarPakai Nayanar gives his wife as a gift to Lord Shiva, who came as a Brahmin, and then accompanies them to the boundary of Kaveri Poompattinam. In the middle, the Nayanar fights all the opposing relatives and sends them to the celestial world. Upon reaching Thiruchaikkadu, the boundary of Kaveri Poompattinam, the Lord in the form of a Brahmin tells IyarPakai Nayanar to return. Hearing this, IyarPakai Nayanar humbly returns to Kaveri Poompattinam. As he returns, the Brahmin marvels at IyarPakai Nayanar's true love, which is devoid of falsehood, as he walks without even looking back. The Lord Himself praises him, saying, "He has a mind devoid of falsehood." This means that IyarPakai Nayanar is a blessed one who has been freed from the bonds of Anava and other impurities that cling to souls, causing falsehood and leading them to evil. IyarPakai Nayanar walks without even looking back. Sekkizhar's statement that the Nayanar walks without even looking back indicates that even the subtle impressions of past karmas (vasana mala) have been completely eradicated in IyarPakai Nayanar. Thus, IyarPakai Nayanar walks with a truthful mind, devoid of falsehood. The previous statement, "He has a mind devoid of falsehood," indicated the sign of liberation from bonds. Now, the statement "He has a truthful mind" means that having been liberated from bonds, and with the vasana mala also eradicated, he stands ready to attain Shiva's grace. "Mei" means "Sat" (truth, reality). The Lord is Sat. Therefore, "Mei Tharu Sindhaiyar" means one whose mind is filled with the true essence, the Lord, having removed all other unreal things. Thus, seeing the Nayanar walking without even looking back, the Lord calls out to him. Sekkizhar states that He cries out. "Olamiduthal" (crying out) refers to calling out to those who are stronger and higher than oneself to remove fear when one is afraid. Here, the Brahmin, that is, Lord Shiva in the form of a Brahmin, cries out to IyarPakai Nayanar, who is walking without even looking back. How? "IyarPakai Muniva Olam! Meendu Nee Varuvaai Olam! AyarppilaathaanE Olam! AnbanE Olam Olam! Seyarkarum Seigai Seitha TheeranE Olam! Endraan Mayakkaru Marai Olittu Maal Ayan Theda Nindraan." This is Sekkizhar's verse. Its meaning is: The Lord, who stood to be sought by Brahma and Vishnu, and whom the Vedas, which dispel confusion, cried out to many times, called out to IyarPakai Nayanar, saying, "O IyarPakai, the ascetic! Olam! Come back here, Olam! O you who never forgets my thoughts! Olam! O beloved one, Olam, Olam! O brave one who performed a deed difficult for anyone else to do, Olam!" Thus, the Brahmin, the Lord, cries out many times to IyarPakai Nayanar. Sivakkavimani provides an explanation for each of Sekkizhar's cries. First, the Brahmin calls out, "IyarPakai Muniva Olam!" "Munivar" means ascetics. Here, IyarPakai Nayanar is one who has renounced Kama (lust). He has completely and entirely renounced and cast aside all worldly attachments and desires. Therefore, Lord Shiva, from within the guise of the Brahmin, calls IyarPakai Nayanar "IyarPakai Muniva" (O IyarPakai, the ascetic). The Lord Himself is an ascetic. As Thirukalippattiyar, verse 69, states, "Than Seiyum Thanmaigalum Aakkidum Anbarkku Avan" (He bestows His own qualities upon His beloved devotees). Therefore, Lord Shiva, the ascetic, bestows that quality of asceticism upon His devotee, and thus calls IyarPakai Nayanar "IyarPakai Muniva," meaning "O IyarPakai, the ascetic." Next, the Brahmin says, "Eendu Nee Varuvaai Olam!" That is, he cries out, "Come here, Olam!" IyarPakai Nayanar is returning from this grove to his place. IyarPakai Nayanar, who has abandoned all attachments and attained Shiva's grace, is preparing to go to his place. The Brahmin calls him back, saying, "Come here, Olam!" to the one who was trying to go to Kaveri Poompattinam. That is, the Brahmin cries out, "O IyarPakai Nayanar, the place you should be now is not this Kaveri Poompattinam or this earthly world. The place you should be now is my place." This implies that you no longer need to go back to Kaveri Poompattinam or this world. Come back from these and come to my Shiva Loka, where I am. Next, he says, "Ayarppu IllathaanE Olam!" (O one who is without forgetfulness!). "Ayarppu" generally refers to forgetfulness and weariness. He calls IyarPakai Nayanar "O one who is without forgetfulness." IyarPakai Nayanar is one who is without forgetfulness, without weariness. In what? In never forgetting Shiva and His devotees at any time or place. He is one who has taken a vow to give whatever Shiva devotees ask for without saying 'no' and without weariness. Therefore, the Brahmin next says, "AyarppilaathaanE Olam!" Next, he says to IyarPakai Nayanar, "AnbanE Olam Olam!" (O beloved one, Olam, Olam!). IyarPakai Nayanar has abundant love for himself (Lord Shiva) and for Shiva devotees. Love alone is the special cause of everything. Therefore, while he cried "Olam" once in other places, here, when emphasizing his love, he cries "AnbanE Olam Olam" twice. The Lord is one who surrenders Himself only for love. "Bhakti Valaiyil Paduvon Kaanaga" (See, He is caught in the net of devotion) is from Thiruvachagam. We can also see in Thiruvilaiyadal how He made Himself a servant to Panabhadra, saying, "Pathiran Adimai Endraan." "Anbum Sivanum Irandu Enbar Arivilaar. AnbE Sivam Avathu Aarum Arikilaar. AnbE Sivam Avathu Aarum Arindhapin AnbE Sivam Aay AmarndhirundhaarE" (The ignorant say love and Shiva are two. No one knows that love is Shiva. After everyone knows that love is Shiva, they resided as love and Shiva) is Thirumoolar's Thirumandiram, verse 270. This verse appears in the section on "Anbudaimai" (Possession of Love), the 21st section of the first Tantra of Thirumandiram. The Lord is one who merges with love and becomes inseparable. Therefore, He cried "Olam" twice for the beloved. Next, he says, "Seyarkarum Seigai Seitha TheeranE Olam!" (O brave one who performed a deed difficult to do!). We have already contemplated how IyarPakai Nayanar performed a difficult deed. Who is crying out like this? Lord Shiva in the form of a Brahmin. Who is Lord Shiva? Lord Shiva is the one whom the Vedas, which dispel the confusion of living beings, cried out to many times. Confusion here means delusion. The Vedas dispel the delusion of living beings and reveal the truth. The Vedas calling out to the Lord signifies the state where the Vedas, having described the Lord's nature in every possible way, are exhausted and unable to describe it further. It is natural for the sound-form Vedas to cry out to the Lord who is beyond sound. The greatness of the Vedas is immeasurable. Those who follow the Vedas are great. Therefore, the Vedas are greater than those great people. The Lord, who is worshipped by the Vedas and who spoke them, is greater than the Vedas. The fact that such a great Lord cried out to IyarPakai Nayanar indicates his greatness, which is the meaning of this verse. In this verse, Sekkizhar composes it so that the Lord cries out six times. IyarPakai Nayanar conquered the six enemies (of the mind). Therefore, Sekkizhar Swamigal has composed six cries here. Hearing that great sound, that cry, IyarPakai Nayanar quickly returns, saying, "Here I am, I have come! If anyone else stops you, I will send them to heaven too!" IyarPakai Nayanar, who came back quickly, did not see the Shiva ascetic. But he saw only the lady. At that moment, IyarPakai Nayanar saw the Lord appearing in the sky on His bull, with His consort Uma Devi, like a golden mountain shining on a silver mountain. Seeing Him, he fell to the ground and worshipped Him. He prostrated. Then he rose. Then he praised Him. IyarPakai Nayanar, looking at the Lord who appeared in the sky, said, "O Lord, how shall I praise You? I do not know how to praise You. May Your divine grace to me prosper. Praise be to this form You assumed for my sake. You came quickly, bestowed grace, and made me, a very humble one, Your servant. Praise be to that act. You bestowed upon me an endless flood of bliss. Praise be. Praise be to Your sacred feet that dance in the cosmic hall of Thillai." "Solluvathu Ariyen Vazhi Thotriya Thotram Pottri. Vallai Vandharuli Ennai Vazhithondu Kondai Pottri. Ellaiyil Inba Vellam Enakku Arul Seithai Pottri. Thillai Ambalatthul Aadum Sevadikkup Pottri" is Sekkizhar's verse. Sekkizhar sings that the Lord's appearance on the bull was like a golden mountain shining on a silver mountain. Here, the silver mountain is the bull vehicle. Since the bull is white, it is like a silver mountain. The Lord on it shines like a golden mountain. Sages describe the Lord's sacred form as coral-like. Here, Sekkizhar states that the Lord is like a golden mountain. The Lord's coral-like sacred body is adorned with pure white sacred ash, which is like milk. Therefore, Sekkizhar sings that the Lord appears golden, with the coral-like color of His sacred body mixed with the whiteness of the sacred ash. Here, the Lord appears with Uma Devi. It is evident that whenever the Lord comes to bestow grace upon living beings, He appears with Uma Devi, His Shakti. "Sattan Arul Tharil Shakti Arul Undaam. Shakti Arul Tharin Sattan Arul Undaam" (If the Sat (Lord) bestows grace, Shakti's grace will come. If Shakti bestows grace, Sat's grace will come) is from Thirumandiram, first Tantra, 27th section, "Kallunnamai," verse 220. "Udaiyaal Undhan Nadu Irukkum Udaiyaal Naduvil Nee Iruthi" (The Mistress is in your midst, you are in the midst of the Mistress) is from Thiruvachagam, Koyil Mootha Thiruppathigam, first verse. "Etthiram Nindran Eesan Atthiram Avalum Nirpal" (In whatever state the Lord stands, in that state she also stands) is from Sivagnana Siddhiyar, Pramana Iyal, second Sutra, verse 75. Thus, when the Lord comes to bestow grace upon the living beings of the world, He appears with Uma Devi, the Chitshakti. Such a vision on the bull is only possible in the realm of gnana akasha (space of knowledge). It is known only to perfected souls. IyarPakai Nayanar, seeing the Lord standing thus, says, "Solluvathu Ariyen Vazhi" (I do not know what to say). That is, IyarPakai Nayanar says that he is unable to express his joy at seeing the Lord suddenly reveal Himself to him. The state of the Lord is beyond words. That is why he says, "Solluvathu Ariyen." Next, he says, "Thotriya Thotram Pottri" (Praise be to the form that appeared). He praises the Lord for all the forms He has shown, which can be experienced by the five senses, both before and now. He praises the Lord for giving him this birth, which enabled him to become His servant. Next, he praises, "Ennai Vazhithondu Kondai Pottri" (Praise be that you made me your servant). He says, "You considered me as something, and bestowed upon me the grace of a traditional servant, and took me as your own. Praise be to You." Next, he praises the sacred feet that dance in the cosmic hall of Thillai. The dance in the cosmic hall of Thillai is the Chidambaram dance. That dance has five actions. The Lord here shows and gives IyarPakai Nayanar the action of Anugraham (bestowing grace), which is the final of the five actions. Therefore, he praises the sacred feet that perform the five actions of dance. What does the Lord reply to IyarPakai Nayanar, who praised Him thus? Let us continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 09 — IyarPakai Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. In the Periya Puranam, we are contemplating the Puranam of IyarPakai Nayanar. The Lord appears to IyarPakai Nayanar in the sky, in the Gnana Akasha, on a silver bull. Seeing Him, IyarPakai Nayanar praises and worships Him. After IyarPakai Nayanar praises and worships Him, the Lord says to IyarPakai Nayanar, "We are pleased to see the love you have shown towards us. O blameless one, come with your chaste wife and join us," and bestows His grace. Thus, bestowing liberation upon the holy devotee IyarPakai Nayanar and his divinely chaste wife, and after the celestials praised the Lord's divine grace, the Lord, who is eternally established, entered His eternal Chidambaram golden hall. Thus, the Lord, pleased by IyarPakai Nayanar's love, grants him liberation. It is not correct to say, as some commentaries explain, that the Lord tested the Nayanar and that the Nayanar fully passed the test, and that is why the Lord called him "IyarPakai Muniva." The Lord, being omniscient, does not need to test anyone to know anything. Therefore, such an interpretation is wrong. The Lord grants liberation to IyarPakai Nayanar and his chaste wife. How? He says, "Come with your wife and join us." IyarPakai Nayanar's wife had already gone to the Lord before him, guided by IyarPakai Nayanar. Therefore, He says, "You also come with your wife." Thus, because the wife accepted IyarPakai Nayanar's command and went with the Lord, she attained the Lord earlier. Following his wife, IyarPakai Nayanar also attains the Lord. Just as Pattinatthu Adigal's disciples, Bhadragiriyar and Sendanar, attained liberation before their guru, here the Nayanar's wife attains Shiva's grace before the Nayanar attains it. We should note this. As the celestials showered flowers, the Vedas chanted, and the wise sages praised, the blameless holy devotee IyarPakai Nayanar, in the auspicious Shiva Loka, attained the greatness of being eternally with the Lord, along with his wife who had already reached there before him. All the other relatives who fought with IyarPakai Nayanar and died attained the joy of the heroic heaven. The Shiva Loka they attained is the Shiva Loka, an Apara Mukti Sthana (a lesser state of liberation) that grants the bliss of Shiva. Residing with the Lord in Shiva Loka is to experience the bliss of Shuddhadvaita (pure non-duality), being inseparable from the Lord. Thus, IyarPakai Nayanar and his wife, due to their devotion to the Lord and His devotees, attained the permanent liberation of not being reborn. All the relatives who fought against them reached Devaloka, the celestial world, and experienced joy there. The relatives followed worldly dharma. IyarPakai Nayanar and his wife followed Shiva Dharma. The result of worldly dharma is ordinary Pasu Punya (merit for the individual soul). It grants enjoyment in the pleasure realms like Devaloka. The Shiva Dharma followed by IyarPakai Nayanar and his wife bestows Shiva Punya, which leads to liberation. Here, the relatives' opposition was also correct, according to worldly dharma. IyarPakai Nayanar's punishment of them was also correct, according to Shiva Dharma. Therefore, the relatives who followed worldly dharma received the joy of Devaloka as their reward, and IyarPakai Nayanar and his wife, who earned Shiva Punya through Shiva Dharma, received the liberation of Shiva Loka life. The relatives, following worldly dharma, were concerned about protecting themselves and their lineage from disgrace. They were willing to sacrifice their lives for this worldly dharma, and they did. Furthermore, they were punished by IyarPakai Nayanar's pure sword. Therefore, they attained Swarga Loka (heaven) and enjoyed its pleasures. IyarPakai Nayanar and his wife, who practiced Shiva Dharma, which is superior to worldly dharma, attained Lord Shiva and merged with Him, achieving the bliss of Shuddhadvaita, which is liberation. In this history of IyarPakai Nayanar, there are two sacred places: Kaveri Poompattinam and Thiruchaikkadu. When they reached Thiruchaikkadu from Kaveri Poompattinam, the Lord appeared to IyarPakai Nayanar and granted him Shiva Loka. This Thiruchaikkadu is also known as Kaveri Poompattinam Saikkadu and Puhar Saikkadu. This name is due to its proximity to Kaveri Poompattinam. Thiruchaikkadu is a Thevaram-sung sacred place. The temple here is one of the Maada temples (temples built on an elevated platform). Thiruchaikkadu is a place equated with Kasi. Six places are said to be equal to Kasi. Thiruchaikkadu is one of them. In the front hall of the Thiruchaikkadu temple, ancient idols of IyarPakai Nayanar and his wife are installed, and pujas are performed. Next, the town where IyarPakai Nayanar lived is called Puhar, Poompuhar, or Kaveri Poompattinam. This was one of the capitals of the ancient Cholas. It was one of the five great cities where they were crowned. Its glories are beautifully described in very ancient Tamil texts like Pattinappalai and Silappathikaram. Ancient Tamil texts reveal that prominent ancient temples for Lord Shiva and Lord Muruga existed in Kaveri Poompattinam. Silappathikaram mentions the temple of Lord Shiva as "Piravaa Yaakkai Periyon Koyil" (the temple of the great one with an uncreated body) and the temple of Lord Muruga as "Arumuga Sevvel Anithigazh Koyil" (the beautiful shining temple of the six-faced Muruga). "Nuthal Vizhi Naattathu Iraivan Mudhalaai Pathi Vazh Sathukkaththu Theivameera" (The Lord with the eye on His forehead, and the deities of the four intersections of the city) is from Manimekalai. Sivakkavimani's interpretation of IyarPakai Nayanar's Purana is as follows: The Kaveri river flows through the Chola country, enriching all regions, and then the remaining water, not wasted, flows into the sea. The sea, which cannot even cleanse the body, becomes a sacred water that cleanses the soul when the Kaveri joins it. Like this Kaveri, humans should always help others completely. They should not waste even a small part of their possessions but should use them to help others. Giving Shiva devotees what they desire without saying 'no' is the best Shiva Dharma, a Pati Dharma. Fulfilling the wishes of Shiva devotees and making them happy is the greatest blessing of householder life. Women should consider their husbands not just as lords of their bodies, but as lords of their souls, and live by doing whatever they say. That is the true state and glory of chastity and the right of women. Lineage and family welfare are to be cherished and protected. Protecting one's lineage from disgrace is very essential for all those who belong to that lineage. Protecting family welfare and protecting the family from disgrace is superior to life itself. One must protect one's honor even at the cost of life. In these worldly dharmas, desiring another's wife is a great disgrace for a man. A man giving his wife to another is a great disgrace for him. A wife living with another man is a great disgrace for her. These not only affect the respective families but also plunge the lineage and the country into great sin. One must uphold and protect the principles one holds, according to the Dharma Shastras. Shiva Dharmas are superior to worldly ethics and worldly virtues. Worldly virtues like householder life are to be understood within their worldly limits. Living in worldly life like water on a lotus leaf, without attachment, is the way for souls to improve.

The way for souls to improve. When attachments to wife, children, relatives, and other such relationships are severed in the presence of Shiva-related attachments, Shiva will immediately manifest and become our escort. The actions of those whose minds are controlled by Shiva's instruments like sacred ash and Rudraksha cannot be measured by ordinary worldly ethics. If living beings in this world place even a small part of the interest they have in worldly experiences and worldly objects on Lord Shiva, there will be a way for them to be saved. Shiva is the one who bestows grace upon those who approach Him. If one worships Shiva in the sacred guise of Shiva's devotees, Shiva Himself will stand there and bestow grace. Thus, Sekkizhar Swamigal, having narrated the history of IyarPakai Nayanar, continues as follows: Having described how IyarPakai Nayanar, a great devotee, lived by giving whatever Shiva devotees desired without hesitation, remembering only his servitude to the Lord's devotee, his wife, I now begin to praise the greatness of IyarPakai Nayanar's devotion and the greatness of his history, and with its help, I will now begin to praise the abundance of love of Maranayanar, who lived in the town of Ilaiyankudi, possessing lasting fame due to his great love for the Lord's devotees. Sekkizhar thus concludes the Puranam of IyarPakai Nayanar and now begins to narrate the sacred history of Ilaiyankudi Maranayanar. Let us also continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.
