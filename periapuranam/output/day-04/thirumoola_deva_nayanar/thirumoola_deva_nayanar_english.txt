Chapter 01 — ThiruMoola Deva Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 900, by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Vambaraa Varivandu Sarukkam. Thirumoola Deva Nayanar Puranam. The Puranam that follows Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar Puranam in Periya Puranam is Thirumoola Deva Nayanar Puranam. The Nayanar who is greatly renowned as Thirumoolar is our Thirumoola Deva Nayanar. There cannot be any Saivites who do not know Thirumoola Deva Nayanar and the sacred text called Thirumandiram, authored by Thirumoola Deva Nayanar. Beyond Saivites, even among those belonging to other traditions within the Hindu Sanatana Dharma, the influence of Thirumoola Deva Nayanar and his Thirumandiram is immense. Such a glorious Lord, our Embirān Thirumoola Deva Nayanar, is one of the 63 Nayanmar saints. The spiritual work known as Thirumandiram, authored by Thirumoola Deva Nayanar, is the tenth of the twelve Saivite Thirumurais, compiled by Nambiandar Nambigal Swamigal, and has been recited, taught, and heard by Saivite devotees for ages. Even recently, we see Thirumandiram being a subject of extensive research in educational institutions and universities. The Puranam of such a great Lord is known as Thirumoola Deva Nayanar Puranam. As we all know, the Thiruthondar Puranam, which narrates the sacred lives of the Nayanmars praised by Sundaramoorthy Swamigal in his Thiruthondathogai, was authored by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Therefore, we know that the divine Sekkizhar Peruman structured each of the 11 Thiruppadalgals in Thiruthondathogai as a separate Sarukkam. Among them, the fifth Thiruppadal begins with "Vambaraa Varivandu". The Sarukkam corresponding to that Thiruppadal is the Vambaraa Varivandu Sarukkam, which we are now contemplating. That fifth Thiruppadal begins with "Vambaraa Varivandu manam naara malarum madhumalar narkoṇṭraiyaan adiyallāl peṇṇā Embirān Sambandan adiyarkkum adiyēn". Accordingly, we first contemplated the Thirupuranam of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman in this Sarukkam. After that, it comes "Eyarkon Kalikkaman adiyarkkum adiyēn". Accordingly, we contemplated the Thirupuranam of Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar. Then it comes "Nambiran Thirumoolan adiyarkkum adiyēn". Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, in Thiruthondathogai, has praised Thirumoola Deva Nayanar by saying "Nambiran Thirumoolan adiyarkkum adiyēn". Therefore, in accordance with this Thogai Nool (collection), Nambiandar Nambigal Swamigal has also composed a Thiruppadal in the Vagai Nool (classification), Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi. As an elaboration of this, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman has authored Thirumoola Deva Nayanar Puranam as the 30th Puranam. Thus, according to the tradition of our commentator, he has provided commentary in the order of Thogai Nool, Vagai Nool, and then Viri Nool (elaboration). Accordingly, we will first contemplate the meaning of the part related to Thirumoola Deva Nayanar in the Thogai Nool. In the fifth Thiruppadal of Thiruthondathogai, which begins with "Vambaraa Varivandu", it states "Nambiran Thirumoolan adiyarkkum adiyēn". Its meaning is, "I am a servant to the devotees of Thirumoola Deva, who is the great one in our lineage of Gurus." Here, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal praises Thirumoola Deva Nayanar as "Nambiran". The meaning of this is that Thirumoola Deva Nayanar is the foremost in our lineage of Gurus. "Nam" refers to all those in the Saivite tradition. Why? It refers to all humanity, the entire assembly of people. Thus, it refers not only to the Saivite tradition but also to all humans as the foremost in the lineage of Gurus. This is because Thirumoola Deva Nayanar received initiation from Nandiyam Peruman. He was blessed to have Nandiyam Peruman as his Guru. Thirumoola Deva Nayanar received grace from Nandiyam Peruman, who is the primordial Guru for all our Guru lineages. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman is going to sing this as "Nandi Thiruvārul petra". Sundaramoorthy Swamigal sings "Nambiran Thirumoolan adiyarkkum adiyēn". The name "Thirumoolan" is the sacred name that came to this Nayanar. We are going to see the history of how this name came about in the Puranam. Thus, the meaning of "Nambiran Thirumoolan adiyarkkum adiyēn" is "I am a servant to the devotees of Thirumoola Deva, who is the great one in our lineage of Gurus." Now, in the Vagai Nool, Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi, Nambiandar Nambigal praises Thirumoola Deva Nayanar with the following Thiruppadal. This is the 36th Thiruppadal in Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi. "Kudimannu Saathanoor kokkula meippon kurambai pukku mudimannu koornirai piraiyaalan thannai muzhuttamizhinpadi mannu vedathin sorpadiye paravi ittu en uchchi adimanna vaitha piran moolanaagindra angganane" is the Thiruppadal from Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi. Its meaning is: "He is the compassionate one named Moolan, who, entering the body of Moolan, a cowherd from the prosperous and established Saathanoor, praised Lord Shiva, who wears the crescent moon on His head, according to the complete Tamil and the established Vedas, and placed His sacred feet on my head." Thus, Nambiandar Nambigal has very concisely, yet expansively enough for the entire Puranam to unfold, composed the Puranam of Thirumoola Deva Nayanar in this one Thiruppadal. "Kudi" refers to Saathanoor, a prosperous and well-established village. In that village, a cowherd named Moolan was tending cows. After his life departed, through the Siddhi of Parakaya Pravesham (entering another's body), he entered Moolan's body and praised Lord Shiva, who wears the crescent moon on His head, through Tamil, according to the words of the Vedas. The sacred text that he composed is Thirumandiram. Thirumoola Deva Nayanar is the one who authored that sacred text, Thirumandiram. "Tamizhinpadi" means "through Tamil". We see that all three thousand Thirumandirams are composed in four-lined viruthams of the same meter. Thirumoola Deva Nayanar composed this Thirumandiram according to the Vedas and Agamas. Even though it says "Vedathin" here, it refers to both Vedas and Sivagamas. Haradatta Sivachariyar says, "We have not found any difference between Vedas and Sivagamas." Thirumoola Deva Nayanar, in the section called "Payiram" (preface) to Thirumandiram, has spoken about the greatness of Vedas and Agamas. He says, "Agamam seppalutrene" (I began to expound the Agamas) and "Thirukkooththin seelaanga vedathai seppa vandene" (I came to expound the Vedas that are the essence of the sacred dance). Thus, Thirumoola Deva Nayanar combines Vedas and Agamas in Thirumandiram. Nambiandar Nambigal praises such a Thirumoola Deva Nayanar as "the Lord who placed His sacred feet on my head." The meaning of this is that he is the Guru who came first in the lineage of Gurus. Nambiandar Nambigal praises such a great Lord Thirumoola Deva Nayanar as "Angganane" (the compassionate one). "Anggannmai" refers to boundless compassion that extends to all beings. When the cowherd Moolan died, all the cows were distressed. Unable to bear their suffering, Thirumoola Deva Nayanar, out of compassion, entered the body of the cowherd Moolan and pacified the cows. This stands as proof of Thirumoola Deva Nayanar's compassion towards these beings. Thus, the essence of Thiruthondathogai (Thogai Nool) and Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi (Vagai Nool) praise Thirumoola Deva Nayanar in this manner. Now, let us begin to contemplate the Thirumoola Deva Nayanar Puranam, which is part of the Thiruthondar Puranam, also known as Periya Puranam (Viri Nool). Thirumoola Deva Nayanar Puranam is the section that describes the historical life and character of Thirumoola Deva Nayanar. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman elaborately narrates the Puranam of Thirumoola Deva Nayanar in 28 Thiruppadalgals. Lord Shiva revealed 28 Sivagamas. Thirumoola Deva Nayanar rendered these Sivagamas in Tamil as Thirumandiram. Therefore, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman narrating the Thirupuranam of Thirumoola Deva Nayanar in 28 Thiruppadalgals is yet another proof that Thiruthondar Puranam is a divine record. The first two Thiruppadalgals in Thirumoola Deva Nayanar Puranam are as follows: "Andhi ilam pirai kanni annalar Kayilaiyinil Mundai nigazh koyilukku mutharporu nayagamagi Indiran Mal Ayan muthalam imaiyavarkku neriyarulum Nandi thiruvarul petra nanmarai yogigal oruvar Matru avartham animadhi varum siddhi petru udaiyar Kotravanar Thirukkayilai malai nindrum kurunmuni pal Uttradhoru kenmaiyinal udan sila nal uraiyavarkku Natramizhin Podiyamalai nannutharku vazhikkondar" These are the first two Thiruppadalgals. Lord Shiva wears a garland of the young crescent moon that appears in the evening. The term "Piraikkanni" (crescent-adorned) indicates Lord Shiva's grace and His ability to foster the light of wisdom. The crescent moon, being on the Lord's sacred head, a high place, is like a lighthouse, calling those far away by showing its location and nature. Thirukkayilai mountain is a very high mountain. Lord Shiva, the great one, stands tall upon it. This crescent moon is also on His long sacred hair. Therefore, the Puranam of Thirumoola Deva Nayanar is the Puranam of one who came to spread the highest light of wisdom to the world and show the path. Thus, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman begins this Puranam. The ancient temple where such Lord Shiva resides is the ancient temple in Thirukkayilai. It is an eternal temple that will never be destroyed. Thirukkayilai is praised as Thirunodithan Malai. It is a realm of wisdom. Therefore, it is indestructible even during the dissolution of the universe. Nandiyam Peruman is the presiding deity of that temple in Thirukkayilai. "Koyil Nayagam" (Lord of the temple) implies one who has the authority to guard. Therefore, Nandiyam Peruman has the authority to decide when Vishnu, Brahma, Indra, and others who come to worship the Lord can enter to have darshan of Lord Shiva, and when they should be prevented from entering and guided. He is praised as Thirunandidevar, the foremost of all our Saivite Guru lineages. "Nandi" is a sacred name of Lord Shiva. "Nandam" refers to eternal, undiminishing bliss. Lord Shiva, who possesses this, is Nandi. He was the first to receive initiation from Lord Shiva. Therefore, Nandidevar received the sacred name Nandi, which is Lord Shiva's name. One great sage, a Sivayogi of the four Vedas, directly received initiation from such Thirunandidevar. He would later take the sacred name Thirumoola Devar. When he took that name and authored Thirumandiram, he stated in the Thirumandiram Payiram: "Nandi arul petra naathanai naadidin Nandigal nalvar Sivayoga mamuni Mandru thozhutha Pathanjali Vyagramar Endru ivan ennodu enmarumaam." (If one seeks the Lord who received Nandi's grace, there are four Nandis, the great Sivayogi, Pathanjali and Vyagramar who worshipped in the assembly, and I, making eight in total.) Therefore, one Sivayogi who directly received initiation from Nandiyam Peruman comes from Thirukkayilai. He was a great sage who had mastered Sivayoga as spoken of in the Vedas and Sivagamas. It says "yogigalul oruvar" (one among the yogis). "Oruvar" also means "one without equal." This Sivayogi, when he comes to this earthly realm, takes the name Thirumoola Devar after entering the body of the cowherd Moolan. Therefore, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman does not mention his name when he was in Thirukkayilai. Perhaps the divine Sekkizhar Peruman has stated this because it is not required here. Thus, the first two Thiruppadalgals in Thirumoola Deva Nayanar's Thirupuranam are structured, and the Puranam of Thirumoola Deva Nayanar begins in this manner. This Puranam begins with the letter 'A' in "Andhi". We saw that the first Thiruppadal begins with "Andhi ilam piraikkanni", didn't we? So, it begins with the letter 'A'. The concluding word in the final Thiruppadal of this Thirupuranam is "Saatravaam". Therefore, it concludes with the consonant 'M'. This Thirupuranam begins with 'A' and ends with 'M'. This signifies that this sacred history is meant to bestow knowledge in the form of Pranava. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 02 — ThiruMoola Deva Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 901, by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Vambaraa Varivandu Sarukkam. Thirumoola Deva Nayanar Puranam. We saw that there was a Sivayogi in Thirukkayilai. He had mastered the eight great Siddhis, such as Anima. The eight great Siddhis are Anima, Mahima, Laghima, Garima, Prapti, Prakamya, Ishatva, and Vashitva. Among these, Anima means taking a form smaller than an atom. Mahima means attaining greatness that pervades everywhere. Laghima means being light when lifted, even if it is as large as Mount Meru. Garima means being heavy as Mount Meru when lifted, even if it is like an atom. Prapti means having the power to go anywhere. Prakamya means entering another's body (Parakaya Pravesham), moving in the sky, obtaining all desired enjoyments in one's place, and knowing all things on earth and in heaven, and all three periods of time, by the light of one's body. Ishatva means acting like Ishan (Lord Shiva) by performing the three cosmic functions and making even the Devas serve. Vashitva means bringing any living being under one's control. The nature and details of these can be found extensively in the Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam, in the chapter on the exposition of Ashtama Siddhi. They can also be found extensively in Thirumandiram. Although Sivayogis do not desire these Siddhis, these Siddhis follow Sivayogis like a shadow, revealing their greatness to the world. For them, these are like a game. For Gnanis and Yogis in a state of wisdom, these are of no consequence at all. They are very minor matters. If so, why does the divine Sekkizhar Peruman say here that this Sivayogi, who received the grace of Nandiyam Peruman and was a Yogi of the four Vedas, possessed these minor Siddhis? It is because, in the narrative of Thirumoola Deva Nayanar, he is going to perform Parakaya Pravesham into the body of a cowherd to alleviate the suffering of the cows. Also, when he finds his own body hidden, he fully realizes that it is an act of divine grace by the Lord for him to expound the meaning of the Agamas in Tamil. Therefore, these events are going to happen later in the narrative. So, stating that he possessed such Siddhis now serves as a preface to the later events and will be a basis for their outcome. Therefore, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman mentions it here. Such a Sivayogi was a friend of Agathiyar Peruman. During the marriage of Lord Shiva and Goddess Parvati, the northern part of the earth rose, and the southern part sank. To balance the sunken southern part, Lord Shiva sent Agathiyar Peruman, who was a unique great sage, to the south. He sent him to Podigai Malai. So, this Sivayogi of ours was a friend of Agathiyar in Thirukkayilai before that. He wishes to go and stay with Agathiyar for a few days. Podigai Malai is a mountain in the south of the Pandya kingdom. It is the abode of Agathiyar Muni. Why did this mountain get the name Podigai Malai? Mahamahopadhyaya Tamil Thattha U.V. Swaminatha Iyer explains that it got this name because it is a common place for Devas and Munis. Agathiyar Muni resides in this Podigai Malai, which is rich in Tamil. So, this Sivayogi in Thirukkayilai wishes to meet Agathiyar Muni here in Podigai Malai and stay with him. If a Yogi residing in Thirukkayilai, where the Lord resides, wishes to leave Kayilai and go to the mountain where Agathiyar resides to stay with him for a few days, it explains to us the idea that devotees of Shiva and Sivayogis find greater bliss in being with devotees who have embraced Lord Shiva than in being with Lord Shiva Himself. However, this Yogi's wish was not fulfilled. The Lord, out of great compassion, desired that Thirumandiram, praised as Srimandiram, should be revealed for the salvation of beings. Therefore, in the event of this Yogi going to Podigai Malai where Agathiyar resides, the Lord, out of great compassion, prevented it from happening and was about to reveal the Thirumandiram necessary for the beings of the world. Accordingly, we are going to see in the upcoming history that the Lord kept this Yogi in Sivayoga for thousands of years in Thiruvaavaduthurai. Therefore, it is understood from this that no matter how great a person is, even if he is a Sivayogi, things happen only as per the Lord's divine will, and one's intentions do not always materialize. The words of Manikkavachagar Peruman, "Vendi neeyathu arul seithaai yaanum athuve vendinallal" (Whatever grace You bestowed, that alone I desired), come to our mind here. Now, the third Thiruppadal. The Sivayogi departs from Thirukkayilai. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman now sings about his pilgrimage to Bharatadesam. The third Thiruppadal: "Mannu Thirukkedaram vazhipattu mamunivar Pannu pugazh Pasupathi Nepaalathai panindu ethi Thunnusadai Sangaranar etra thoonneer Gangai Annamali aganturai neer arungkaraiyin marungu anainthar." The Yogi who departed from Kayilai comes to Thirukkedaram and worships Thirukkedaranathar. Thirukkedaram is a sacred place in the Himalayas. It is one of the northern sacred places praised in Thevaram. We contemplated in their respective Puranams that Thirugnanasambandha Peruman and Sundaramoorthy Swamigal sang about Thirukkedaram from Kalagasti. Such a Yogi comes to Thirukkedaram and worships Kedareswarar. Then he proceeds to Nepal, which is in the northeastern part of the Himalayas. This Nepal has been ruled for many years and ages by an independent lineage of kings. This country and its kings are very devoted to the Saivite tradition. They are very pious in Shiva worship and in the symbols of Shiva like sacred ash and Rudraksha. This is a country where Rudraksha grows. Many ancient ashrams of great sages still flourish in this sacred land. There are many places where very large Shiva Lingams are worshipped. Shivaratri is celebrated very grandly in this country. There are many rare Shiva temples. Such a Sivayogi comes to Pasupathi Nepal and worships the temples including Pasupathinatham. For Bhagiratha, the Lord received the Ganga in His sacred matted hair and made the Ganga flow for the liberation from the curse of Bhagiratha's ancestors. That is the northern region where Ganga flows. It is the land where Ganga flows, purifying the Sagaras and granting them salvation. It is a very prosperous land due to the Ganga. The Yogi visits the temples on the banks of the Ganga and bathes in the Ganga. The fourth Thiruppadal is as follows: "Gangai needurai aadi karuthurai neel kadaletrum Anganar tham magizhndharulum Avimuththam panindu ethi Mangul valar varaivu indha mannu Paruppatham irainji Thingalani sadaiyar Thirukkalathi malai sernthar." The Yogi bathes in the long ghats of the Ganga river and reaches a place called Avimuththam. It is the place known as Varanasi or Kasi. The phrase "Gangai neelthurai adinar" here indicates that he bathed in the long ghats of the Ganga in Kasi. Although there are many ghats and steps in the Ganga, this specific ghat particularly refers to the Manikarnika Ghat, which is opposite the shrine of the glorious Viswanatha Peruman. After bathing in such a ghat, the Yogi has darshan of Viswanatha Peruman in Avimuktam, Kasi. Kasi is also known as Avimukta Kshetra. "Muktam" means to depart. "Vi" denotes excellence. So, just as "Nayagan" becomes "Vinayagan" and "Nasam" becomes "Vinasam", "Muktam" means to depart, and "Vimuktam" means to depart specially. Before that, another "A" comes, which denotes negation. So, "Avimuktam" means to prevent the departure into this great ocean of birth. That is, its direct meaning is to abolish birth. A story is told from the Kasi Sthala Mahatmyam regarding this "Avimuktam". "Avimuktam" also means not departing even when it is time to depart. There is a story told in the Kasi Sthala Mahatmyam for this. There was a king named Divodanan in Kasi. He performed penance and received the right to rule Kasi from Lord Brahma. He ruled with a boon that Devas should not reside in that sacred place of Kasi. Therefore, to ensure that the boon did not fail, Lord Shiva Himself resided there in an unmanifested form and bestowed grace. It is said that Kasi got the name Avimuktam because of this, according to a story in the Kasi Sthala Mahatmyam. After worshipping Kasi and visiting all the temples, he travels south and worships the sacred place called Thiruparuppatham, also known as Srisailam. It is a place where Lord Shiva always resides permanently on that mountain, which is Nandi. He worships the Lord there and then reaches the sacred hill of Thirukkalathi, the Lord's abode. Now, the fifth Thiruppadal: "Needu Thirukkalathi nilavu Thaanuvai vanangi Aadu Thiruvarangaan Aalavanam thozhuthu ethi Thedum iruvarkku ariyaa Thiru Ekambaram panindu Maaduyar mamathil Kanchi valanagarin vaikinar." The Yogi comes to Thirukkalathi and worships Lord Sthanu, who is permanently enshrined on the hill there. Then, he departs from there and comes to Thiruvalangadu, which is one of the five great halls where the Lord performs His divine dance, the Ratna Sabha. It is known as Aalavanam. It is praised as Vadaranaya Kshetra. The Sivayogi comes to Thiruvalangadu, where the Ratna Sabha is located. We saw that Thiruvalangadu Ratna Sabha is one of the five halls where the Lord performs His divine dance. The other halls are Chidambaram, which is Kanaka Sabha (Golden Hall); Madurai, which is Rajatha Sabha (Silver Hall); Thirunelveli, which is Thamira Sabha (Copper Hall); and Thirukutralam, which is Chithira Sabha (Picture Hall). So, Thiruvalangadu Ratna Sabha is one of these halls, a place of great glory where Karaikkal Ammaiyar always remains beneath the Lord's raised foot. He comes there and worships Vadaranayeswarar. Then he comes to Kanchipuram and worships Thiru Ekambaram. "Ambaram" means mango tree. "Ekambaram" means one mango tree. It is a unique mango tree. It is a mango tree in the form of the Vedas. The four branches of this mango tree are the four types of Vedas; the Vedas themselves stand as this mango tree in this sacred place. It is a place where the Goddess worships the Lord under this mango tree. Such a Sivayogi worships the Lord in Ekambaram in sacred Kanchipuram. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 03 — ThiruMoola Deva Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 902, by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Vambaraa Varivandu Sarukkam. Thirumoola Deva Nayanar Puranam. The Sivayogi, who departed from Thirukkayilai, comes to Thirukanchipuram and worships Ekambaram. The sixth Thiruppadal: "Narpati angu amar yoga munivargalai nayanthu poi Karpurisai Thiruvathigai kalanthu irainji karaikandar Arputha koothu aadugindra Ambalam soozh Thiruveethi Porpathiyam Perumparrappuliyoor vandhu anainthar." Kanchipuram is referred to as "Narpati" (good city) in this Thiruppadal. This is because it is the place where Kamakshi Ammaiyar performs the compassionate act of demonstrating Shiva Puja to beings and enabling them to attain salvation, hence it is a good city. Moreover, Kamakshi Ammaiyar fosters and performs all dharmas there, making it a good city. Many munis with firm minds in Sivayoga reside there. Therefore, our Sivayogi also gladly stays with these yogic munis. He is also a Sivayogi. He is going to meet Agathiyar Muni due to his friendship with him. However, since there were yogic munis equal to this Sivayogi in Kanchipuram, he stays with them for a few days. After staying there, he then goes to Thiruvathigai Veerattanam and worships the Lord. This sacred place is where the Lord performed heroic deeds by shooting down the three fortresses. After worshipping the Lord there, he departs and reaches Chidambaram, also known as Perumparrappuliyoor, a golden city with sacred streets surrounding the Thirumandiram where the Lord, who has a blue throat, performs His wondrous divine dance. The dance performed by the Lord there is a wondrous dance. "Arputham" (wonder) means being new and fresh every time it is seen. The hall where the Lord dances is called Ambalam. The sacred street that encompasses this Ambalam is Thiruveethi, the sacred street of Thillai. The city that contains this sacred street is Chidambaram, the golden city. Chidambaram is a golden city. It is a sacred place where many works of gold have been done. Devas, Hiranyavarman, and others performed renovations with gold for the Golden Hall. Hence, it shines as a golden city. It is a great hall renovated by the Chola king who adorned it with pure gold. It is a place praised as "Pottrillai Koothu" (Golden Thillai Dance) in the Thirumandiram section. Some also refer to Thiruthillai as Hiranyamaya Kosam. Such a place is Chidambaram, also known as Perumparrappuliyoor. Perumparrappuliyoor is another sacred name for Chidambaram. This sacred place creates great attachment even for ascetics who have renounced all other attachments, hence it is Perumparrappuliyoor. The Yogi arrives there. Now, the seventh Thiruppadal: "Evvulagum uyya eduthu aruliya sevadiyarai Sevviya anpura vanangi sinthai kalivara thilaithu Vavviya meyyunarvin kan varum aananda koothai Avviyalbil kumbittu angu araamai amarndhirundhar." This is the seventh Thiruppadal. The Lord of Dance, who has raised His sacred foot for the salvation of all worlds, resides in Chidambaram. He performs the divine dance. It is a place where the five cosmic functions are performed for the salvation of all worlds, including the heavens, the earth, and others, and for the salvation of all beings in these worlds. It is a place that lifts one out of the great pit of birth. The Sivayogi deeply contemplates the nature of the five cosmic functions performed by the Lord, focusing on His raised sacred foot, and immerses himself in bliss. To immerse oneself in bliss after realizing the limits of the instruments and senses is one state. But beyond that, the experience of Sivabodha manifests in a state of lost self-awareness. It is a Sivabodha experience that transcends the knower, knowledge, and the known. It is a great experience of Sivananda without the triad. Becoming one with that Sivabodha state, the Sivayogi experiences the Lord there. This is the state of Sivananda experienced by Yogis in a timeless state of Shiva Anubhuti. In this same state, the Sivayogi is going to remain in Thiruvaavaduthurai for three thousand years. Thus, after having darshan in Chidambaram, he remains there, worshipping the Lord, unable to feel fully satisfied. Now, the eighth Thiruppadal: "Thadanilai maligai Puliyoor thannil urainthu irainji poi Adal vidaiyin mel varuvar amuthu sei anjadhe Vidam alithathu ena karuthi methinikku valam niraiththe Kadal vayiru niraiyaatha Kaaviriyin karai anainthar." In this Thiruppadal, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman praises the Kaveri. The Sivayogi, after staying and worshipping in Perumparrappuliyoor, which is filled with grand mansions, now reaches the bank of the Kaveri. What kind of Kaveri is this? It is the Kaveri river that gives all its wealth to this land and carries only a small remaining portion of water to the sea. By carrying such a small amount of water to the sea, the sea's belly remains unfilled. The nature of the Kaveri, which does not fill the sea's belly, is praised here. This is because, considering that the sea fearlessly offered poison to the Lord who comes on the powerful bull, the Kaveri gives most of its wealth, including water resources and other resources, to the lands it flows through, and only then does it give the remaining small portion of water to the sea. Unlike other rivers, the Kaveri does not merge into the sea. All its water wealth, from its very origin, is primarily used to enrich the lands. Only a very small amount of water flows into the sea near Kaveri Poombattinam at the confluence of the Kaveri. Why does the Kaveri do this? To emphasize the rule that one should not associate with those who have committed offenses against Shiva. Thiruvichaippa says, "Kanaa kan vaai pesatha peygalode" (with sightless eyes and speechless demons). Thiruvachagam says, "Avarai kandal ammana anju maare" (If I see them, oh mother, how I fear). Therefore, in accordance with the divine word that one should not associate with those who have committed offenses against Shiva, the Kaveri carries only a small amount of residual water to the sea because the sea gave poison to the Lord. Based on this same idea, in Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam, Paranjothi Munivar, in the chapter where he instructs Vadavoor Adigal, praises the Vaigai river in a similar manner. All rivers flow into the sea. But only the Vaigai river does not flow into the sea. It flows into a large tank called Periya Kanmai in Ramanathapuram. This is because the sea gave poison to the Lord. Therefore, the Vaigai river, out of devotion and detachment, flows into that large tank in Ramanathapuram, thinking, "I will not join the sea." This Thiruppadal sung by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman serves as the basis for Paranjothi Munivar's song. Now, the ninth and tenth Thiruppadalgals, which are a continuous pair. "Kaaviri neer perum theertham kalanthu aadi kadanthu eri Aavin arum kandru uraiyum Aavaduthan thurai anainthu Sevil varum Pasupathiyar sezhung koyil valam vanthu Mevu perum kaathalinaal paninthu angu viruppuruvar Annilai thanathai agalathathoru karuthu Munni ezhum kurippinaal moolum aadharavu eytha Pinnum agandru eguvar pena varum kokkulangal Ponni nadhikarai puravil pulambuvana ethirkandar." The Sivayogi bathes in the great sacred water of the Kaveri. "Theertham" means that which purifies. But Kaveri water should not be considered ordinary river water. It purifies not only the body but also the inner self. That is why the divine Sekkizhar Peruman says "Kaaviri neer perum theertham" (the great sacred water of Kaveri) instead of just "Kaveri neer" or "Kaveri theertham". While water cleanses the body, the sacredness of Kaveri water also purifies the soul. After bathing in such Kaveri water, the Yogi crosses the Kaveri and ascends its southern bank. On that southern bank is the sacred place called Thiruvaavaduthurai. We have contemplated in the history of Thiruvaavaduthurai that Goddess Uma, at one time, came here with a cow, performed penance, shed her cow form, worshipped the Lord, and resides here. Because of this, the Lord also has the name Pasupathiyar. "Aavaduthurai" can be split as "Aa aduthurai", where "Aa" means cow-nature (pasutvam), and "aduthal" means to remove. Therefore, Thiru Aavaduthurai means the place where the pasutvam of beings is removed. For the same reason, it also has another name, "Kokkazhi". There resides Pasupathiyar, the Lord of all beings. He is the Lord of Goddess Uma, who came and worshipped here as a cow. He is also the Lord of all beings who possess pasutvam. Therefore, He resides as Pasupathiyar. "Komuktheeswarar" is the sacred name of the Lord of this temple. "Maasilamaneeswarar" is another sacred name. The Yogi reaches such Thiruvaavaduthurai. There, he circumambulates and worships the Lord's glorious temple. As the Lord's grace enlightened this Yogi from within, a great love for this sacred place of Thiruvaavaduthurai arose in him. Therefore, while worshipping in that temple, the thought arose in the Yogi's mind that he should not leave this place. This thought arose due to the Lord's grace. Therefore, the desire to stay in this place arose in the heart of this Sivayogi. We see that the Sivayogi departed from Thirukkayilai and has been visiting various sacred places. Just as he worshipped other places on the way and departed from them, he should have departed from Thiruvaavaduthurai as well. But the thought arose in the Yogi's heart that he should not leave Thiruvaavaduthurai but stay here. This thought arose in his mind without his conscious effort. Even though this thought arose, the Yogi tries to leave the place. At that time, the Yogi sees herds of cows lamenting on the bank of the Kaveri river. These herds of cows were brought for grazing and left to graze in the pasture on the Kaveri river bank. This place is called "puravu". That is, small forests filled with grass, plants, etc., where cows graze and stay on the river bank, are called "puravu". This is a grazing ground. In common parlance, it is called "meichal purambokku" (grazing wasteland). In that land, those herds of cows are lamenting. They are crying out. They are calling out "Amma" (mother). They are shedding tears. They are hanging their heads. The Sivayogi sees this. The 11th Thiruppadal: "Anthar tham Saathanoor aa meippar kudithondri Munthai murai nirai meippan Moolan ennum peyarudaiyan Vanthu thani meikkindran vinai maala vazhnalai Venthozil valvidam unna veedi nilathidai vizhnthan." In this Thiruppadal, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman reveals the reason why those herds of cows are crying. Saathanoor is a village near Thiruvaavaduthurai. It is a village where many Brahmins live. It is said to be a village located to the south of Thiruvaavaduthurai. There is also a custom of referring to it as "Santhaiyur ani Aavaduthurai" (Santhaiyur near Aavaduthurai). It is a village filled with excellent Brahmins. We see in Thiruvichaippa by Senthanar Peruman, dedicated to Thiruvaavaduthurai, that the Brahmins of this village serve the Lord in Thiruvaavaduthurai. It says, "Poiyatha vediyar Santhai meip pugazhalar aayiram poosurar meiye thiruppani sei seer" (The truthful Brahmins of Santhai, a thousand renowned Brahmins, truly perform sacred service). It also says, "Sothi mathilani Santhai meichurudhi vidhi vazhiyor thozhum Aadhi amarar puranan" (The ancient Lord of the Devas, worshipped by those who follow the Vedic rules, in Santhai adorned with shining walls). And "Oli ontrilaa Ponni theerthamum munikkodi kodiya moorthiyum Azhivontrilaa ellam selva Santhayur ani Aavaduthurai aadinaan" (He danced in Thiruvaavaduthurai, adorned with Santhayur, which has the incomparable Ponni water, countless crores of sages, and imperishable wealth). These lines appear in the first, second, and eighth Thiruppadalgals of Thiruvichaippa. Therefore, Saathanoor and Santhai refer to the same place. It is a village where many Brahmins live. There, a cowherd named Moolan used to graze cows. This Moolan, without any other help, used to bring these cows and graze them in this pasture. When his physical life reached its end, and all the karmic experiences he brought with him were exhausted, he died due to snake venom and fell to the ground. The reason for his sudden death that day, after bringing the cows from his home to graze, is understood to be snake venom. "Vidam unnal" (consuming poison) is the external cause for the arrival of that fate. In reality, it happened because his karma was exhausted, meaning the karmic experience he had to undergo was completed. This is the internal reason. So, he died, and his body lay there, while all the cows he brought were distressed. This Yogi, who possessed great penance, sees this. He understands what has happened by the grace of the Lord. This Sivayogi resolves in his heart that he will alleviate the suffering of these cows. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 04 — ThiruMoola Deva Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 904, by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Vambaraa Varivandu Sarukkam. Thirumoola Deva Nayanar Puranam. Thiruppadalgals 12 and 13. "Matru avan than udambinaiye akkokkulangal vanthu anainthu Sutri miga katharuvana suzhalvana moppanavaga Natrava yogiyar kanaa Nambar arulale Aavutra thuyar ivai neenga ozhippan ena unargindrar. Ivan uyir petru ezhi anri aakkal idar neenga endru avan udalil tham uyirai adaivikka arul puriyum thavamunivar tham udalukku aran seithu tham muyandra pavana vazhi avan udalil tham uyirai paichinar." All those herds of cows gather around Moolan's body. They cry out, circle around, sniff, and grieve. This Yogi, who possesses great penance, sees all this. These actions indicate the great love the cows have for their herdsman. Sages often cite cows as examples of pure, guileless love and a state of melting heart. "Katraavin manam pola kasindhuruga venduvane" (I wish to melt like the heart of a cow that has calved) is a sacred saying from Thiruvachagam. We see many other sacred sayings that cite cows as examples of pure, guileless love. Therefore, through the actions of these cows, we understand that the cowherd had nurtured and protected them with great love. We have already seen in the Chandeesa Nayanar Puranam the great love the cows had for Chandeesa Nayanar, who nurtured them with love, and the events that transpired because of it. The Sivayogi thinks in his mind, "I will alleviate the suffering of these cows." Why did this feeling come to the Sivayogi? This thought came to the Sivayogi by the grace of Lord Shiva, who is the Lord. This is because it is not appropriate for a Yogi of such high stature, who was on his way from Kayilai to meet the great sage Agastya in Podigai Malai, to focus his mind on the joys and sorrows seen among the people of this world and to suffer himself by being subject to these joys and sorrows. This world, the beings in it, and these people will experience a mixture of joy and sorrow. It is for experiencing these karmic results that the Lord makes these people come here. Therefore, the Lord bestows joy and sorrow upon each being according to their karmic differences. Therefore, birth, death, joy, and sorrow are all ordained by the Lord to make beings experience the fruits of their past actions. Therefore, great souls would not think of interfering and saying, "I will revive all the dead, I will cure all the sick." People belonging to other traditions might propagate such ideas. But those are actions of the ignorant. However, our great Paramacharyas have also performed some miracles. We have been seeing them in this Thirupuranam. Thirunavukkarasu Peruman cured the snake venom that bit the son of Appoodhi Adigal Nayanar. Thirugnanasambandha Peruman transformed bones into a woman. He cured the disease of Muyalagan. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal cured the hunchback and blindness of those attendants. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal brought back the child who was caught in the crocodile's mouth. But the underlying meaning and intention of these are different. These are all acts of divine grace. The sacred texts say, "Naan azhiya vallathanal than seyyum thanmaigal aakki idum anbarkku aran" (Because I am capable of destruction, I create the qualities that protect my devotees). All these acts of divine grace, these miraculous deeds, are the Lord's actions. The Lord made our Paramacharyas perform them. Therefore, we have seen that we cannot investigate the reasons for the Lord's actions. Here, the Sivayogi's action and intention in coming here are different. But now, the thought that arises in his heart is different. This thought arose in his heart due to divine grace. What is the Lord's divine intention? It is not merely to alleviate the suffering of those cows. Thirumandiram must be revealed to guide the cows, which are the beings in this world, to overcome the suffering of birth and attain salvation. That is why the Lord inspires the Yogi's heart in this manner. Therefore, the thought that arose in the Yogi to alleviate the suffering of these cows, out of compassion for them, is a pretext. The true purpose is to bestow the sacred text Thirumandiram upon the beings of this world, who are like cows, so that they may overcome the suffering of birth and attain the bliss of liberation. This event is an act of divine grace by the Lord through this Yogi. That is why the divine Sekkizhar Peruman states that he understands it "Nambar arulale" (by the grace of the Lord). Here, we see the cows suffering from the loss of their caretaker and wishing to regain him. Similarly, in this world, ripe souls seek the path they should take, the way they should go, lamenting that they cannot see their Lord, and that they cannot find the way to attain their Lord. The Lord's divine intention is that the book Thirumandiram should be bestowed upon these suffering souls through the Yogi as spiritual instruction. That is the inner meaning of this narrative. Therefore, this Yogi thinks that the suffering of these cows will not cease unless this cowherd comes back to life. He considers that the only way to alleviate the cows' suffering is for the cowherd to come back to life. To make the cowherd come back to life, bringing his own soul back is one way. But the Yogi did not wish for that soul to return for the sake of that Moolan, that cowherd. He wishes for the cowherd to rise for the sake of alleviating the suffering of those cows. Moreover, the cowherd's lifespan was over. All the karmas he had brought were exhausted. Therefore, bringing him back again would not be the right thing to do. It would also be an unnecessary act. So, the Yogi's intention was to make him come back to life for a short while, just for the sake of these cows, alleviate their suffering, take them back to their respective homes, and then return to his own body as before. Therefore, he decides to transfer his soul into the cowherd's body for this purpose. The Yogi intends to enter the cowherd's body with his soul, take the cows to their respective homes with that body, and then return to his own body. Therefore, until this task is completed, he keeps his own body very safely. While this Yogi is in the cowherd's body, he ensures that his own body is protected from harm by animals like foxes, eagles, dogs, and other people, and then the Yogi transfers his soul into the cowherd's body. The Yogi did this through the technique of controlling the prana vayu, which he had mastered. This is an act that can be performed through the Siddhi called Prakamya. This is also called Parakaya Pravesham or "koodu vittu koodu paithal" (transmigration of the soul from one body to another). This is the ability of a soul to enter another body. Sivagnanabodham, the second Sutra, third Adhikaranam, is the section that proves that souls are reborn. It is the section that emphasizes that souls take many births according to their karma. In it, Sri Meykandadevar Nayanar mentions Parakaya Pravesham as an example. Here, the soul of the cowherd, whose karma was finished and lifespan ended, departed according to its karmic state. Therefore, this Yogi intended to use this body only as a tool to alleviate the suffering of the cows, without any karmic connection or involvement. Therefore, just as water, which naturally flows downwards, is poured from one pot to another, he transferred his soul from above into the cowherd's body, which lay on the ground. Thus, he arose as Thirumoolar. The 14th Thiruppadal: "Paithiyapin Thirumoolarai ezhalum pasukkal ellam Naathazhumba nakki moanthu anainthu kanaippodu nayanthu Vaiththezhuntha kalippinaal valeduthu thulli pin Neetha thuyarinavagi niranthu poi meinthinaval." After transferring his soul, the Yogi arose as Thirumoolar. The body, when it lay fallen, is referred to as Moolan, and when it arose with life, it is referred to as Thirumoolar by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Therefore, although the body is one, it received two names, one superior and one inferior, due to the difference in the soul within. Thus, the truth we learn here is that the greatness of a body is measured by the greatness of the soul within it. We previously saw how the cows cried out due to sorrow. Now, all the cows are filled with great joy. They approach their master and show affection, lick him, sniff him, express joy with moans, wag their tails and jump. Then they go to graze. Thus, the Sivayogi arose as Thirumoolar. The previous body was poisoned and had a toxic nature. So, is that body now fit for the Yogi to reside in? What happened to that toxicity? If such questions arise, the poison that caused the death of the cowherd Moolan was due to his karma. That life ended. That is, the purpose was for that soul to leave that body. The disease served as the instrument for that. After this event, there was no need for that cause. Therefore, that disease has no connection to affecting another soul that has now entered that body. So, there is no harm from it. Now, after this Yogi entered this body, it is the Lord's will that the Yogi should remain in this body for thousands of years in yoga and compose Thirumandiram. So, it is correct to say that the divine will itself made that body suitable for it. Even if the cowherd had not died, this body might have served as an instrument for his soul for a few more years. But if this Yogi, after entering, makes this body remain as an instrument for many years without decaying, we understand that it is due to the nature of the Yogi's soul and the nature of divine grace. The 15th Thiruppadal: "Aavin nirai magizhuvurak kandu ali koornthu arulinarai Meviya avai meividathu pinsendru meinthavai tham Kaaviri mun thurai thanneer kalanthu undu karaiyera Pooviri than puravin nizhal inithaga purangathar." Seeing the cows filled with joy as described before, the Yogi, filled with immense compassion, leads them to all the places where they graze. The Yogi performed this act desiring that the cows should be free from suffering and be joyful, and to witness it. Therefore, seeing it happen, the Yogi himself rejoices. The Yogi, filled with compassion for the cows, rejoices. He follows the cows to their grazing grounds and guides them. This is to protect them from any fear or suffering even in their grazing grounds. After the cows graze, they drink the clear water flowing in the front ghats of the Kaveri. Generally, cows drink water from the ghats on the river bank. If they go into the middle, it is deep, and the water flows fast. So, they cannot drink water peacefully. Therefore, it is natural for cows to stand on the bank and drink water from the front ghats. Although it is said here that they drink water from the Kaveri, there are many distributaries of the Kaveri in that area. So, the river flowing near Saathanoor is considered to be the Veerasozhan river, one of the distributaries of the Kaveri. When cows graze, they spread out into many groups and graze. They go and find food, eating as much grass as they need. But when they come to the water ghat to drink water, all the herds of cows gather together to drink water; this is their nature. Due to the availability of good water for all cows in one place, they have the habit of drinking water in that manner. Therefore, cows and other animals graze separately. Then they gather to drink water. Then they sit in the shade and chew their cud. When evening comes, they slowly walk towards their homes, thinking of their calves. These are all the habits of cows and their way of life. After eating grass and drinking water, and satisfying their hunger and thirst, cows naturally rest in shaded areas, chewing their cud. We have seen all these in the Puranams of Chandeesa Nayanar and Aana Nayanar. Thus, the Yogi protects the cows in the shade during midday, preventing them from being affected by the heat of the sun. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 05 — ThiruMoola Deva Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 904, by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Vambaraa Varivandu Sarukkam. Thirumoola Deva Nayanar Puranam. Thirumoola Deva Nayanar spends the entire day grazing and guarding the cows. The day ends, and evening arrives. The 16th Thiruppadal: "Veyya sudar kathiravanum melai malai anaiya Saiva neri mei unarnthor aaninangal thame mun Paiya nadappana kandrai ninainthu padarvanavagi Vaiyanigal Saathanoor vanthu eytha pin ponar." The sun, with its fierce rays, reaches the western mountain. As it is evening, the cows return from their grazing grounds to their respective homes. It is the nature of cows to go to their own place by habit, without anyone else driving them. The cows have grazed to their fill. So, they walk very slowly. Moreover, walking slowly is also the nature of cows. Some cows, those that have calved, walk a little faster, thinking of their calves. Walking thus, all the cows go to Saathanoor, which is renowned in this world. The cows all go to Saathanoor, which is very famous in the world due to the presence of Brahmins and the significance of this narrative. This Yogi did not even know that these cows belonged to Saathanoor or that it was Saathanoor. But if he follows the cows, they will go to their own village and home by their natural instinct. So, this Yogi follows the cows. The 17th and 18th Thiruppadalgals: "Ponavar tham pasukkal ellam manai thorum puga nindrar. Manamudai manaiyalum vaigiya pin thazhthar endru Aana payathudan sendre avar nindra vazhi kandal. Eanam ivarkku aduthathu ena meithinda atharku isaiyar Angu avalum makkaludan arunjutram illathal Thangi veruvura mayangi en seithir ena thalara Ingu enakku unnudan anaivu ondru illai ena ethirmaruthu Pongu thavathor aangor pothumadathinul pugunthar." As mentioned before, Thirumoola Deva Nayanar, who followed the cows, stands at the boundary until all the cows enter their respective homes. All the cows gathered together up to the village boundary. After reaching the village boundary, the cows started to disperse to go to their respective homes. Thirumoola Deva Nayanar, who had followed the cows until then, had nothing more to do. So, he stands there, watching the cows go to their homes. At that time, the wife of the cowherd Moolan arrives there. She is a woman of great chastity and honor. She had been waiting for her husband Moolan, who was delayed even after dusk. Now, seeing the cows returning home, she sees Thirumoola Deva Nayanar standing there. But her inner intuition tells her that something has happened to her husband. So, she approaches him with fear. Thinking that some harm has befallen him, the wife, mistaking him for her husband, tries to touch his body to take him home. But Thirumoola Deva Nayanar, who was in the cowherd's body, did not consent. The wife of Moolan laments, "What have you done?" and her heart sinks. To that, Thirumoola Deva Nayanar says, "I have no connection with you here," and rejects her. After rejecting her, he goes and stays in a public monastery in that village. This public monastery is a place where everyone in the village has the right to stay. He, Thirumoola Deva Nayanar, who is a great ascetic, goes and stays there. He was a great ascetic who had performed great penance earlier in Kayilai as a Sivayogi. Moreover, he is also a great ascetic who will perform great penance for a long time under the Bodhi tree in Thiruvaavaduthurai to bestow grace upon the world. Therefore, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman praises him as "Pongu thavathor" (one with overflowing penance). Now, the 19th, 20th, and 21st Thiruppadalgals follow consecutively. "Illalan iyalbu veru aanamai kandu iravellam Solladathu irunthavar pal anaiyathu thuyilathal Pallar mun pitrai nal avarkku adutha parisuraikka Nallargal avar thiram the nadiyae nayanthuraipar Pithutra mayal andru perithoru sarbu ullathu andru Sitha vikarpam kalainthu thelintha Sivayogathil Vaitha karuthinaragi varambil perumaiyil irunthar Itthagaimai alapparithal yaralum ena uraippar Patrarutha upadesa paramar patham petrar pol Mutrum unarnthana aagum unnai nilaimaiyil ungal Sutra iyalbu enakku eythar endru uraippa thuyarai eythi Matru avalum maiyalura marungul ullor kondu agandrar." The woman, realizing that her husband's nature had changed, spent the entire night without speaking to anyone, unable to approach her husband, and unable to sleep. She was tormented all night, unable to sleep, thinking that her husband, who had maintained a marital relationship until then, was now rejecting it and acting differently, and also because she could not be with her husband. Then, the next morning, she goes to all the elders in that place and tells them all these things. The people in that place are good people. They are good people who cannot bear to see others' suffering and act with compassion. She goes to them and says, "Look at my husband's nature," and tells them everything that happened. They also go and see. Seeing that Yogi, all the good people in that place say, "Woman, this is not a delusion caused by madness, nor is it a delusion caused by some other external influence. He has overcome all mental distortions and is in a clear state, immersed in Sivayoga, possessing immeasurable greatness. His nature cannot be measured by anyone." Thus, the good people in that village, realizing the nature of Thirumoola Deva Nayanar, explain it to the woman. She had told the people in the village that her husband's intellect had changed, hadn't she? So, for a change in intellect to occur, there must be two types of causes: internal and external. At least one of these two types of causes must be present. One is the internal cause, where the intellect becomes distorted due to excessive bile or other diseases within the body. The other is the external cause, such as affliction by ghosts or other entities. So, in these two ways, a person's intellect can become distorted and deviate. These good people, seeing the Sivayogi, tell the woman that none of these are present. They say that this Siddhar, this Yogi, is in a clear state of Sivayoga, having overcome mental distortions. "Chitta vikarpam" refers to the delusion that arises when the mind follows the deceptive senses. "Vikarpam" means to mistake one thing for another. To mistake falsehood for truth and truth for falsehood. Thirukkural describes this as "marul" (delusion) that perceives non-existent things as existent. They say that this Yogi, Thirumoola Deva Nayanar, has overcome all such mental distortions and is in a clear state of Sivayoga. "Sivayogathil vaitha karuthu" means one whose mind is immersed in and inseparable from the Sivagnanam (divine knowledge) of Lord Shiva. The wife, seeing the Yogi's state, concluded that harm had befallen him. What she said was that the Yogi's changed mental actions and external appearance were perceived in the same way by those who saw them. But the good people in that place, seeing the Sivayogi's state, realized that it was not a low state, not a base state arising from worldly attachments, but an immeasurably great state of divine attachment. They also say that this state cannot be measured by anyone else. Even though they could not understand the subtlety that the Sivayogi's soul had entered this body through parakaya pravesham, they recognized his greatness by observing his state. Thirumoola Deva Nayanar is a Gnani who has realized everything at once, like a Jivanmukta who has attained the Lord's feet through spiritual instruction that severs both types of attachments. Those good people also console the woman by saying that he will not be in the nature of your family relationship according to his previous state. "Upadesam" (instruction) comes from the spiritual instruction of a Satguru. It comes from the spiritual instruction of a Gnana Guru. They may not know the truth that he received the grace of Nandi, but due to that instruction, he is a Jivanmukta whose nature is to be one with Shiva's feet in this very birth, possessing undivided knowledge and having forgotten the world. He is one who has attained complete knowledge. He is one who has attained the true knowledge that knows everything. Realizing all this, they console the woman in this manner. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 06 — ThiruMoola Deva Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 905, by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Vambaraa Varivandu Sarukkam. Thirumoola Deva Nayanar Puranam. Thiruppadalgals 22 and 23. "Innilaiyil irunthar ezhunthirunthu aangu aaniraigal Vantha neriye sendru vaitha kaappinil uytha Munthai udal porai kaanaar muzhuthunarntha meignana Sinthaiyinil vantha seyal aaraainthu theligindrar. Thannilavar sadaiyar tham thantha aagamap porulai Mannin misai Thirumoolar vaakinaal Tamil vaguppa Kanniya aththiruvarulaal avvudalai karappikka Enniraintha unarvudaiyar Eesar arul ena unarnthar." These are the Thiruppadalgals. After all these events transpired – the Sivayogi, intending to alleviate the suffering of the cows, performed parakaya pravesham and led the cows to their homes in the village in the cowherd's body; the cowherd's wife came to touch him, but he rejected her; he stayed in a public monastery; and the villagers gathered, saw the Yogi's state, and advised the woman – Thirumoola Deva Nayanar, who was in the public monastery, rose from there and followed the path the herds of cows had taken. He follows the path the cows took, intending to find his body and re-enter it. He does not find his previous body, which he had kept safely. When he goes to the place where his previous body was, he does not find it. Here, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman refers to the body as "udal porai" (burden of the body). That is, it means the burden that is the body. For Yogis who seek liberation, even the body appears as a burden. Therefore, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman refers to it as a burden. And Thirukkural says, "Thodarpaadu evankol pirapparukkalutraarkku udambum migai" (What connection is there for those who seek to cut off birth? Even the body is superfluous). Chapter 07 — ThiruMoola Deva Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 906, by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Vambaraa Varivandu Sarukkam. Thirumoola Deva Nayanar Puranam. In the concluding part of the commentary on Thirumoola Deva Nayanar Puranam, the editor has included a section containing very rare information about Thirumandiram, authored by Thirumoola Deva Nayanar, and about the sacred history of Thirumoola Deva Nayanar. Let us contemplate them. Thirumandiram, authored by Thirumoola Deva Nayanar, is praised as Tamil Moovayiram (Three Thousand Tamil Verses). This book, Tamil Moovayiram, is named "Mantiram". Tholkappiyar states in his Sutra what a mantra is: "Niraimozhi maandhar aanaiyil kilantha maraimozhithane mantiram enba" (The secret words uttered by perfected beings by divine command are called mantras). According to this, since it was composed by Thirumoola Deva Nayanar, a perfected being, by the command of Lord Shiva's divine will, with verses that convey the meaning of the Vedas, it was called "Mantiram". It is named Thirumandiram. The book Thirumandiram has ten parts: one Payiram (preface) and nine Tantras. The Payiram section has three parts: Sirappu Payiram (special preface), Podhu Payiram (general preface), and Thatchirappu Payiram (self-special preface), comprising verses from 1 to 156. First, Sirappu Payiram. Sirappu Payiram was composed by the disciples of Thirumoola Deva Nayanar. It contains sections on the history of the Guru lineage and the greatness of the Thirumandiram collection. Next, Podhu Payiram. Podhu Payiram contains five types of invocations: to God, rain, ascetics, dharma, and crowned kings. According to the Sutra "Aayiram mugathal agandrathayinum payiram illathu panuval andre" (Even if it is vast with a thousand facets, a text without a preface is not a text), Thirumoola Deva Nayanar has provided a preface for the book he authored. In the preface, sections one, two, three, and four are invocations to God; sections five and six are invocations to ascetics; section seven is an invocation to crowned kings; section eight is an invocation to rain; and sections nine and ten are invocations to dharma. Now, the third part of the preface is Thatchirappu Payiram. Thatchirappu Payiram is the section where Thirumoola Deva Nayanar speaks to his disciples about his Guru lineage and his own history. In the section called "Kadavul Vazhthu" (invocation to God), our Thirumoola Deva Nayanar states that God is one, and that one is the one who kicked Death, the Lord worshipped by Devas as Nakkan, and His name is Shiva. He also extensively described the qualities of Lord Shiva in 50 verses. Our Thirumoola Deva Nayanar, by saying "Seelaanga vedathai seppa vandene" (I came to expound the Vedas that are the essence of sacred dance) and "Sinthai seithu Agamam seppalutrene" (I contemplated and began to expound the Agamas), has extensively elaborated on the Vedas and Agamas in the sections "Veda Sirappu" (greatness of Vedas) and "Agama Sirappu" (greatness of Agamas) with the help of the Lord's grace at that time. He has stated that there is no dharma without the Vedas, that all dharmas to be recited are in the Vedas, that scholars attained liberation by reciting the prosperous Vedas, abandoning logical debates, that Vedas and Agamas were revealed by God, that Vedas are general and Agamas are special, and that the Agamas we received through Nandiyam Peruman, such as Karanam, Kamikam, Veeram, Chintyam, Pathulam, Vyalam, Kalottaram, Suprabham, Makutam, and others, are the essence of these Agamas, which he sang as nine Tantras. Therefore, he has also stated the benefit of this book, Thirumandiram, which has a preface and nine Tantras: "Kalai ezhunthu karuthu arinthu othidin Gnalath thalaivanai nannuvar" (If one rises in the morning, understands the meaning, and recites it, one will attain the Lord of the world). Thus, the Payiram section is structured. Now, the first Tantra. This is the first Tantra of the book Thirumandiram, which is structured into nine Tantras. This section comprises Thirumandirams from 157 to 322. Thirumoola Deva Nayanar, who came to speak about the four principles starting with Gnana – Charya in Gnana, Kriya in Gnana, Yoga in Gnana, and Gnana in Gnana – first states that just as Pati (God) is beginningless among the three, Pati, Pasu (soul), and Pasam (bondage), so too are Pasu and Pasam beginningless. He states that the soul, bound by Pasam, cannot attain God by itself, but by God's grace, the impurity in the souls will subside, Gnana will shine, and the soul will experience the supreme bliss of Sivananda. He also explains how God purifies souls. To remove the impurity in souls, God, out of compassion, bestows Tanu (body), Karana (instruments), Bhuvana (worlds), and Bhoga (experiences) upon souls. When a soul experiences worldly pleasures with these Tanu and Karanas, God has revealed Vedas and Agamas to help it discern good from bad, and what is permanent from what is impermanent, so that it may attain salvation. He explains the impermanence of youth, wealth, and the body, and instructs to perform dharmas prescribed in the Vedas such as non-killing, abstaining from meat, not coveting others' wives, having love, impartiality, abstaining from alcohol, etc. He also instructs to analyze the Tattvas, arranging the 36 Tattvas as 36 steps, and to transcend all these Tattvas in a step-by-step manner. He describes the nature of Tattva Rupam, Tattva Darshanam, Tattva Suddhi, Atma Rupam, Atma Darshanam, Atma Suddhi, Shiva Rupam, Shiva Darshanam, Shiva Yogam, Shiva Bhogam, and explains that the state where the soul attains God is to realize these and transcend them step by step, entering the incomparable inner light of bliss, seeing the ineffable Shiva, and remaining in that state. He gives the example of salt dissolving in water and becoming one, and states that the Jiva will merge into Shiva like that, thus concluding this first Tantra. It is noteworthy that the dharmas stated by Thirumoola Deva Nayanar in the first Tantra are also stated by Thiruvalluva Deva Nayanar in Thirukkural, in the sections on Dharma and Wealth. Now, the second Tantra. This section comprises Thirumandirams from 323 to 530. In this second Tantra, Thirumoola Deva Nayanar emphasizes that beings should follow the path of dharma by illustrating, through Puranic stories, the benefits attained by those who follow the path of dharma and moral conduct described in the first Tantra, and the suffering experienced by those who do not. The history of Agastya, by the command of Parameswara, fighting and defeating the three cities' demons in Vindhya, Krauncha, and other places, and then going to Podigai Malai and preventing them from going north of Podigai Malai, because the rule of the three cities' demons deviated from impartiality and caused suffering to the people of the world, is narrated under the title "Agathiyam". The eight heroic deeds of Parameswara, such as the slaying of Andhakasura in Thirukkovalur, the destruction of Daksha's sacrifice in Thiruppariyalur, the severing of Brahma's head in Thirukkandiyur, the slaying of Jalandhara in Thiruvirkodi, the burning of the three cities in Thiruvathigai, the flaying of the elephant in Thiruvaluvoor, the kicking of Yama in Thirukkadavoor, and the burning of Kama in Thirukkurukkai, are narrated under the title "Pativali". The penance of Goddess Parvati, the granting of refuge to the three cities' demon kings Tarakan, Kamalakan, and Vidyunmali, the granting of life to Brahma, the discus to Vishnu, and the days to Ravana, the granting of the Chandikeswara status to Visara Sarma, the slaying of Surapadman, etc., are narrated by Thirumoola Deva Nayanar in this second Tantra under the title "Linga Puranam". The story of Damodaran, who received the discus, throwing it at Veerabhadra during Daksha's sacrifice, and Veerabhadra wearing it as one of the white skulls in his garland, and Vishnu worshipping Lord Shiva to regain the discus, is narrated under the title "Chakra Peru". Later, when Vishnu and Brahma, out of ignorance, argued that they themselves were the Lord, Paraman stood as a pillar of fire between them. Vishnu could not find the foot, and Brahma could not find the head, and they stood exhausted. The history of Lord Shiva bestowing grace upon them is narrated under the title "Adimudi Thedal". It is stated that bones and skulls are the primary cause of creation, and that if Lord Shiva did not hold bones and skulls, the five cosmic functions of creation, etc., would not take place. This is narrated under the title "Elumbum Kapalamum". The explanation of the five cosmic functions under the titles of creation, preservation, destruction, concealment, and grace, and the details of how the worlds created by Ayan, Mal, Rudran, Maheswaran, and Sadashivan, and the 84 hundred thousand types of living beings are governed, are also narrated. The method of birth of Jivas with one, two, or three impurities is narrated under the title "Karu Utpatti" (origin of embryo). Thirumoola Deva Nayanar explains in this second Tantra that if the born Jivas do not commit blasphemy against Shiva, Maheswara, or Guru, but take the help of elders, discern between worthy and unworthy recipients, and conduct themselves accordingly, they will attain the golden feet of God. The third Tantra. This section comprises mantras from 531 to 863. In this Tantra, he speaks about the eight types of Yogas: Yama, Niyama, Asana, Pranayama, Pratyahara, Dharana, Dhyana, Samadhi, and the benefits derived from them, and the eight types of Siddhis that arise. He also describes the ways to keep the body immortal and free from disease, which is the instrument for attaining Siddhis, under the title "Kayasiddhi". To make the body suitable for the Supreme Being to reside within, he first explains the necessary practices such as Varasaram, Varasoolai, Amoridharana, Ayul Parikshai, Kalachakram, etc., and then elaborates on Kesari Yoga, Paryanga Yoga, Chandra Yoga, and so on. Kesari Yoga is part of Hatha Yoga. It is very difficult, hard to practice, and not everyone can perform it. Paryanga Yoga is performed with one's spouse. It is a practice performed by a lover and beloved with unified minds and affection. This is not possible for everyone. Finding a suitable beloved is rare, rarer than rare. This is suitable for Lord Sundaramoorthy Nayanar, Paravai Ammaiyar, Sangili Ammaiyar, and for Thiruvalluva Deva Nayanar, Vasuki Ammaiyar, and others. Chandra Yoga is explained as a Yoga practiced during waxing and waning moons, knowing the 16 phases of the moon, 12 phases of the sun, 64 phases of fire, and 4 phases of stars, totaling 96 phases. He also states that among all, the eight-fold Yoga is the best. The Ashtanga Yoga described by Patanjali is different from the Sivayoga described in the Sivagamas, which can be understood through proper Guru Upadesam. Now, the fourth Tantra. This section comprises mantras from 864 to 1393. The Thiru Ambalachakram is embedded in the secret chamber of Chidambaram. The Thiru Ambalachakram is also present in the form of Thiyagaperuman in Thiruvarur. The Ajapa mantra is explained separately. Its details are to be revealed by the Guru to those who have received initiation. Now, the fifth Tantra. This section comprises mantras from 1394 to 1547. In this Tantra, he extensively describes the Saiva divisions, the four paths of Charya, Kriya, Yoga, and Gnana, the four margas of Sanmargam, Sagamargam, Satputra Margam, and Dasa Margam, the four states of Salokam, Samipam, Sarupam, Sayujyam, and the four levels of Mandam, Mandataram, Theeviram, Theevirataram, in 20 chapters. He states that the six religions are like the story of blind men encountering an elephant. Therefore, he explains that after seeing the six religions, understanding their principles, and becoming clear, everyone will ultimately realize that the six religious paths are flawed, and that only the path of Shiva leads to salvation, while others lead to worldly existence. This is explained by Thirumoola Deva Nayanar in this fifth Tantra. Now, the sixth Tantra. This section comprises mantras from 1548 to 1675. In this, Thirumoola Deva Nayanar describes in 14 chapters how Lord Shiva comes as a Guru to those mature individuals among humanity who are afflicted by the three impurities, removes their impurities through the Outhri Diksha (initiation) by His divine gaze, touch, word, contemplation, scripture, and yoga, gives them instruction, speaks of renunciation, makes them undertake penance, and bestows liberation. Now, the seventh Tantra. This section comprises mantras from 1676 to 2072. This Tantra contains 30 sections. Thirumoola Deva Nayanar has explained the six Adharas (psychic centers) and the Lingams such as Anda Lingam, Pinda Lingam, Sadashiva Lingam, Atma Lingam, Gnana Lingam, Shiva Lingam, from one to seven chapters, under the category of "Shiva Linga Uruva Vilakkam" (explanation of Shiva Linga form). Under the titles of Sampradayam, Thiruvarul Vaippu, Arul, Oli, Shiva Puja, he has explained Shiva Puja. Under the titles of Guru Puja, Maheswara Puja, Adiyar Perumai, Bhojana Vidhi, Bhiksha Vidhi, etc., he has explained Maheswara Puja. He states that if the body of a Gnani is burnt by fire, the whole country will burn, and if rain does not fall on earth, the world will suffer from famine, and countless kings will lose their kingdoms. Therefore, he states that the body of a Gnani should be interred in a Samadhi, and under the title "Poorana Gugai Neri Samadhi Kriyai", he explains the method of Samadhi, making a cave, and the rituals performed in Samadhi. Under the titles of Vindutpannam, Vindujayam, Bhogasara Ottam, he has described the method of semen production, controlling semen, and the way of life for those who are like "blind eyes and deaf ears" looking outwards through the fallen rod. Then, he describes the state of Aditya and the characteristics of Atmas, and clarifies the method of controlling the five senses by saying, "Anju madakkil ajadanam aam endittu anju madakka arinthene" (If one controls five, one becomes inert; thus, I knew how to control five). Then, in the last five chapters of this Tantra, he speaks about Satguru and Asatguru, condemns improper conduct, and gives ethical instructions for people to follow the good path. In this ethical instruction, the famous saying "Ondre kulamum oruvane devanum nandre niminin naman illai naaname sendre pugum gathi illenum chiththathu nindre nilapera neer ninainthuyimine" (One clan and one God; think well, there is no Yama; there is no path to enter without shame; stand firm in your mind and meditate to attain salvation) is found in this section. The famous saying "Ondre kulamum oruvane devanum" is found in this section. In this section of ethical instruction, Thirumoola Deva Nayanar has also said, "Ikkāyam nīṅki iṉi oru kāyaththil pukku piṟavāmal pōm vaḻi nāṭumiṉ ekkālaththu ivvuṭal vanthu emakku āṉathu eṉṟu akkālam uṉṉa aruḷ peṟalāmē" (Seek the way to depart from this body and not be born again in another body. When this body came to us, by contemplating that time, one can attain grace). Now, the eighth Tantra. This Tantra is composed of 33 sections, comprising mantras from 2083 to 2600. In sections one, two, and three, he speaks about the subtle body called Poriyatta Kayam, the method of transmigration of the soul from one body to another, and the different states of consciousness. In section four, he speaks about the knowledge of self. In section five, he speaks about the six ends: Vedanta, Siddhanta, Nadanta, Bodhanta, Yoganta, Kalanta. In section six, he speaks about the three categories: Pati, Pasu, Pasam, and also about light atom, material atom, life atom, etc. In section seven, he explains the meaning of Thadalai (Thal Thalai - foot and head). He clarifies by saying, "Kālum thalaiyum ariyār kalathikaḷ kāl antha sakthi aruḷ enpar kāraṇam pāloṉṟu gnānamē paṇpār thalai uyir eṉṟu kūṟi kālantha gnānaththai kāṭṭa vīṭākumē" (Those who do not know foot and head are ignorant. They say foot is the power of grace. The reason is that unified knowledge is the head, which is life. By showing that knowledge, one attains liberation). From section eight to section seventeen, he describes the three faults: Kama (desire), Veguli (anger), Mayakkam (delusion); the three words: Tat, Tvam, Asi; the three supreme states: Shivaparam, Paramparam, Tatparam; the three Turiyas: Shiva, Jiva, Para Turiyas; the three liberations: Jivanmukti, Paramukti, Shivamukti; the three forms: Aru (formless), Aruvuruvu (formless-form), Uru (form); the three Karanas: Shiva, Para, Jiva Karanas; the three voids: Mayaippal, Bodhappal, Vyomappal, and so on. In section 18, the seven Upadhis (adjuncts) of Jiva, which are effects, and the seven Upadhis of Para, which are causes, are described. In section 19, he speaks about Upashanta (tranquility). In section 20, he speaks about the method of experiencing one who has realized the inner self without speaking of the outer. In section 21, he speaks about the light that is absorbed within the Sushumna Nadi. In section 22, he speaks about the state of consciousness that arises during the vision of the non-light. In sections 23 to 27, he speaks about the way Maya is removed, the three types of Lakshanas (indicative meanings): Vitta, Vidama, Vittuvidatha, and that the Mahavakya "Tat Tvam Asi" refers to the Siddhanta conclusion, and the state where the body, freed from Maya, attains a luminous form. In sections 28 to 32, he describes truthfulness, cessation of desire, the actions of a Gnani, devotion, liberation, and so on. In section 33, he describes the omnipresent nature of God, like the sap in the sugarcane, the butter in milk, the juice in fruit, and the fragrance in a flower. He concludes this eighth Tantra by saying that the soul, like a golden creeper that seeks its beloved, will unite with God in the mind and rejoice. Now, the ninth Tantra. This section comprises mantras from 2601 to 3000. He has structured this as 22 sections, which are the essence of the Agamas. In sections one to four, he speaks about Guru Matha Darshanam, Gnana Guru Darshanam, Pranava Samadhi, and Oli (light). In sections five to seven, he explains the nature of Sthoola, Sukshma, and Ati Sukshma Panchaksharas. In section eight, he describes the five cosmic dances of God: Arputha Koothu, Thathpara Koothu, Pottrillai Koothu, Sundara Koothu, Porpathi Koothu, Sivananda Koothu, and also about Ananda Thandavam. In sections nine to sixteen, he speaks about Akasha Peru, Gnanodayam, Satya Gnananandam, Swarupa Udayam, Oozh (fate), Shiva Darshanam, Shiva Swarupa Darshanam, Mukti Bhedam, etc. In section 17, he presents a collection of verses containing hidden meanings. In sections 18 to 22, he speaks about Mona Samadhi, Varaiyarai Maatchi, Anainthor Thanmai, etc., and concludes the ideas of the Agamas by worshipping and praising our omnipresent Lord. Thus, we have briefly contemplated the essence of the ideas in the nine Tantras of the sacred text Thirumandiram, what topics these nine Tantras explain, and how they explain the ideas of the Agamas. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 08 — ThiruMoola Deva Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periya Puranam, Part 907, by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Vambaraa Varivandu Sarukkam. Thirumoola Deva Nayanar Puranam. The commentator Sivakavimani C.K. Subramania Mudaliar Aiya explains the special features of Thirumandiram. The entire Thirumandiram is composed in a unique type of Kaliviruttha meter with four lines. This is a unique feature. Our ancestors used this meter as it is very suitable for expressing and praising the experiences of Sivananda. The beginning of the divine invocations in Periya Puranam, Kandapuranam, Kandapurana Surukkam, and Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam are all composed in this type of Viruttha poem. Kandar Anubhuti is also composed in the same meter. The name of this book is Thirumandira Malai. Dharumayadhinam Velliyambalavana Munivar Piran extensively quotes Thirumandiram verses in his Gnanaavarana Vilakka Perurai. Generally, Thirumoolar extracted the essence of Sivagamas and composed them in nine Tantras with 3000 Thiruppadalgals. Sivagamas explain the true meaning by dividing it into four paths: Charya, Kriya, Yoga, and Gnana. However, some Agamas elaborate more or less on certain sections among these. Among the 28 Sivagamas, ten, from Kamikam to Suprabhedam, are classified as Shivabheda Agamas, and eighteen, from Vijayam to Vathoolam, are classified as Rudrabheda Agamas. The names of the Kamikadi Agamas, their classifications, origins, number of verses, names of those who heard them, their Upagamas, and other details are extensively described in the Tantravatara Padalam, the first section of the Sivagamas. It is mentioned that 66 people heard the Agamas. They are the Sivarudras, from Pranavar to Mahakalar. In Kamikam Tantravatara Padalam, slokas 22 and 23, which begin with "Ishana Vatra" and "Shatshashtibhih", contain information such as 10 Shivabhedas, 18 Rudrabhedas, and 66 listeners. In Thirumandiram, the meaning of the Vedas is generally explained, and the meaning of the Sivagamas is extensively and widely expounded. "Tantra" means that which protects those who attain it. It explains the meaning of Tattva and Mantra. Tantra, Siddhanta, Agama, Mantra are all different terms that convey the same meaning. Now, these 28 Agamas have Upagamas. They are 207, from Narasimham to Visvanmakam. Due to differences in the maturity of the listeners and differences in the subject matter explained, some parts of the Sivagamas may appear to differ. However, the part that conveys the meaning in the Gnana Pada (path of knowledge) does not have differences. The Agamas are structured by the Lord in this manner, following the tradition of explaining things according to the varying degrees of impurity in souls, using the Sthula Arundhati Nyaya (gross-subtle analogy). Therefore, to resolve the confusion in the Gnana Pada of the Sivagamas, the Lord, after classifying all these Gnana Pada meanings as general and true, composed them into 12 Sutras and instructed them to Nandiyam Peruman. This is the history of Siddhanta. The details of this can be found in the special preface commentary of Sivagnana Mapadiyam and other sections. Thus, to resolve the confusion in the Gnana Pada of the Sivagamas, Nandiyam Peruman instructed the meanings he heard from the Lord to Sanatkumara and other sages. Thirumoola Deva Nayanar received initiation in that lineage. He composed the essence of the Sivagamas he heard into 3000 Thirumandirams through nine Tantras. Therefore, Thirumandiram is the distilled essence of all Sivagamas. Thus, Thirumandiram cherishes in its heart the truth of the three categories (Pati, Pasu, Pasam) spoken of in all Agamas, and the conclusions about the four paths of Charya, Kriya, Yoga, and Gnana. Among the three Vedas called "Trayi" – Rig, Yajur, Sama – the middle one is Yajur Veda. In its fourth, middle section, in the middle of the 11th Anuvaka, in the sixth Sukta, shines Sri Rudram. In its middle is Sri Panchaksharam, and in its middle is the sacred name Shiva. This is referred to as the "Aksharatvayam" (two letters) that shines in the middle of the Vedas. It is said that the Veda Purusha cherishes Shiva in his heart. Similarly, here, Thirumandiram, which is the essence of Sivagama, cherishes the truths of the three categories and the four truths starting with Gnana in its heart. How? Thirumandiram has nine Tantras. The middle one among them is the fifth Tantra. In it, Thirumoola Deva Nayanar extensively explains the four paths of Saivism, starting with Charya, and their states and types. This truth is derived because the divine Sekkizhar Peruman repeatedly emphasized it three times in the history of Thirumoola Deva Nayanar: first, in the middle, and finally, starting with "Ullunarvana Gnanam muthaliya oru nangu unmai thellu thentamizhal seppum Thirumoolar" (Thirumoolar who expounds the four truths, starting with inner wisdom, in clear sweet Tamil), and then "Gnana muthal nangu malar natthirumandira malai" (the excellent sacred garland of mantras, the four flowers starting with wisdom), and "Nalan sirantha Gnana Yoga Kriya Chariyaiyellam malarntha mozhi Thirumoola Devar" (Thirumoola Devar whose words blossomed all the excellent Gnana, Yoga, Kriya, and Charya). The fifth Tantra among the nine Tantras in Thirumandiram is the central one. The conclusion of the Gnanis is that the meaning of the four Vedas and the meaning of the Agamas are one. "Mantra" means that which protects the one who meditates on it. Mantra Upadesam (initiation into mantra) is given to those who are mature and come seeking liberation. It will be beneficial only if the listener has mental enthusiasm and determination in listening to the mantra and benefiting from it. If these are absent, it will not be beneficial. Therefore, the preface is given at the beginning to bring about these (mental enthusiasm and determination). Both Vedas and Sivagamas were created by God. Therefore, they are authoritative texts. Among these, the Vedas are general, and the Agamas are special. The Vedas are like Sutras, and the Agamas are like Bhashyas (commentaries). Thirumandiram states: "Vedamodu Agamam meyyam Iraivan nool Othum pothuvum sirappum endru unnuga" (Consider that Vedas and Agamas are the true scriptures of God, both general and special). Thirumandiram also states: "Siddhantam Vedantam kattum Sivanaiye" (Siddhanta shows the same Shiva that Vedanta shows). Therefore, here, Veda refers to the Upanishads, which are the Gnana Padas (paths of knowledge) praised as the head of the Vedas, also known as Vedanta. Among them, the Atharva Shika and other Upanishads explain the true meaning in a partial manner, following the Sthula Arundhati Nyaya, for listeners to understand according to their respective stages. Sivagamas explain the ultimate and clear meanings among them. That is why they are called Siddhanta, the ultimate conclusion. We see in Sivagnana Siddhiyar: "Vedanta thelivam Saiva Siddhanta thiran" (The essence of Vedanta is the power of Saiva Siddhanta). Sivagnana Siddhiyar states: "Veda nool Saiva nool endru irande noolgal Aadhi nool anaadhi amalan tharu nool irandum Aarana nool pothu Saivam arunjirappu noolam Neethiyinal ulagarkku sakthi nipatharkku nigazhvithathu" (There are only two types of scriptures: Vedic scriptures and Saivite scriptures. Both are primordial scriptures given by the eternal, pure Lord. Vedic scriptures are general, and Saivite scriptures are special. They were revealed for the world and for those with divine grace, according to justice). In the Puranam of Thirugnanasambandha Peruman, we saw: "Ulagiyal Veda nool ozhukkam enbathum Nilavu meinneri Sivanneri enbathum" (That the Vedic scriptures are worldly conduct, and the established true path is the path of Shiva). We also see the sacred sayings: "Vedam pasu athan pal mei Agamam" (Veda is the cow, its milk is the true Agama). If so, why are there two scriptures, Vedas and Agamas? Could they not be one? For those who have not reached the maturity to learn the special scriptures, general scriptures are needed. For those who have reached that maturity, special scriptures are needed. We also see that Vedas are for the three varnas, and Agamas are for the four varnas. We also see that the Kamikam and other Agamas, which describe the methods for Shiva Puja and other rituals prescribed in the Vedas, are indispensable. We find that Pancha Brahma, Pranava, Panchakshara, Prasada and other mantras, explanations of categories like Pati, Pasu, Pasam, applying sacred ash as Udhdhulanam and Tripundaram, wearing Rudraksha, Shiva Puja and other Shiva dharmas, and other things are found similarly in both Vedas and Sivagamas. Therefore, both are authoritative texts. Both refer to the same meaning. Lord Shiva, under the Banyan tree, instructed the four sages on the four principles of Dharma, etc., and made them attain the state of silence. From Mahendra mountain, He revealed the Sivagamas, which are the culmination of knowledge. It is said that the purpose of scriptures is to attain Dharma, Artha, Kama, and Moksha. This is a general rule for all scriptures. Some scriptures provide only one of these. The Vedas provide all four Purusharthas. Among them, after understanding the first three, one must abandon them to attain the scripture of Moksha, which then becomes beneficial. Therefore, Moksha is attained through renunciation and other causes, and in general, the Vedas provide peace to that extent. Sivagamas begin from the state of attaining peace by abandoning these, and by revealing Sivagnana, they reveal Moksha and enable seekers to attain it through practices. The Vedas, by explaining the four Purusharthas of Dharma, etc., and showing the nature of worldly life, make beings stand on the path to Moksha. We also see that Agamas, by imparting Shiva dharmas, bestow Moksha. We also see that Vedas are called worldly, and Agamas are called Shiva dharma. In Thirumandiram, we see Thirumoola Deva Nayanar speaking of the Vedas as "Vedathai vitta aram illai. Vedathul othathagum aram ellam ula" (There is no dharma without the Vedas. All dharmas to be recited are in the Vedas), and of the Agamas as "Paranai paroparam katti ulagil tharanai Siva dharmam thane sorkalam" (Showing the Supreme as transcendent, in the world, the Shiva dharma itself is the golden age). Oh good disciple, to be freed from the suffering of birth and attain liberation is the duty and action of wise people. This cannot be achieved without attaining Lord Shiva. Lord Shiva alone is Pati. There is no deity equal to Shiva, even if sought. He is older than the Trinity. He is better than a mother. Everyone worships Him, but no one is worshipped by Him. He is one whose hands have never joined in prayer. There is no other unique deity apart from Shiva's grace. Brahma, Vishnu, and others perform their respective duties by Shiva's command, by the power of authority and merit given by Him. By Shiva's grace, for the salvation of beings and to attain liberation, Lord Shiva Himself revealed the Vedas and Sivagamas. Follow their path and attain liberation. I will elaborate on their nature above. Lord Shiva instructed the Agamic principles to Nandiyam Peruman. He, in Kayilai, instructed them to Sanatkumara and other sages, and through that lineage, to me and some others. The Lord, out of grace, made me come here from Kayilai to expound those principles in Tamil for the salvation of Tamil Nadu. "Aranadi nalthorum sinthai seithu Agamam seppalutren. Thirukkooththin seelaanga vedathai seppa vanden." (I contemplated the feet of Hara daily and began to expound the Agamas. I came to expound the Vedas that are the essence of the sacred dance.) "Naan petra inbam iv vaiyagam peruvathaga" (May the joy I received be received by this world). Listen to these, follow their path, and you too attain salvation. Thus, the book begins with the instruction of Thirumoola Deva Nayanar. Therefore, this preface is given to create mental enthusiasm in the disciple for listening to the mantra initiation. Thirumandiram has nine Tantras. Among them, the fifth Tantra, which is in the middle, explains the life-giving truths of Saiva Siddhanta. Therefore, the first four Tantras, which precede the fifth Tantra, can be said to generally explain the means for a good disciple to make himself worthy, both internally and externally, to attain that Sivagnana. The four Tantras from six to nine, which follow, can be said to generally explain the benefits to be found and attained in the state of attaining Gnana. Even if milk is a good substance, if it is kept in an unclean vessel, it will spoil and become useless. Similarly, Sivagnana is good. But when it comes to one who is not pure, both internally and externally, it becomes useless and even causes harm. Therefore, one who strives to attain Gnana must first make himself worthy of it, both internally and externally. The first Tantra emphasizes this and instructs the means for it, both positively and negatively. It also mentions some other necessary things. One who is proud of his body, soul, wealth, and youth, and suffers under their influence, is not worthy of attaining Gnana. He will not have the enthusiasm to attain Gnana. Realizing the impermanence of body, wealth, and youth, and attaining a state of mind that does not cling to them even while being amidst them, like a tamarind fruit, is the first step to attaining Gnana. Therefore, in this first Tantra, Thirumoola Deva Nayanar imparts this understanding through sections like "Yakkai Nilayamai" (impermanence of the body), "Selvam Nilayamai" (impermanence of wealth), "Ilamai Nilayamai" (impermanence of youth), "Uyir Nilayamai" (impermanence of life), etc. The 145th mantra, "Oorellam koodi okka azhuthittu Perinai neekki pinam endru perittu Sooraiyam kattidai kondupoi suttittu Neerinil moozhgi ninaippu ozhinthare" (All the village gathered and cried together, removed the name and called it a corpse, took it to the cremation ground and burnt it, immersed in water and forgot), was instructed by Thirumoola Deva Nayanar to emphasize the impermanence of the body. "Mookkinil kaivaithu moodittukkondupoi Kaakkaikku kaippali kaattiyavare" (Placing hand on nose, covering it, and taking it to show as an offering to crows), and "Idappakkame irai nondhathe endrar Kidakkap paduthar kidanthu ozhinthare" (They said, 'The left side of the Lord is in pain,' laid down, and remained laid down). These are the beautiful and firm instructions given by Thirumoola Deva Nayanar. Now, the impermanence of wealth is emphasized in the 171st mantra: "Eettiya then poo manam kandirathamum Kootti konarnthoru kombidai vaithidum Ootti thurandhittu athu valiyar kola Kaatti koduthu avai kaivittavare" (The honey gathered, seeing the fragrance of flowers, brought together and placed on a branch, then abandoned it, and when strong ones took it, they showed it and gave it up). The 177th mantra also states: "Kizhakkil ezunthodiya nayiru merke Vilakkandum therar vizhi ila maandhar Kulakkanru mooththiruthay sila naalil Vilakkandum therar viyanulagore" (The sun that rose in the east runs to the west. Blind people do not understand even when they see the light. The calf of the family grows old in a few days. Even when they see the light, the people of the vast world do not understand). Thus, through these, he emphasizes the impermanence of wealth. He emphasizes the impermanence of life in the 187th mantra, saying, "Azhakkindra pothu ariyaar avarthame" (They themselves do not know when they are called). Now, merely having this feeling of impermanence is not enough. One must also refrain from evil deeds such as killing, eating meat, coveting others' wives, lust, drinking alcohol, etc. These cause harm to both body and soul simultaneously. They destroy knowledge. They do not give any room for Gnana. He emphasizes all these. He says, "Sakthiyai vendi samayathor kallunpar Sakthi izhanthu thammai marathalar" (Those of certain religions drink alcohol seeking power, but they lose power and forget themselves). He emphasizes non-killing by saying, "Patraya narkuru poosaikkum panmalar Matror anukkalai kollamai" (For the worship of the good Guru, many flowers, and not killing other beings). He emphasizes abstaining from meat by saying, "Kolaiye kalavu kal kamam poikural Malaivana pathakamaam avai neekki" (Murder, theft, alcohol, lust, lying are great sins; avoid them). He emphasizes not coveting others' wives in the 201st mantra: "Aaththa manaiyal agaththilirukkave Kaaththa manaiyalai kamurum kalaiyar Kaichcha pallavin kaniyunna maattathar Eechcham pazaththukku idarutravare" (Young men who desire another's wife while their own beloved wife is at home, are like those who cannot eat the fruit of a ripe jackfruit but suffer for a date fruit). He also speaks about poverty and harm, and the ability to prevent them. Thirumoola Deva Nayanar emphasizes that one who is afflicted by poverty will not have his mind inclined towards Gnana. He emphasizes this by saying, "Pudavai kizhithathu poyitru vazhkai Adaiyappattarum anpilar anar Kodaiyillai kolillai kondattammillai Nadaiyillai nattil iyungindrarkatke" (The cloth is torn, life is gone, those who were attained are without love, there is no giving, no taking, no celebration, no movement for those who move in the country). The importance of performing dharma is explained under the titles "Thana Sirappu" (greatness of charity) and "Aram seivanathu thiram" (the ability to do dharma). The indispensability of love and the ability of Shiva, who resides within love, to transform those who love into Shiva, are instructed by the author under the title "Anbudaimai anbu udaiyarai arivan Sivan" (Shiva knows those who possess love). The well-known Thirumandiram verse is: "Anbum Sivvum irandu enbar arivilar Anbe Sivvamavathu yaarum arikilar Anbe Sivvamavathu yaarum arinthapin Anbe Sivvamai amarndhirundhare" (The ignorant say love and Shiva are two. No one knows that love itself is Shiva. After everyone knows that love itself is Shiva, they remained as love itself, as Shiva). Therefore, although the title is "Anbe Sivam" (Love is Shiva), its meaning is "Love itself is Shiva". Thirumoola Deva Nayanar emphasizes the indispensable greatness of education and learning in three ways: "Kalvi" (education), "Kallamai" (ignorance), and "Kelvi kettu amaithal" (listening and settling). He emphasizes education by saying, "Katrarivalar karuthilor kannundu Katravar perinbamutra nindrare" (The learned have an eye in their mind. The learned stood attaining supreme bliss). He shows the loss that comes from ignorance by saying, "Kallathar inbam kaankilare" (The unlearned do not see joy), and "Ella uyirkkum Iraivane ayinum Kallathar nenjathu kana onnathe" (Though God is for all beings, He cannot be seen in the hearts of the unlearned). We see the sacred sayings: "Kallar nenjil nillan Eesan" (The Lord does not reside in the hearts of the unlearned) and "Kattravar vizhungum karpaga anniye" (The wish-fulfilling tree swallowed by the learned). Now, he shows that "Kelvi" (listening) is very much needed for a Gnana disciple. In the 300th mantra, he emphasizes the greatness of listening: "Aram kettum anthar vaaimozhi kettum Maram kettum vanavar manthiram kettum Puram kettum ponnuraimeniyam Eesan Thiram kettum petra Sivagathi thane" (Having heard dharma, having heard the words of the Brahmins, having heard the trees, having heard the mantras of the celestials, having heard the external, having heard the glory of the Lord with a golden body, one attains Shiva's path). That is, the truth of this is that education is the education that learns about Lord Shiva. Listening is the listening that listens about Lord Shiva. The education and listening spoken of in Thirukkural and other ethical texts are different. They are meant to increase general knowledge about worldly affairs. But here, this education and listening are conducive to Sivagnana concerning the nature of the soul. The topics discussed under the title "Naduvu Nilaimai" (impartiality) are also to be understood in this way. The sections "Agni Karyam" (fire ritual) and "Anthar Ozhukkam" (conduct of Brahmins) explain the disciplines necessary for attaining Gnana. It is the duty of the king to protect all these and those who strive for Gnana. Therefore, to explain this and instruct, sections like "Arasatchi Murai" (method of governance) and "Raja Raja Dosham" (faults of kings) are included. The section "Vana Sirappu" (greatness of rain) explains its indispensability because without the world being established, nothing, including Gnana, can function. For that, Gnanis also need the world to exist, and for that, rain is needed. Thirumoola Deva Nayanar structures these sections. Although these are beneficial in worldly life, the emphasis here is on their role in fostering the pursuit of Sivagnana. The conclusion of scholars is that the penance of a great ascetic cannot take place without the protection of a protector. Therefore, we can recall here the sections where the divine Sekkizhar Peruman commented on the Thiruppasuram composed by Thirugnanasambandha Peruman: "Vazhga anthar vanavar aaninam Vizhga thanpunal vendhanum onguga" (May Brahmins, celestials, and cows prosper; may cool water fall; may the king flourish), and "Ulagam inbamura santha velvigal muthal Sankararkku mun vantha archanai vazhipadu manna" (For the world to be joyful, may the worship and offerings that came before Shankara, starting with Vedic sacrifices, remain), and "Vizhpunal nalum archanai nalluruppu athalal" (Since falling water is a good part of daily worship), and "Mannanai vazhthiyathu archanai moolum matrivai kaakkum muraiyal" (Praising the king is because he protects these, which are the source of worship). Therefore, we also see here that Thirukkural emphasizes, "Anthar noorkkum arathirkkum aathiyai nindrathu mannavan kol" (The king's scepter is the origin of the Brahmin's scripture and dharma).
