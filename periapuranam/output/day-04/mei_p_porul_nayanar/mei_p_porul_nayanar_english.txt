Chapter 01 — Mei-p-porul Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. In the second chapter of Periya Puranam, the Thillai Vazh Andhanar Sarukkam, we first saw the greatness of the Thillai Vazh Andhanars, who are Thokaiyadiyars, in the section titled Thillai Vazh Andhanar Puranam. Next, we contemplated the sacred histories of Thiruneelakandathu Kuyavanar, Iyarpagai Nayanar, and Ilaiyankudi Mara Nayanar. Following this, the fifth history, the fifth Purana, is the Mei-p-porul Nayanar Puranam. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal, in the first verse of his Thiruthondathogai, sings, "Adiyen to the devotees of Thillai Vazh Andhanar, Adiyen to Thiruneelakandathu Kuyavanar, Adiyen to Iyarpagai who never said 'no', Adiyen to Ilaiyan Than Kudimaran's devotees." Sundaramoorthy Swamigal then sings, "Adiyen to Mei-p-porul, who is very skilled in victory." That is, here, Sundaramoorthy Swamigal sings, "Adiyen to Mei-p-porul Nayanar." Therefore, following this, Sekkizhar Swamigal also composed the Mei-p-porul Nayanar Puranam as the fifth Purana. Sundaramoorthy Swamigal sings in Thiruthondathogai, "Adiyen to Mei-p-porul, who is very skilled in victory." The meaning of this line is, "I am a servant to Mei-p-porul Nayanar, who is very skilled in the path of victory." This Purana is the history of the Nayanar named Mei-p-porul and describes his qualities. In the sequence, the Mei-p-porul Nayanar Puranam is narrated by Sekkizhar as the fifth in the Thillai Vazh Andhanar Sarukkam. First, let us contemplate a summary of Mei-p-porul Nayanar's sacred history, and then we can delve into the Mei-p-porul Nayanar Puranam in detail. In Tamil Nadu, various kingdoms existed during different periods. During the time of Mei-p-porul Nayanar's history, a country called Nadu Nadu flourished between the Chola country and Thondai country. Within Nadu Nadu, there was a sub-division called Chedi Nadu. There, a city called Thirukkovalur or Thirukkovilur is located. The capital of Chedi Nadu, Thirukkovalur, was ruled by Shaivite kings belonging to the Malaiyaman lineage. Our Mei-p-porul Nayanar was born into that lineage. Mei-p-porul Nayanar rules as king, with Thirukkovalur as his capital, in Chedi Nadu, which is in Nadu Nadu. Mei-p-porul Nayanar lives according to the Vedic principles. Not only that, but he also rules his country in such a way that Vedic principles flourish. He himself adheres to the Shaivite path. Similarly, he rules his country so that Shaivism flourishes. Mei-p-porul Nayanar is one who understands the wishes of Shiva's devotees and serves them. He is a ruler who governs without deviating from political ethics. He conquers all enemy kings with his might. He always keeps the sacred form of Shiva's devotees in his mind. He rules his kingdom ensuring that daily pujas, special pujas, and festivals are regularly conducted in all Shiva temples within his dominion. He lives without relying on anything other than the sacred feet of Shiva's devotees. He considers all his wealth to be the wealth of the devotees and joyfully gives whatever they need to all devotees who come to him. During this time, a particular king repeatedly came to war with Mei-p-porul Nayanar and was repeatedly defeated and fled. Whenever he invaded, Mei-p-porul Nayanar would defeat him and drive him away. Because this enemy king, despite repeatedly waging war, could not defeat Mei-p-porul Nayanar, he decided to defeat Mei-p-porul Nayanar through deceit.

This enemy king, having heard that Mei-p-porul Nayanar adheres to the Shaivite path and highly respects and provides for Shiva's devotees who wear sacred ash, decides to defeat Mei-p-porul Nayanar by adopting the guise of a wearer of sacred ash. For this, one day, he dons the guise of a Shaivite. He smears sacred ash all over his body. He ties his hair into matted locks. Thus, adopting the guise of a Shaivite devotee, he takes palm-leaf manuscripts in his hands. He hides a weapon in between these palm-leaf manuscripts. Hiding a weapon between the palm-leaf manuscripts, he arrives at Thirukkovalur in a false ascetic guise. The name of this enemy king is Mutthanathan. Mutthanathan, the enemy in false disguise, arrives at Thirukkovalur, which is ruled by Mei-p-porul Nayanar. Then he goes to the king's palace. All the guards at the palace, seeing his guise as a Shaivite devotee, open all the gates and allow him inside. This is because Mei-p-porul Nayanar had issued an order that Shiva's devotees, whenever they arrive, should be allowed inside, to honor and worship them. Therefore, all the gates open for Mutthanathan, who comes in this false ascetic guise. Thus, passing through all the gates, he reaches the innermost chamber where Mei-p-porul Nayanar is. There, the guard protecting Mei-p-porul Nayanar is a guard named Thatthan. Thatthan, who is guarding the last gate, addresses Mutthanathan, who has reached it, saying, "O devotee, the king inside is sleeping. Therefore, you should bless him at an opportune time." But Mutthanathan, who came in a false ascetic guise, says, "I have come to impart divine instruction to your king. Therefore, just as the other guards stepped aside and made way, you too step aside and make way." Saying this, he opens the door and goes inside. Inside, King Mei-p-porul Nayanar is sleeping with his wife. Even seeing the king sleeping with his wife, Mutthanathan approaches the king. The king's wife, realizing his arrival, wakes Mei-p-porul Nayanar. Mei-p-porul Nayanar, waking from his sleep, stands bowing to the Shiva devotee who has arrived. The king asks Mutthanathan, bowing, "For what auspicious purpose have you, O venerable one, come here?" He bows to Mutthanathan, who has come in the guise of a Shaivite. To this, Mutthanathan, who has come in the guise of a Shaivite, says, "I possess an Agamic text that Lord Shiva, your master, revealed earlier. This Agamic text is not found anywhere else. Therefore, I have come to impart its instruction to you." Hearing this, Mei-p-porul Nayanar, with folded hands, asks, "Is there any greater blessing for me than this? Please impart the instruction to me." Hearing this, Mutthanathan says, "Ask your wife to leave this place. You and I must be alone for me to impart the instruction to you." Immediately, the Queen goes outside. Mei-p-porul Nayanar offered a seat to Mutthanathan, who came in the ascetic guise, and sat below him, requesting, "Now, please impart the instruction." Thus, Mei-p-porul Nayanar stood humbly, looking at the ground, before Mutthanathan, who came in a false ascetic guise. Thinking this was the opportune moment, Mutthanathan, pretending to untie the palm-leaf manuscripts, took out the weapon hidden inside and carried out the act he had intended to do. Thatthan, the guard who had been carefully observing from outside since Mutthanathan, in his false ascetic guise, went inside, quickly rushes in. Seeing his king, Mei-p-porul Nayanar, bleeding profusely, he lunges at Mutthanathan, who came in the false ascetic guise and was responsible for it. As his guard, Thatthan, lunges at Mutthanathan, Mei-p-porul Nayanar gestures with his hand. Gesturing with his hand, he stops Thatthan and says, "Thattha, he is one of us. Do not attack him." The king, Mei-p-porul Nayanar, says this. Thatthan, obeying the king's command, asks, "What should I do?" To this, King Mei-p-porul Nayanar says, "When this ascetic devotee leaves, ensure that no harm comes to him. Take him to the border of our country and leave him there." He also says that no harm should come to this devotee from their guards or their people, and that he should be protected and safely left at the border of their country. Thatthan also walks as a protector for that devotee. Many people, learning of this incident, intercept Mutthanathan on his way, surrounding him, saying, "We will kill this one who harmed our king through deceit." Then Thatthan says, "It is the king's command that this devotee should leave our country safely without any harm." Thus, announcing the king's command, he disperses everyone and escorts Mutthanathan, who came in the false ascetic guise, to a forest at the country's border, accompanying him until the forest, and then Thatthan returns to the palace. At the palace, the king, bleeding profusely, is waiting for Thatthan. Thatthan quickly returns to the palace and tells King Mei-p-porul Nayanar, "I have safely taken that devotee to the country's border and left him there." Thus, he reports that he has successfully carried out the king's command. The king, who had been holding onto his life just to hear this news, upon hearing that the Shiva devotee had safely reached his destination, thought of Lord Shiva's feet and said, "Lord Shiva has bestowed such great grace upon me today." He then gave a message to his retinue, the prince who would rule after him, the ministers, his wife, and everyone around him. He said, "All of you should always love the devotees who wear sacred ash, protect them tirelessly, and always cherish Shiva's devotees." After giving this instruction to everyone, he meditated on the raised sacred foot of Lord Shiva, who performs the dance of grace in Thillai. As Mei-p-porul Nayanar meditated on His sacred foot, the Lord appeared before him, granted him a vision, and blessed Mei-p-porul Nayanar to join Him in the shade of His sacred feet. This is a brief summary of Mei-p-porul Nayanar's history. Now, let us contemplate in detail the sacred history of Mei-p-porul Nayanar as narrated by Sekkizhar in his Periya Puranam.

Chapter 02 — Mei-p-porul Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. We are contemplating the history of Mei-p-porul Nayanar in the second chapter of Periya Puranam, the Thillai Vazh Andhanar Sarukkam. We have seen a summary of Mei-p-porul Nayanar's story. Now, let us continue to contemplate Mei-p-porul Nayanar's history in detail and expansively through Sekkizhar's Periya Puranam. When introducing Mei-p-porul Nayanar, Sekkizhar says: "In the good country of Chedi, in the long-established Thirukkovalur, where the Lord, who is half-female, resides, the chieftain of the Maladar, who follows the path of love, upheld the truth of the good Vedic path, attained greatness, and served the Lord with love, understanding the minds of His devotees." Mei-p-porul Nayanar's country is Chedi Nadu. It is Sekkizhar's custom to introduce the country, town, and lineage of the Nayanmars in each Puranam. Similarly, here he states that Mei-p-porul Nayanar's country is Chedi Nadu. The country located between Thondai Nadu and Chola Nadu in Tamil Nadu is called Nadu Nadu. Chedi Nadu is one of the many countries within this Nadu Nadu. Nadu Nadu is situated between Thondai Nadu and Chola Nadu. Furthermore, Thondai Nadu is located to the north of Nadu Nadu, referred to as Vadai Nadu, and to the south of Nadu Nadu are Chola Nadu and Pandya Nadu. Thus, because it is situated in the central part of all these countries, it came to be known as Nadu Nadu. There are many countries in this Nadu Nadu. One of them is Chedi Nadu. The lineage that ruled this Chedi Nadu was called the Chediyar lineage. Therefore, this country is called Chedi Nadu after the name of the Chediyar lineage. Just as a country ruled by Cholas is called Chola Nadu, and a country ruled by Pandyas is called Pandya Nadu, named after the ruling royal lineages, similarly, this country came to be called Chedi Nadu because it was ruled by the Chedirayas. Chedirayar, who composed Thiruvichaippa, was one of the kings from this lineage. In this lineage of minor kings, there are three sub-divisions: Malaiyaman, Nathaman, and Suruthiman. Among them, our Mei-p-porul Nayanar belonged to the Malaiyaman division. Sekkizhar refers to this Chedi Nadu as Chedi Nannadu. Nannadu means a country possessing goodness. What is goodness? A country rich in many resources is called a good country. A country rich in all resources like mountains, forests, rivers, and fields is called Nannadu, a good country, a country of goodness. Among these, the greatest good, liberation or Mukti, is considered the supreme good by the elders. Therefore, if a country is rich in resources and also excels in the good of Mukti, then that country is a Nannadu, a country of goodness. Here, Chedi Nadu is called Chedi Nannadu by Sekkizhar. This is because the capital of this Chedi Nadu is a city called Thirukkovalur. This Thirukkovalur is one of the eight places where Lord Shiva performed heroic deeds. Devotees praise Lord Shiva for performing eight heroic deeds. Thus, the places where these eight heroic deeds occurred are known as Ashtaveerasthana temples in Tamil Nadu. Among them, the sacred place where the heroic deed of slaying Andhakasura took place is this Thirukkovalur. The eight heroic deeds performed by Lord Shiva are: the slaying of Andhakasura, which occurred in Thirukkovalur. Similarly, cutting off Daksha's head at Thiruppariyaloor, plucking Brahma's head at Thirukkandiyoor, slaying Jalandharasura at Thiruvitkudi, burning the three cities at Thiruvathigai, slaying Gajasura at Thiruvazhuvur, conquering Yama at Thirukkadavur, and burning Kama at Thirukkurukkai. These are the eight heroic deeds of Lord Shiva. Places where such heroic deeds occurred are called Ashtaveerasthanas and in Tamil, Atta Veeratta Thalangal. Among them, the slaying of Andhakasura took place in Thirukkovalur. Andhakasura was the son of Hiranyaksha. Once, when Umaiyamai playfully covered Lord Shiva's eyes with her hands, the whole world became dark. It was then that Andhakan was born. He is Andhakasura. Hiranyaksha raised him as his son. He performed penance and received great boons from Lord Shiva. Therefore, fearing Andhakasura, all the Devas, including Thirumal, assumed the form of women and lived hidden among the companions of Parvati Devi on Mount Kailash. Andhakasura, learning of this, intended to wage war on Kailash. At that time, all the Devas prayed to Lord Shiva, and Lord Shiva commanded Bhairava Moorthy to destroy Andhakasura. Bhairava Moorthy slew Andhakasura with his trident, impaled Andhakasura on the tip of his trident for a long time, causing him suffering, and then granted Andhakasura liberation. This heroic deed is called Andhakasura Vadham. The place where this occurred is Thirukkovalur. This Thirukkovalur served as the capital of Chedi Nadu. It is in this Thirukkovalur that Mei-p-porul Nayanar rules as king. Andhakasura was a great devotee of Shiva. Therefore, no one else could destroy him except by Shiva's power. So, Lord Shiva sent Bhairava Moorthy to slay Andhakasura. Our Shiva Agamas state that those who have received Shiva initiation, if they commit a crime or sin, will not be punished by Yama. They will be punished directly by Lord Shiva. We can understand this concept through the slaying of Andhakasura. Furthermore, Mrityu has the name Andhakam. Andhakan means one who brings an end. Mrityu also means the same. However, in Shaiva Siddhanta scriptures, the name Mrityu is used for Anava Mala (the impurity of ego). Overcoming Mrityu, that is, overcoming death, means overcoming Anava Mala and attaining Mukti. The inner meaning of Lord Shiva slaying Andhakasura with his three-pronged trident is that Lord Shiva destroys Andhakasura, who represents Anava Mala, using his power, which operates in the form of Ichcha Shakti (will power), Kriya Shakti (action power), and Gnana Shakti (knowledge power). The one who performed the slaying of Andhakasura was Lord Bhairava. However, Lord Shiva accomplished this act through Lord Bhairava. Therefore, since Lord Shiva is the instigator of the action, this is revered as one of the eight heroic deeds performed by Lord Shiva. The place where this heroic deed was performed is Thirukkovalur. It is called Thirukkovalur Veerattam. Currently, this place is known by various names such as Thirukkovilur and Thirukkovalur. Here, the Lord's sacred name is Veeratteswarar. The Goddess's sacred names are Sivanandavalli, Periyanayagi, Brihannayagi. The sacred trees are Vilvam and Konrai. The sacred river is Thenpennaiyaru. Dakshina Pinakini is another name for Thenpennaiyaru. The Sthala Mahatmyam of Thirukkovalur states that by separating the name Thirukkovalur as "Thiru-ku-oval-oor," it means a place that removes ignorance and bestows grace upon living beings. Therefore, due to its nature of removing defects from living beings and bestowing grace, it is called Thirukkovalur. This temple is located on the southern bank of the Pennaiyaru river. The sanctum faces west. Thirukkovilur is the name of the town and the sacred place. Veerattanam is the name of the temple. The Pennaiyaru river here is a sacred water that cures both physical and spiritual ailments. From the bank of the Pennaiyaru river, we can see the peak of Thiruvannamalai. This glorious Thirukkovalur is the capital of Chedi Nadu. Our Mei-p-porul Nayanar rules there. Our Mei-p-porul Nayanar's lineage is that of Shiva devotees. He is born into a lineage that holds love for Lord Shiva, who is half-female, and excels in both his maternal and paternal lines. Thus, Mei-p-porul Nayanar comes from a lineage that has traditionally loved Lord Shiva, who is half-female, through both his mother's and father's lines. Here, Sekkizhar refers to this as a lineage that follows the path of love for the Lord who is half-female. In the Shaivite tradition, love is what living beings have for Lord Shiva. Grace is what Lord Shiva has for living beings. But here, Sekkizhar mentions "following the path of love for the Lord who is half-female." Sivakavimani explains this by considering love here to signify the source of grace. Its meaning is that our Mei-p-porul Nayanar comes from a lineage of love that flourishes in this world due to the grace Lord Shiva has for living beings. Furthermore, Mei-p-porul Nayanar belongs to a lineage that has traditionally loved Lord Shiva through his mother's side. He also comes from a lineage that has traditionally loved Lord Shiva through his father's side. Not only that, but Mei-p-porul Nayanar is born here due to the merit of our penance, having loved Lord Shiva in many previous births. Mei-p-porul Nayanar rules in Thirukkovalur. How? Sekkizhar says he rules, upholding the truth of the good Vedic path and attaining greatness. Here, "Veda Nanneri" (good Vedic path) refers to Siddhanta Shaivism. Siddhanta Shaivism is that which encompasses and manifests all Vedic principles as its integral parts. Many things stated in the Vedas become parts of Shaivism. As the parts become clear, the whole also becomes clear, and Mei-p-porul Nayanar rules according to the good Vedic path. By saying "Veda Nanneri" instead of just "Veda Neri," and furthermore by Sekkizhar saying "Veda Nanneriyin Vaimai Vilangida" (so that the truth of the good Vedic path may shine), Sekkizhar here refers to the inner, true meaning of the Vedas, which is the Shaivite path. Moreover, the lines "Veda Neri Vilangida Menmai Poonda" (upholding the Vedic path and attaining greatness) imply two meanings here: that Mei-p-porul Nayanar ensured that all the virtues prescribed in the Vedas were practiced in his country and protected them, and that he especially understood the wishes of Shiva's devotees and served them. Mei-p-porul Nayanar is a king, so he has the duty to protect all virtues. Thus, as a king, he protected all virtues and demonstrated the Shaivite path by adhering to Shaivite conduct. The meaning here is that he not only followed it himself but also led the world to follow it. Furthermore, here Mei-p-porul Nayanar understands the wishes of Shiva's devotees and acts according to their commands. "Karutharindhu Nadathal" (acting by understanding intentions) here implies that Mei-p-porul Nayanar is capable of understanding the unexpressed thoughts of Shiva's devotees through their gestures and performing the services they require. Thus, Mei-p-porul Nayanar protected all the virtues mentioned in the Vedas in his country. He ensured their practice. He himself adhered to the Shaivite path. He also led others to stand firm and guided them. Now, Sekkizhar says that he ruled without deviating from ethical principles in his political administration. He protected the rules prescribed in ethical texts regarding the path of governance without deviation. The rules prescribed in ethical texts refer to the political ethics stated in Thiruvalluvar's Thirukkural and all other ethical texts. Sekkizhar does not simply say "political path" but states that he ruled without deviating from ethical principles in the political path. There are also unethical paths in politics. Therefore, our Mei-p-porul Nayanar suppressed those unethical paths and governed through ethical politics. Mei-p-porul Nayanar, who protected ethical political principles and the good Vedic path within his country, also protected his country from enemies with the strength of his shoulders. Sekkizhar describes Mei-p-porul Nayanar's shoulders as "varai nedunthol" (mountain-like long shoulders). This means shoulders that are tall and long like a mountain. Just as a mountain is unmoving, unshaken, tall, and strong, so too were Mei-p-porul Nayanar's shoulders strong, firm, long, and tall. Through the strength of those shoulders, Mei-p-porul Nayanar protected his country from enemies and governed. Why does Sekkizhar say that Mei-p-porul Nayanar, a king who ruled without deviating from dharma, conquered enemies and ruled? Mei-p-porul Nayanar ruled without deviating from dharma. However, if other kings, deviating from dharma, invaded to seize his country, Mei-p-porul Nayanar used his valor to fight and protect his country. War is waged for two reasons: to protect one's country from enemies who deviate from dharma, and if necessary, to bring other kings back to the path of dharma. Therefore, in both these ways, Mei-p-porul Nayanar conquered enemy kings and upheld dharma. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 03 — Mei-p-porul Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. In Periya Puranam, in the Thillai Vazh Andhanar Sarukkam, we are contemplating the history of Mei-p-porul Nayanar. Mei-p-porul Nayanar resides in Thirukkovilur, the capital of Chedi Nadu, and rules Chedi Nadu. He worshipped the sacred form of Shiva's devotees. Here, by stating that he worshipped the form of Shiva's devotees, it should be understood that he worshipped them considering only their Shiva form, without contemplating their lineage, character, or other qualities. If one were to contemplate anything other than the Shiva form, distinctions would arise, such as "this is a true devotee," "this is a false devotee," and so on. Therefore, without considering anything else, he loved Shiva's devotees, contemplating their attire as Shiva, as Lord Shiva himself. He lived considering the form of Shiva's devotees as the true essence (Mei-p-porul). Although Mei-p-porul Nayanar was a Shaivite, he supported all religions practiced by his subjects. We previously contemplated in the history of Manu Needhi Chozhan that one of the five duties of a king is to protect his people from the fear of enemies. Thus, he protected his subjects from the fear of enemy kings and ruled. He ruled in such a way that all temples where Lord Shiva, who is half-female, resides, flourished according to Agamic principles. By specifically mentioning Lord Shiva as the supreme deity, it should also be understood that he ruled and protected all other temples dedicated to all other deities according to their respective traditions without deviation. In accordance with the saying, "Whatever deity you see there, Lord Shiva, who is half-female, will manifest as that deity," it is the conclusion of the scriptures that Lord Shiva manifests within the deities worshipped by individuals according to their spiritual maturity. Therefore, Mei-p-porul Nayanar ruled so that the temples of Lord Shiva, the primordial deity, and all other temples, flourished with their respective worship performed properly, without any deficiencies in daily pujas, special pujas, or festivals. Thus, honoring all temples, including Shiva temples, is considered a duty of kings according to the Indian tradition. Even though all virtues and all religions flourished, Mei-p-porul Nayanar ruled, standing firm in the Shaivite path, being a Shaivite, and with immense love for Shiva's devotees, in his state of servitude to Shiva. Mei-p-porul Nayanar ruled with great devotion to devotees, considering both the wealth he earned through his efforts and the wealth inherited from his ancestors as belonging to the devotees of the Lord who performs the dance of grace in Thillai. During this time, he had an enemy king. This king had previously waged war against him many times and fled in defeat. This enemy king, driven by desire and greed, repeatedly invaded Mei-p-porul Nayanar's country, fought with Mei-p-porul Nayanar, was defeated, and repeatedly lost his elephants, horses, guards, and many other things, returning humiliated. Having been defeated and humiliated so many times, he realized that he could not defeat Mei-p-porul Nayanar directly on the battlefield. He understood and knew Mei-p-porul Nayanar's Shaivite principles. Therefore, he devised a plan to defeat Mei-p-porul Nayanar deceitfully, using the very Shaivite principles that Mei-p-porul Nayanar cherished and protected as his life. He decided to accomplish this by adopting the sacred ash attire, which Mei-p-porul Nayanar considered as his very life. The white ash attire is a glorious attire praised in the Vedas. "Sacred ash bestows greatness upon all who wear it with care," says Gnanasambandar. Therefore, that enemy king smeared sacred ash all over his body. He tied his hair into matted locks. He held palm-leaf manuscripts in his hands, hiding a weapon in between them. Thus, he adopted a false ascetic guise. Sekkizhar here names this enemy king Mutthanathan. Mutthanathan, the enemy king, thus comes to Thirukkovalur in a false ascetic guise. This enemy king, Mutthanathan, belonged to an external religion. Therefore, when he adopted a false ascetic guise, he smeared sacred ash all over his body. He was not a Shaivite but belonged to an external religion. Therefore, he did not know where sacred ash should be applied and where it should not. Thus, ignorantly, he smeared sacred ash all over his body. He twisted his topknot into matted locks and made it a matted crown. Thus, he newly adopted this false ascetic guise. If this Mutthanathan had already been wearing sacred ash and matted locks, Mei-p-porul Nayanar would not have gone to war with him. He would not have harbored enmity. Therefore, it is understood that he belonged to an external religion and was now newly adopting this false ascetic guise. The complete ascetic guise is the sacred attire of Shaivism, consisting of sacred ash, rudraksha, and matted locks. Moorthy Nayanar, who ruled with all three of these, is mentioned. We can see this in his history. "Adiyen to Moorthy, who ruled the world with all three," says Thiruthondathogai. Here, Mutthanathan, who adopts a false ascetic guise, also smears sacred ash all over his body, ties his hair into matted locks, and holds a book in his hand. He has hidden a weapon inside that book. Thus, Mutthanathan comes with deceit in his heart, disguised as Shiva outside. Sekkizhar describes this with a simile. Inside a lamp, a small blackness appears around the wick. Outside, there is a brightly shining flame. Therefore, Sekkizhar says here that Mutthanathan, like a lamp with darkness inside and light outside, arrived at Thirukkovalur with deceit hidden within him and a false ascetic guise outside. When a lamp burns, we can see a blackness surrounding the wick, rising upwards. Outside of that, there is the flame. The flame glows and emits light. That flame first appears red, and then the outer part becomes a white flame. So, the light of the lamp, around the wick, will have three parts: a blackness in a bluish hue, then a red flame, and then a white flame. The source of the lamp's flame is ghee. It melts and turns into ghee vapor. That, with a blue color, becomes the inner part of the flame surrounding the wick. This is what causes the lamp's flame to burn. Therefore, the dark part, which is the inner part of the flame, is what causes the flame to burn and appear. Here too, the false ascetic guise adopted by Mutthanathan is caused by the deceitful blackness he held in his mind. Sekkizhar Peruman here presents this rare and appropriate simile, fitting for two contrasting things: light and darkness. This is one of the rarest similes presented by Sekkizhar Peruman. Sivakavimani says that it is rare to find this simile in any other poet. Here, what Mutthanathan adopted was a false ascetic guise. Its meaning is that falsehood came with him in the guise of an ascetic. The ascetic guise adopted by devotees is never false. But here, we should understand that falsehood brought that ascetic guise. Thus, even though Mutthanathan came in a false ascetic guise, Mei-p-porul Nayanar worshipped him as a true ascetic guise, as the true essence. Therefore, even if his guise was a false ascetic one, due to Mei-p-porul Nayanar's supreme spiritual maturity, he considered it a true ascetic guise and worshipped that true ascetic guise as the true essence. Sekkizhar will explain this event in more detail in some later verses. This attire worn by Shiva's devotees, that is, sacred ash, matted locks, and the book of knowledge held in the hands of Shiva's devotees, are special characteristics of Shiva's attire. These three are the symbols of Shaiva Gnanaacharyas (spiritual teachers of Shaivism). Gnanaacharyas are those who have transcended the three malas (impurities) and also remove the three malas of their disciples. The sacred ash they wear removes the Maya Mala (impurity of illusion) of their disciples through its burning quality. The matted locks of the Gnanaacharyas, that is, their tying up their matted hair, is a symbol of removing the Karma Mala (impurity of action) of their disciples, which spreads widely. The book of knowledge held in the hands of Gnanaacharyas is a symbol of removing the Anava Mala (impurity of ego), which is ignorance, from their disciples. Sivakavimani says that the inner meanings of these can be understood by learning from appropriate teachers. Gnanaacharyas are those who have thus removed the three malas. They have the power to remove the three malas of those who come to them. Only by being freed from attachments and attaining good consciousness can one guide others. They are the true gurus. They are the true teachers. Therefore, sacred ash, matted locks, and the book of knowledge constitute the true ascetic guise. But Mutthanathan adopts these as a false ascetic guise and arrives at Thirukkovalur. However, even this false ascetic guise produced good results. Thus, Mutthanathan, in his false ascetic guise, arrives at Thirukkovalur. How are the streets of Thirukkovalur? There are many mansions there. In every part of the mansions, women are dancing. White flags are flying above the mansions. In the halls of the mansions, women are dancing. White flags are flying above the mansions. Mutthanathan is walking through the streets of Thirukkovalur in a false ascetic guise. He arrived at night. Therefore, the white flags are very clearly visible at night. In the halls below the mansions, dancing women are performing, their dark hair swaying. Thus, Sekkizhar Swamigal describes two flags flying: the dark hair of the dancing women in the mansions, like black flags, and the white flags above the mansions. In the halls of the mansions, as the women dance, their dark hair also dances. That dark hair appears like a black flag. Sivakavimani writes that as Mutthanathan, with the black thought of deceit in his mind, approaches, these black flags wave as if to say, "Get out!" and show resistance to Mutthanathan. Even today, black flags are used to express protest, aren't they? Similarly, when Mutthanathan comes through the streets, the hair of the women in the mansions sways like black flags, protesting against him and revealing the deceit in his mind to everyone, says Sivakavimani subtly. Now, what do the white flags flying high above the mansions signify? Even though Mutthanathan has come in a false ascetic guise, the matted locks and sacred ash he wears, that sacred attire, will triumph. Even in this, Mei-p-porul Nayanar will achieve victory. Sivakavimani subtly says that the white flags flying above the black flags, which are lowered, signify that Mutthanathan will be defeated. Mutthanathan, wearing a false ascetic guise, thus reaches the palace of Thirukkovalur. He reaches the palace gate. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 04 — Mei-p-porul Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. In the second chapter of Periya Puranam, we are contemplating the history of Mei-p-porul Nayanar. We have contemplated up to the point where Mutthanathan, who had repeatedly been defeated in battle by Mei-p-porul Nayanar, the king of Chedi Nadu, decided to take revenge on the Nayanar through deceit. He adopted a false ascetic guise, the guise of a Shiva devotee, and approached Mei-p-porul Nayanar's palace. Now, let us continue to contemplate. As Mutthanathan, in his false ascetic guise, the guise of a Shiva devotee, stood before the palace gate, all the doors opened very quickly for him. Because Mei-p-porul Nayanar always cherished Shiva's devotees, loved them, welcomed them, and honored them, it was customary for his servants to allow Shiva's devotees into the palace at any time. The guards, seeing Mutthanathan's attire, similarly opened all the palace doors without any objection and allowed Mutthanathan inside. The chief of the guards there was Mei-p-porul Nayanar. But Mei-p-porul Nayanar considered himself a servant of Shiva's devotees and loved them. Therefore, Mei-p-porul Nayanar considered Shiva's devotees as his masters. So, it was customary for the guards, upon seeing Shiva's devotees, who were also masters to their master Mei-p-porul Nayanar, to allow them inside. Following this custom, they allowed Mutthanathan inside. Mutthanathan, who quickly went inside, reached the door of the chamber where Mei-p-porul Nayanar was sleeping. That is, he reached the last door. There, the guard protecting Mei-p-porul Nayanar at that last door was a guard named Thatthan. Thatthan, seeing Mutthanathan, the Shiva devotee, who had reached the door he was guarding, very humbly submitted a prohibition to the devotee, saying, "Our master, Mei-p-porul Nayanar, is sleeping inside. Therefore, this is not an opportune time. So, please do not enter." In accordance with the saying, "As the king, so the people," just like King Mei-p-porul Nayanar, his servant also humbly stopped the Shiva devotee. Why did Thatthan stop him? Because Mei-p-porul Nayanar was sleeping inside. Therefore, if the Shiva devotee went inside now, Mei-p-porul Nayanar, who would bow and welcome the Shiva devotee as soon as he saw him, would be sleeping, and thus unable to perform the humble greetings and respects due to the Shiva devotee, an impropriety would occur. Not only that, but the Shiva devotee would be prevented from bestowing grace upon the king. Therefore, to prevent error on both sides, Thatthan humbly requested Mutthanathan, who came in a false ascetic guise, saying, "You should bless our king at an opportune time. He is sleeping now. Therefore, please bestow your grace at another time." Hearing these humble words of prohibition from Thatthan, Mutthanathan said, "I have come to impart instruction to your king. Therefore, just as the other guards made way for me without hindrance, you too stand aside without hindering me." Saying this, he pushed Thatthan aside and entered. Thus, Mutthanathan entered the chamber where Mei-p-porul Nayanar was sleeping. There, Mei-p-porul Nayanar was sleeping on a golden bed. Beside him sat his wife, the Queen, Mei-p-porul Nayanar's wife, with fragrant hair. The Queen, Mei-p-porul Nayanar's wife, sat beside him while Mei-p-porul Nayanar was sleeping. When describing the Queen, Sekkizhar Peruman refers to her as the Queen with fragrant hair. This is because chaste women naturally have fragrance in their hair. Therefore, Sekkizhar here refers to the Queen, the chief queen, the Mahadevi, as the Mahadevi with fragrant hair, as she is endowed with many auspicious qualities associated with royalty, wealth, and valor. The king is sleeping. His queen is beside him. In accordance with the saying, "The innocent one who massages his feet and sleeps after him, and wakes before him," it is the characteristic of a wife to serve her husband by massaging his feet, fanning him, and so on, until he sleeps, and then to sleep herself after he has slept. Furthermore, it is also a characteristic of a wife to wake up before her husband and serve him. According to this characteristic, the Mahadevi, Mei-p-porul Nayanar's wife, sat on the bed beside him, massaging his feet and serving him until he slept. Even seeing Mei-p-porul Nayanar sleeping there and his wife, the Queen, sitting beside him on the bed, Mutthanathan quickly went inside. Any person of good conduct, seeing two people in such a private setting, would turn back the way they came without approaching them. But Mutthanathan, even after seeing this scene, advanced forward. Seeing this, the Mahadevi immediately rose from her seat. She rose from the bed. The Mahadevi, who was sitting on the bed, realizing that Mutthanathan was approaching, quickly rose and woke Mei-p-porul Nayanar from his sleep. As the Mahadevi woke him, the king, realizing it, rose with folded hands above his head, saying, "A devotee of Lord Shiva, the God of gods, has come here." Mei-p-porul Nayanar was sleeping. Beside him sat the Mahadevi. Mutthanathan, in a false ascetic guise, a Shiva guise, entered. The Queen woke Mei-p-porul Nayanar, saying, "A Shiva devotee has come." As soon as the feeling that a Shiva devotee had come arose, Mei-p-porul Nayanar folded his hands. It was already his custom to fold his hands as soon as he heard the names of devotees. According to that custom, Mei-p-porul Nayanar's hands folded above his head even before he rose. Thus, Mei-p-porul Nayanar rose with folded hands. If he were to rise first and then bow to the devotee, the fault of delaying the bowing would occur. Therefore, Mei-p-porul Nayanar rose while bowing. Manikkavasagar's Thirukkovaiyar mentions that if one rises from sleep while bowing to God, the accumulation of one's karma will be shattered. Professor, commenting on this, explains it thus: that is, if a person, upon waking from sleep, bows to God, it means that if they were always contemplating God in their conscious state (Jagrata Avastha) before sleeping, then when they transition from the state of deep sleep (Sushupti Avastha) to the waking state (Jagrata Avastha), they will wake up bowing to God. Thus, for those who are always bowing, both in the Jagrata Avastha and when transitioning from the Sushupti Avastha back to the Jagrata Avastha, their karma will not increase. The karma they had already accumulated will also be shattered, according to Thirukkovaiyar. Here too, when the feeling that a Shiva devotee had come arose in our Mei-p-porul Nayanar, his hands folded above his head as he rose from the bed. He rose from sleep. Generally, Shaiva Siddhanta defines the states of Jagrata, Swapna, Sushupti, Turiya, and Turiyatita. It defines Jagrata Avastha as the state where the soul resides in the center of the eyebrows and functions with 35 instruments. When the soul resides in the throat and functions with 25 instruments, it is the Swapna Avastha. When the soul is in the Anahata chakra with three instruments, it is the Sushupti Avastha. Similarly, when it is with two instruments, it is Turiya, and with one instrument, it is Turiyatita, according to Shaiva Siddhanta's definition of these states. We can study this in detail in Shaiva Siddhanta texts like Sivagnana Siddhiyar. This is mentioned here as supplementary information. Mei-p-porul Nayanar thus rose with folded hands. Having risen, he then properly bowed to Mutthanathan, who had come in the guise of a Shiva devotee, in a false ascetic guise. Here, "properly bowing" should be understood as the formal prostrations of Ashtanga Namaskaram and Panchanga Namaskaram. After performing these prostrations, he stood before Mutthanathan, who had come in the Shiva devotee's guise, and said, "It is as if my life has become auspicious and you have graced this place." Mei-p-porul Nayanar says this humbly, standing. Therefore, the truth we can infer here is that one should not sit before Shiva's devotees. Thus, speaking humbly, Mei-p-porul Nayanar stood bowing, asking, "What is the purpose of your visit? What is your mission?" To this, Mutthanathan said to Mei-p-porul Nayanar, "I possess an Agamic text that Lord Shiva, your master, revealed earlier. It is a rare Agamic text not found anywhere else on this earth. I have brought it here to impart its instruction to you." Here, Mutthanathan addresses Mei-p-porul Nayanar as "your master, Lord Shiva." Sivakavimani explains that "your master" here signifies a great sense of intimacy. Calling Shiva "your master" was meant to convey the idea that Lord Shiva had a very close relationship with Mei-p-porul Nayanar. Furthermore, Mutthanathan says, "I possess an Agamic text that Lord Shiva, your master, spoke earlier." "An Agamic text spoken earlier" refers to the beginning of creation. Therefore, Mutthanathan tells Mei-p-porul Nayanar that he has brought one of the Agamic texts that Lord Shiva spoke at the beginning of creation. Agama is a text that removes the bonds of living beings and grants liberation (Mukti). Agamic texts are also those that reveal the nature of Pati (Lord), Pasu (soul), and Pasa (bonds) and convey the truth. Mutthanathan states that this rare Agamic text is not found anywhere else on earth. It is within these palm-leaf manuscripts that Mutthanathan has hidden the weapon with which he intended to carry out his cruel act. Thus, the inner meaning here is also that the book he brought, with a weapon hidden inside, was indeed not found anywhere else on earth. Furthermore, Mutthanathan says that he brought that Agama to impart its instruction. Mei-p-porul Nayanar also humbly requests, "Please impart the instruction." Through this, that is, through Mei-p-porul Nayanar's humble request, it is revealed to us that the Agamas, which are texts of knowledge that ensure the supreme liberation of souls, are worthy of being heard at all times and in all places, without regard for time, place, and so on. Therefore, Mei-p-porul Nayanar, without considering that it was nighttime, or sleeping time, or his bedroom, that is, without considering time and place, requests to be instructed at that very moment, in that very place. Thus, the rule that one should listen to texts of knowledge whenever and wherever possible is conveyed to us here. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 05 — Mei-p-porul Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. In the second chapter of Periya Puranam, we are contemplating the sacred history of Mei-p-porul Nayanar. Mutthanathan, the enemy king, in order to take revenge on Mei-p-porul Nayanar, entered the palace in a false ascetic guise and reached the place where Mei-p-porul Nayanar was sleeping. Mei-p-porul Nayanar, realizing that a Shiva devotee had arrived, rose with folded hands and humbly bowed, offering service. Mutthanathan then said, "I possess a rare Agamic text that Lord Shiva, your master, spoke earlier. I have come here to impart its instruction." Hearing this, Mei-p-porul Nayanar asked, "Is there any greater blessing for me than this? Please recite and impart the instruction of that unchanging Agama revealed by the Lord." Here, Sekkizhar Peruman uses the epithet "unchanging Agama" for the Agamas. "Unchanging Agama" means an Agama that has no opposition or equal. That is, all Agamas are consistent with each other, being uniformly true throughout, and they are Agamas that do not deviate in the slightest from that state of truth. Even the Vedas are revealed by God. But the Vedas speak of many deities according to the spiritual maturity of living beings, according to the differences in their maturity. However, even though the Vedas appear to differ in their outward presentation, Gnanaacharyas (spiritual teachers) understand that the Vedas convey a single meaning. But the Agamas, unlike the Vedas, are constant truths that speak of the same truths without any internal contradictions at any stage. Therefore, Sekkizhar uses the epithet "unchanging Agamas" for the Agamas. Thus, Mei-p-porul Nayanar humbly requests Mutthanathan, who came in a false ascetic guise, to impart the instruction of that Agama. To this, Mutthanathan says, "If I am to impart the instruction of the Agama, then you and I must be alone here. Only you and I should be present. Therefore, tell your Queen to go elsewhere." Sekkizhar Swamigal composes this by saying, "I and you must be in a different place." That is, Mutthanathan says to Mei-p-porul Nayanar, "I and you must be in a different place." Sivakavimani writes that the inner meaning of this is that "I," meaning Mutthanathan, is about to commit an offense against Mei-p-porul Nayanar, a Shiva devotee. For that great sin, he is about to enter the burning hell. Therefore, "I," meaning Mutthanathan, will commit an offense against a Shiva devotee and enter the burning hell for that great sin, and "you," meaning Mei-p-porul Nayanar, will enter the supreme bliss of Mukti, which is the sacred feet of God. Thus, the inner meaning is implied. When Mutthanathan said that he and Mei-p-porul Nayanar should be alone, Mei-p-porul Nayanar, hearing this, looked at his wife, who stood beside him like Lakshmi, and mentally instructed her to go to the inner chambers. The Queen also went to the inner chambers. The Queen is the crowned Queen. Therefore, Sekkizhar here describes the Queen as the Mahadevi who is like Lakshmi, as she embodies many auspicious qualities such as sovereignty, wealth, and valor. Furthermore, Mei-p-porul Nayanar quickly signals the Mahadevi to leave. That is, one should not delay in listening to instructions and studying Agamic texts. Therefore, Mei-p-porul Nayanar tells the Queen to leave quickly, implying this message. The Queen also goes to the inner chambers. Once the Queen has gone to the inner chambers, Mei-p-porul Nayanar seats Mutthanathan, who came in a false ascetic guise, on a seat and prepares to listen to the instruction humbly before him. It is customary when receiving spiritual instruction for the disciple to sit humbly on the ground while the Guru (spiritual teacher) is seated on a higher seat. Thus, he seats Mutthanathan on a seat, and Mutthanathan is about to impart spiritual instruction. Therefore, Mei-p-porul Nayanar praises him. It is customary to praise the Acharya (teacher) before receiving instruction. Mei-p-porul Nayanar also praises him in this manner. Thus, Mei-p-porul Nayanar, standing below, seating Mutthanathan on a higher seat, praises him and humbly stands on the ground, looking down, and requests, "Now, please impart the instruction." Mutthanathan, who had been waiting for this opportune moment, placed the bag of books he had brought with deceit on his lap, and pretending to untie the palm-leaf manuscripts, took out the weapon he had hidden inside. He then carried out the act he had intended to do, precisely as Mei-p-porul Nayanar was bowing. Sekkizhar Peruman presents this in the verse: "He placed the deceitful bundle from his hand on his lap, and as he pretended to untie the book-like palm-leaf manuscripts, when the devotee was bowing, he took out the weapon and did exactly what he had intended, and Mei-p-porul, who worshipped the true ascetic guise as the true essence, triumphed." Mutthanathan, to complete the task he had intended, caused Mei-p-porul Nayanar's body to bleed profusely, and Mei-p-porul Nayanar fell to the ground, utterly weakened. Mei-p-porul Nayanar falls to the ground, bleeding. But Sekkizhar writes that he triumphs in his principle of worshipping the true ascetic guise as the true essence. Sekkizhar here states that Mutthanathan, to take revenge on Mei-p-porul Nayanar, came inside with a hidden weapon and accomplished his intended task with that weapon. It is Sekkizhar Peruman's tradition not to even speak of the sufferings that befall Shiva's devotees. Therefore, considering it a great sin to explicitly state the harm that the enemy Mutthanathan inflicted upon Mei-p-porul Nayanar, Sekkizhar writes in the verse that Mutthanathan, who had come, simply did what he had intended to do. Here, we must praise and revere Sekkizhar Peruman's devotion and love for God's devotees, his mindset, and his steadfastness in the tradition that one should not speak of the sufferings that befall Shiva's devotees. Here, Sekkizhar Swamigal says that Mei-p-porul Nayanar triumphed by worshipping the true ascetic guise as the true essence. That is, the true ascetic guise, meaning the sacred Shiva attire adorned with sacred ash, matted locks, and so on, is the true ascetic guise. It is in this guise that Mutthanathan comes. As far as Mutthanathan is concerned, he adopted a false ascetic guise. But to Mei-p-porul Nayanar's eyes, it was a true ascetic guise. Thus, the sacred Shiva attire adorned with sacred ash, matted locks, Rudraksha, and so on, is the true ascetic guise. That true ascetic guise is the true essence. Why is this true ascetic guise the true essence? Because sacred ash, matted locks, Rudraksha, and so on, are things that reveal the truthful God to us and enable us to attain God. Therefore, these, which are the true ascetic guise, are the true essence. All other things are not true essence. They are false things, is the meaning here. The ascetic guise consists of three things: sacred ash, Rudraksha, and matted locks. It is noteworthy here that Moorthy Nayanar ruled Madurai with these three, that is, sacred ash, Rudraksha, and matted locks, as his royal attire. In the 12th Sutra of Sivagnana Bodham, "Malar Neeyam Malindhavar Vedamum Aalayam Thaanum Aran Enath Thozhumai" (The form of those filled with love for Shiva and His temple should be worshipped as Shiva Himself), the nature of this sacred attire and the benefits of worshipping it are extensively explained. Why are sacred ash, Rudraksha, and matted locks considered truthful attires? Why are they eternal attires? Because these three are worn by God on His sacred body. God is eternal. Therefore, these sacred attires, sacred ash, Rudraksha, and matted locks, which He wears, also become eternal. Sacred ash is on God's sacred body during the Mahasamhara (Great Dissolution). It is worn by God on His sacred body. Thus, when we see that sacred ash shining on God's sacred body, that sacred ash reveals to us God's nature, that is, the nature of God who wears it, God's imperishable nature, and the perishable nature of the world. As sacred ash reveals this, love for God arises in us. This gives assurance to our soul. Therefore, this attire, this ascetic attire of sacred ash, gives us, living beings, assurance. It provides the path to liberation. In the sacred place of Thirunelvaayil Arathurai, Lord Shiva bestows a pearl palanquin upon Thirugnanasambandar. Thirugnanasambandar sees the sacred ash shining in that pearl palanquin given by God and praises the light of the sacred ash. Similarly, the greatness of sacred ash also appears in the history of Cheraman Perumal Nayanar. Cheraman Perumal Nayanar is riding on an elephant. A washerman is walking towards him. Washermen use a substance called "uvarman" (alkaline earth) to wash clothes. They bring this white, chalk-like "uvarman" from various places to wash clothes. Here, the washerman coming towards Cheraman Perumal Nayanar has tied this "uvarman" into a bundle and is carrying it on his head. It starts to rain. Due to the rain, the "uvarman" tied on the washerman's head dissolves and spreads all over his body, making it white. In this state, the washerman comes before Cheraman Perumal Nayanar. Cheraman Perumal Nayanar, a great king riding on an elephant, dismounts from the elephant upon seeing the washerman whose body is covered in white powder, and bows to him. This is because the white "uvarman" powder covering the washerman's body appears to Cheraman Perumal Nayanar as if the washerman has smeared sacred ash all over his body. Therefore, as soon as he sees that sacred ash attire, that sacred ash, he dismounts from the elephant and pays his respects to him. Cheraman Perumal Nayanar pays his respects to the washerman. The washerman, seeing King Cheraman Perumal Nayanar bowing to him, says with agitation and humility, "O King, who do you think I am that you are bowing to me? I am a very humble person. I am a washerman." Hearing this, Cheraman Perumal Nayanar bows, saying, "I am Cheran, your servant." We can see this history in detail in the Cheraman Perumal Nayanar Puranam. Such is the greatness of sacred ash. Similarly, Rudraksha and matted locks are also glorious. Thus, these three attires—sacred ash, Rudraksha, and matted locks—are important components of Shiva's attire. It is the path shown to us by devotees that one should bow as soon as one sees them. These three things—sacred ash, Rudraksha, and matted locks—constitute the Shiva ascetic guise. They are the true ascetic guise. These three reveal the true essence to us. They enable us to attain the true essence, the Supreme Being. We saw in the 12th Sutra of Sivagnana Bodham that this sacred attire of Shiva devotees, this true ascetic guise of Shiva, and the Shiva temples where Lord Shiva resides, should be worshipped as Lord Shiva Himself. God is omnipresent and pervades all places. The Supreme Lord is present everywhere, at all times, without distinction. He is pervasive. In the Sri Rudram of the Vedas, we can see the state of Lord Shiva, who is pervasive everywhere. The purport of Sri Rudram in the Vedas is that by individually listing all living and non-living things, it states that the Supreme Lord, Sri Rudra, is present in all such things. It is also the meaning of Sri Rudram. Furthermore, in the Nindra Thiruththandaakam sung by Thirunavukkarasu Swamigal, the Tamil Veda, we can see the nature of God's omnipresence in all living and non-living things without distinction. Thus, both the Vedas and the Tamil Vedas praise and extol God's pervasive nature. Therefore, God is omnipresent. However, the 12th Sutra of Sivagnana Bodham states that the sacred attire, that is, God's devotees, and Shiva temples should be worshipped as the Supreme Lord Himself. Why does the Sivagnana Bodha Sutra say that the omnipresent God should be worshipped primarily in these two forms? Because God is unmanifest in other places and other things. But God manifests brightly in His devotees and in temples. That is, God manifests unmanifestly in all things and all places, like ghee in milk. Ghee is present in milk, but it does not appear clearly to us. We also do not know it. Thus, God is pervasive in all places and all things like ghee in milk. But He remains unmanifest, not appearing openly. However, in Shiva's devotees and in temples, God manifests brightly, like ghee in curd. He stands brightly and clearly. Therefore, the Sivagnana Bodha Sutra says that we should see the Supreme Lord, God, in Shiva's devotees and in Shiva temples, and worship Shiva's devotees and Shiva temples as Shiva Himself. For ordinary souls bound by attachments, God will remain unmanifest, like ghee inside milk. He will be unmanifest. But in Shiva's devotees, who are freed from attachments, He will be manifest and bright, like ghee in curd. The Supreme Being, who is beyond speech and mind, out of compassion, wishes for all worldly beings bound by attachments to know Him. Therefore, God gives His form, which is His attire, sacred ash, Rudraksha, and so on, to His devotees, Shiva's devotees. Thus, by giving His attire to Shiva's devotees, and through Shiva's devotees, He compassionately makes other souls understand their knowledge. To Shiva's devotees, the Supreme Lord, God, grants the realization of "Soham Bhavana" (I am He) and makes them realize Himself. Thus, because Lord Shiva reveals Himself to Shiva's devotees in the Soham Bhavana, God appears brightly in Shiva's devotees, like ghee in curd, to those devotees who realize Him as He is, transcending everything. We can see this example extensively in Madhava Sivagnana Swamigal's Sivagnana Mapadiyam. Here, Mei-p-porul Nayanar is also a Jivanmukta (liberated while living). Therefore, as mentioned above, he worships the form of Shiva's devotees as Shiva Himself. Mutthanathan was an enemy who had repeatedly been defeated by Mei-p-porul Nayanar and fled. But he has come here in the guise of a Shiva devotee. Therefore, Mei-p-porul Nayanar does not see Mutthanathan, but sees the Shiva guise that Mutthanathan has adopted, and worships that guise as Shiva. These are the actions of Jivanmuktas. But ordinary people are those who see the Shiva devotee, not just the Shiva guise. Therefore, it is very difficult for ordinary people to truly understand the actions of Jivanmuktas like Iyarpagai Nayanar, Mei-p-porul Nayanar, and other Nayanars. Mutthanathan comes here in a false ascetic guise and resolves his enmity towards Mei-p-porul Nayanar. Mei-p-porul Nayanar falls to the ground, bleeding. But Sekkizhar says that Mei-p-porul Nayanar is the one who triumphed here. To us ordinary people, it would seem that Mutthanathan triumphed. But Sekkizhar Peruman here proves that Mei-p-porul Nayanar is the one who triumphed. What does it mean to triumph? If anyone deviates from their principles, they are defeated. Those who steadfastly adhere to their principles are truly the victors. According to this principle, who is the victor here? Who is the defeated? Mutthanathan does not belong to the Shaiva religion. He belongs to an external religion. Therefore, he had hitherto avoided wearing Shaiva religious attire like sacred ash, Rudraksha, and so on. This is because, according to his external religious principles, he did not wear them. He did not adopt Shiva's attire. What is Mei-p-porul Nayanar's principle? Mei-p-porul Nayanar is one who understands the wishes of Shiva's devotees and serves them. He is one who always keeps the sacred form of Shiva's devotees in his mind. He is one who provides for those who come in Shiva's attire without any deficiency. He lives by this principle. Mei-p-porul Nayanar upholds this principle even when his life is at its end, and he triumphs. That is, even knowing that it is Mutthanathan who has come, he worships the Shiva attire he wears and does not deviate from his principle. But Mutthanathan, the enemy, abandoned his religious principles and, to accomplish his intended task, adopted the Shiva attire of sacred ash, matted locks, and so on, of the Shaiva religion, which was opposed to his own. Therefore, the enemy Mutthanathan descended from his principles and came wearing Shiva's attire. He came wearing the attire of a Shiva devotee. Thus, because he descended from his principles, because he deviated from his principles, Mutthanathan is defeated here. Mei-p-porul Nayanar, on the other hand, stands firm in his principle of worshipping the attire of God's devotees, even to the point of losing his life. Even at the moment of death, he bows to the one who came in the guise of a Shiva devotee and humbly prepares to receive instruction. Therefore, Mei-p-porul Nayanar is the one who triumphed by not deviating from his principles. Not only that, but our Mei-p-porul Nayanar, due to his principles, due to the Shaiva principles he upheld, even made Mutthanathan, the enemy king who belonged to an external religion, wear Shiva's attire. Therefore, Mei-p-porul Nayanar triumphed here. Mutthanathan was defeated here. Mei-p-porul Nayanar triumphed because he did not deviate from his principles. Mutthanathan was defeated because he deviated from his principles. That is why our Sundaramoorthy Swamigal beautifully says in the Thiruthondathogai, "Adiyen to Mei-p-porul, who is very skilled in victory." Mei-p-porul Nayanar led an army and defeated Mutthanathan in battle many times. The same Mutthanathan, when he came in Shiva's attire, Mei-p-porul Nayanar even conquered his deceit through his principles. He triumphed in valor before. He triumphs now by worshipping. We can see this principle in more detail in the history of Enathi Nayanar. We will continue to contemplate the history of Mei-p-porul Nayanar. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 06 — Mei-p-porul Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. In the second chapter of Periya Puranam, the Thillai Vazh Andhanar Sarukkam, we are contemplating the sacred history of Mei-p-porul Nayanar. Mutthanathan, the enemy king who intended to defeat Mei-p-porul Nayanar through deceit, came in Shiva's attire and approached Mei-p-porul Nayanar, claiming to impart an Agama. At that moment, when Mei-p-porul Nayanar was bowing to Mutthanathan, Mutthanathan carried out the act he had intended. Thatthan, Mei-p-porul Nayanar's guard, who had stopped Mutthanathan at the gate, had been carefully observing what was happening inside from the moment Mutthanathan entered. As soon as Mutthanathan carried out the act he had intended, Thatthan, realizing that something untoward had happened inside, quickly rushed in and attempted to strike Mutthanathan with the sword he held in his hand. But Mei-p-porul Nayanar, at that moment, when a large amount of blood was flowing from his body, witnessed this scene. He saw Thatthan attempting to strike Mutthanathan. Immediately, Mei-p-porul Nayanar called Thatthan with his hands and said, "Thattha, he is one of us," thus stopping Thatthan's action. Mei-p-porul Nayanar was bowing to receive instruction. But due to the incident that occurred, a large amount of blood was flowing from his body. Therefore, Mei-p-porul Nayanar, who was bowing, fell to the ground. He fell to the ground. Before he fell, he saw Thatthan, Mei-p-porul Nayanar's guard, attempting to strike Mutthanathan with his sword. Upon seeing this, Mei-p-porul Nayanar stopped Thatthan and then fell to the ground. Therefore, we must see here that Mei-p-porul Nayanar's true state of mind, which always contemplated those who wore Shiva's attire with his mind, speech, and body, remained unchanged even in that state of falling to the ground, bleeding profusely. Mei-p-porul Nayanar, with his long arms, called Thatthan and gestured, saying, "Thattha, he is one of us." He thus stopped Thatthan, who was attempting to strike Mutthanathan. Sekkizhar says that Mei-p-porul Nayanar's arms were long. One of the characteristics of kings, according to Samudrika Lakshanam (science of body features), is that their arms should be long. Long arms also signify generosity and benevolence. Such long arms are referred to as "Ajanubahu" in Sanskrit. That is, arms that reach up to the knees are called Ajanubahu. Mei-p-porul Nayanar stopped Thatthan, saying, "Thattha, he is ours." The meaning of this is, "Thattha, you are trying to strike him with a sword, thinking he is a stranger. You should not do that. He is one of us," thus stopping Thatthan. Rajaraja Chola had installed the images of the Nayanmars in the Thanjai Periya Kovil (Brihadeeswarar Temple) at Thanjavur. Among the images of the Nayanmars he installed, below the image of Mei-p-porul Nayanar, he inscribed his name as "Mei-p-porul Nayanar, Thattha Namare Kaan." Even though Mutthanathan, who came in a false ascetic guise, a false Shiva devotee's guise, thus committed an offense against Mei-p-porul Nayanar, and even when Mei-p-porul Nayanar's life was at its end, and even when his guard Thatthan attempted to kill Mutthanathan, Mei-p-porul Nayanar was steadfast and firm in ensuring that no harm should come to Mutthanathan, who had come in the guise of a Shiva devotee. Mei-p-porul Nayanar's principle was the noble one of upholding Shiva Dharma even at the moment of his life departing. We must consider what Mutthanathan's situation would have been if he had not worn Shiva's attire but had come in another form. Therefore, the firmness and steadfastness of Mei-p-porul Nayanar's principle regarding the sacred attire of Shiva's devotees become very clear to us. As Mei-p-porul Nayanar stopped him, Thatthan also ceased his action, bowed and accepted the king's command, and then, supporting and embracing the king, humbly asked Mei-p-porul Nayanar, "What should I do?" To this, Mei-p-porul Nayanar said, "Ensure that no harm comes to this Shiva devotee, who is the devotee of our Lord, on his way back from anyone else. Guard him and take him and leave him." Mei-p-porul Nayanar also said that this task, this duty, should not be entrusted to others, but that Thatthan himself should accompany him, guard him, and leave him. Thatthan, the king's bodyguard, was trustworthy, fearless, and valiant. Therefore, Mei-p-porul Nayanar said, "You yourself take him and leave him." Thatthan also walked with the Shiva devotee as his guard. Thus, all the people gathered, saying, "We will kill this false ascetic, this one who came in a false guise and harmed our king." At that time, Thatthan stopped all of them, saying, "It is our king's command that this person should go safely alive and that I should accompany him as his guard." Thus, Thatthan stopped those who stood in opposition. Hearing Thatthan's words, everyone made way for them and left. Thatthan crossed the sacred city of Thirukkovalur, guarded Mutthanathan, and when he reached a forest where no one could approach, he left Mutthanathan there and immediately returned quickly to the palace. Mei-p-porul Nayanar had commanded Thatthan to safely take this ascetic Mutthanathan and leave him. Therefore, Thatthan conveyed that command to the people of the country who had come to kill him. So, all the people, fearing and being afraid, stepped aside and made way for them. There were two reasons for this. One was the king's command that he should not be attacked and no harm should come to Mutthanathan. They had attempted to kill him without knowing that the king's command was thus. Therefore, after learning the king's command, they were afraid that they had attempted to act against the king's command and stepped aside. Secondly, Sivakavimani writes that they were afraid and stepped aside, thinking that they were about to commit a sin against Shiva by killing this person who had adopted the guise of a Shiva devotee. Thatthan thus left Mutthanathan in a forest at the city's border, and after ensuring that no further harm would come to Mutthanathan from anyone else, he quickly returned to the palace. Sekkizhar, in narrating this event, refers to Mutthanathan as "Vevvinai Kodiyon" (one who is cruel due to evil deeds). He calls Mutthanathan "Mutthan, the cruel sinner, who is the embodiment of fierce evil deeds." Mei-p-porul Nayanar is one who always contemplates Shiva's attire. Mei-p-porul Nayanar celebrates this Mutthanathan, who adopted a false guise, as "Namar," "one of us." That, standing firm in Mei-p-porul Nayanar's principle, became the cause of Mei-p-porul Nayanar's salvation. But in reality, he becomes "Vevvinai Kodiyon." If viewed from Mutthanathan's perspective, he is a cruel sinner, the embodiment of evil deeds. Because he harmed God's devotees, he became cruel and is about to enter the burning hell. Therefore, Sekkizhar Swamigal composes this verse, referring to him as "Vevvinai Kodiyon." Thus, Thatthan quickly returns to the palace after leaving Mutthanathan, the "Vevvinai Kodiyon," in the forest. There is room here to interpret it in two ways: that Thatthan left Mutthanathan in the forest, and that Mutthanathan left Thatthan. It is indeed proper to abandon this Mutthanathan, who committed a heinous sin. Mutthanathan is truly one who should be abandoned. Those who have no love for Shiva should be abandoned. We should distance ourselves from them, says Sivagnana Siddhiyar. "Those who have no love for God, no love for devotees, no love for any living being, no love for themselves, what is there to say? If we associate with ignorant corpses, they will entangle us in life and death. Leave them," says the 12th Sutra, second verse of Sivagnana Siddhiyar. Thatthan goes to the palace. There, Mei-p-porul Nayanar is holding onto his life, with blood profusely flowing from his body. Why? Because the king is holding onto his weakening life just to hear the good news that Thatthan has safely taken Mutthanathan, who came in Shiva's attire, and left him without any harm, just as he was told. Thus, Thatthan comes before King Mei-p-porul Nayanar, who is in this state, and delivers the news. Only after hearing the good news that no harm came to Mutthanathan, who came in the ascetic guise, does Mei-p-porul Nayanar find peace. It is a principle that those who hold a message as dear as their life will not depart until it is fulfilled. We can see the truth of this here. Blood flows profusely from Mei-p-porul Nayanar. Because of this, he became weak and fell. Even after that, the blood continued to flow, and along with it, Mei-p-porul Nayanar's life also began to ebb. Mei-p-porul Nayanar, with his mental strength, prevented his ebbing life from departing. He is Mei-p-porul Nayanar, who is difficult to conquer. He is a Nayanar who always triumphs. Therefore, Sekkizhar Peruman here uses the epithet "Kottravan" (victor) for Mei-p-porul Nayanar. He is a wise person who, with his victorious nature, held back his departing life until he heard Thatthan's words. Therefore, Sekkizhar Swamigal here refers to Mei-p-porul Nayanar as "Kottravan." He triumphs over all enemies, including Mutthanathan, in battle. He triumphs over the deceit of the enemy Mutthanathan by worshipping the attire of a Shiva devotee. Therefore, he triumphs by preventing harm to his enemy. Furthermore, when Thatthan attempts to strike Mutthanathan with a sword, Mei-p-porul Nayanar stops Thatthan and triumphs by preventing harm to Shiva's devotees. He also triumphs according to the principle, "Inna Seitharai Oruththal Avar Naana Nannayam Seithuvidal" (To punish those who do evil is to make them ashamed by doing good to them). What is Parimelazhagar's commentary on the Kural, "Inna Seitharai Oruththal Avar Naana Nannayam Seithuvidal"? He says that compassionate people punish those who do harm to them. How do compassionate people punish? Compassionate people do good to those who harm them, making them ashamed. Thus, compassionate people punish those who do evil to them by doing good to them. Doing good to those who do evil is the punishment of compassionate people. It is their method of punishment. Here, the Kural says, "Nannayam Seithuvidal" (doing good). Parimelazhagar interprets the word "vidal" here as "forgetting both." That is, if someone harms compassionate people, as a punishment for that harm, compassionate people will do good to them. Thus, by doing good to them, they punish them and forget both the harm they did and the good they did to them, writes Parimelazhagar. He writes a beautiful commentary. If we apply this to the history of Mei-p-porul Nayanar here, Mei-p-porul Nayanar, being a compassionate person, sends Mutthanathan, who harmed him, away safely, ensuring that no harm or damage comes to Mutthanathan. And he also forgets the good he did to Mutthanathan. He does not even consider what Mutthanathan did as harm. Therefore, Mei-p-porul Nayanar stands a step above Parimelazhagar's commentary here. All Nayanmars are those who transcend worldly dharma and adhere to Shiva dharma. This is also an example of how they ascend many steps above worldly dharma and protect Shiva dharma. Therefore, Mei-p-porul Nayanar triumphs here as well. Furthermore, he holds back his life until the news comes that Thatthan has safely taken Mutthanathan and left him. In this too, Mei-p-porul Nayanar triumphs. Thus, Sekkizhar Swamigal here refers to Mei-p-porul Nayanar as "Kottravan" to signify the various triumphs Mei-p-porul Nayanar achieved. We will continue to contemplate. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 07 — Mei-p-porul Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. In Periya Puranam, Thillai Vazh Andhanar Sarukkam, we are contemplating the history of Mei-p-porul Nayanar. Thatthan, after leaving Mutthanathan in the forest at the village border, returned quickly to the palace and, bowing, told Mei-p-porul Nayanar that he had safely taken Mutthanathan and left him. Hearing these words, Mei-p-porul Nayanar thanked Thatthan, saying, "Who else could have done what you, my master, have done for me today?" Sekkizhar writes here, "He looked at the one who stood, saying, 'Who else could have done what my master did for me today?' and bestowed great compassion upon him." Every word in this noble statement made by Mei-p-porul Nayanar to Thatthan, "Who else could have done what my master did for me today?" is highly appropriate and deeply meaningful. Mei-p-porul Nayanar says to Thatthan, "When a king is in full power and authority, everyone obeys the king's command due to fear of the king, or to gain some benefits from the king, and for various other reasons. But here, at this moment, when Mei-p-porul Nayanar has lost his strength, is bleeding profusely, has fallen to the ground, and his life is ebbing, it is very rare for someone to listen to my words, that is, to listen to my command and act accordingly." Therefore, Mei-p-porul Nayanar says to Thatthan, "Who else would do what you have done for me today?" That is, Thatthan obeyed Mei-p-porul Nayanar's command when Mei-p-porul Nayanar was a powerful king. Now, even in this state where Mei-p-porul Nayanar is bleeding profusely, has fallen to the ground, and his life is about to ebb, he listens to the king's command and carries it out exactly as instructed. Therefore, Mei-p-porul Nayanar addresses Thatthan as "Aiyan" (master). In reality, Thatthan is Mei-p-porul Nayanar's servant, his guard. But Mei-p-porul Nayanar now calls him "Aiyan." Thatthan attempted to strike Mutthanathan with a sword. But he obeyed the king's command and did not do so. Therefore, Thatthan thought that the king's command was greater than his own opinion and obeyed. Therefore, Mei-p-porul Nayanar calls him "Aiyan," referring to Thatthan's greatness and nobility. Thus, Mei-p-porul Nayanar called Thatthan "Aiyan" to acknowledge the goodness of the duty Thatthan performed for Mei-p-porul Nayanar, and for helping to uphold the principle of Mei-p-porul Nayanar, who revered Shiva's attire. Mei-p-porul Nayanar called him "Aiyan" as a gesture of gratitude to Thatthan. Furthermore, he praises him by asking, "Who else could have done what Thatthan did?" He praises him, saying that Thatthan alone is capable of doing this work and no one else would do it, and Mei-p-porul Nayanar casts his gaze, filled with great compassion, upon Thatthan. Through this, Mei-p-porul Nayanar blesses Thatthan with his mind, speech, and body. Then, Mei-p-porul Nayanar imparts instruction to the prince who is to rule after him, the ministers who will assist in governing the kingdom, the queens who are grieving due to their love for him, and all his relatives. He gives them the message as his command: "All of you should always protect the love you have for sacred ash without weakening, and live in this world." Mei-p-porul Nayanar considered his foremost duty to be upholding and adhering to the principle of protecting the sacred attire and Shiva's devotees, and the sacred attire of Shiva's devotees, until the very end. He considered this his foremost duty. That is why he held onto his life until Thatthan delivered the news, that is, until he heard the good news that no harm had come to Mutthanathan, who wore the sacred attire of God's devotees. That was Mei-p-porul Nayanar's primary duty, the principle of protecting the attire of Shiva's devotees. After that, he thought of his second duty, his duty as a ruler, and gave divine instruction to the prince who would rule after him, the ministers, and others. After that, he thought of his beloved wives and relatives. At the moment of his life departing, he first blessed Thatthan, who had fulfilled his principle of protecting Shiva's attire and Shiva's devotees' attire. After that, he issued a divine command to the prince and others who were to fulfill the second duty, the political duty. Then he instructed his beloved wives and all relatives about their duties. Therefore, if we look at this sequence, Sekkizhar Peruman's idea that Shiva's work and the work of Shiva's devotees should come first, followed by political matters, and then family matters last, becomes very clear to us. Mei-p-porul Nayanar thus gives divine instruction to everyone. What is it? He says that one should protect the conduct of love for sacred ash and other sacred attires without weakening and live in the world, stating that this is the definitive truth he can tell them in this concluding state. Mei-p-porul Nayanar's conviction is that adhering to this one goal will bring divine bliss to everyone. Having said this and completed all his duties, Mei-p-porul Nayanar meditates on the beautiful sacred feet of Ambalavanan, who performs the dance of grace in Chidambaram, in Thiruchitrambalam. Mei-p-porul Nayanar meditates on the sacred feet of Thiruchitrambalavanan, whom he had worshipped daily as his personal deity. At the moment of his life departing, he meditates on the sacred feet of Thiruchitrambalanathan, the Lord whom he had worshipped daily as his personal deity. When life departs from this body, one should remember Lord Shiva. It is said that one becomes what one thinks of at the time of death. Since desire is the seed of birth, one should not think of any object or desire anything when the body leaves, but should think of the sacred feet of Lord Shiva with a melting heart. When one melts thus, one's hair should stand on end, and tears of joy should flow from one's eyes. One should meditate on Lord Shiva without interruption. At the end of our life, when the five senses are confused, the path is obscured, knowledge is destroyed, and the life force rises upwards, struggling, at that final moment when life departs from the body, there is the agony of death for living beings. Knowledge becomes confused. At the moment when one's life departs, all one's relatives will be around. All relatives will suffer, thinking of their life departing thus. All relatives will cry. Due to such various circumstances, remembering the sacred feet of God when one's life departs is not an easy task. Remembering God's sacred feet in this situation is a very rare and difficult task. That is why elders say that when someone is dying, those around them should not suffer or cry. At that time, they should recite divine hymns like Thevaram, Thiruvasagam, and so on. However, even if everyone around a dying person recites Thevaram and Thiruvasagam, for the person on the verge of death, remembering God is only possible through God's grace, as a result of having thought of, meditated on, sung, praised, and prayed to God throughout their life. This too can only happen through God's grace. Otherwise, it cannot happen through our own efforts. Nor can it be given by others. It is because of this rarity and greatness that our Paramacharya, the Gnanamoorthy Appar Peruman, who was a complete renunciate king and did not ask for any boons from God for himself, repeatedly pleaded for this one boon from God. "May you come as my companion when I die, and say 'Fear not,' O Lord of Aavaduthurai," and "When I die, may the five sacred letters of your name appear, O Lord of Aiyaru," and "Grant me to speak of you on the day I die, O our Shankara," we can see various Thevarams. Here, Mei-p-porul Nayanar also, as a result of his many days of worship, meditation, and prayer, at that moment when his life was departing, God granted him to contemplate the beautiful feet of Thiruchitrambalanathan, who performs the divine dance in the hall. As he contemplated, God stood before him, before Mei-p-porul Nayanar. At the moment of his life departing, God appeared before Mei-p-porul Nayanar. How? God appeared in the very form that Mei-p-porul Nayanar had worshipped and meditated on throughout his life. Mei-p-porul Nayanar had worshipped Thiruchitrambalavanan as his spiritual master in his heart throughout his life. Therefore, accordingly, God now appeared before Mei-p-porul Nayanar as Ambalavanan with Sivakamavalli Thayar and granted him a divine vision. God, who had hitherto appeared within Mei-p-porul Nayanar's heart-lotus, now appeared to Mei-p-porul Nayanar, expanding within and without, everywhere. The sacred feet of God thus appearing are the sacred feet of God that are unattainable even for the Devas, who are superior in knowledge, lifespan, penance, light, and many other things among the three types of high-caste beings: humans, Devas, and infernal beings. They are the sacred feet unattainable even for the celestial beings, Brahma, Vishnu, Indra, and others who rule the upper worlds, which are the cosmic eggs. These sacred feet are like shade from the scorching sun of the suffering of birth, bringing peace to living beings. As it is said, "The dancing feet that are sought are the feet that grant liberation." Mei-p-porul Nayanar contemplated those very sacred feet. Therefore, God showed those sacred feet to Mei-p-porul Nayanar and blessed Mei-p-porul Nayanar to always be merged in the pervasiveness of His sacred feet. God granted Mei-p-porul Nayanar the liberation of continuously worshipping God without interruption. Thus, attaining God's sacred feet and liberation, and continuously worshipping God without interruption through His grace, is the definition of liberation according to Shaiva Siddhanta. Even in the state of liberation, souls remain servants of God and are immersed in supreme bliss by worshipping Him; this is the definition of liberation in Shaiva Siddhanta. God grants Mei-p-porul Nayanar that bliss of liberation. Now, Sekkizhar says the following:

"Even after seeing the enemy, the false ascetic, taking his dear life, I have only spoken a little of the greatness of Mei-p-porul Nayanar, the king of Chedi Nadu, who, with love for the sacred attire of Shiva's devotees, cherished that sacred attire and protected his principle of contemplating it without weakening, to the extent of my ability. Placing the golden lotus-like sacred feet of that Mei-p-porul Nayanar on my head, and wearing the golden feet of the Nayanar on my head, with their support, I now venture to narrate the history and qualities of the sacred service performed by Viranmeenda Nayanar." Thus, Sekkizhar Peruman says this and then begins to narrate the history of Viranmeenda Nayanar. Sivakavimani states the following as the lessons we should learn from the history of Mei-p-porul Nayanar: It is the duty of kings to uphold political ethics without deviation, to protect their subjects from the five fears, including enemies, and to govern according to the rules of justice. The form of Shiva's devotees should be contemplated and worshipped as Shiva Himself. It is also a major duty of kings to protect the truths stated in the Vedas and to ensure that daily pujas and special ceremonies are performed without fail in all temples of God. According to the proverb, "As the king, so the people," the conduct of the king spreads to the subjects, citizens, and all other relatives. Therefore, the king and others in high positions in this world should live with good conduct. If those in high positions and ruling authority go astray, the world will also go astray. If the world thus goes astray and commits sin, that blame and sin will fall upon those who ruled and those in high positions. Here, observing the actions of Mei-p-porul Nayanar's guards and others, and how their thoughts followed the king's conduct, this world should be reformed. It is the duty of chaste wives to serve their husbands by his side and to sleep after he sleeps. Agamas alone are capable of elevating souls to a higher state. They reveal the characteristics of Pati, Pasu, and Pasa, and serve as the cause of knowledge. We must contemplate that the Agamic truths, as expressed here, without regard for differences in time or place, caused the Nayanar to look upon the sacred attire and bow to the deceitful ascetic, and thus led to this historical event. The understanding of Agamas is superior to all else. It is improper to even speak of the sufferings and dangers that befall great people. Speaking of them brings sin and blame. Guards should be vigilant and dedicated in protecting their master. Guards and servants should carry out the tasks assigned by their master, even if they are contrary to their own opinions, and act with integrity. Citizens and all others should fear and obey the king's command. It is far better to obey commands when one is weak than when one is strong. It is beneficial for the government and all others to protect and uphold the path of love for sacred ash. Remembering and contemplating God at the time of death brings assurance to the soul. This is possible only through the grace of God, as a result of many years of practiced Shiva contemplation, and not in any other way. If one contemplates God at the moment of death, God, Lord Shiva Himself, will appear and bestow His grace upon us. Thus, He will grant us liberation, and continuously worshipping Lord Shiva without interruption is the bliss of liberation. Thus, we conclude the Purana of Mei-p-porul Nayanar, and as Sekkizhar said, we can now contemplate the Purana of Viranmeenda Nayanar. Praise to the sacred feet of Mei-p-porul Nayanar. Praise to the sacred feet of Sekkizhar Swamigal. Praise to the sacred feet of Sundaramoorthy Swamigal. Thiruchitrambalam.
