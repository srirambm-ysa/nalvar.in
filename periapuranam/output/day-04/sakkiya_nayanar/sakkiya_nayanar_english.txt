Chapter 01 — Sakkiya Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Part 915, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Varkonda Vanamulaiyal Sarukkam, Sakkiya Nayanar Puranam. We know that the Periyapuranam, also known as Thiruthondar Puranam, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, consists of 13 Sarukkams. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman composed this sacred Periyapuranam with 11 Sarukkams, one for each of the 11 Thiruppadals in the Thiruthondathogai composed by Sundaramurthy Swamigal, and in addition, the Thirumalai Sarukkam as the first Sarukkam and the Vellana Sarukkam as the concluding Sarukkam. Of these, we have so far pondered upon six Sarukkams. We have so far pondered upon six Sarukkams corresponding to the five Thiruppadals in the Thiruthondathogai. Now, the seventh Sarukkam is the Varkonda Vanamulaiyal Sarukkam. This Sarukkam corresponds to the sixth Thiruppadal in the Thiruthondathogai. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman has named the Sarukkams by taking the first part of the Thiruppadals in the Thiruthondathogai. Accordingly, the sixth Thiruppadal in the Thiruthondathogai begins with "Varkonda Vanamulaiyal". In accordance with this, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman has named this Sarukkam "Varkonda Vanamulaiyal Sarukkam". The sixth Thiruppadal of the Thiruthondathogai is as follows: "Varkonda Vanamulaiyal Umaipangan kazhale maravathu kallerindha Sakkiyarkkum adiyen. Seerkonda pugazhvalla siruppulikkum adiyen. Sengattangudi meya siruthondarkku adiyen. Karkonda kodaikkazharchitrarivarkkum adiyen. Kazharkali Ganathan adiyarkkum adiyen. Arkonda verkutran Kalandaikkon adiyen. Aruran Aruril Ammanukku ale." This is the sixth Thiruppadal of the Thiruthondathogai. The Nayanmars praised in the sixth Thiruppadal of the Thiruthondathogai are Sakkiya Nayanar, Siruppuli Nayanar, Siruthonda Nayanar, Kazharchitrarivar Nayanar, Ganathar Nayanar, and Kootruva Nayanar - six Nayanmars. Accordingly, these six Thiruppuranams are also found in the Varkonda Vanamulaiyal Sarukkam. We know that the main divisions of Periyapuranam are Sarukkams, and the smaller divisions within those Sarukkams are Puranams. Accordingly, the first devotee praised in the sixth Thiruppadal of this Thiruthondathogai is Sakkiya Nayanar. Based on this, the 34th Puranam is the Sakkiya Nayanar Puranam. This section describes the life history and character of Sakkiya Nayanar. In the order of presentation, the life history of Sakkiya Nayanar is the first among the six devotees described in the Varkonda Vanamulaiyal Sarukkam. Sundaramurthy Swamigal has praised Sakkiya Nayanar in the Thiruthondathogai as follows: "Varkonda Vanamulaiyal Umaipangan kazhale maravathu kallerindha Sakkiyarkkum adiyen." This means, "I am a servant to Sakkiya Nayanar who, without forgetting the sacred feet of Lord Shiva, who has Umaiyamman, with her beautiful, bound breasts, as one half of His body, threw stones and thereby attained Sivagati." This is its meaning. The Thiruppuranam of Sakkiya Nayanar is a very wonderful Thiruppuranam. "Sakkiyar" refers to Buddhists. This Nayanar initially followed the path of the Buddha's dharma and studied and researched various ways to attain good knowledge through Buddhist texts. This Nayanar realized that the religious knowledge of the Buddha, the conclusions of other external religions like Jainism, and their knowledge were all not the ultimate truth, and that the good path of Shiva was the true essence. Therefore, he realized that attaining Lord Shiva was the way to salvation. He realized this through divine grace. He concluded that no matter what state one is in or what guise one adopts, the true essence lies in never forgetting the divine grace of Lord Shiva. Therefore, even though he was in a Buddhist guise, he continued to perform service to Lord Shiva without abandoning that adopted Buddhist guise.

He clearly understood that worshipping the Shiva Lingam was the best way to attain Lord Shiva. He adopted the rule of worship that he should only eat after seeing a Shiva Lingam every day. Therefore, he found a Shiva Lingam in an open space. His adopted guise was that of a Buddhist. The Nayanar, who had decided to worship Lord Shiva no matter what state he was in, was at a loss as to what to do when he saw the Shiva Lingam. He was agitated. He picked up a stone nearby and, with great love, threw it at the sacred form of the Shiva Lingam as an act of worship. The next day, when he went to worship, he realized that the previous day's act, that is, throwing a stone at the Shiva Lingam and performing it as an act of worship, had happened due to the divine grace of Lord Shiva, and he continued to do so. Through the practice of love, his act of throwing stones became Shiva Puja, an excellent Shiva Puja. Sakkiya Nayanar was one who attained the Lord in this manner. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman has blessed us with his sacred history in 18 Thiruppadals. This Nayanar was born and lived in Thirukanchipuram. Therefore, Sundaramurthy Swamigal sang in the Thogai Nool, "Varkonda Vanamulaiyal Umaipangan kazhale maravathu kallerindha Sakkiyarkkum adiyen." In this, Sundaramurthy Swamigal has briefly described the sacred history of Sakkiya Nayanar, yet in such a way that the full history unfolds when one learns it. Here, why did Sundaramurthy Swamigal mention the sacred name of Umaiyamman? Because in Kanchipuram, the Lord, in the form of Kamakshi Ammai, bears the marks of her bangles and breasts, and gives darshan with those marks. To indicate that such a Lord was moved even by the stone thrown by Sakkiya Nayanar and accepted it with grace, he sang, "Varkonda Vanamulaiyal Umaipangan kazhale maravathu kallerindha Sakkiyarkkum adiyen." "Sakkiyar" is not the Nayanar's birth name. It is a name derived from his adopted Buddhist affiliation. "Sakkiyar" means Buddha. Since he loved Shiva while remaining in that Buddhist guise without abandoning or discarding it, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman has structured the Thiruppuranam and named the Nayanar with the same name. To explain the truth that Lord Shiva is the head of all religions, Sakkiya Nayanar worshipped Lord Shiva in a Buddhist guise. Thus, the meaning of those lines praising the Nayanar, composed by Sundaramurthy Swamigal in the Thogai Nool, is established. Now, in the Vagai Nool, Thiruthondar Thiruvandhadhi, the 41st Thiruppadal composed by Nambiandar Nambigal praising Sakkiya Nayanar is as follows: "Thagadan adaiyan Sakkiyan makkattadavaraiyan magal thanam thakka kuzhaindha thinnitholar vankambar sempon thigazh tharumeniyil sengal erindhu Sivapurathu pugazhdharap pukavan oor Sangamangai puvaniyile." This is the Thiruppadal. Sakkiyar, who wore garments like plates, was one who, by throwing a red brick onto the golden, shining form of Thiruvekambar, whose firm shoulders were softened by the embrace of Parvati Ammaiyar, the daughter of the great Himalayas made of large rocks, entered Sivapuram with glory. His village is Sangamangai on this earth.

Lord Shiva, who stands as the head, became his devotee, and Sakkiya Nayanar was a devotee who came in the form of an external religion, Buddhism. Because he had the conviction that Saivism is the true religion, he rejoiced upon seeing the Shiva Lingam and threw stones, and I begin to praise, to the extent of my knowledge, his attainment of the sacred feet of Lord Shiva, which removes the imperfections of living beings, says the divine Sekkizhar Peruman, composing this first Thiruppadal. Lord Shiva stands as the head of the six religions. The six religions are divided into four categories: Purappuram (outermost), Puram (outer), Agappuram (inner-outer), and Agam (innermost). Each of these four categories has six religions. In the Purappuram category, there are six religions: Lokayatam, and the four types of Buddhism, and Jainism. In the Puram category, there are six religions: Tarkam, Mimamsai, Ekanmavadham, Sankhyam, Yogam, and Pancharatram. In the Agappuram category, there are six religions: Pasupatham, Maviratham, Kapalam, Vamam, Bhairavam, and Aikkyavada Samayam. In the Agam category, there are six religions: Padanavada Saivam, Bhedavada Saivam, Siva Samavada Saivam, Siva Sankrantavada Saivam, Ishvara Avikaravada Saivam, and Sivadvaita Saivam. Thus, each of the four categories is divided into six religions. Therefore, Lord Shiva is the head of all these six-fold religions. The nature of these religions and the differences between them have been distilled and presented by Sri Madhava Sivagnana Munivar in the Avaiyadakkam section of his Sivagnana Mapashyam, under the heading "Puran". The differences between these and the ultimate Siddhanta Saivam, and why they were classified as religions, can be seen in detail there. Therefore, "Arusamayam" here refers to the four types of religions, each divided into six categories. Lord Shiva is the head of all such religions. We see this in various sacred verses. "Ottaru samayangatku oru thalaivan" is from Thirugnanasambandar Peruman's Thevaram. "Aru samayathu avaravarai thetrum thakaiyan" is from Thirunavukkarasar Peruman's Thevaram. "Arivinan mikka aruvakaisamayathu avvavarkku ange ararul purindhu" is from Sundaramurthy Swamigal's Thevaram. "Aruvakaisamayathorkkum avvavar porulai" is from Sivagnana Siddhiyar. Through these, these truths are known. Why were so many religious differences ordained by God? Are all these necessary? If one asks whether all these cause conflict and strife among people, the answer is that because the levels of maturity of living beings are many and varied, all these types of religions are required according to those levels of maturity, which is the true conviction stated in the Siva Agamas. Therefore, we should not quarrel over these, but rather understand and clarify these religions in stages, according to how the wisdom texts state them. It is the duty of wise people to listen to these truths as advice from elders. Thus, our Lord stands as the head of the six religions. Therefore, the Lord Shiva, as the indweller within each living being, explains things according to their maturity, so that the followers of each religion may believe that their own religious doctrine is the true conclusion, and He arranges all these within His pervasive nature as individual parts. Thayumanavar Swamigal also states this beautifully, explaining the idea of "Samaya kodigalellam thatham deivam en deivam endru engum thodarnthu ethir vazhakidavum nindrathu ethu?" (What is it that stands when all the crores of religions contend, saying "my God is the God"?) The divine Sekkizhar Peruman shows us all this here. Because, by explaining all these and describing the Lord in this way, the Nayanar of this Puranam, Sakkiya Nayanar, belonged to the Sakkiya religion, which is Buddhism, an external religion.

The divine Sekkizhar Peruman structures it this way to show that the Lord Himself is the head of that Buddhist religion, which is a division within the external religions. Sakkiya Nayanar worshipped Shiva while remaining in a Buddhist guise. Is it permissible to do so? To clarify this doubt for those who might have it, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman begins this sacred Puranam by stating that Lord Shiva is the head of the six religions, including the Buddhist religion, and therefore, the act of worshipping Lord Shiva in that guise does not deviate from the religious path, and that the Nayanar did not stray from the religious path. Thus, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman begins this sacred Puranam by stating that Lord Shiva is the head of the six religions and continues to sing about how the Nayanar, who performed service to Shiva without abandoning the guise of an external religion, Sakkiya Nayanar, surrendered to the sacred feet of Lord Shiva, which removes all imperfections and impurities, and attained Sivapuram, and we begin to praise that sacred history as we know it. Let us also continue to ponder. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 02 — Sakkiya Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Part 916. Varkonda Vanamulaiyal Sarukkam, Sakkiya Nayanar Puranam. The second Thiruppadal in Sakkiya Nayanar Puranam. "Thalalar Thiruchangamangaiyinil thagavudaiya velalar kulathu udithar. Mikka porul therindhu unarnthu kolagi palluyirkkum aruludaiyarai kezhumi niladhu pirandhu irakkum nilai ozhiven ena nirpar." This is the Thiruppadal. Our Nayanar was born in the village of Thiruchangamangai, near Kanchipuram, in the Velalar community, who are known for their diligence. The Velalar community is a "thagavudaiya Velalar kulam". "Thagavu" means excellence, greatness, and glory. What kind of greatness or excellence? It is the excellence that comes from engaging in agriculture, which involves compassionately caring for and protecting all living beings in the world. Parimelazhagar, in his commentary on Thirukkural, mentions this "thagavu" as a factor that leads to the upliftment of a family. Our Nayanar was born in such a Velalar lineage. The Nayanar engaged in religious inquiry and made great efforts. What is the true substance? What is the ultimate reality? What is the best of all things? What is the enduring truth that remains after all the falsehoods of this worldly life have passed? The Nayanar pondered all these questions. The Nayanar lived with great love and compassion for all living beings. This life, with its cycle of birth and death, birth and death, is a continuous chain of cause and effect. The Nayanar strives to escape from this state. The Nayanar also lived a life of compassion for all beings. The Nayanar's natural love and compassion for living beings led him to practice "pasu dharmas" (duties towards living beings). The Nayanar, engaged in such religious inquiries, left his village of Thiruchangamangai and went to Kanchipuram, which was nearby. He went to Kanchipuram for religious inquiry. There, he sought many ways to attain true knowledge. We learn from history that in those days, Kanchipuram was a hub for various religious institutions and centers of learning, assisting those who sought religious inquiry. People of all religions lived in Kanchipuram, and especially, scholars of all religions lived there in those days. Therefore, there were many universities, religious educational institutions, etc., in Kanchipuram. We also see historical accounts that Kanchipuram itself was previously divided and referred to as Saiva Kanchi, Vaishnava Kanchi, Buddhist Kanchi, and Jain Kanchi. Even in Manimegalai, in the chapter on entering Kachi Nagar, we see that there were many such religious educational institutions. In Kanchi Puranam, Sri Madhava Sivagnana Munivar, in his description of Kanchipuram, explains how people with sharp intellects, like the tip of a darbha grass, had been practicing listening, religious studies, and logic from ancient times. Therefore, besides having various universities and religious educational institutions, the city also had external religious centers like Buddhist and Jain monasteries, sangams, and schools, which built these institutions and spread their religious nets to attract people, as we learn from history. The Chinese traveler Xuanzang, who visited our country in 640 AD, stayed in Kanchi. He wrote that there were over a hundred Buddhist schools everywhere and thousands of Buddhist monks.

In this situation, our Nayanar also undertook religious inquiry and went to Kanchi to attain true knowledge. Similarly, in those days, there was a city called Padaliputhiram in the central region, which is now known as Thiruppathirippuliyur. We saw in the Puranam of Thirunavukkarasar Peruman that there were many Jain educational institutions, Jain gurus, and Jain centers there. We also saw that Thirunavukkarasar Peruman, realizing the impermanence of life, went to Padaliputhiram and joined a Jain school there. We also saw that they too spread their nets and caught people by showing them false "pasu dharmas" (duties towards living beings) and false doctrines. In Kanchipuram, our Nayanar, who had begun religious inquiry and sought many ways to attain good knowledge, first encountered and was able to join the Sakkiyar Sangam, that is, the Buddhist Sangam. Therefore, it is understood that Buddhists extensively spread their religion in Kanchipuram in those days. Buddhism teaches that dharma is the ultimate truth under the heading of Buddhist dharma. "Sakkiyar" and "Buddhists" are one and the same. The principles of these Buddhists have been compiled by Sri Madhava Sivagnana Munivar in his Sivagnana Mapashyam, under the heading "Buddhism" in the "Avaiyadakkam" section. In the "Parapaksham" of Sivagnana Siddhiyar, Buddhism, its principles, its four divisions, their arguments, and refutations are extensively discussed. In the Puranam of Thirugnanasambandar Peruman, we saw in detail how Thirugnanasambandar Peruman, while passing through the village of Bodhimangai, was confronted by Buddhists who tried to create conflict, and how he engaged in a detailed debate with them, presenting the Buddhist arguments and refuting them with Saiva Siddhanta principles, thereby defeating the Buddhist doctrine and converting Buddhists to Saivites. Therefore, our Nayanar joined this Sakkiyar Sangam, which he encountered, and lived as one of the Buddhists, following their practices. He lived following Buddhist practices such as wearing ochre robes. Our Nayanar truly sought to attain true knowledge, didn't he? For those who seek salvation, true knowledge is bestowed by the grace of God through a guru, according to the conclusion of the Saiva Agamas. We see in the Puranam of Sri Manikkavasagar Swamigal, in the Puranam of Thiruvathavooradigal, that Thiruvathavooradigal used to question knowledgeable people who came to him about spiritual knowledge and liberation, and that Manikkavasagar Swamigal was like someone who, despite having an ocean of water nearby, could not quench his thirst with it, because he had drunk from it many times in many places, yet it was not enough. That is, Vathavooradigal used to ask scholars who came to him about this self-inquiry and knowledge inquiry. But true knowledge was not fully explained to him by anyone. In a state where those who are very thirsty, even if there is an ocean nearby, cannot quench their thirst with that ocean water, Vathavooradigal, restless and without peace of mind, sought a Satguru, as we see there. Therefore, Saiva Siddhanta states that tattva gnana (knowledge of reality), meyyunarvu (true understanding), and unmai gnana (true knowledge) are bestowed by the grace of God through a guru. Our Nayanar, Sakkiya Nayanar, while engaged in religious inquiry following the Buddhist dharma, continued to search for the path that would cut off this impermanent cycle of birth. The fourth Thiruppadal. "Annilaimai Sakkiyar tham arungalai nool oodiyathu thannilaiyum purachchamaya sarbugalum porul alla ennum athu thelindhu Eesar arul kooda eeril Siva nanneriye porulavathu ena unarvu nattuvvar."

This is the Thiruppadal. In that state, the Nayanar studied the rare art books of the Sakkiyas. This book is the Tripitaka, the Buddhist scripture. He investigates its ultimate conclusion. Not only that, but he also examines the conclusions of other external religious texts, such as Jainism. In this way, he investigates and understands the truth. How did he clarify that truth? At that time, by the grace of the Lord, he realized that the good path is indeed the path of Shiva. Having clearly understood that the conclusions presented by other religions, including Buddhism and Jainism, are not the ultimate truth, and by the grace of Lord Shiva, the Nayanar firmly established his mind in the realization that the eternal Saiva Nanneri (good path of Shiva) is the true essence. He realizes that the true and eternal essence is the good path of Shiva. The reason for this is that the grace of the Lord was bestowed upon him. Therefore, the bestowal of the Lord's grace is the cause. The realization that the good path of Shiva is the essence is the effect. The bestowal of the Lord's grace means the descent of Shiva's power. This is called Shakti Nipatham. Earlier, in the Puranam of Thirunavukkarasar Peruman, we saw that he "kuruguvar aman samayam" (approached the Jain religion) due to the absence of the Lord's grace. Therefore, the reason for not understanding the Saiva truth and thus entering other religions is the absence of the Lord's grace. Similarly, the reason for understanding the truth from an external religion and entering the Saiva path is the bestowal of the Lord's grace. We see the divine Sekkizhar Peruman presenting these truths both positively and negatively in the Puranam of Thirunavukkarasar Peruman and the Puranam of Sakkiya Nayanar. Therefore, the bestowal of grace is the reason for acquiring true religious knowledge. The absence of Shiva's grace is the reason for not acquiring true religious knowledge. If the question arises, "Is not the Lord equally gracious to everyone?", the answer is that the Lord bestows grace or withholds it based on the varying maturity of their karmas. The Nayanar confirms this realized truth through various questions and answers. This realization is a general understanding in the Parapaksham of Sivagnana Siddhiyar that all external religious affiliations are not the truth, and therefore, the remaining path of Shiva is the truth. This occurs through the refutation of external doctrines. What follows is a special understanding. It is to stand firm in the path of Shiva, having confirmed it with the knowledge of one's own doctrine, based on that conclusion. Therefore, our Sakkiya Nayanar, with the bestowal of the Lord's grace, realizes that all external religious paths are not the true essence, refutes them, and stands firm in the good path of Saivism, having clearly understood and confirmed it. We will continue to ponder. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 03 — Sakkiya Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Part 917, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Varkonda Vanamulaiyal Sarukkam, Sakkiya Nayanar Puranam. We saw that Sakkiya Nayanar realized that the good path of Shiva is the ultimate truth. Now, the fifth Thiruppadal, a very, very important Thiruppadal for Saiva Siddhantins. This Thiruppadal is also a very important Thiruppadal in this Nayanar's Puranam. It is a very important Thiruppadal in Periyapuranam, showcasing the divine poetic skill of Sekkizhar Peruman, who profoundly explains the unique doctrine of Karma (action), which is exclusive to Saivism, in a single Thiruppadal. The fifth Thiruppadal. "Seyvinaiyum seyvanum adhan payanum serppanum mey vagaiyal nanku agum vidhitha porul enakkonde ivviyalbu Saiva neri allavatrukku illai ena uy vagaiyal porul Sivan endru arulale unarnthu arindhar." This is the Thiruppadal. Sakkiya Nayanar further realizes, by the grace of God, that there is one action performed, one doer (karta), one fruit of that action, and one prime mover who bestows and makes one experience the fruit of the action. Thus, if one truly observes, the things obtained by divine decree are four. Sakkiya Nayanar gains this clarity. He also understands and concludes, through penance and the help of Lord Shiva, that this special characteristic is not found in other paths apart from the Saiva path, and that Shiva is the ultimate reality. This is an excellent passage that highlights the unique characteristic of Saivism, which is not found in other religions. This is a conclusion reached by someone who stood in an external religion, conducted a true inquiry, compared, and analyzed. Therefore, it holds a very special place. This Thiruppadal, which simultaneously presents the refutation of external religions and the establishment of Saivism, is a profound truth that we should all deeply understand and rejoice in, as it is revealed through Sakkiyar in this Puranam. The four elements - the action performed, the doer, the fruit of the action, and the one who bestows the fruit - are essential for a true religion. There are religions that do not fully accept all four of these. Such religions have such shortcomings. Saivism, which proves all four of these, is a religion that possesses the complete truth, which is the truth discovered by the Nayanar. The first point is that there is such a thing as "seyvinai" (action) or karma. There are religions that do not accept the existence of karma. Next, the soul, which is the doer of action, is referred to here as "seyvan" (doer). There are religions that do not accept the existence of such a soul. Next, religions that accept the existence of action do not accept the existence of the fruit of action. There are such religions that do not accept the fruit of action. Now, even if action exists and its fruit exists, and even if the fruit of action is received by the soul, there are religions that do not accept the existence of a God who bestows this fruit of action upon the respective living beings. That is, there are religions that state that karma itself will give its fruit. Therefore, there are many religions that accept and reject one or more of these four elements, and do not fully accept all four. Lokayatam, Ekanmavadham, Sankhyam, Buddhism, Jainism, and the six schools of thought are examples of such religions. The Nayanar observed the doctrines of these religions and understood their shortcomings and the complete truth and greatness of Saivism.

The divine Sekkizhar Peruman beautifully describes this. Saivism proves that there is such a thing as karma. The truth of the three entities is a tenet of Saivism, as we all know: Pati (God), Pasu (souls), and Pasam (impurities). In Pasam, there are three: Anavam, Kanmam, and Mayai. Therefore, Saivism states and proves that Kanmam is one of the impurities. Kanma malam is what causes souls to be born and die and suffer, and what enables them to escape from those sufferings and join the God who lives as the life of life, and attain bliss. We saw that there are three types of Pasam, three types of impurities: Anavam, Kanmam, and Mayai. Pasam obstructs the Ichcha (will), Gnana (knowledge), and Kriya (action) powers of the soul. These three, Anavam, Kanmam, and Mayai, bind living beings. Due to the way they bind living beings, they are named Pratibandham, Anubandham, and Sambandham. Anavam is called Pratibandham, Kanmam is called Anubandham, and Maya malam is called Sambandham. Why is Anavam called Pratibandham? Just as a person unconscious from poison has their sensation, action, etc., obstructed, in the Anadi Kevalam (primordial state of the soul), Anava malam obstructs the Ichcha, Gnana, and Kriya of the soul from occurring. Because it opposes and obstructs the soul's Ichcha, Kriya, and Gnana, Anava malam is called Pratibandham. Now, Karma malam is called Anubandham. Just as a person unconscious from poison, when given medicine by others, recovers slightly and regains some sensation, similarly, for the soul that was in the Anava Kevalam without any action, Lord Shiva, with great compassion, associates it with Tattvas like Kala, which slightly removes the obscuring power and creates a slight awakening. This awakening is Karma malam. Because it is given by Lord Shiva subsequent to Anava malam, this Karma malam is called Anubandham. Is it appropriate to call Karma malam, which is the cause of awakening, a "bandham" (bondage)? Yes, in this context, it can be said so because it binds the soul. Just as a physician prescribes a diet to completely remove the effects of poison, Lord Shiva reveals the rules of "hitam" (beneficial) and "ahitam" (harmful) to completely remove the obscuration of malam. This is called Karma. The Karma malam mentioned earlier is Anadi Nittam (eternal without beginning). The Karma now being discussed is Pravaha Anadi (eternal in flow), say the Saiva Siddhanta texts. Just as a physician instructs about the vessels for preparing the diet, God provides the Vach (speech) from Suddha Maya, the Manas (mind) from Asuddha Maya, and the Kaya (body) from Prakriti Maya as instruments for understanding the "hitam" and "ahitam" ordained by Him. Therefore, this body and its instruments, given by Maya, serve as vessels for consuming the medicine of Karma. Others often remind the patient of the rules prescribed by the physician. Similarly, the Vedas and Agamas state all the rules of "hitam" and "ahitam". Lord Shiva Himself bestows those Vedas and Agamas. Therefore, since Maya malam is given in relation to understanding "hitam" and "ahitam", it is called Sambandham. Among these three malams, the Karma malam, which is called Anubandham, was investigated and proven by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman in this Thiruppadal. Another point is that among the three malams, Karma malam and Maya malam are called "agantukam" because they attach to the soul later, while Anava malam is called "sahajam" because it has been with the soul's consciousness from eternity, say the texts. Due to this Karma malam, the subtle body and the gross body, and the actions of "hitam" and "ahitam" performed by that gross body, and again the body, all continue to come. These follow each other like a tree from a seed and then a seed from a tree. Thus, living beings perform actions, and they perform these actions due to their likes, dislikes, and efforts. These actions, which are called merits and sins, are accumulated through the dharma and adharma performed by mind, speech, and body. The actions performed by living beings are classified by God as good deeds and bad deeds. Merits arise from good deeds, and sins arise from bad deeds. Pleasure comes to the soul from merits, and suffering from sins. Thus, living beings must experience the fruits of the good and bad deeds they have performed. This is an unchangeable law ordained by God. To experience and exhaust the accumulated karmas, living beings have to take many births. When experiencing the fruits of karma in successive births, new karmas accumulate due to the likes, dislikes, and efforts that arise. The karmas accumulated in one birth are not exhausted by experiencing their fruits in that same birth. Therefore, all the karmas and their fruits accumulated by a soul over many births are aggregated. This aggregate of karmas and their fruits is called Sanchita. From this Sanchita, God, knowing the measure of karma to be experienced in the next birth according to the maturity of the karma, causes the respective living beings to experience it. Therefore, we see that these karmas have three states. Karmas accumulated over many births and not yet experienced, which are bound and aggregated, are called Sanchita karmas or old karmas. A portion of these is taken and experienced in successive births, which are the fruits of karma to be experienced in a single birth. These are called Prarabdha karmas. When experiencing this Prarabdha karma in the taken birth, the new karmas accumulated by living beings due to the thoughts, likes, and dislikes that arise are called Agamya karmas. Living beings must experience these three types of karmas. When they experience them, new karmas arise, and therefore, births recur. In those births, karmas are again accumulated. Thus, karmas continue to flow eternally without end. There is a time when these will come to an end, which we will see later. If these karmas are bestowed upon living beings, then these karmas are inert objects. The body is also an inert object. Living beings cannot know that "I have performed these actions" due to the obscuring power of God. Therefore, living beings do not have the intelligence to distinguish between good and bad deeds they have performed. They do not have the awareness that this will bring pleasure and that will bring suffering. All these happen to living beings due to the obscuring power of God. Therefore, living beings do not go and experience the fruits of their actions by themselves. Inert karma does not reach the soul by itself. So, how do living beings experience these karmas? God Himself, with His supreme knowledge, omniscience, and omnipotence, knows the soul and the fruits of the actions performed by that soul through His mere will and bestows them. God bestows them with His power. He bestows these fruits of karma without any deviation. Therefore, God is impartial. Karmas are on one side, and the living beings who performed them are on the other. God acts as a mediator, connecting the two. God operates these with His power of grace. Therefore, God, through His command, causes the living beings who performed these actions to experience them without any deviation from the rules. Therefore, God is supremely compassionate. He always has only compassion for living beings. God, who leads living beings to His state, bestows good upon those who do good and evil upon those who do evil. To correct those who commit sins, He gives them punishments and makes them experience them. Siddhanta calls these two states "arakkarunai" (compassionate grace) and "marakkarunai" (stern grace). Siddhanta texts explain God's impartial compassion towards living beings through two examples. A physician performs surgery on a patient to remove a disease and cure the patient. Therefore, the physician is given as an example for God. At the same time, God acts like a king. Just as a king dispenses justice to his subjects, God does the same. The Vedas and Agamas, revealed by God, are like the laws of a country. A king rewards and honors those who abide by the laws and punishes those who violate them. He gives punishments so that those who violate the laws may reform. Sivagnana Siddhiyar states this as "manniya vinaippayan tharum aran tharaniyodu tharapathi pola" (God, who bestows the fruits of deeds, is like a king on earth). Therefore, a living being experiences pleasure and suffering. We see this. If we too are subject to pleasure and suffering, the reason for that is the two types of karma. That is what is called "seyvinai" (action performed) in this poem. Siddhanta states that the fruits of karma bestowed by God for these actions are of six types. It says, "peru, izhavu, inbamodu, pini, muppu, sakkadu ennum arum" (gain, loss, pleasure, disease, old age, death - these six). Due to the body we have taken, the pleasure and suffering that come to us in this birth, such as gaining wealth, suffering in poverty, being happy, being sad, rising in status, falling in status, come in these ways. Therefore, God bestows the appropriate fruits based on the shortcomings of living beings in following the rules and exceptions ordained by God, which are stated in the Vedas and Agamas revealed by God Himself. If a sick person desires and eats a forbidden food, they suffer. If they eat a prescribed food, they experience pleasure. Similarly, if a living being performs "ahitam" (harmful actions) from what is stated in the Vedas and Agamas revealed by Lord Shiva, they will suffer, and if they perform "hitam" (beneficial actions), they will experience pleasure. We saw that living beings accumulate and perform actions through mind, speech, and body. The "hitam" of the mind, that is, good deeds performed by the mind, include having compassion for other living beings, wishing to help, and meditating on Lord Shiva. The "hitam" of speech includes speaking the truth, speaking words that bring joy to others, reciting sacred texts, and chanting the Panchakshara mantra. The "hitam" of the body includes helping others as much as possible, performing temple worship, and bowing to devotees of Shiva. Actions contrary to these are all "ahitam". We saw that these "hitam" and "ahitam" actions, when they mature and come to be experienced, are classified into three types: Agamya, Sanchita, and Prarabdha. The actions performed by living beings cling to the intellect of the doer as a support. That intellect clings to Maya. By divine grace, these grow and mature. In this intermediate period, they are called Sanchita, Punya-Papa (merit-demerit), and Apurva. The pleasures and sufferings that come to be experienced in a particular birth from this are called Prarabdha. This Prarabdha is of three types: Jati (birth), Ayul (lifespan), and Bhogam (experience). Jati refers to being born in high or low castes. Ayul refers to dying in untimely deaths despite having a lifespan. Bhogam refers to experiencing divine pleasures, human pleasures, etc. When experiencing Bhogam, that experience is of three types: Adidaivikam, Adiyatmikam, and Adibhaudikam. Adidaivikam refers to pleasures and sufferings like childhood, youth, old age, disease, etc., that come by divine presence without the presence of a living being. Adiyatmikam refers to pleasures and sufferings that come by the presence of living beings. Adibhaudikam refers to pleasures and sufferings that come from physical objects like lightning and thunder. Thus, due to Karma malam, action arises, and due to action, merits and demerits arise, and due to merits and demerits, pleasures and sufferings arise respectively. If Karma itself gives such results, then it should not and cannot. Karma perishes at the very place where it was performed. Therefore, the soul, lacking the supreme knowledge to know and choose its own fruits, is of limited knowledge. Therefore, God Himself is the one who bestows the fruits of Karma. Thus, the three terms "seyvanum" (doer), "ser payanum" (fruit to be attained), and "koduppanum" (bestower) are explained. We saw that the actions performed by living beings are good deeds and bad deeds. These are further classified into two types: Abukti Purvam and Bhukti Purvam. Among them, Abukti Purva good deeds are when thinking evil results in good, speaking evil results in good, and doing evil results in good. Abukti Purva bad deeds are when thinking good, speaking good, and doing good results in evil. Bhukti Purva good deeds are good deeds performed by mind, speech, and body, with conscious intent. Bhukti Purva bad deeds are evil deeds performed by mind, speech, and body, with conscious intent. Thus, when pleasure and suffering come as a result of actions, they come through the path of "hitam" (beneficial) and "ahitam" (harmful). When experiencing the pleasures and sufferings that come as a result of past actions by the grace of God and through others, the thought that "this has come to me due to my past actions" does not arise, but rather the thought that "this has happened by myself and by others," and the likes and dislikes that arise from this thought, are the cause of Agamya karma. So, this cycle continues. How can a soul escape from this cycle of karma and attain the feet of God? When a soul experiences the pleasures and sufferings that come as a result of its two types of karma, due to the Shiva Punya (merit) that is different from the two karmas, Shiva Punya will arise. The cause of Shiva Punya is that for the souls lying in the Anadi Kevalam (primordial state), at the time of the first creation by God, the Lord Himself takes the form of Sthalam (sacred place), Tirtham (sacred water), and Murtham (sacred image). Among these, Shiva Punya arises from being born, residing, and dying as worms and other creatures in special Tirthams, from being born, residing, and dying as fish and other creatures in special Tirthams, from four-legged animals and others drinking those Tirthams, from immersing in them, from being born as crops in the place where that Tirtham flows, from birds and other creatures eating those grains, and from being born, residing, and dying in a sacred place with a special temple. For the souls that have thus experienced Shiva Punya, the inclination towards Shiva Punya arises, and it becomes a cause for performing more Shiva Punya. Shiva Punya is of four types: Chariya, Kriya, Yoga, and Gnana. Among these, Upaya and Unmai (truth) are each of two types. These are further divided into Bhuddhi Purvam (intentional) and Abuddhi Purvam (unintentional), making a total of 16 types. Abuddhi Purva Upaya Chariya is when wealth and other things acquired as Pasu Punya (merit for living beings) become helpful to Kriya (ritual), Guru (teacher), Linga (symbol of Shiva), and Jangama (moving devotees). Abuddhi Purva Upaya Kriya is like the blind woman who performed Vilva Archana on an old Shiva Lingam at Gokarnam in the middle of the night. Abuddhi Purva Upaya Yoga is when a person who intends to steal sacred ornaments or a temple guard meditates on the sacred image itself. Abuddhi Purva Upaya Gnana is the desire for fame that arises from reciting Vedas and Agamas and clearly explaining their meanings to others. Now, Bhuddhi Purva Upaya Chariya is giving one's wealth to Kriya, Guru, Linga, and Jangama, and performing acts like sweeping and cleaning in the temple. Bhuddhi Purva Upaya Kriya is personally touching and worshipping those three forms. Bhuddhi Purva Upaya Yoga is believing that those three forms are the ultimate reality and meditating on them. Bhuddhi Purva Upaya Gnana is investigating the Vedas and Agamas, understanding that the Kriyacharyar (ritualistic teacher) is an invoked form, and realizing that Paramasivan manifests as Brahma, Vishnu, and other nine forms, and as the eight forms of the sun, moon, etc., and establishing oneself in that understanding with love arising for that truth. Abuddhi Purva Unmai Chariya is worshipping the Gnana Acharyar (spiritual teacher) without considering him as the supreme Shiva in human form, but only as a highly respected person. Abuddhi Purva Unmai Kriya is worshipping the Gnana Acharyar with the above understanding. Abuddhi Purva Unmai Yoga is meditating with the above understanding. Abuddhi Purva Unmai Gnana is investigating Gnana Sastras with the above understanding. Now, Bhuddhi Purva Unmai Chariya is worshipping the Gnana Acharyar with the clear understanding that he is the supreme Shiva. This is called "Kettal Gnanam" (knowledge gained by listening). Now, Bhuddhi Purva Unmai Kriya is worshipping the Gnana Acharyar with the above understanding. This is "Sindhithal" (contemplation). Bhuddhi Purva Unmai Yoga is meditating with the belief that he is the ultimate reality, by the grace of the Gnana Acharyar. This is "Thelithal" (clarification). Bhuddhi Purva Unmai Gnana is attaining the sacred feet by entering into Nishtha (meditative absorption). This Nishtha, which is Bhuddhi Purva Unmai Gnana, will have ten stages: Tattva Rupam, Tattva Darisanam, Tattva Suddhi, Anma Rupam, Anma Darisanam, Anma Suddhi, Siva Rupam, Siva Darisanam, Siva Yogam, Siva Bhogam. Siddhanta explains these as "Dasakariya Vilakkam" (explanation of ten stages). Among the 16 types of Shiva Punya mentioned above, by practicing the first 12, even though the two types of karmas, "hitam" and "ahitam", result in different outcomes of pleasure and suffering, both pleasure and suffering, merit and demerit, are alike in bringing about the result of birth and death. This leads to an equal outlook on merit and demerit. This is called "Suka Dukha Abhedam" (non-difference between pleasure and suffering), which is "Iruvinai Oppu" (equanimity towards the two karmas). When this arises, the intellect becomes firm in true Chariya and other practices. When this is attained, the influence of the "yan enathu" (I and mine) malam decreases, and an attachment to the supreme Shiva and a sense of servitude to the supreme Shiva arise. These are Malaparipakam (maturity of impurities), Shakti Nipatham (descent of divine power), and Sivagnanam (divine knowledge). When this arises, the soul attains the sacred feet of Lord Shiva. For such realized souls who have attained the sacred feet of Lord Shiva, since the sense of "I and mine" is absent, Agamya karma does not accumulate. For such Jivanmuktas, Agamya karma does not accumulate. Due to the manifestation of knowledge, the Sanchita karma, which is supported by the intellect, perishes like darkness before a lamp. Prarabdha karma remains as long as the body exists and is exhausted at the level of the body. Thus, when experiencing, the two karmas that arise as a cause of that experience do not lead to further Agamya karma. This is because the sense of "I and mine" is absent, and actions are performed with the thought that they are service to God. The Thirukkural emphasizes this: "Irul ser iruvinaiyum sera Iraivan porul ser pugazh purindhar mattu" (The two karmas that cause darkness do not affect those who praise God with true understanding). The experience that comes as Prarabdha also does not affect the Jivanmuktas who are immersed in the supreme bliss of Shiva. It comes, but it does not affect them. Regarding this, Sivagnana Bodham states: "avan arul allathu ondraiyum seiyanagave agnana kanmam pravesiya agalan" (Since he does nothing without His grace, ignorant karma does not enter). Thirukkural states: "Uzhaiyum uppakkam kanbar ulaivinri thazhadhu ugnatrupavar" (Those who strive tirelessly will see even fate turn its back). Therefore, from what has been said so far, the awakening that arises from the combination of Kaladi Tattvas is Karma malam, and the Agamya karma, which is the two types of karma, arises from performing "hitam" and "ahitam" due to that awakening. When that karma matures, it becomes Sanchita, which is merit and demerit, and when it comes to be experienced, it becomes Prarabdha, which is pleasure and suffering. When the Prarabdha, which is pleasure and suffering, is experienced, it will come to be experienced through the path of the two karmas. If one thinks that "I did these actions" or "others did them," then further Agamya karma will accumulate. Thus, these two types of karmas that occur lead to bondage. And there are 16 types of Shiva Punya for liberation from bondage. Among them, Bhuddhi Purva Unmai Gnana will have 10 stages. Those who practice these will attain the sacred feet of Lord Shiva and experience bliss. For them, Agamya karma will not accumulate, Sanchita karma will perish, and Prarabdha karma will be exhausted by experience. And that experience will not trouble those Jivanmuktas. Including all these truths, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman composes this Thiruppadal: "Seyvinaiyum seyvanum adhan payanum koduppanum meyvagaiyal nanku agum vidhitha porul enakkonde ivviyalbu Saivaneri allavatrukku illai ena uyvagaiyal porul Sivan endru arulale unarnthu arindhar" (He realized by grace that action, the doer, its fruit, and the bestower are truly four ordained entities, and that this characteristic is not found in paths other than the Saiva path, and that Shiva is the ultimate reality for salvation). Tolkappiyam and Sivagnana Siddhiyar state that there are eight elements for an action to occur. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman shows us the truth of Anava malam and Maya through this one concept of karma, by stating that the fruit of karma comes to the soul because God gave it to remove the darkness of the soul's Anava malam, and that God gives the soul the Tana (body), Karana (instruments), Bhuvana (worlds), and Bhoga (experiences) from Maya for the soul to experience these two karmas. And there is a doer of this action, which is the soul, or Pasu. We see that souls experience different pleasures and sufferings in different bodies according to different karmas. Therefore, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman explains the truth that there are many souls in the single term "seyvan" (doer). He refers to these as coming due to the fruit of karma in the term "vinaippayan" (fruit of karma). The types of karmas that come due to the fruit of karma, and the way they come, and the existence of many births, are all stated in this. Now, the soul has no independence, and therefore it is bound and associated with malam. It does not take its own fruits of karma; there must be one who bestows them. This is explained in the fourth term, "koduppan" (bestower). The divine Sekkizhar Peruman explains the Siddhanta conclusion that this bestower, God, will rescue souls from this cycle of karma, enlighten them, and then bestow blissful enlightenment, by explaining these four elements. In addition, he also refutes other religious views. This characteristic, these four elements, are exclusive to Saivism. Thus, the Nayanar realizes that Lord Shiva is the true reality and the path of Shiva is the true path, through the grace of God, due to the refutation of external doctrines and the conviction of Saivism, as stated so far. Therefore, the divine poetic skill of Sekkizhar Peruman, who, through this poem, has made us understand the entire truth of Siva Siddhanta, which includes the three entities of Pati, Pasu, and Pasam, and their characteristics, means, and benefits, is something we should experience and enjoy. Let us continue to ponder. Thiruchitrambalam.

Chapter 04 — Sakkiya Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Part 918, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Varkonda Vanamulaiyal Sarukkam, Sakkiya Nayanar Puranam. The sixth Thiruppadal, following the fifth Thiruppadal, is also a very, very important Thiruppadal. "En nilaiyil nindralum ekkolam kondalum manniya seer Sankaran thal maravamaip porul endre thunniya vedam thannaith thuravathe thuya Sivam thannai mikum anbinnal maravamai thalai nirpar." This Thiruppadal states that no matter what state one is in or what guise one adopts, the true essence is never to forget the sacred feet of Sankaran, who possesses enduring glory. Our Sakkiya Nayanar, having decided this, without abandoning the Sakkiya guise, the Buddhist guise, that he had adopted, excelled in never forgetting the pure emblem of Shiva with immense love. "En nilaiyil nindralum" (no matter what state one is in) appears here. This "nilai" (state) refers to one's condition. It can be interpreted as "samayam" (religion) or "asramam" (stage of life). Therefore, no matter what religion one belongs to or what stage of life one is in, and "ekkolam kondalum" (what guise one adopts), meaning what form or appearance one takes, "manniya seer Sankaran thal maravamaip porul" (never forgetting the sacred feet of Sankaran, who possesses enduring glory) is the true and ultimate reality for the soul, as the Nayanar understood. "Sankaran" means "one who bestows happiness." "Sam" refers to happiness and bliss. "Karan" means "doer." Thirunavukkarasar Peruman praises Sankaran's feet as "Arutchevadi" (feet of grace) in his Thevaram. He praises them as "azhagu ezhuthaga Arutchevadi" (beautifully written feet of grace) in Thiruvadi Thandakam. Thirunavukkarasar Peruman composed a Thandakam called Thiruvadi Thiruthandakam, praising the Lord's feet. Thayumanavar Swamigal says that whether it is the Lord's feet or His crown, they are both divine grace. Therefore, the sacred feet of Lord Shiva are an honorific term; they refer to the Lord's divine grace. "Maravathu iruthal" (never forgetting) means meditating continuously. In the Puranam of Vayilar Nayanar, we see "maravamaiyal amaitha manakkoyil ul iruthi" (establishing in the mind-temple built by not forgetting). This "maravathu iruthal" (never forgetting) is shown to us as the state of all Nayanmars in their Puranams. We see that never forgetting the Lord's feet is the state of true liberated souls. Ammai also prays, "maravamai vendum" (I need not forgetting). The 11th Sutra, second Adhikaranam, of Sivagnana Bodham states: "Ini bhaktiyinal maravathu etha avanathu seepathathai anaiyum endrathu" (Now, by worshipping without forgetting with devotion, one will attain His sacred feet). Sri Madhava Sivagnana Munivar explains this in the Mapashyam. Ichcha (will), Gnana (knowledge), and Kriya (action) naturally occur simultaneously. Whatever is known (Gnana) will simultaneously evoke Ichcha and action. Therefore, no matter in what order these three are stated, it is appropriate. However, in this 11th Sutra, since it states "ayara anbil aran kazhal sellum" (will reach the Lord's feet with tireless love), Sri Meykandar Devanayanar also states here "maravathu bhaktiyinal etha" (to worship without forgetting with devotion). "Ettuthal" means to follow the path of grace. Love, devotion, and passion, though slightly different in meaning, all refer to the arising of desire for something. Therefore, it is understood that devotion exists even in the state of supreme liberation. This is because, even in the state of supreme liberation, if the soul does not experience desire, then the experience of supreme bliss will not occur. Other traditions that do not understand this

Some traditions say that devotion is a means that the soul undertakes to attain the state of Jivanmukti. Siddhantins, however, argue that devotion is not only a means but is also required in liberation. Therefore, Sivagnana Bodham states that by worshipping without forgetting with devotion, by following the path of grace, one will attain His sacred feet. Therefore, this "maravamai" (not forgetting) refers to not forgetting the sacred feet, the divine grace of God, and offering devotion to Him, not forgetting the help God renders to living beings. Siddhanta explains that even in the state of supreme liberation, the soul will not forget the divine grace of God and the help God renders, and will continue to offer devotion. Thus, he realizes that no matter what state one is in or what guise one adopts, the true essence is never to forget the sacred feet of Sankaran, who possesses enduring glory. "Porul" means truth, knowledge, and benefit. It refers to the liberation, which is the attainment of the soul's ultimate goal. It is understood that true knowledge is to follow His path of grace with tireless love, never forgetting. We saw in the Puranam of Thirugnanasambandar Peruman that he said, "Gnanam enbathu Eesan pal anbu" (Knowledge is love for God). Therefore, our Nayanar considered never forgetting the sacred feet of God as the ultimate truth. Thus, the Nayanar, without abandoning the Sakkiya guise, the Buddhist guise, that he had adopted, excelled in immense love for pure Shiva. "Sakkiyar," that is, the symbols of Buddhists, their attire, are called "tuvara adai" (ochre robes) and "kudai" (umbrella). They also carry a kamandalam (water pot). Without abandoning or discarding such a Buddhist guise, our Nayanar excelled in never forgetting God with immense love for pure Shiva. He firmly stood in never forgetting God, making it his supreme vow, and remained steadfast in his service to God. This Sakkiya Nayanar, even though he did not adopt the Saiva symbols like sacred ash and rudraksha prescribed in the Siva Agamas, and continued to wear the Sakkiya guise, remained steadfast in his service to God with tireless love. "Ayara anbu" means love that is the cause of tirelessness. Sivagnana Bodham states that due to that tireless love, one will reach the Lord's feet. "Ayarama" means not forgetting. Therefore, we can understand that for those who follow the path of intense devotion, knowledge, and detachment, these external guises are not necessary. This rule applies only to Jivanmuktas, who are free from the egoistic sense of "I" and "mine," and not to others. We must very carefully understand and realize this. These are all applicable only to Gnanis (realized souls). The scriptures state that for Gnanis, for Muktas (liberated souls), for those who follow the path of intense devotion, knowledge, and detachment, these guises are not necessary. We must very carefully understand this. Ordinary people should not abandon religious practices by looking at them. They should not deviate from discipline. If we too abandon religious practices by looking at them, we will become fallen and deviate from discipline. We must very carefully keep this in mind. The scriptures state that this rule applies only to Gnanis. Thirumandiram, 1668th mantra: "Gnanamilar vedam poondum naragathar gnanam ullar vedam indrenin nanmuthar gnanam unathaga venduvaar nakkanpal gnanmula veda nanni nirpare." Those who lack divine knowledge, even if they wear the guise of Shiva's devotees, will go to hell. Those who possess divine knowledge, even if they do not wear the guise of Shiva's devotees, are truly liberated. Those who desire to attain divine knowledge will wear the guise that is eternally filled with knowledge, seeking Shiva. This mantra states that wearing a guise always reminds one of God. Therefore, wearing a guise is essential. But if a child wears a king's attire, or a madman wears a minister's attire, there is no benefit for either of them from wearing that attire. However, Gnanis are those who seek Shiva through knowledge and never forget Him. Therefore, for them, wearing guises that remind them of Lord Shiva is not necessary. This is the meaning. We saw that Thirumooladevar Nayanar expressed the same idea in Thirumandiram, which the divine Sekkizhar Peruman also expresses. The 284th Thiruvirutham of Sivagnana Siddhiyar also states this. Those who are in Gnana Nishtha (meditative absorption in knowledge) do not perform any actions. Even if they perform actions, those actions are considered as divine actions, and they do not separate from His golden sacred feet, says this Thiruvirutham. Arulnandi Sivacharyar Peruman states: "Gnanamilarkku nammaiyodu theemai illai. Nadvathu ondru illai. Seelam illai. Thavam illai. Virathamodu achirama seyal illai. Dhyanam illai. Siththa malam illai. Kolam illai. Pulan illai. Karanam illai. Gunam illai. Kuri illai. Kulamum illai. Balarudan unmaththar pisasar gunam maruvi padalinodu adal ivai payindridinum payilvar endru." For those who have attained Gnana Nishtha in this world, there is no good or evil for them. There is nothing to seek. There is no discipline. There is no penance. There are no vows or ashrama activities. There is no meditation. There are no impurities of the mind. There is no external appearance. There are no senses. There are no instruments of knowledge. There are no qualities. There are no goals. There is no caste. These Gnanis, like children, madmen, or those possessed by ghosts, will sing and dance as they please, immersed in supreme bliss. In this Thiruvirutham, Arulnandi Sivacharyar Peruman shows that for the devotees of Shiva, the Sivagnanis, who live in this world as Jivanmuktas and are firmly established in that state, never deviating from contemplation of Shiva even for a moment, there are no specific rules they must follow in their daily lives. He lists, one after another, the Vedic rules they do not need to observe. In the 113th Thiruvirutham of Siddhiyar, it is stated that the rules ordained by God's compassion are: "Ozhukkam, anbu, arul, acharam, upacharam, uravu, seelam, vazhakkila thavam, thanangal, vandhithal, vanangal, vaymai, azhukku ila thuravu, adakkam, arivodu archithal aadhi izhukkila arangal anal iranguvan pani arangal." Here, for those who have attained the state of Jivanmukti and are firmly established in it, never deviating from contemplation of Shiva even for a moment, none of these previously mentioned virtues are necessary. This is because they have already lived by these principles. They are not accustomed to evil conduct. Therefore, all these good qualities naturally reside in them. When they attained the state of Jivanmukti, there were no benefits to be gained from these. Therefore, in life, Jivanmuktas are those who are self-forgetful. The author describes them as "balarudan unmaththar pisasar gunam maruvi" (having the qualities of children, madmen, and those possessed by ghosts). He says they are like children. Children perform some actions. When they do so, they do not consider any benefit. Similarly, those in the state of Jivanmukti perform some actions. But they do not do those actions for their own sake. They do not do them with any benefit in mind. All their actions will be Shiva Punya (meritorious actions for Shiva). Therefore, since their actions are like those of children, he says Jivanmuktas are "balar gunam maruvi" (having the qualities of children). Now, he says the actions of Jivanmuktas are "unmaththar gunangal maruvi" (having the qualities of madmen). A madman is someone who is insane. Therefore, Jivanmuktas also behave like madmen. We see madmen in this world. They do not care about taste, hunger; they run, sing. Sometimes they do one thing, sometimes they don't. They do everything self-forgetfully. They do things without the thought that they are doing them. They babble. They do things as they please, day and night. They don't listen even if others tell them. Similarly, those who have attained the state of Jivanmukti also babble like these madmen. Sometimes they talk to Shiva, cry, laugh, worship, dance. This is the nature of Jivanmuktas. This is what Arulnandi Sivacharyar Peruman shows as "unmaththar gunam maruvi". Now, he says "pisachar gunam maruvi" (having the qualities of ghosts). "Pisachar" means those possessed by ghosts. The Shiva Lingam is of three types: Avyakta Lingam (unmanifest), Vyakta-avyakta Lingam (manifest-unmanifest), and Vyakta Lingam (manifest). The Avyakta Lingam is one with a pedestal and a Lingam. Bana Lingams, Ratna Lingams, etc., are Chala-achala Lingams. If a Lingam is consecrated and worshipped in a Vedic altar, it is an Achala-chala Lingam. Bana Lingams and others are considered Ishta Lingams (personal worship). If worshipped as an Anga Lingam (part of the body) in the presence of Shiva during Parartha Puja (worship for others), it is a Chala Lingam. If established for Parartha, it is an Achala Lingam, hence it is called Chala-achala. If consecrated and worshipped in a Vedic altar as prescribed for initiation, it is an Achala Lingam. If worshipped daily as an Ishta Lingam, it is a Chala Lingam, hence it is called Achala-chala. The Stupi (finial) is a Sthula Lingam (gross Lingam). The Shiva Lingam enshrined in the Vimana (sanctum) is a Sukshma Lingam (subtle Lingam), meaning the Shiva Lingam enshrined in the Garbhagriha (sanctum sanctorum) is a Sukshma Lingam, and the Bali Peetam (altar) in front of it is a Bhadra Lingam. This truth is revealed by Thirumooladeva Nayanar in Thirumantiram: "The pure Vimana is the gross, the good Sadashiva is the subtle, the widespread Bali Peetam is the Bhadra Lingam, those who understand will realize the divine state." The Basma Jabala Upanishad states that the Shiva Lingam should be worshipped three times a day: morning, noon, and evening. The Katha Upanishad states that the Purusha, of the size of a thumb, resides in the center of the heart. He is the Lord of past and future, and therefore fears no one. The one described as thumb-sized is Lord Shiva Himself. This is evident from the word "Ishana" which is a conventional term found in the Katha Upanishad mantra. Based on the meaning of this Katha Upanishad, which states that Lord Shiva, for the sake of His devotees, stands in the center of their hearts in the form of a Lingam, Sage Vyasa composed a Sutra in the Vedanta Sutras, interpreting the Shruti "Saptadeva Pramitaka" as "measured by the thumb-size." Appayya Dikshitar, having thoroughly researched and understood this, also stated and established the same in his Shiva Tattva Viveka. As seen in the Shruti, there are two types of Lingams: Sthavara Lingam (immovable) and Jangama Lingam (movable). The Shiva Agamas state that the Sthavara Lingam is the Shiva Lingam enshrined in a temple, and the Jangama Lingam is the Lingam in the hearts of devotees. Lingams are of two kinds: Chala Lingam and Achala Lingam. The Shiva Agama states that if the Jangama Lingam, which is a Chala Lingam, is disrespected, then the Sthavara Lingam, which is an Achala Lingam, will be of no use. A metallic Lingam is 100 times more potent than that. A gem Lingam is infinitely more potent than that. A Mani Lingam (gem-like Lingam) is a lakh times more potent than that. A Bana Lingam is a crore times more potent than that. A Rasa Lingam (mercury Lingam) is a crore times more potent than that. Even more potent than that is a Swayambhu Lingam (self-manifested Lingam). Now, Lingams are of seven types: Swayambhu Lingam, Devi Lingam, Divya Lingam, Arshaka Lingam, Manusha Lingam, Rakshasa Lingam, and Asura Lingam. Besides these Dvadasha Lingams, there are five Panchabhuta Mahalingams that can remove sins by mere thought. These are the Prithvi-maya Ekambara Lingam in Kanchipuram, the Appu-maya Jambukeswara Lingam on the banks of the Kaveri, the Agni-maya Arunachala Lingam in Arunachalam, the Vayu-maya Sri Kalahasti Lingam on the Ponmukali river, and the Akasha-maya Thirumoola Lingam in Chidambaram. The Kotirudra Samhita states that by merely seeing, hearing, speaking, or meditating on these five Lingams, one becomes pure and attains desired perfections. Furthermore, there are Upalingams for those 12 Jyotirlingams. If all of them were to be mentioned here, it would become too extensive. Why so much? There is no count for Shiva Lingams. Now, the Shiva Agamas describe the methods of worship for those who follow the paths of Charya, Kriya, Yoga, and Jnana, and how the Lord bestows grace upon them. Those who worship the Shiva Lingam enshrined in a temple as Lord Shiva Himself are followers of the Charya path and other paths. For followers of the Charya path, Lord Shiva manifests implicitly, not explicitly. Those who worship by performing Mantra Nyasa, believing that Lord Shiva, who is the Nishkala (formless) being, took the form of the Shiva Lingam and other idols through Mantras like Ishana, are followers of the Kriya path. For them, Lord Shiva, like fire appearing when churned, manifests in the desired form in those idols at those times, according to the respective Mantras, and bestows grace. Now, those who follow the Yoga path worship by believing that Lord Shiva, who pervades their hearts, also resides in the Shiva Lingam and other forms, and accepts worship through Sadhya Mantras. For them, Lord Shiva, like milk appearing immediately after milking, manifests in the desired form in those idols at those times, according to the respective Mantras, and bestows grace. Now, those who follow the Jnana path worship with love alone. For them, Lord Shiva, like the milk from the udder of a cow that remembers its calf, manifests eternally and without separation, out of abundant compassion, and bestows grace. This truth is stated in Sivagnana Siddhiyar. Sivagnana Siddhiyar explains this in the Thiruvirutham that begins with "Thirukoyil Ullirukkum Thirumeni Thannai." Among all pujas, Shiva Lingam puja is the best. The Manu Smriti states that Brahmins, who are devoted to the five yagnas, should worship the five deities: Aditya, Ambika, Vishnu, Ganesha, and Mahadeva. So, if the question arises whether these five should be worshipped equally or if one of them should be worshipped as superior to all others, then according to the rule that doubts arising in Smritis should be resolved by the Moola Pramana (root authority) of the Vedas, we can find the answer to this question in the Upanishads, which are part of the Vedas. If we look at the Svetasvatara Upanishad, it states that there is no one equal to or greater than Lord Shiva. The Veda praises Vishnu, Brahma, and others. Does it praise anyone else as much as it praises Lord Shiva? The Vedas praise other deities merely as a formality. The Atharva Sikha Upanishad, which is the crest jewel of all Upanishads, which are the heads of the Vedas, states the following: "Meditating on Ishana, who is most worthy of meditation, Brahma, Vishnu, Rudra, and Indra are born. All senses and elements are born with them. The cause and the creators of causes and those who conceived them are not born. The cause, the one endowed with all prosperity, the Lord of all, Shambhu, is the one to be meditated upon in the sky. He is the one to be meditated upon." Thus, the Atharva Sikha Upanishad explains other deities. When the Atharva Sikha Upanishad concludes, it states, "Shiva Eko Deyaka," meaning that Lord Shiva alone, who bestows happiness, is to be meditated upon. Thus, in its final part, this Upanishad excludes everyone else and states that Lord Shiva alone, among the Panchamurtis, is worthy of worship and should be worshipped. Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, and Saturn worshipped the Shiva Lingam in Thiruvidaimarudur, Madurai, Sethu, Thiruvengadu, and Vedaranyam respectively, and obtained their desired boons, were freed from suffering, and live happily. The seven Rishis worshipped Sabhanayaka in Chidambaram and obtained their abode, the Saptarishi Mandala, above all planets, and received the grace to attain liberation in the human body. Dhruva worshipped Lord Kasi Viswanatha and stands like a pivot above the Navagraha Mandala, Nakshatra Mandala, and Saptarishi Mandala, making them revolve. Adisesha worshipped Lord Shiva in Kumbakonam and bears the burden of the earth. Whoever is the lord of whomever, they have all obtained those positions through the grace of Lord Shiva. Due to the power of Shiva Lingam puja, some became Siddhas. Some became the nine Siddha Yogis like Kapila. Due to the power of Shiva Lingam puja, some have attained Shiva Salokyam, some Shiva Samipyam, Shiva Sarupyam, and Shiva Sayujyam. Now, regarding Parartha Lingam, the Karana Agama states that a Lingam specially established according to rules in villages and other places by Devas, Rishis, and humans for the sake of bestowing grace upon all souls in the world is called a Parartha Lingam. And an Ishta Lingam, which everyone worships in their own home for the protection of their soul, is called an Atmartha Lingam. Thus, the Karana Agama speaks about Atmartha Lingam and Parartha Lingam. The explanation of Atmartha Lingam is as follows: When the Acharya performs a special initiation and instructs the disciple, "Worship this daily without fail as long as you live," and the initiated one vows, "I will not consume anything without performing Shiva Puja as long as my body exists," and the Acharya gives it and the disciple receives and worships it, that Lingam is called an Ishta Lingam or Atmartha Lingam. Ishta Lingams are of six types: Shaila Lingam (stone), Ratna Lingam (gem), Lokaja Lingam (metal), Daruja Lingam (wood), Mrinmaya Lingam (clay), and Kshanika Lingam (temporary). Brahmins and the other four varnas, and the six Anulomas (children of higher caste fathers and lower caste mothers), making ten castes in total, who are without physical defects, are eligible for Ishta Lingam puja. Among these, only those who are free from illness, have the necessary resources, servants, and knowledge of Shiva Puja rules and expiatory rites, and are capable of performing them as known, can have Shaila and other Shiva Lingams consecrated and perform Shiva Puja. All others should perform Kshanika Lingam puja. The methods of examining Shiva Lingams are extensively described in the Shiva Agamas. A Kshanika Lingam is a Lingam that is discarded after worship. Kshanika Lingams are of twelve types: sand, rice, cooked rice, river clay, cow dung, butter, Rudraksha, sacred ash, sandalwood paste, Kurcha (grass bundle), flower garland, and sugar. It was stated above that the Shiva Lingam is in the form of a pedestal and a Lingam. This Shiva Lingam is one of the five Sadakhyas: Shiva Sadakhya, Amurti Sadakhya, Murti Sadakhya, Kartru Sadakhya, and Karma Sadakhya. This Shiva Lingam is the Karma Sadakhya. The Atmartha Puja performed by Shiva devotees with love is of three types: Suddha (pure), Kevala (exclusive), and Mishra (mixed). Worshipping only the Shiva Lingam is Suddha Puja. Worshipping the Shiva Lingam along with Goddess Uma is Kevala Puja. Worshipping Ganesha, Skanda, and Rishabhadeva along with Shiva and Uma, making a total of five, is also Kevala Puja. Worshipping by expanding to include Surya, Ganesha, and ending with Chandeswara, is Mishra Puja. When performing Atmartha Shiva Puja, one should worship with Shodasha Upacharas (sixteen services), or at least Dasha Upacharas (ten services), or at least Pancha Upacharas (five services). Invocation, seat, foot-washing water, offering water, sipping water, bathing, clothing, sacred thread, fragrance, flowers, incense, lamp, food offering, mouth freshener, praise, and circumambulation with prostration are called Shodasha Upacharas, the sixteen services. These sixteen services are also described in another way: seat, invocation, foot-washing water, sipping water, offering water, bathing, clothing, fragrance, flowers, food offering, betel leaf, incense, lamp, Aratrika (waving of lights), sacred ash, and sipping water from the palm. If one cannot perform these, at least ten services should be performed: seat, invocation, foot-washing water, sipping water, offering water, bathing, clothing, fragrance, flowers, and food offering. Or at least five services should be performed: fragrance, flowers, food offering, incense, and lamp. The philosophical meaning of Padya, Achamana, etc., is explained. Padya (foot-washing water) is a symbol of attaining the sacred feet of Lord Shiva. Achamana (sipping water) is a symbol of attaining His gracious divine face. Arghyam (offering water) is a symbol of attaining His sacred head, which is beyond all sounds. Flowers are a symbol of attaining the bliss of liberation. Thirumanjanam (sacred bath) is a symbol of purifying the impurities of the soul. Incense is a symbol of removing the ignorance. The lamp is a symbol of the knowledge that shines when that ignorance is removed. Food offering and mouth freshener are symbols of removing the ego-consciousness, which is the individual self, so that love arises in the mind. This truth of performing these services to Lord Shiva is known through the Sadashiva Roopa Sutras. The Sri Kalottara Agama states that detachment from the world is fragrance, abandoning dualities is flowers. Circumambulation is pervading everywhere through grace along with Shambhu, Lord Shiva. Just as a river merges with the ocean and becomes one with it, when the soul unites with Shiva, the soul attaining the qualities of Shiva is Namaskaram (prostration). Thus, the Sri Kalottara Agama explains the philosophical meaning of these. Now, the Antaryaga Puja (internal worship) is described. The Rig Veda states that when performing external puja, Antaryaga Puja must also be performed. It states that those who grasp the supreme Lord, who resides as the inner controller in people, along with Shiva-Shakti, within themselves, are the ones who consume food with their tongue. The Parasara Purana, which is an Upabrumhana (elaboration) of this mantra, also states: "He who always desires Lord Shiva, who is to be worshipped along with His gracious Shakti, within himself, consumes the nectar-filled ambrosial water with his tongue. He who does not desire Lord Shiva along with His consort Bhavani Shakti within himself, undoubtedly consumes feces with his tongue." When performing Antaryaga Puja, the eight-finger-sized lotus originating from the navel should be considered as the Nivrutti Kala (stage of cessation) in the form of Prithvi (earth). The eight petals of that lotus should be considered as the Pratishtha Kala (stage of establishment) in the form of the 23 Tattvas like Appu (water). The stamens should be considered as the Vidya Kala (stage of knowledge) in the form of Vidya Tattva. The pericarp should be considered as the Shanti Kala (stage of peace) in the form of Suddha Vidya, Ishwara, and Sadakhya. The seeds should be considered as the Shantyateeta Kala (stage beyond peace) in the form of Bindu and Nada. When the 36 Tattvas stand as the seat, for those who are clearly enlightened, as it is said that the Jiva (individual soul) is the Shiva Lingam, worshipping the soul, which has Lord Shiva as its life, as the Lingam, is Antaryaga Puja. Our Thirunavukkarasu Peruman has sung this: "Making the body a temple, the firm mind a servant, truth as purity, the mind a gem-like Lingam, with love as ghee and milk, bathing it with abundant water, we have shown this worship to Ishana." Here, "mind" is taken as a name for the soul, and the soul-Lingam is called the mind-gem-Lingam. Thus, the Antaryaga Puja, described in the Rig Veda mantra, its Upabrumhana, and the Shiva Agamas, is extensively explained in Sivagnana Siddhiyar in the Thiruvirutham that begins with "Antaryagam Thannai Mukti Sadhanama." Chapter 08 — Sakkiya Nayanar

Thiruchitrambalam. Periyapuranam, Part 922, composed by the divine Sekkizhar Peruman. Varkonda Vanamulaiyal Sarukkam, Sakkiya Nayanar Puranam. The 12th and 13th verses. He continued the duty of the service he had begun, without ceasing, and seeing that, he threw stones. He did not abandon the Buddhist attire. He understood that all actions belonged to Pashupati. Therefore, the great ascetic, with devotion, performed this discipline without fail. If one were to describe the nature of this service performed by the Nayanar, it is a great puja for the pure Lord, born of true love and performed according to the custom of the path of love. Thus, from the first day he began to worship the Shiva Lingam by throwing stones, he realized that it was the grace of the Lord that caused it to happen. His love overflowed, and he continued this as an uninterrupted, disciplined duty. Seeing that this was an act born of grace, he continued to worship the Shiva Lingam by throwing stones, without fail, according to this discipline. He did not abandon the Buddhist attire, which is a saffron robe. The great ascetic Nayanar clearly understood the truth that all actions are the gracious acts of Pashupati, Lord Shiva. Therefore, he performed this disciplined act with great love. If one were to describe the nature of this service performed by the Nayanar, it is a great puja for the pure Lord, born of true love and performed according to the custom of the path of love. Pashupati, being the Lord of all beings, we see here the nature of the Lord who guides souls according to their maturity. This practice is a practice of the path of love. There are two types of practices: the path of rules and the path of love. Sakkiya Nayanar followed the path of love. Although it seems to transcend rules, since the Lord accepts it out of love, it belongs to the path of love. The 14th verse. If one sees that even the act of throwing stones was out of love, then the sandal-beaten feet of the hunter with the bow were placed on His sacred head. Good people will consider his actions as born of love and desire them, but others will call them stones. For Arana, they are flowers. This is a very beautiful verse. If one examines the act of throwing stones and sees that it was a service performed out of love, then we have seen how the sandal-beaten feet of Thirukannappa Nayanar, the hunter with the bow, were placed on the Lord's sacred head. Here, similarly, others would call the actions of Sakkiya Nayanar, his service, as merely throwing stones. But for Lord Shiva, they become flowers. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman here uses the incident of Thirukannappa Devanayanar, the hunter with the bow, whose sandal-beaten feet touched the Lord's sacred head, as an example to illustrate this loving service performed by Sakkiya Nayanar. We saw in the Thirukannappa Devanayanar Puranam, "He replaced the flower on His head with the golden sandal that bent his foot." Since "seruppadi" (sandal-beaten feet) is mentioned, we should not only consider that, but also include the other actions and services performed by Thirukannappa Devanayanar, such as spitting mouth-water, adorning the Lord with a garland of flowers tucked in his hair, and offering meat as food. All these were accepted and approved by the Lord with great joy as appropriate. This service performed by Thirukannappa Devanayanar was praised by great saints like Manikkavasagar Peruman of Thiruvathavur. Therefore, those who do not follow the path of love will call the stones thrown by Sakkiya Nayanar as mere stones. But for the Lord, they become flowers. Thirunavukkarasu Peruman praises this loving act of Sakkiya Nayanar by saying, "He made the stones thrown by the Buddha, who never forgot, into fresh flowers." If one calls what he threw as stones, it becomes a slander against Lord Shiva. Slandering a devotee is slandering Arana. It is also slandering the compassion of Arana, which is the sweet nectar born of love. Thus ends this verse. The 15th verse. One day, by His grace, he forgot and was about to eat. "I forgot my Lord without knowing," he said, and rising with overflowing love, he quickly set out and appeared before the Lord, who wears the hide of a white elephant. One day, while following this disciplined practice, Sakkiya Nayanar, by divine grace, forgot to throw stones at the Lord and was about to eat. Such forgetfulness of a discipline is also bestowed by the Lord. As he was about to eat, he remembered. Our Sakkiya Nayanar was one who showed unwavering love. If he made a mistake now, it must surely be due to divine grace. Similarly, when he remembered again, that too happened by divine grace. Forgetting and then remembering immediately is the stage when one is about to attain the Lord's feet, meaning Sakkiya Nayanar is about to attain Sayujya Mukti (union with the Lord). That day was therefore such that divine grace caused both forgetting and remembering. Having forgotten earlier, he remembered and realized that he had erred and broken his discipline. Agitated by this, he quickly set out with great eagerness and swiftly arrived before the Shiva Lingam, where the Lord resides. The 16th and 17th verses. He picked up a stone and threw it with precision. The Lord with the frontal eye, who had seen his desire to escape the deed of eating and his fear, appeared before the devotee in the vast sky with His consort, showering gracious glances. He came riding on a bull, and the devotee, who had attained His feet, saw Him, prostrated with folded hands, and the Lord, bestowing a gracious glance, granted him eternal service in the supreme Shiva Loka and departed. These are the verses. The Nayanar, who appeared before the Lord's divine body, picked up a stone that was there, and leaving aside even the act of eating, he came running with fear and great desire to see Him. The Lord with the frontal eye, seeing him, showered gracious glances and appeared before the devotee in the vast sky with His consort, Goddess Uma. The divine Sekkizhar Peruman refers to the stone picked up by the Nayanar as "oru kal" (a stone). "Oru kal" can also mean "a unique stone." If interpreted that way, the stone the Nayanar picked up that day was a unique stone. At that moment, Lord Shiva appeared, and the Nayanar saw Him. Therefore, this act of throwing stones concludes here, and the Nayanar is about to join Shiva Loka. It was a unique stone that supported this, hence "oru kal." It was a stone that had a special significance compared to the stones the Nayanar had thrown on previous days. The Lord, who saw the Nayanar's act of throwing the stone with the intention of understanding Shiva as the means to salvation by His grace, and the desire within it, now decided that the time had come to bestow grace, having observed the Nayanar's actions until now. He appeared in the vast sky, not the physical sky but the sky of knowledge, with Goddess Uma, who is the power of grace, showering gracious glances. The speed with which the Nayanar ran signifies his maturity to escape from the cruel cycle of birth and the consequences of his actions and to seek refuge at the Lord's feet. Seeing this, the Lord appeared there. The unique act of the Lord, who appeared on a young bull with Goddess Uma. The Nayanar, who had attained the Lord's feet, saw Him, prostrated with folded hands, and the Lord, bestowing a gracious glance, granted him eternal service in the supreme Shiva Loka, which is superior to all other abodes, and departed. The Lord united the Nayanar with Shiva Loka, which is superior to all other abodes. As per the saying, "I desired to see the assembly of ancient devotees," and as stated in Kandar Kalivenba, "He sent me and united me with the ancient devotees, and uplifted me to the supreme abode," the Nayanar joined Shiva Loka and is enjoying bliss. Thus, having narrated the sacred history of Sakkiya Nayanar attaining the Lord's feet, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman concludes with the 18th verse. He sings, "Only the Effulgent One knows the intention of the flawless, excellent devotee who daily threw stones and approached the Primeval One. What else can one say? I have placed His feet on my head and, with their help, I am about to narrate the history of Sirappuli Nayanar, thereby removing evil." Thus, the divine Sekkizhar Peruman concludes the sacred history of Sakkiya Nayanar and begins to narrate the sacred history of Sirappuli Nayanar. We too will continue to contemplate.
